## A common-phase endpoint is impossible

Invariant endpoints are already excluded. A common-phase Q can be
normalized to phase zero by the allowed parameter rescaling. Up to
simultaneous cyclic type rotation, its non-invariant multiplicities
are exactly
\[
(0,0,0,4),(0,0,1,3),(0,0,2,2),(0,0,3,1),
\quad(0,1,0,3),(0,1,1,2),(0,1,2,1),(0,2,1,1).
\]
The other two cyclic orbits of four-label multiplicities are invariant
and were excluded earlier. The opposite endpoint H remains arbitrary:
all binomial(119,4)=7940751 multisets, with repetitions, are tested
for each of these eight first endpoints. No relative phase is fixed.

Set a=E_Q/eta, b=C_Q/eta, c=C_H/eta, d=E_H/eta. The old moment
determinant is
\[
ad-bc-ax-d\bar x+b\bar y+cy+x\bar x-y\bar y=0.\tag{1}
\]
Writing x=x0+beta x1 and y=y0+beta y1 over K+, the seven nonscalar
coordinates are affine linear and the scalar coordinate is one
quadric. The complete results are

| First multiplicities | Linear contradictions | Scalar contradiction | Retained determinant points |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| (0,0,0,4) | 7940721 | 1 | 29 |
| Each of the other seven rows | 7940722 | 0 | 29 |

For each first endpoint, the29 retained second endpoints are exactly
the balanced ones with phase phi in mu29. Every retained affine
linear system has rank four, and its unique determinant point is
\[
x=\phi^8,\qquad y=[10]\phi^{-5}.
\]
It makes c-bar(y)=d-x=0. The other old moment vector
(a-bar(x),b-y) is nonzero because Q is non-invariant. Hence the
original equations require epsilon=0, forbidden. No affine family
or nonzero scalar point remains. Endpoint interchange proves the
same statement for H.

## Both endpoint odd determinants are invertible

The received endpoint classification says that a zero odd character
means either an invariant endpoint or a one-phase adjacent triple/single
pattern. Both are now excluded. Its determinant classification gives
\[
D_R=C_{R,3}U_{R,2}-C_{R,2}U_{R,3}\ne0\qquad(R=Q,H).\tag{2}
\]
This is an all-endpoint conclusion, not a generic open condition.

## The reciprocal zero-character boundary is empty

Suppose pi3(epsilon)=pi3(epsilon^-1)=0. Write
epsilon=a+b+d and epsilon^-1=A+B+D with the components in G1,G2,G4.
Since epsilon is outside L, b is nonzero. The eigenvalue2 and3
components of their product1 give
\[
A=-aB/b,\qquad D=-dB/b.
\]
Here B cannot be zero, since that would make the entire inverse zero.
The eigenvalue4 component consequently gives
\[
b^2=2ad.\tag{3}
\]
Put p=C_Q,2, q=C_Q,3, u=U_Q,2, t=U_Q,3,
v=C_Q,4, w=U_Q,4, and D0=qu-pt. The eigenvalue3 components of
the second old equation and first fourth trace, with pi3(epsilon)=0,
are
\[
aq+bv+dp=0,\qquad at+bw+du=0.
\]
Using D0!=0, these give a/b=(pw-uv)/D0 and d/b=(tv-qw)/D0.
Thus(3) requires
\[
D0^2=2(pw-uv)(tv-qw).\tag{4}
\]

This is another single-endpoint test. Write
p0=T2(Q;5), q0=T3(Q;5), u0=T2(Q;17), t0=T3(Q;17),
v0=T4(Q;5), w0=T4(Q;17). In F25 coefficient codes its two sides are
\[
D0=[5]q0u0-[17]p0t0,
\]
\[
(pw-uv)(tv-qw)
=[9]p0t0v0w0-[8]p0q0w0^2-[6]u0t0v0^2+[10]u0q0v0w0.
\]
The four constants come directly from c2,c3,c4,f2,f3,f4:
c2 f4 f3 c4=[9], c2 c3 f4^2=[8], f2 f3 c4^2=[6],
f2 c3 c4 f4=[10]. They are checked in the exact projection algebra.

Normalize one Q occurrence to label0 as in the received classification.
Of266916 covering representatives,266840 have multiple phases and
D0!=0. None satisfies(4). The
[relative-field program](../../scripts/arithmetic/klein_four_reciprocal_zero_character.cpp)
and [independent absolute-field verifier](../../scripts/arithmetic/verify_klein_four_reciprocal_zero.cpp)
both cover the whole set, in opposite iteration orders. The second
uses F5[zeta]/m14, checks beta^2=beta+3 and f7(zeta)=0 with an
explicit beta vector, and reconstructs all coefficients directly.
Its [complete receipt](../../../litt3-computation-data/quadratic_scalar_reply_20260926/verify_reciprocal_zero.log)
passes. Compile both sources as C++17 with assertions enabled;
the first takes an output TSV path and the second takes no arguments.
The first's output has only its header. No scalar or moment search
is substituted for this endpoint classification.

## The oriented chart and forced moments

At least one of pi3(epsilon),pi3(epsilon^-1) is therefore nonzero.
Endpoint interchange preserves the original equations, so orient the
data with epsilon3=pi3(epsilon)!=0. The coefficient c3 is a nonzero
basis of G3 over K. Put kappa=epsilon3/c3 in K* and r=epsilon/kappa.
Then r=c3+a0+a2 c2+a4 c4 for unique a0,a2,a4 in K.

Because E_H,3=V_Q,3=0, the eigenvalue3 projections of the second
old equation and first fourth trace give exactly
\[
\eta y\,\epsilon_3=\pi_3(\epsilon C_Q),\qquad
\eta x^{625}\epsilon_3=\pi_3(\epsilon U_Q).
\]
The scale kappa cancels. Both quotients lie in K. The inverse of
the625-power map on K is the5^10-power map, since4+10=14.
This proves the stated moment formulas with no choice of a root.
All other coordinates of the original equations remain conditions;
neither the normalization nor projection is asserted to satisfy them.
