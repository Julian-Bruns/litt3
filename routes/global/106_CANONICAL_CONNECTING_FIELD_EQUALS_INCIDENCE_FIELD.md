# The canonical connecting field equals the incidence field

**Status: author proof, 2026-09-05. Not independently audited.**

For the specified canonical differential unit, the connecting coefficient
map of file 105 has exactly the same normalized image field as the original
divisor-family map, throughout the strict degree range. The reverse
inclusion follows on the generic fiber of the original incidence quotient.
It requires no Galois closure and no comparison of constants on disconnected
components of a pulled-back divisor.

## 1. Statement

Retain the canonical setup of files 101, 103, and 105. Thus X and Y are
smooth projective connected curves of genus at least two over an
algebraically closed field k; Gamma is an integral divisor in X x Y;
its normalization Z has both projections f:Z->X and g:Z->Y finite etale;
and

\[
 \mathcal O(\Gamma)=A\boxtimes B,\qquad
 M=\omega_X\boxtimes\omega_Y^{-1}.
\]

The exterior-product expression follows from the Hom(J(X),J(Y))=0
hypothesis in the original application. Fix n>0 such that M^n|Gamma is
trivial. Use the descended section t=\tau^{-n} of M^{-n}|Gamma, where
tau is the specified differential trivialization on Z. Put

\[
 L_X=\omega_X^nA^{-1},\qquad L_Y=\omega_Y^nB^{-1},
 \qquad \deg L_X>0,\quad\deg L_Y>0.
\]

Let q_0:Y->D_0 be the factorization of the original divisor-family map
y |-> [Gamma_y] through the smooth normalization of its image. Write
E=k(D_0), viewed as a subfield of L=k(Y). Let K_n be the subfield of
k(Y) generated by the projective coordinate ratios of

\[
 \delta_Y(t)\in
 H^1(X,\omega_X^{-n}A^{-1})\otimes H^0(Y,L_Y).
                                                        \tag{106.1}
\]

Theorem 103.1 ensures that these coefficients define a morphism on all of
Y; positive degree makes it nonconstant.

### Theorem 106.1

Inside k(Y), one has

\[
                              K_n=E.                   \tag{106.2}
\]

Equivalently, the connecting coefficient map and the original divisor-family
map have the same finite map from Y to their normalized image, up to the
unique isomorphism identifying their function fields. This holds for every
admissible n in the strict range. The symmetric statement holds on X.

## 2. The original incidence square

The original divisor-family construction supplies a line bundle B_0 on D_0,
an integral divisor Gamma_0 on X x D_0, and exact identities

\[
 B=q_0^*B_0,\qquad
 \mathcal O(\Gamma_0)=A\boxtimes B_0,\qquad
 \Gamma=(1\times q_0)^*\Gamma_0.                        \tag{106.3}
\]

Let Z_0 normalize Gamma_0, with maps s:Z_0->X and phi:Z_0->D_0.
The incidence descent of Proposition D.2 in
`MINIMAL_COMMON_COVER_AND_INCIDENCE_DESCENT.md` gives

\[
 Z=\operatorname{Norm}(Z_0\times_{D_0}Y),\qquad
 Z\xrightarrow{r}Z_0\xrightarrow{s}X
 \text{ finite etale},\qquad \phi r=q_0g.              \tag{106.4}
\]

In particular q_0 is separable. The map phi is also separable: the generic
algebra

\[
                   k(Z_0)\otimes_E L=k(Z)              \tag{106.5}
\]

is a field, and differential base change, using separability of Z->Y,
gives
Omega_{k(Z_0)/E} tensor_{k(Z_0)} k(Z)=Omega_{k(Z)/L}=0.

Consequently the generic fiber

\[
 C_0=\Gamma_0\times_{D_0}\operatorname{Spec}E
       \subset X_E
\]

is the finite etale E-scheme Spec k(Z_0). Its scalar extension to L is
the generic fiber C of Gamma->Y. In particular no singular-normalization
or nonreduced-fiber issue occurs on this generic fiber.

## 3. Generic-fiber descent of the specified unit

Choose a nonzero rational differential eta on D_0 and a nonzero rational
frame beta of B_0. Since q_0 is separable, q_0^*eta is a nonzero rational
frame of omega_Y. Choose also a nonzero rational differential alpha on X.
On Z_0 the ratio

\[
                         c=\frac{s^*\alpha}{\phi^*\eta}
                         \in k(Z_0)^\times             \tag{106.6}
\]

is defined because both maps are separable. It supplies the section

\[
                  v_0=c^n\alpha^{-n}
                      \in H^0(C_0,\omega_X^{-n}|_{C_0}).
                                                        \tag{106.7}
\]

The expression is independent of the chosen alpha. On Z, the inverse
canonical differential unit, after dividing its omega_Y^n factor by
(q_0^*eta)^n, is

\[
 \left(\frac{f^*\alpha}{g^*q_0^*\eta}\right)^n
                     \alpha^{-n}.
                                                        \tag{106.8}
\]

The commutative square (106.4) identifies the differential ratio in
(106.8) with r^*c. Therefore the restriction of the actual descended
section t/(q_0^*eta)^n to C is exactly the scalar extension of v_0.
This is an equality of the specified differential sections; it does not
infer equality of arbitrary componentwise trivializations.

Write F_0 for a defining section in (106.3). Over E, use beta to regard
F_0/beta as a section of A_E. It defines C_0 and gives the restriction
sequence

\[
 0\longrightarrow\omega_X^{-n}A^{-1}
 \xrightarrow{\ F_0/\beta\ }\omega_X^{-n}
 \longrightarrow\omega_X^{-n}|_{C_0}\longrightarrow0
                                                        \tag{106.9}
\]

on X_E. Let W=H^1(X,omega_X^{-n}A^{-1}). Its connecting map sends v_0
to a vector

\[
                              e_0\in W\otimes_k E.
                                                        \tag{106.10}
\]

This vector is nonzero, since v_0 is nonzero and
H^0(X_E,omega_X^{-n})=0.

After scalar extension E->L, (106.9) is exactly the generic-fiber
restriction sequence defining (106.1), when its line factor L_Y is
framed by (q_0^*eta)^n/(q_0^*beta). Naturality of connecting maps
therefore identifies the normalized generic connecting vector with
e_0 tensor_E L. In particular all its projective coordinate ratios lie
in E. We have proved

\[
                              K_n\subseteq E.           \tag{106.11}
\]

Theorem 105.1 supplies the opposite inclusion E subseteq K_n in the
strict degree range. This proves (106.2). A rational factorization through
D_0 also extends to a morphism because D_0 is a smooth projective curve
and the projective target is proper. Equality of the function fields then
identifies the smooth normalized images. \(\square\)

## 4. Meaning and boundary

The canonical connecting morphism can give different projective embeddings
as n changes, but its normalized quotient is always the old incidence
quotient in this range. It does not give a finer intermediate field. The
basepoint-free coefficient spaces and the resulting descent restrictions
on the prescribed bundles omega_Y^n remain valid additional structure.

The reverse inclusion (106.11) itself did not require strictness. Equality
does: at the zero-degree endpoint the connecting morphism is constant,
whereas the divisor-family map is nonconstant. File 103 treats that
endpoint separately by its common-projective-line theorem.

The proof uses the differential origin of t in (106.8). It makes no
claim about arbitrary P,Q and arbitrary trivializations in the general
setup of file 105. It also produces no map from Z_0 to the fixed Y beyond
the normalized fiber-product square already present in incidence descent.
