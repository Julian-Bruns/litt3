# A fixed regular frame and its single infinity transition

30 September2026.
[Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/degree_ten_fixed_frame_moments.md).
The polynomial identity P2|(Q-B0^5) makes q regular on every
finite chart, including cubic branch points. The finite estimates
for Ej are therefore direct source-root estimates. At a selected
point phi has order three. The weighted second moment cancels the
possible pole-three term of E2, leaving order at least-2. For E3
and E4 the direct order bound is-3. Thus t2,t3,t3 respectively
clear the selected finite poles.

At a pole of w of order m>=1 in this regular frame, phi has pole
5m, so the contribution to Ej has order at least(5-j)m-j.
E2 is regular; E3 has at most a simple pole and E4 at most a
triple pole. Such points occur only in h_*G. Multiplication by
v and v3 respectively removes these possible poles. Other finite
points give regular contributions. These are estimates on actual
split completed source algebras, not discriminant parity tests.

At infinity use w_s=b+L0/y, q_s=(Q-L0^5)/y5. The known local
frames give pole(q_s)=7. The difference
\[
a=(B_0-L_0)/y,\qquad w=w_s+a,
\]
has pole17, and da has pole18. In the short frame the weighted
moment identities and the actual source pole orders give
\[
\operatorname{ord}(E_{1,s})\ge7,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(E_{2,s})\ge4,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(E_{3,s})\ge1,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(E_{4,s})\ge-2.
\]
Here E1=df*z and ord(z)>=15, while df has pole8. For E2 the
sum of squares of the leading small-root coefficients cancels its
order-three term. For E3 and E4, a large source root of pole m>=2
has order bound(5-j)m-j and a small root of pole at most one has
bound7-2j. These give the last two entries. Also E0=Tr(1/phi)=0.

Expand Ej=Tr((dw_s+da)^j/phi). For j=2,3,4 the possibly smallest
order comes from j da^(j-1) E1,s, yielding25-18j. Hence in the
finite frame the bounds are-11,-29,-47. Multiplying by t2,
v t3,v3 t3 costs at most18,37,57 at infinity. The resulting
differential pole bounds are29,66,104. Division by omega0^j,
whose divisor is16j O, gives61,114,168.

When the quartic remainder is zero, E1,s is identically zero.
The smallest remaining order is then supplied by the E2,s term:
for j=3 it is-14, and for j=4 it is-32. Clearing the same finite
supports gives the scalar bounds89+deg_O(v) and123+3deg_O(v).
The fixed profiles have deg_O(v)=0 or3. The regular finite frame
does not assert invariance of Ej under a different base primitive;
the displayed transition is retained throughout.

This proof introduces no new finite coefficient enumeration. The
small-moment numerator theorem makes these rational trace conditions
accessible in the critical algebra of dimension two or three.
Their simultaneous coefficient-locus decision remains open.
