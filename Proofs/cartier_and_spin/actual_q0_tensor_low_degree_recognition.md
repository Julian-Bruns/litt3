# Proof: actual q0-tensor recognition in étale degree at most two

Version1,3 October2026. Pending independent review. Inputs are the [actual exact-root bridge](actual_q0_tensor_exact_root_self_span.md), the settled fixed-X identity Aut(X)=C3, and the explicitly stated fixed polynomials in [Cartier–Petri excess one](cartier_petri_excess_one.md). No ordinary or supersingular Jacobian assertion about the original endpoints is imported.

## 1. The elliptic quotient and its marked automorphisms

The genus31 root curve X′ has the degree-three quotient E:w³=q0(x), obtained by forgetting y. This is a smooth genus-one curve. Let I be its unique infinity point, and A,B the two points where w=0. With I as origin, div(w)=A+B−2I, so B=−A. Moreover div(x−α)=3A−3I at a root α of q0; thus A is nonzero three-torsion. The order-three automorphism w↦ζw fixes A,B,I, so E has j=0. In characteristic5 its origin-preserving automorphisms form μ6, generated by this order-three action and negation.

Every automorphism of E preserving the unordered divisor A+B fixes I. Indeed write it as translation by t followed by u∈μ6. Since uA=±A, preservation of the pair gives 2t=0 and t∈{0,2A,−2A}; the nonzero alternatives are three-torsion, so t=0. The induced action on x is therefore either identity or the reflection x↦−b−x, where q0=x²+bx+c. In our fixed polynomial b=2.

## 2. Actual degree-two spans

The common-root construction gives actual finite étale maps H1,H2:S′→X′ of the same degree d≤2. Their compositions e1,e2:S′→E pull A+B back to the SAME divisor: on X′ this divisor is the six-point zero support of w⁸θ, and the two pulled differentials are proportional.

If d=1, S′≅X′. Its elliptic degree-three quotient is unique: two distinct elliptic subfields generating k(X′) would give the Castelnuovo–Severi bound g(X′)≤3+3+(3−1)²=10, contradicting31; and an intermediate composite has index dividing3, whose degree-three case gives equal elliptic fields. Thus the elliptic fields of e1,e2 agree.

For d=2, let D be the smooth projective curve with field k(e1*E)k(e2*E)⊂k(S′). Put e=[k(S′):k(D)]. Since both elliptic maps have degree SIX, e divides6. All ramification indices in S′→E are either1 or3: the degree-two map to X′ is étale, and X′→E is a tame cubic cover. The induced local extensions in either intermediate tower have the same restriction on their ramification indices.

If e=1, the two degree-six elliptic maps generate S′, so Castelnuovo–Severi gives g(S′)≤6+6+25=37. But étale Hurwitz gives g(S′)=61. If e=2, the map S′→D is étale, because its degree is two while its local ramification indices divide3. Hence g(D)=31; its two degree-three elliptic maps generate its field, giving g(D)≤10, again impossible. If e=6, both elliptic fields already equal k(D).

It remains e=3. Then each D→E has degree TWO and is étale, because its local ramification indices divide3. Thus D has genus one, and the two maps are separable isogenies followed by translations. Equality of their pulled A+B divisors descends from S′ to D, since pullback along a finite surjective map is injective on divisors. If their order-two translation kernels H1,H2 differed, their generated group would have order FOUR. The common reduced degree-four divisor would be one orbit of that group. Its image under the first isogeny would then be a two-point coset differing by nonzero two-torsion. But its image is A+B, whose difference is nonzero three-torsion. This is impossible. The kernels therefore agree, so the two elliptic subfields agree.

In every case e1 and e2 differ by an automorphism of E preserving A+B. Section1 shows x2=x1 or x2=−b−x1. These identities already hold in k(S), since both x-coordinates belong to the original source field. In the reflection case the two original maps S→P1 have the same branch locus, namely the ten P-roots and infinity, since their X-maps are étale. The reflection fixes infinity, hence permutes the ten P-roots. It consequently lifts to a nontrivial automorphism of X acting on x, contrary to Aut(X)=C3. Thus x2=x1; the identity y2³=y1³=P(x1) implies y2/y1∈μ3. This is exactly h2=γh1. The original Y-leg, if present, is never replaced.

## 3. Automorphisms of the marked genus-eleven root

Any automorphism of Z normalizes its unique trigonal subfield k(x): two distinct degree-three rational subfields would generate k(Z), since their common composite index divides3, and would imply genus≤(3−1)²=4. An automorphism preserving kη preserves D=U+V, and induces a Möbius transformation preserving the two q0 roots and the eleven points consisting of the P-roots and infinity. The subgroup preserving a pair of points is a torus normalizer. Its finite rotation subgroup acts freely on the eleven-point complement, so has order dividing11. An order-eleven rotation would make the binary branch polynomial a sum of eleventh powers of two linear forms. Its affine second derivative would vanish in characteristic5, whereas P″≠0. Hence only a possible reflection remains.

A reflection fixing infinity would lift to X, contradicting Aut(X)=C3. Any remaining reflection takes infinity to a root r of P, and necessarily is
\[
M_r(x)=r+\frac{q0(r)}{x-r}.
\]
Invariance of the binary degree-eleven branch polynomial requires
\[
(x-r)^{11}P(M_r(x))=λP(x),\qquad λ=q0(r)P′(r).
\]
The coefficient at x=r gives P′(r)²=q0(r)⁹. The bounded new computation over F25 gives gcd(P,P′²−q0⁹)=x−(4+a), where a²=a+3. For that sole candidate the full displayed substitution has NONZERO polynomial residual. Therefore no reflection survives. The kernel on k(x) is the trigonal C3, which acts on η by a nonzero constant. Thus Aut(Z,kη)=C3. Since divη=10D, this is also Aut(Z,D).

This last computation consists only of one univariate extended gcd and one direct polynomial substitution, with direct Bezout multiplication checked; it does not factor P or search correspondences. The [source](../../scripts/genus_two/oct03_q0_root_reflection_gate.py) and [small exact receipt](../../../litt3-computation-data/oct03_q0_root_reflection_gate/bezout.json) record the polynomials, Bezout coefficients and nonzero reflection residual. In the source encoding c+5d means c+da. An initial attempted gcd-equals-one assertion failed: its useful single candidate was retained and then excluded by the full substitution. No conclusion relies on that failed assertion.

The degree-two proof uses the genus bounds and marked elliptic-isogeny argument, not the genus-eleven automorphism calculation. For arbitrary degree the elliptic joint field can have higher genus and the same bounds leave room; neither argument proves unrestricted tensor recognition.
