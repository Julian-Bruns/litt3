# Proof: the weak cyclic lift has incompatible regular-unipotent classes

Version1,3 October2026. Independent whole general-scope review PASS: [audit supplement](../../../Research/audits/CANONICAL_TEN_POSITIVE_FULL_TRACE_EXCLUSION_AUDIT_2026_10_03.md). See the [statement](../../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/local_actions/weak_cyclic_reflection_representation_obstruction.md). No computation or finite-group classification is used.

Put n=p−ONE, so FOUR divides n and n≥FOUR. Suppose the asserted projective representation exists.

## Prescribed cyclic lifting gives inverse branch classes

The weak Cp germ is y^p−y=t⁻¹, after changing the local base coordinate. Over W(k)[ζp], let ε=ζp−ONE. Normalize the local Kummer lift
\[
U^p=1+\frac{\varepsilon^p}{T}.
\tag{1}
\]
Substituting U=ONE+εY gives the special Artin–Schreier equation. The TWO generic branches, at T=ZERO and T=−ε^p, have simple pole and zero respectively. Their total tame different is TWO(p−ONE), equal to the special weak different. The different criterion makes this a smooth lift. This construction is [Obus–Wewers, Annals180(2014), printed page240, equation(4)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v180-n1-p05-p.pdf), with break ONE.

Patch THIS prescribed local lift with the ordinary C2 lift using the local-global principle; [Pop, Annals180(2014), Fact4.13(1)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v180-n1-p06-p.pdf) gives the principle for a chosen base lift. Its construction preserves the supplied local covers. The global generic cover is a connected G-cover of a genus-ZERO curve with THREE branch values of orders p,p,TWO. At the zero and pole in (1), the SAME cyclic deck generator U↦ζp U has tangent eigencharacters ζp and ζp⁻¹. Thus the two generic Cp branch classes are inverse conjugacy classes in G.

Spread the generic cover to a finitely generated characteristic-zero field and embed it into C. Branch cycles supply a,b,c generating G, with abc=ONE, a,b of order p, and c of order TWO. The class of c is the original tame inertia class, and b is conjugate to a⁻¹ in G. Separate global paths may conjugate the two p-generators differently; no claim that they lie in the same cyclic subgroup is used.

## Scott's inequality forces regular blocks and rigidity

For three operators g1g2g3=I on a D-dimensional space W, their fixed spaces satisfy
\[
\sum_i\dim W^{g_i}\le D+\dim W^{\langle g_i\rangle}+\dim(W^*)^{\langle g_i\rangle}.
\tag{2}
\]
An elementary proof uses Ui=(gi−I)W and the map Ψ(u1,u2,u3)=u1+g1u2+g1g2u3. Its rank is D minus the common fixed-space dimension in W*: an annihilator of its image fixes g1, then g2, then g3 successively. Its kernel contains the image of w↦((g1−I)w,(g2−I)w,(g3−I)w), of dimension D minus the common fixed-space dimension in W. Counting dimensions gives (2).

Lift the projective images of a,b uniquely to unipotent matrices A,B. In characteristic p their projective pth powers are scalar and scalars have unique pth roots. The normalized eigenvalues are ONE, hence det A=det B=ONE. Put C=(AB)⁻¹. Its repeated eigenvalue λ has multiplicity n−ONE and its other eigenvalue is −λ. Since n is even,
\[
\lambda^n=-1.
\tag{3}
\]
The linear group generated by the tuple is irreducible, even though the original projective representation may have kernel.

Apply (2) to End(V), with D=n². Common invariants and dual invariants each have dimension ONE by Schur and the trace pairing. The centralizer of C has dimension (n−ONE)²+ONE. Each of A,B has centralizer dimension at least n. Consequently
\[
2n+(n-1)^2+1=n^2+2
\]
is both a lower and upper bound for the centralizer sum. Both unipotents have minimal centralizer dimension n, so each is one regular Jordan block Jn. In particular neither projective p-generator has been killed by the representation.

For any other irreducible product-ONE triple in the same three individual conjugacy classes, apply (2) to Hom(V,V'). The individual fixed dimensions again sum to n²+TWO. The common invariant dimensions in the two opposite Hom spaces therefore sum to at least TWO. A nonzero intertwiner is invertible by irreducibility. The tuple is simultaneously rigid.

## The tuple is over F_(p²), and its projective image is determinant one

Equation(3) puts λ among the 2n-th roots of unity. Since 2n divides p²−ONE, all these roots belong to F_(p²). Entrywise p²-Frobenius therefore preserves all three conjugacy classes: regular unipotent blocks and the exact tame eigenvalues. Rigidity gives simultaneous Frobenius conjugacy. Lang's theorem for GLn converts this into a basis fixed by p²-Frobenius. Thus A,B,C are all in SLn(F_(p²)).

Let Q be their projective image. It is generated by the images of A,B, both of order p. The determinant-mod-nth-powers homomorphism
\[
Q\longrightarrow \mathbf F_{p^2}^{\times}/(\mathbf F_{p^2}^{\times})^n
\]
has target of order n=p−ONE. It kills both generators, since p does not divide n. Hence Q⊂PSLn(F_(p²)), without any assumption on G's quotients.

The inverse conjugacy b∼a⁻¹ in G passes to Q. An SLn(F_(p²)) lift of the projective conjugating element gives an honest conjugacy B=H A⁻¹ H⁻¹: the possible scalar must be ONE, because both matrices are unipotent.

## The companion determinant contradicts that inverse conjugacy

Write AB=ρ(I−TWO E), where E is the rank-ONE idempotent on its simple eigenspace. Here ρ^n=−ONE. Put M=ρB⁻¹, so M−A has rank ONE, and A,M have characteristic polynomials (x−ONE)^n and (x−ρ)^n.

There is a common companion basis over F_(p²). To see this, write M−A=vℓ. Irreducibility makes v A-cyclic and ℓ A-cyclic in the dual: a proper cyclic span, or a nonzero common kernel of all ℓA^j, would be invariant under both A and M. Choose q annihilated by ℓ,ℓA,…,ℓA^(n−TWO), but not by ℓA^(n−ONE). The vectors q,Aq,…,A^(n−ONE)q are independent, by applying those rows successively to a dependence in descending degree. On the first n−ONE such basis vectors A and M agree. Thus their matrices are simultaneous companion matrices.

Let e be the first vector in this companion basis. For M, its positive cyclic basis has determinant ONE. Since B=ρM⁻¹, the determinant of B's cyclic basis is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\det(e,Be,\ldots,B^{n-1}e)
&=\rho^{n(n-1)/2}(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}(\det M)^{-(n-1)}\\
&=-\rho^{n(n-1)/2}=\rho^{n/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
In the first equality multiply all columns of the negative M-cyclic basis by M^(n−ONE), then reverse the columns. FOUR divides n, so n(n−ONE)/TWO is even; also det M=ρ^n=−ONE and n−ONE is odd. For the final equality put κ=ρ^(n/TWO). Then κ²=−ONE and n−ONE≡THREE moduloFOUR, so −κ^(n−ONE)=κ.

Thus κ has exact order FOUR. The cyclic-basis matrix in (4) conjugates B to A. Any other conjugator differs by a centralizer element of the regular block A, whose determinant is an nth power. On the other hand A and A⁻¹ have an SLn conjugator: the inverse cyclic-basis determinant is
\[
(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}(\det A)^{-(n-1)}=1.
\]
Therefore the previously obtained determinant-ONE inverse conjugacy between A and B requires κ to be an nth power in F_(p²)×.

Its nth-power subgroup has order
\[
\frac{p^2-1}{p-1}=p+1\equiv2\pmod4.
\]
It contains no element of order FOUR, contradicting (4). This proves the theorem.

The congruence p≡ONE moduloFOUR is used twice: the inverse cyclic-basis sign is ONE, and the nth-power subgroup has no order-FOUR element. No result for p≡THREE moduloFOUR is asserted. For characteristic FIVE the theorem applies to the original target group and coefficient representation of the actual canonical-TEN positive full trace. Both original endpoint maps remain on their SAME source; this new argument uses their accepted trace consequences, not a one-leg replacement.
