## 1. Exact hypothesis and the one shared pairing

Let k be algebraically closed, W,T smooth projective connected curves,
g(T)≥2, H=H₁×H₂ a finite group acting on W, and r:W→T NONCONSTANT.
Neither freeness nor separability is assumed until §5. Assume

    r_*(a_*−1)(b_*−1):J(W)→J(T) is zero for every a∈H₁,b∈H₂.       (1)

For an Abel embedding j:T→J(T), set A=j r. The mixed difference

    c(a,b)=A(abw)−A(aw)−A(bw)+A(w)                              (2)

is independent of w: its induced Jacobian homomorphism is(1).
Splitting the mixed difference for aa' into that for a at a'w and
that for a' at w gives c(aa',b)=c(a,b)+c(a',b); similarly in b.
Thus c:H₁^ab×H₂^ab→J(T)(k) is biadditive, independent of j.
Its values are killed by both element orders and both abelianization
exponents. This uses actual Jacobian points, not division by group
orders or reducedness of a torsion group scheme.

Write K_i=Stab_H_i(r), D_i=[H_i,H_i], E_i=[D_i,D_i]D_i²,
where D_i² is generated by squares. Then

    c(D₁,H₂)=c(H₁,D₂)=0,           c(K₁,H₂)=c(H₁,K₂)=0.           (3)

The second assertion uses commutativity of the two factors.

**Zero-pairing lemma.** If c(a,b)=0, either ra=r or rb=r, except
possibly when T is hyperelliptic. With its involution i, the extra
alternative is

    rab=i r,              rb=i ra,              ra=i rb.        (4)

In that alternative ra²=r and rb²=r. Indeed(2) means

    r(abw)+r(w) ∼ r(aw)+r(bw) in Pic²(T).

On a nonhyperelliptic curve equivalent effective degree-two divisors
are equal. Their two unordered matchings give the closed equalizers
ra=r or rb=r. On a hyperelliptic curve unequal equivalent pairs both
belong to the unique degree-two pencil, giving the additional closed
locus(4). These finitely many closed loci cover irreducible W, so one
alternative is global. Compose(4) with a or b to obtain the square
identities. This also works in characteristic2: a purely inseparable
degree-two map to P¹ would make the positive-genus curve rational,
so its hyperelliptic involution exists.

## 2. Retained universal axis and solvable-quotient theorem

Under §1, either one entire H_i fixes r (axis descent), or:

- for NONHYPERELLIPTIC T, both D_i fix r;
- for HYPERELLIPTIC T, each D_i-orbit of r has at most two elements.
  Its stabilizer L_i≤D_i has index≤2 and contains E_i. Thus r
  descends through W/(L₁×L₂), and through W/(E₁×E₂).

Consequently either a perfect factor, or gcd(|H₁|,|H₂|)=1, forces
axis descent. There is no restriction on the characteristic or on
separability of r. The quotient H_i/D_i is abelian; H_i/E_i is
metabelian with elementary-abelian2 derived subgroup. The subgroups
E_i are characteristic in H_i. These statements have no degree bound.

For the proof, assume neither factor fixes r and choose b∈H₂ with
rb≠r. Equations(3) and the zero-pairing lemma say every a∈D₁ fixes r
in the nonhyperelliptic case. In the hyperelliptic case ra is one of
r,i rb; hence the D₁-orbit has size≤2. Its stabilizer L₁ is normal
in D₁ with quotient trivial or C₂, so E₁⊂L₁. Repeat for D₂.
If H₁ is perfect, D₁=H₁ and no index-two subgroup is possible;
the supposed non-axis alternative is a contradiction. The same
argument applies if H₂ is perfect.

For coprime orders, biadditivity gives c=0. Choose a∉K₁,b∉K₂.
Only(4) could remain, forcing a²∈K₁,b²∈K₂. At least one group
has odd order; its chosen element is a power of its square, a
contradiction. This preserves the earlier coprime theorem, including
arbitrary nonconstant r. Its proof is different from the
[cyclic-atlas norm argument](38_COPRIME_DESCENT_THROUGH_THE_CYCLIC_ATLAS.md).

## 3. Exact nonhyperelliptic kernels and the stronger residual group

Assume T is nonhyperelliptic and neither whole factor fixes r.
For every b∉K₂, the zero-pairing lemma and(3) give

    ker c(−,b)=K₁;     likewise ker c(a,−)=K₂ for every a∉K₁.     (5)

Thus K_i are normal, Q_i=H_i/K_i are nontrivial abelian, and the
full radicals of c are exactly K_i. The descended pairing on
Q₁×Q₂ is nonzero on EVERY pair of nonzero elements.

**Version2 refinement.** Both Q_i are elementary abelian for ONE
COMMON prime ℓ. Choose a∈Q₁ of prime order ℓ. For every b∈Q₂,

    c(a,ℓb)=c(ℓa,b)=0.

Injectivity in the second slot for this nonzero a gives ℓQ₂=0.
Choose a nonzero b∈Q₂, now of order ℓ. The symmetric argument
gives ℓQ₁=0. This is stronger than merely saying Q_i are abelian;
no prime-to-characteristic hypothesis was introduced.

The full-radical identification is ONLY for the non-axis case.
If an entire factor fixes r, c=0 and both full radicals are whole
factors, even if the other stabilizer K_i is proper.

## 4. Uniform index bounds: two distinct geometric hypotheses

Put g=g(T), d=18g(g−1), B=binomial(d²−1,2). Under §1:

- if T is nonhyperelliptic, then in the non-axis case |Q_i|<B;
- if char(k)≠2, T is hyperelliptic of genus g≥3, and J(T)
  contains NO elliptic abelian subvariety, then [H_i:K_i]<2B.
  In this second case K_i need not be normal; no exact radical
  identification or elementary-abelian quotient is asserted.

The group and index arguments use neither freeness nor separability
of r. The finite etale application, with those extra hypotheses,
is stated separately in §5.

### Difference-surface geometry

Use reduced images throughout. Let S=T−T⊂J(T) and
∂:T×T→S, (x,y)↦j(x)−j(y).

For NONHYPERELLIPTIC T, ∂ induces an isomorphism
(T×T)\Δ→S\{0}. Indeed equality of nonzero differences gives equality
of ordered pairs by the degree-two-divisor argument. The two Abel
tangent lines are distinct because the canonical map is an embedding
in every characteristic. The restricted proper map is universally
injective and unramified, hence a closed immersion onto its reduced
image. Thus S is smooth away from0.

This is the characteristic-independent proof of the near-match
[de Gaay Fortman–Schreieder, Lemma2.4, p.1411](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X25007171).
That paper works over C; its later multiplication-map and Gauss-map
claims are NOT imported into positive characteristic.

For HYPERELLIPTIC T, let L₂ be the hyperelliptic pencil. Then

    S=W₂(T)−L₂,           [x−y]=[x+i(y)−L₂].

The Abel map Sym²T→W₂(T) has the single positive-dimensional fiber
|L₂|=P¹ and is an isomorphism elsewhere. Outside it h⁰(D)=1;
the differential kernel has dimension h⁰(D)−1, including repeated
points, by [Milne, Theorem5.1(b), pp.16–19](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/JVs.pdf).
Properness and universal injectivity give the stated isomorphism.
Consequently S is smooth off0 and all nonzero fibers of ∂ are finite.

In BOTH cases, the curves j(T)−j(x) through0 have tangent lines
spanning T₀J(T). Since g>2=dim S, the origin is singular. It is
the unique singular point, so Stab(S)(k)={0}. This is a statement
about geometric translation points, not a stabilizer group scheme.

Embed J(T) by 3Θ. On T×T the theorem of the square gives

    ∂*Θ ≡ (g−1)(F₁+F₂)+Δ,       (∂*Θ)²=2g(g−1),

using F_i²=0, F₁F₂=F_iΔ=1 and Δ²=2−2g. The generic degree of ∂
is1 in the nonhyperelliptic case and2 in the hyperelliptic case.
Hence deg_(3Θ)S is respectively18g(g−1) or9g(g−1), in either
case at most d. The nonhyperelliptic numerical class is
[S]=2Θ^(g−2)/(g−2)!; only the degree bound is needed below.

### Finite translations on a curve in S

Let a finite subgroup A≤J(T)(k) preserve an integral curve D⊂S.
Then |A|<B. For A={0} this is immediate. Otherwise choose0≠a∈A.
Since S+a≠S, D is an irreducible component of S∩(S+a).
Projective Bezout, including its component bound for excess
intersections, gives deg D≤d². Equivalently project the two
surfaces generically to P³ with distinct images, preserving the
degree of D, and use ordinary Bezout there.

The normalization D~ has genus≥2. In the nonhyperelliptic case
the inverse of ∂ off0 gives a nonconstant map D~→T via at least
one projection. This forces genus≥2 even if inseparable: remove
Frobenius, then use separable Riemann–Hurwitz. In the hyperelliptic
case this instead uses the NO-ELLIPTIC hypothesis: an abelian variety
contains no rational curves, and a genus-one normalization would
map onto a translate of an elliptic abelian subvariety.

A birational plane projection of D bounds g(D~)≤(deg D−1)(deg D−2)/2
≤B. The translations lift faithfully and freely to D~, since a
fixed point would map to a translation-fixed point in J(T).
The constant-group quotient is etale even when char(k) divides |A|.
Riemann–Hurwitz gives

    0<g(D~)−1=|A|(g(D~/A)−1),       |A|≤g(D~)−1<B.               (6)

### Apply the translation bound to the actual group action

For b∉K₂ let δ_b(w)=j(rbw)−j(rw). It is nonconstant: a constant
nonzero value would trap (rb,r)(W) in a finite fiber of ∂, and
constant zero would mean rb=r. Its image D_b⊂S is a curve, and

    δ_b(aw)=δ_b(w)+c(a,b).

Thus im c(−,b) preserves D_b and has order<B. In the nonhyperelliptic
case(5) identifies this image with Q₁, proving its bound.

In the hyperelliptic case let R₁=ker c(−,b). Then K₁⊂R₁ and
[H₁:R₁]<B. The zero-pairing lemma says the R₁-orbit of r lies
in {r,i rb}; hence [R₁:K₁]≤2 and [H₁:K₁]<2B. This includes the
zero-image case [H₁:R₁]=1. Repeat for the other factor. Full
radicals contain K_i but are not identified with them here.

## 5. The same actual etale maps, and the remaining limits

Now suppose H acts FREELY and the original r:W→T is finite ETALE.
Every quotient used in §§2–4 is an actual intermediate of W→W/H.
Its map from W and its map to W/H are finite etale. If r factors
through it, the map to T is also finite etale: its function field
is a separable intermediate and 1=e(r)=e(first)e(second).

In particular, with V=W/H and U=W/(K₁×K₂), the non-axis bounds give

    W→U→T,       U→V,      all finite etale.

For nonhyperelliptic T, U/V is connected Galois with elementary-
abelian group Q₁×Q₂ for the same prime ℓ, and deg(U/V)<B².
For hyperelliptic T under §4, deg(U/V)<4B²; this need not be
Galois. If both H_i are abelian it is abelian Galois with group
(H₁/K₁)×(H₂/K₂). There is NO bound on deg(U/T) independent of V.

There is one additional nonhyperelliptic factorization: inverse
ordered-pair recovery gives W→D~_b→T for each of the maps r,rb.
Both factors are etale because the original map is etale. This
ordered-pair recovery is NOT asserted in the hyperelliptic case,
where ∂ has generic degree2; the quotient U supplies the retained
actual map there.

For an application of(1), let P_mix⊂J(W) be generated by the images
(a_*−1)(b_*−1)J(W). It suffices that Hom(P_mix,J(T))=0; over Fbar_p
this follows if P_mix is ordinary and J(T) has no ordinary simple
isogeny factor. Neither this ordinarity nor mixed vanishing is automatic.
The [two-prime rectangles](GENERAL_TWO_PRIME_RECTANGLES_AND_AXIS_DESCENT.md)
must still prove their own properness/ordinarity inputs.

The [explicit C₃×C₃ etale example](NONZERO_MIXED_TORSION_CONSTANT_IN_AN_ACTUAL_ETALE_C3_PRODUCT.md)
realizes nonzero c with trivial radicals, so unrestricted axis descent
is false. Positive-dimensional W₂-translate intersections alone would
not suffice either: for c=[x−y], x+T⊂W₂∩(W₂+c). The proof needs
the actual translation group preserving the SAME difference curve.
Finally g≥3 and the hyperelliptic no-elliptic hypothesis are substantive:
for genus2, S=J(T), even if J(T) is simple. No cofinality, common core
or exclusion of arbitrary common covers follows.
