# Finite bad cosets for cyclic triples of genus-two curves

Over k=F̄_5, let Y have genus two and q:U→Y be a connected
finite étale cyclic cover of degree three. No ordinariness
assumption is imposed on either curve.
On scalar Frobenius twists put
\[
J=J(U^{(1)}),\quad A=\operatorname{im}(q^{(1)*}),\quad
P=(\ker\operatorname{Nm}_{q^{(1)}})^0,\quad Q=J/A,
\]
with quotient \(\pi:J\to Q\), restriction \(\rho=\pi|_P\), and
\[
B=\{z\in Q:\pi^{-1}(z)\subset\Theta_U\}.
\]
Then B is a finite closed set of geometric points and 0∉B. Every
other coset has a nonempty open locus where H^0(B_{1,U}⊗N)=0. This
includes all geometric points, without a torsion-order restriction.

For α∈P(k), define
\[
\delta_\alpha=\operatorname{generic}_{L\in J(Y^{(1)})}
h^0(U^{(1)},B_{1,U}\otimes\alpha\otimes q^{(1)*}L).
\]
Only finitely many α have δ_α>0, and every such δ_α is exactly one.
The latter assertion follows from the genus-four theorem and needs
no ordinariness hypothesis. Finiteness now has the same scope.
Let Λ_0 be the finite subgroup
generated by those exceptional α of order prime to five. For any
finite prime-to-five character subgroup Λ⊂P(k), form its connected
abelian étale character cover on U^(1) and untwist to b_Λ:W_Λ→U.
Then
\[
\operatorname{generic}_L
h^0(W_\Lambda^{(1)},B_{1,W_\Lambda}\otimes(qb_\Lambda)^{(1)*}L)
=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}\delta_\alpha.
\]
These generic defects are uniformly bounded over Λ and constant
whenever Λ contains Λ_0. The constant may be positive; each Λ has
its own generic open set.

This does not eliminate isolated bad cosets, imply ordinarity of
arbitrary covers, bound their a-numbers, or construct a second map.
