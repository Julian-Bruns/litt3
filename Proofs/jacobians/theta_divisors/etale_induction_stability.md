# Proof: etale induction preserves generic stability

[Statement](../../../Theorems/jacobians/theta_divisors/etale_induction_stability.md).
The separate short addendum to the
[bounded audit](../../../Research/audits/RAYNAUD_JUMP_DIVISOR_AUDIT_2026_09_15.md)
passes all characteristics, the proper-kernel test, and descent.
This generalizes the degree-zero line proof in the earlier restricted
theta route; it does not use a claim that tensor products of arbitrary
stable bundles are stable.

Take a connected Galois closure pi:W->X of f, with group G and
distinct sheets r_i:W->Z. Joint minimality makes the maps
h_i=g r_i:W->Y pairwise distinct: agreeing on both k(X) and k(Y)
would make the two embeddings agree on k(Z).

Their pullbacks h_i^*:J(Y)->J(W) are also distinct. Indeed equality
would make the two Abel maps a_Y h_i and a_Y h_j differ by a constant
translation. Translation would then induce an automorphism v of Y
acting trivially on its Jacobian. Such an automorphism is the identity
in every characteristic. One way to check the last assertion is to
take the finite cyclic group generated by v and use rational l-adic
cohomology, l different from the characteristic: the quotient curve
has the same H1 as Y because all invariants are fixed, hence the
same genus. Riemann--Hurwitz for the separable quotient of a curve
of genus at least2 forces its degree to be1. This also covers
characteristic2, where differential faithfulness alone is insufficient.

Write V_W=pi^*V, stable of rank r. Flat base change gives
\[
\pi^*(V\otimes f_*g^*M)=\bigoplus_i(V_W\otimes h_i^*M).\tag{1}
\]
All factors are stable of the same slope. If the i-th and j-th
factors are isomorphic, their determinants imply
\[
[r]\bigl(h_i^*-h_j^*\bigr)(M)=O.\tag{2}
\]
For i!=j this is a proper closed kernel: multiplication by the
positive integer r is a finite isogeny and cannot kill a nonzero
homomorphism of abelian varieties. This includes r divisible by
the characteristic. Avoid these finitely many kernels. Then every
factor in(1) is stable and the factors are pairwise nonisomorphic.

Equation(1) implies that V tensor f_*g^*M is semistable. If a
nonzero proper subbundle had equal slope, its pullback would be a
subobject of this direct sum in the abelian category of semistable
bundles of that slope. A subobject of a direct sum of pairwise
nonisomorphic simple objects is a sum of some of them. Descent
makes the chosen subset G-invariant. G acts transitively on the
sheets, so that subset is empty or all. This proves stability.
No averaging by |G| or semisimplicity of modular representations
is used.

For the Frobenius application, the etale Cartesian Frobenius square
identifies pi^(1)*B_X with B_W. The bundle B_W is stable for every
smooth curve W of genus at least2; this is Joshi's theorem, with
a direct proof in
[Tong, Section1.2.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.2046).
Thus the hypothesis on V holds for B_X. Apply the same argument
with the legs reversed for B_Y. Rank and slope follow from the
projection formula and Riemann--Hurwitz. No property of the
canonical theta section follows merely from this stability proof.

## Strong stability, the converse, and the actual algebraic comparison

Take V=O in the preceding argument, with the legs exchanged. On the
individual Galois closure of g, E_eta=g_*f*L_eta is a direct sum of
the distinct lines h_i*L_eta. For every Frobenius power p^e these
lines remain distinct: multiplication by p^e cannot kill the nonzero
difference homomorphism h_i*-h_j*. Its kernel is a proper closed
subset, which does not contain the geometric generic parameter.
The same transitive descent proof therefore makes every Frobenius
pullback of E_eta stable. No simultaneous avoidance by a k-rational
point is claimed. The earlier Abel-map argument already treats
hyperelliptic X and characteristic two, so neither needs exclusion.

Conversely, if the span is not jointly minimal, let D be the smooth
projective curve with field k(X)k(Y) strictly contained in k(Z).
All maps in Z->D->X,Y are finite étale: they are intermediate
covers of the two actual étale legs. Write h:Z->D. The unit
O_D->h_*O_Z has locally free cokernel, even in p-divisible degree,
since étale locally it is the diagonal inclusion O->O^deg(h).
After twisting by f_D*L_eta and pushing down by g_D this gives
a proper degree-zero subbundle
\[
(g_D)_*f_D^*L_\eta\ \subsetneq\ g_*f^*L_\eta.
\]
Étale Riemann--Roch gives degree zero on both sides. Thus E_eta
is not stable. This proves the exact characterization.

For the one-step construction use E=E_eta on Y^(1)_Omega, and the
normalized Poincaré line P on Y^(1) times S. Set N=pr_Y*E tensor P.
For every M, both N_M and F_Y*N_M are stable of degree zero and
rank m>1. They have no H0: a nonzero section would give, after
saturation, a nonnegative-degree line subbundle of a slope-zero
stable bundle of larger rank. Their H1 dimensions are the constant
m(g(Y)-1). Frobenius pullback here is along the curve factor;
the same distinct-line proof applies to relative Frobenius.

Push forward the actual relative-Frobenius unit sequence
\[
0\to N\to(F_Y\times1)_*(F_Y\times1)^*N
\to B_Y\otimes N\to0
\]
along the projection to S. Cohomology and base change make the
two R1 images locally free of rank m(g(Y)-1), because their R0
images vanish and the fiber H1 dimensions are constant. The unit
induces phi_eta between them. The long exact sequence and derived
base change give the displayed exact sequence on every fiber.
All objects are algebraic over Omega. No formal matrix has been
algebraized by assumption, and no iterated-Frobenius limit replaces
this single comparison.

These refinements come from returned Pro report06, Sections8--9,
with its unnecessary nonhyperelliptic and genus-two restrictions
removed using the existing general proof. Its genus-two matrix is
the special case of rank m. The source and both maps have not been
replaced. The remaining problem is the actual determinant, not the
existence of the comparison morphism or stability of its curve bundle.
