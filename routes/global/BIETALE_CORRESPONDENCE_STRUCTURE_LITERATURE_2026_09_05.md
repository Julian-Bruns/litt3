# Structure of actual bi-etale correspondences — 5 September 2026

Bounded primary-source review. No solution of Litt Problem 3. Throughout,
curves are smooth, projective and connected over an algebraically closed
field, and endpoint genera are at least two. The observations explicitly
marked deductions below are elementary consequences, not newly located
literature theorems.

## 1. Operations that preserve both maps

Mochizuki, *Correspondences on Hyperbolic Curves*, Definitions 1.1–1.5,
defines ordered finite-etale spans, transpose, and composition by fiber
product. The source may be disconnected. These operations make common
finite-etale covering an equivalence relation. His paper assumes
characteristic zero; these definitions and the fiber-product argument
work in every characteristic.

[Author's primary PDF](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/Correspondences%20on%20Hyperbolic%20Curves.pdf).

**Deductions for this project.** Given X <- Z -> Y and Y <- T -> V,
every connected component of Z x_Y T maps finite etale and surjectively
to both X and V. This fiber product is already smooth and normal:
normalization is unnecessary in the genuinely etale setting. Replacing
Z by any finite-etale cover W of Z preserves both legs.

Do not mistake transpose for an inverse in the category of spans.
For a degree-d cover f:Z -> X, the span X <- Z -> X with both maps f
has degree d on both legs and is not the identity span if d>1.
A groupoid obtained by formally inverting covering maps, or identifying
refinements, forgets this information. Its connectivity detects the
existence relation; individual arrows require further bookkeeping.

## 2. The one-base Galois category, and its precise limitation

Stacks Theorem 58.6.2 identifies FEt(X) with finite continuous
pi_1(X)-sets. Connected covers correspond to open subgroups H;
normal open subgroups give Galois covers. Section 58.7 gives Galois
domination of every connected finite-etale cover, and simultaneous
domination of finitely many Galois covers of the same base.

[Theorem 58.6.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BND),
[Galois covers](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03SF),
[finite limits and base change](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BL6).

**Deductions.** For G=pi_1(X), replace H by its normal core
intersection_{g in G} gHg^{-1}. Thus a cover is a quotient W/H' of
one finite Galois cover W -> X. Fiber products correspond to products
of G-sets; their connected components correspond to orbits (or double
cosets for transitive factors).

This describes all covers of X, but does not specify a second geometric
map to Y. An actual span gives open embeddings of the same pi_1(Z)
into pi_1(X) and pi_1(Y). An abstract isomorphism of open subgroups
does not, by this Galois-category theorem, realize those groups as the
fundamental group of one curve with both requested maps.

Even two actual spans with fixed endpoints need not have a common
refinement commuting with both legs. For a curve X with a nonidentity
automorphism sigma, the spans (id,id) and (id,sigma) cannot have such
a refinement: their left legs force its two maps to X to agree, while
the right legs force sigma to fix that surjective map. Fiber products
over just X therefore do not make the category of two-map spans
cofiltered. Covers refining one already fixed span do have common
refinements, by fiber products over its source Z.

## 3. The exact finite simultaneous-Galois criterion

Krishnamoorthy, *Correspondences without a core*, published Lemmas
4.2–4.3: a hyperbolic-source correspondence admits a core if and only
if some finite dominating curve is Galois over both endpoints.
The proof uses the finite group generated by the two deck groups
inside the automorphism group of the dominating hyperbolic curve;
conversely it takes the Galois closure over the core. Lemma 4.3 also
works over an algebraic closure of a finite field without hyperbolicity.

Section 4 alternates Galois closure over the two endpoints. Lemma 4.6
and Corollary 4.7 identify the resulting field independently of which
endpoint starts the procedure: it is the minimal extension of k(Z)
normal over both endpoint fields. For coreless spans the tower never
stabilizes. Corollary 5.8 identifies this field with that of the generic
graph. These are precise structural results, not a termination heuristic.

[Published primary PDF, Sections 4–5](https://msp.org/ant/2018/12-5/ant-v12-n5-p05-p.pdf).

**Etale deduction.** Every finite stage remains etale over both endpoints:
take the etale Galois closure over one endpoint, then compose its etale
map to the previous source with the other endpoint map. If a finite
simultaneous normal extension exists, all these stages lie in it, so
the tower stabilizes. Therefore a given bi-etale span has a finite
**bi-etale** simultaneous-Galois refinement exactly when it has a core.
The source paper's finite closure over the coarse core might initially
be ramified; using the alternating tower is what supplies this stronger
etale formulation without that oversight.

Consequently, controlling all spans whose two legs are simultaneously
Galois, and all their intermediate quotients, only covers the
core-bearing class. It does not exhaust arbitrary common covers.
Taking Galois closure of just one leg is always legitimate and keeps
the second leg etale, but generally makes no Galois assertion about it.

## 4. What remains and what was searched

The useful organizational split is therefore exact: finite simultaneous
deck-group envelopes versus the canonical infinite alternating tower.
Finite Galois quotients generate one-base covers; this is different from
cofinal simultaneous Galois refinements of two-map spans. Any proposed
reduction to a class of covering groups must explain its behavior under
these alternating steps or independently handle coreless spans.

The primary-source searches included Galois categories, bi-etale spans,
common envelopes, commensurators, and a date-restricted search for
June–5 September 2026. No new general two-map generation or termination
theorem was located. This is a search result, not an assertion that none
exists. The characteristic-zero commensurator classification supplies
no characteristic-five realization theorem for arbitrary geometric
profinite-group correspondences.
