# Proof: the quotient-multiplication sequence

This integrates the third manually returned quadratic-flag reply.
All maps and line subbundles are on the specified actual source.
The construction below is on the first Frobenius twist; it is NOT
the multiplication on the Frobenius-pulled-back augmentation ideal.

## Image and discriminant

Let tilde S be the inverse image of S in F_*O_Y. Multiply two
representatives and reduce modulo tilde S. Changing a representative
by a Frobenius constant changes its product by tilde S. This defines
mu:Sym^2 S->B_Y/S. At P_*, use primitive fiber orders(3,4) and
s=t^5. Modulo S the products t^6,t^7,t^8 give s[t],s[t^2],s[t^3].
The image is exactly s(B_Y/S). The unit minors persist for regular
horizontal lifts of these fiber generators. Away from P_*, one
primitive has order one. The other has order2,3 or4; quotient
multiplication has rank two in the first two cases. Order4 would
give a raw Wronskian zero of order at least two, excluded by the
simple residual zeros. Thus the image is exactly(B_Y/S)(-P1).
Taking determinants gives H=L^4 omega^-2(2P1).

For a generic calculation use primitives f=t, g=t^2+b*t^3+c*t^4,
where b,c are Frobenius constants, t^5=s. The quotient matrix on
f^2,2fg,g^2 is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}-b&2&s(c^2-2b^2c)\\-c&2b&1-2bc^2s\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A kernel vector is(2-bc^2s-b^3cs, b-c^3s, 2(c-b^2)). Its
discriminant is c(1-2b^5s+c^5s^2). The raw Wronskian is
2+bt+2ct^2, whose fifth power is twice the second factor.
These polynomial identities pass the focused Sage check.
The factor c detects the alternating pairing. Dividing the
multiplication matrix by the common equation at P1 removes its
fourth power from the discriminant of the kernel minors.
Hence the residual divisor is exactly D_S+Q11+Q21.

## Isotropic factor and its contacts

For isotropic S, the nowhere-zero binary quadratic kernel is
everywhere a square, since the characteristic is not two.
It gives a saturated line kappa. Pairing identifies B_Y/S with
S^vee omega. The identity <[r^2],[r]>=C(r^2 dr)=0 gives
bar mu(v^2)=alpha(v)J(v), where J is the alternating identification
and alpha:S->omega L^-1(-P1) is surjective. Its kernel is
kappa=L^2 omega^-1(P1). This determines the line itself, without
an unspecified two-torsion factor.

At P_* the primitive kernel line has order4, hence its evaluated
form has order3. At each simple residual zero it has primitive
order3, hence form order2. These already total7, equal to the
adjunction degree of a degree-minus-one line, so there are no
further zeros. For an orbit line lambda_i, multiplication
lambda_i^2->P_i/lambda_i has divisor R_i^(1): near R_i the
primitive is t^3 and its square is s[t]. Composing with the
pairing quotient adds E_i. Factoring instead through alpha adds
q^(1)*P1 and the intersection divisor C_i with kappa. Equating
them gives C_i=(R_i^circ)^(1)+E_i.

The old radical maps to P_i/lambda_i with divisor Delta_i.
Pairing that quotient with S adds E_i; evaluating on kappa
adds C_i. This proves the second exact contact identity.
At the common fiber, kappa evaluates with order3 and lambda_i
with order2, so their directions differ. Thus C_i, and hence E_i,
avoid that fiber. All constructions are intrinsic to the full
orbit; sum E_i descends and has degree5M/8. No individual descent
follows from this sum.

## Nonisotropic case

D_S has degree two and contains P1. If D_S=2P1 the odd part
of the discriminant is Q11+Q21. If D_S=P1+R1 it has four odd
points unless R1 equals a Qnu1, when the odd part is P1 plus
the other Q. It is always nonempty, so the quadratic splitting
extension is ramified and is absent from every etale source field.

For the loss calculation, take lambda_i=e1 and a regular symplectic
frame with S generated by e1 and e2+s^r f1, r=mult_(P1)D_S.
Its orthogonal maps into N_lambda with image(e2,s^r f2).
The degree9d HN line has a unit f2 coefficient at the common fiber:
otherwise P_i=S in that fiber, but S evaluates entirely to zero
and ell_i evaluates nontrivially by the fixed disjoint divisors.
The intersection therefore loses exactly r at every point above
P1. This proves the full common-fiber assertion.

If the remaining pairing point is R1, total loss is between9d and16d,
so its R1-part has degree between d and8d. When R1=Qnu1, avoiding
that loss requires the unique special direction at the simple
Wronskian zero. At most k_nu of the M new lines do so at a point.
Transitivity gives total loss at least16d-8d*k_nu/M>=11d.
All these losses give nonpositive intersection lines in the
semistable orthogonal. No contradiction has been derived.
