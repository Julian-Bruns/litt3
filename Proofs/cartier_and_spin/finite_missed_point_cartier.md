# Proof of the single finite-point Cartier obstruction

24 September 2026. This is a local continuation of the incoming
[seven-point result](seven_point_norm_cartier.md), with new calculations.
It is not a replay of the1716-case statement.

Use its21-dimensional W=H0(omega(R_X)), explicit monomial basis and Cartier
matrix M. For a chosen finite P let J be the three numerator-jet rows
imposing a double zero. Define
\[
J_0=J,\qquad J_{i+1}=(J_iM)^{[5]},\qquad
R_d=\begin{pmatrix}J_0\\\vdots\\J_d\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If alpha is Cartier-fixed and J alpha=0, its coefficient column belongs
to every ker R_d. Indeed C(v)=M v^{[1/5]}=v, so any row condition Nv=0
also gives \((NM)^{[5]}v=0\). This uses coefficient Frobenius throughout.

The field is F25[r]/A(r), followed by s^3=P(r), of size5^24. Take
P=(r,s); the twelve finite support points form one orbit under the
25-power coefficient Frobenius. Exact calculation gives the nullities
of R_0,...,R_9:
\[
18,16,14,12,10,8,6,4,2,1.
\]
The remaining kernel is generated by an explicit column k, and
\(M k^{[1/5]}=0\). The verifier exhibits a nonzero20x20 minor of R_9,
checks R_9 k=0 and k!=0, and checks that last Cartier identity. Thus
every hypothetical fixed alpha is a scalar multiple of a Cartier-killed
vector and must be zero. Arithmetic conjugation proves the same statement
at all twelve finite support points. This is a matrix argument over a
finite field, hence valid after every scalar extension, not a search for
finite-field fixed vectors.

The new
[standard-library verifier](../../scripts/arithmetic/verify_finite_missed_cartier.py)
reconstructs the field, points, jets and matrix from the independent
portable implementation in the original support package. It does not
import the accelerated exploration routines. Its
[exact certificate](../../../litt3-computation-data/degree55_rank_support_replies_20260924/local/finite_missed_cartier.json)
records the field, point orbit, kernel, minor rows/columns and determinant.
The
[executed log](../../../litt3-computation-data/degree55_rank_support_replies_20260924/logs/finite_missed_cartier.log)
passes. The broader exploration of missed subsets and repeated Cartier
images is retained separately; it is not needed for this concise proof.

For an actual etale primitive q, the divisor of dq/q is
2Delta-E+5G. Its norm logarithm has at worst simple poles on R_X and a
double zero at every missed base point. If any finite point is missed,
the proved scalar theorem forces this logarithm to vanish. The norm is
a fifth power, and reduction of its divisor modulo5 forces each
occupancy to be divisible by5. The primitive-quotient descent and the
norm-class calculation are exactly those in the seven-point proof;
neither uses the number seven once vanishing has been established.

For r positive occupancies on the primitive quotient, write them as5r_P.
Their sum is5m and each is at most m. Therefore
\(1\le r_P\le\lfloor m/5\rfloor\) and \(\sum r_P=m\), proving the
stated integer bound. Trivial norm is not injective descent for a line
bundle, and this proof does not assert that it is.
