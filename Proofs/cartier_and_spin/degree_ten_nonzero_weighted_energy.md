# The two pole-two sheets prevent total cancellation

30 September2026.
[Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/degree_ten_nonzero_weighted_energy.md).
Let u be a uniformizer at O, q_s=q_-7 u^-7+O(u^-6). Exactly two
large roots have pole two; write them r1 u^-2+O(u^-1) and
r2 u^-2+O(u^-1), with r1,r2 nonzero. All other large roots have
pole at least three. The five small roots have leading terms
c_i u^-1+O(1).

The order-two coefficient of Tr(w_s4/phi)=0 comes only from the
two pole-two roots and gives r1^-1+r2^-1=0. Thus r2=-r1.
The order-four coefficient of Tr(w_s3/phi)=0 gives
\[
q_{-7}^{-1}\sum_i c_i^3+r_1^{-2}+r_2^{-2}=0.
\]
Every other large root begins at order at least six in this
second identity. In the weighted quadratic energy, the small-root
sum at order two is q_-7^-1 sum c_i3, and the two pole-two
contributions are4(r1^-2+r2^-2). Their total is therefore
\[
3(r_1^{-2}+r_2^{-2})=6r_1^{-2}=r_1^{-2}\ne0.
\]
All other contributions have higher order. This proves exact
order two, and simultaneously gives the nonzero order-one leading
coefficient of the short-frame cubic energy.

For finite poles compare with the globally regular finite primitive
w=w_s+a, a=(B0-L0)/y. The zero quartic remainder gives E1=0,
and differentiating Tr(w_s2/phi)=0 gives
Tr(w_s dw_s/phi)=df*c, where c=s3/tau is translation invariant.
Hence
\[
J_{1,\mathrm{finite}}=\mathcal J+aE_2+2da\,df\,c.
\]
The weighted finite-frame theorem bounds J1 and E2 by poles two
at the selected t-points and no other finite poles. The function
a has only simple finite poles, at the cubic branch points y=0;
da has poles at most two there. The functions c and df are regular
there, including the linear-v marked point, by actual source-root
estimates in the finite frame. At selected t-points, a and da are
regular, c has pole at most three, and df has order two. Thus J
has poles at most two at all these finite points, removed by y2 t2.

At O, y2 and t2 have poles20 and18, while omega0² has zero32.
Exact order two of J therefore gives exact pole68 of U. This is
the displayed Riemann--Roch assertion. The last assertion does not
claim invariance under an unrelated map or equality of endpoint
differentials: it is a nonvanishing statement on the X-side of an
actual admissible source.
