# Cross-correspondence and Gram-rank restrictions at \(M=9\)

## Status and purpose

**Status: conditional result only; the original unconditional statement
failed independent audit.**  **Audit: FAIL AS STATED; conditional PASS if
all six cross maps are birational** --
`/root/m9_rank_obstruction_audit`, 2026-09-04.
[Audit record](audits/48_M9_RANK_OBSTRUCTION_AUDIT.md).

This note records a conditional sharpening of the extremal case left by
files 40, 42, and 45.  In the degree-nine seven-diamond, each map

\[
             (a,a\beta^j):V\longrightarrow Y\times Y,
             \qquad 1\leq j\leq6,
\]

has generic image degree \(1\) or \(3\).  Comparing its effective
pushforward cycle with all graphs of automorphisms of \(Y\) gives an
unconditional two-sided trace bound, not merely the one-sided coincidence
bound of file 42.  If all six generic image degrees are \(1\), combining
the resulting Rosati-norm bound with the full circulant Gram matrix excludes
the smallest Honda-theoretically possible orbit span.  The cubic image case
has not been excluded; file 49 reduces it to an integral endomorphism-lattice
problem and kills only its \(K\)-component.

Concretely, **conditional on all six maps being birational**, the primitive
\(J(Y)\)-isotypic part generated by the seven
translates of \(a^*J(Y)\) contains at least four copies of \(J(Y)\), not
merely two.  Equivalently, the full cyclic orbit has

\[
                 \mathscr D\text{-rank }5\text{ or }7,
\]

where \(\mathscr D=\operatorname{End}^0(J(Y))\).  The remaining ranks
are not excluded here.  The argument also does not decide whether the
norm map is injective on \(J(Y)[2]\).

Throughout,

\[
 k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5,
 \qquad Y:z^2=1-t^{31},
 \qquad J=J(Y),
\]

and the exact diamond is

\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 V&\xrightarrow{a}&Y\\
 \downarrow p&&\\[-2mm]
 C&\xrightarrow{c}&X,
 \end{array}
 \qquad
 \deg a=\deg c=9,
 \qquad \deg p=7,
\tag{48.1}
\]

with \(p\) generated by the fixed-point-free automorphism \(\beta\).
Put

\[
 e=a^*:J\longrightarrow J(V),\qquad T=\beta^*,
 \qquad u_j=e^\dagger T^je=a_*\beta^{j*}a^*.
\tag{48.2}
\]

Thus \(u_0=[9]\) and \(u_{7-j}=u_j^\dagger\).

## 1. The unresolved cubic image degree

For \(1\leq j\leq6\), let \(\Gamma_j\) be the reduced image of

\[
                 \nu_j=(a,a\beta^j):V\longrightarrow Y\times Y
\tag{48.3}
\]

and put \(e_j=\deg(V/\Gamma_j)\).

### Proposition 48.1 (what is actually known)

For every \(j=1,\ldots,6\), one has

\[
                         e_j\in\{1,3\}.                 \tag{48.4}
\]

The possibility \(e_j=3\) is presently open.  If \(e_j=1\), then \(V\)
is the normalization of \(\Gamma_j\), both normalization projections are
etale of degree nine, and \(\Gamma_j\) has bidegree \((9,9)\).  If
\(e_j=3\), its normalization has two etale degree-three projections to
\(Y\).

#### Proof

Both component maps of (48.3) have degree nine.  Hence \(e_j\mid9\),
and the two projections of the normalization of \(\Gamma_j\) have
common degree \(9/e_j\).

Suppose first that \(e_j=9\).  Both normalized projections then have
degree one, so there is an automorphism
\(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y)\)
such that

\[
                         a\beta^j=\sigma a.             \tag{48.5}
\]

Iteration gives \(a=\sigma^7a\), and the surjectivity of \(a\) gives
\(\sigma^7=1\).  But

\[
                   \operatorname{Aut}(Y)=C_{31}\times C_2
\]

by file 20, so it has no nontrivial element of order seven.  Thus
\(\sigma=1\), contradicting the distinctness of \(a\) and \(a\beta^j\).

Thus only \(e_j=1\) and \(e_j=3\) remain.  The tempting inference
\([F_j:F_0\cap F_j]=3\) in the cubic case is false in general; the audit
record gives an explicit order-seven-conjugate subgroup counterexample.
No geometric argument currently repairs it. \(\square\)

## 2. Testing against the automorphism graphs

Let \(\rho(t,z)=(\zeta_{31}t,z)\), and let \(\iota\) be the
hyperelliptic involution.  The automorphisms

\[
                       \rho^b,\quad\iota\rho^b,
                       \qquad b\in\mathbf Z/31,
\tag{48.7}
\]

induce respectively \(q_b\) and \(-q_b\) on \(J\).

### Proposition 48.2

For every \(j=1,\ldots,6\) and every \(b\in\mathbf Z/31\),

\[
 \left|
   \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(u_j^\dagger q_b\mid
                     H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(Y,\mathbf Q_\ell)\bigr)
 \right|\leq18.                                      \tag{48.8}
\]

In particular, if

\[
 I_j=\deg(a,a\beta^j)^*\Delta_Y,
\]

then

\[
                           0\leq I_j\leq36.             \tag{48.9}
\]

#### Proof

Use the effective pushforward cycle

\[
                Z_j=(a,a\beta^j)_*[V]=e_j[\Gamma_j].
\]

It has bidegree \((9,9)\) and induces \(u_j\), independently of whether
\(e_j=1\) or \(3\).  For two correspondence classes on \(Y\times Y\),
the product of their fiber parts contributes the sum of the two products of projection
degrees, while their correspondence parts pair by minus the
cohomological Rosati trace.  Since \(Z_j\) has bidegree \((9,9)\)
and the graph of an automorphism has bidegree \((1,1)\), this gives

\[
 Z_j\cdot\operatorname{Graph}(\rho^b)
       =18-\operatorname{Tr}(u_j^\dagger q_b\mid H^1(Y)),
\tag{48.10}
\]

and

\[
 Z_j\cdot\operatorname{Graph}(\iota\rho^b)
       =18+\operatorname{Tr}(u_j^\dagger q_b\mid H^1(Y)).
\tag{48.11}
\]

The effective cycles have no common component because their reduced
supports have different projection degrees.  Both intersection numbers
are consequently nonnegative, proving (48.8).

Take \(b=0\).  Cohomological trace is unchanged by Rosati adjunction, so

\[
                         -18\leq\operatorname{Tr}(u_j)\leq18.
\]

File 42 gives \(I_j=18-\operatorname{Tr}(u_j)\), which proves (48.9).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 48.3

For the image \(D=(p,a)(V)\subset C\times Y\) of file 45, put

\[
                         \delta=I_1+I_2+I_3.
\]

Then

\[
 \delta\leq108,
 \qquad
 D^2=2\delta-252\leq-36,                              \tag{48.12}
\]

and, for \(s=(p_*a^*)^\dagger(p_*a^*)\),

\[
             162\leq\operatorname{Tr}(s\mid H^1(Y))
                  =378-2\delta\leq378.                 \tag{48.13}
\]

#### Proof

This follows immediately from (48.9), the symmetry \(I_j=I_{7-j}\),
and the conductor, self-intersection, and trace identities in files 42
and 45. \(\square\)

## 3. The circulant Gram matrix has rank five or seven

Let

\[
 \mathcal E:J^7\longrightarrow J(V),
 \qquad
 (x_0,\ldots,x_6)\longmapsto
                 \sum_{i=0}^6T^ie(x_i).                \tag{48.14}
\]

Its Rosati Gram matrix is the circulant matrix

\[
                 G=\mathcal E^\dagger\mathcal E
                   =(u_{j-i})_{0\leq i,j\leq6}.        \tag{48.15}
\]

Put \(\mathscr D=\operatorname{End}^0(J)\), the degree-three division
algebra over its center \(E\) from file 40, and let

\[
 r=\dim_{\mathscr D}
       \operatorname{span}_{\mathscr D}\{e,Te,\ldots,T^6e\}.
\tag{48.16}
\]

### Proposition 48.4

Before using effectivity, the cyclic representation forces

\[
                              r\in\{3,5,7\}.            \tag{48.17}
\]

#### Proof

The invariant part of the cyclic span has \(\mathscr D\)-dimension
exactly one.  It is at most one because the invariant projections of all
seven generators are equal, and it is nonzero because

\[
              (1+T+\cdots+T^6)e=p^*p_*a^*\ne0
\]

by Lemma 40.2.

Let \(m=r-1\) be the \(\mathscr D\)-dimension of the primitive part.
It is nonzero, since otherwise \(Te=e\), which would force
\(a\beta=a\).  The polynomial \(\Phi_7\) is irreducible over \(E\), and
the action on the nonzero primitive part therefore gives an embedding

\[
 E(\zeta_7)\hookrightarrow M_m(\mathscr D).             \tag{48.18}
\]

The field on the left has degree six over \(E\), while the central
simple algebra on the right has degree \(3m\).  The centralizer theorem
forces \(6\mid3m\), so \(m\) is even.  Finally \(m\leq6\), because the
primitive cyclic span is generated by the six powers below degree six.
Thus \(m\in\{2,4,6\}\), proving (48.17). \(\square\)

### Theorem 48.5 (conditional exclusion of the rank-three orbit)

Assume in addition that

\[
                         e_j=1\quad(1\leq j\leq6).
\]

Then, in the degree-nine diamond,

\[
                              r\in\{5,7\}.              \tag{48.19}
\]

Equivalently, the primitive \(J\)-isotypic abelian subvariety generated
by the translates of \(a^*J\) has dimension at least \(60\).

If \(r=5\), one moreover has the quantitative bound

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^6
   \operatorname{Tr}(u_j^\dagger u_j\mid H^1(Y))
                         \geq972.                       \tag{48.20}
\]

#### Proof

Set

\[
 Q_j=\operatorname{Tr}(u_j^\dagger u_j\mid H^1(Y)),
 \qquad Q=\sum_{j=1}^6Q_j.
\]

Theorem 45.4 shows that each \(u_j\ne0\).  More importantly, Proposition
48.1 lets us apply the exact delta formula (45.17) directly to the
bidegree-\((9,9)\) curve \(\Gamma_j\), whose normalization is \(V\):

\[
 0\leq\delta(\Gamma_j)
       =9^2+14\cdot9-\frac12Q_j
       =207-\frac12Q_j.
\]

Consequently

\[
                              Q_j\leq414,
 \qquad                         Q\leq2484.              \tag{48.21}
\]

The Rosati-positive matrix \(G\) has rank \(30r\) on the rational first
cohomology of \(J^7\).  Its trace and squared trace are

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(G)=7\cdot30\cdot9=1890,
\tag{48.22}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(G^2)
   =7\left(\operatorname{Tr}([81]\mid H^1(Y))+Q\right)
   =7(2430+Q).                                         \tag{48.23}
\]

The Cauchy inequality for the nonzero eigenvalues of a positive
semidefinite operator gives

\[
 1890^2\leq30r\cdot7(2430+Q),
\]

or equivalently

\[
                         Q\geq\frac{17010}{r}-2430.
\tag{48.24}
\]

If \(r=3\), (48.24) says \(Q\geq3240\), contradicting (48.21).  Hence
\(r=5\) or \(7\).  When \(r=5\), (48.24) is exactly (48.20). \(\square\)

## 4. Boundary of the result

Conditional on the six birationality assumptions, this argument replaces
the formal possibility

\[
          \text{one invariant copy plus two primitive copies}
\]

by

\[
          \text{one invariant copy plus four or six primitive copies}.
\]

Without those assumptions, rank three remains open because an
\(e_j=3\) term has \(u_j=3w_j\), while the delta bound applies to the
bidegree-\((3,3)\) correspondence representing \(w_j\), not directly to
the reduced degree-nine image used in (48.21).  File 49 records the exact
small-endomorphism problem.

The rank-five and rank-seven alternatives both still fit in \(J(V)\):
they use respectively \(75\) and \(105\) of its \(127\) dimensions.
The frame inequality is therefore not by itself a contradiction.

Nor does the argument prove that

\[
                       p_*a^*:J(Y)[2]\longrightarrow J(C)[2]
\]

is injective.  Oddness of the covering degrees makes each individual
pullback split on \(2\)-torsion, but it does not control the intersection
of \(a^*J(Y)[2]\) with the primitive kernel of \(p_*\).  Any proof of
injectivity must use additional data, such as the normed Weierstrass
divisors, rather than the rational Honda algebra and the Gram estimates
used here.
