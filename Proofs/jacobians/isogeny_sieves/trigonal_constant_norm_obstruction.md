# A structural obstruction to every constant-Q norm chart

## General criterion

Let F in F25[x] be squarefree of degree10, and X:y^3=F. Suppose its
Frobenius25 polynomial P_X reduces modulo2 to an irreducible degree18
polynomial whose roots have multiplicative order N, with phi(N)>18.
Then there are NO rational functions P,R in bar(F5)(x) satisfying

                         P^3+F=R^2.                         (1)

In particular no polynomial degree bounds are needed for this assertion.
For the fixed X, N=171 and phi(N)=108, so all three constant-Q charts
(Pdegree6,4,or<=3) are excluded together. A nonzero constant Q in the
original P^3+FQ^3=R^2 is reduced to (1) by dividing P by Q and R by a
chosen Q^(3/2) over the algebraically closed field.

## 1. The actual sixth-root curve and its character factors

Let D be the smooth projective model of z^6=F, over F25. It is connected:
the valuations of F at its ten simple roots are1. Its group mu6 is
defined over F25. Let H:z^2=F be the quadratic quotient. The genera are

                    g(X)=9, g(H)=4, g(D)=22.

The first two follow from the tame cyclic-cover formula. For D the ten
finite branch points have index6, while infinity has two points of
index3, giving 2g(D)-2=-12+10*5+2*(3-1)=42.

In H1(D,Q_l), l different from2,3,5, the characters of orders3,2,6
give respectively H1(X), H1(H), and a primitive-sixth summand of rank18.
The associated rational idempotent defines an abelian factor A of J(D),
of dimension9, over F25. Write P_A for its integral monic Frobenius
polynomial, of degree18. Thus

                         P_D=P_X P_H P_A.                 (2)

The idempotents are used in rational endomorphisms only; no integral
splitting at2 is asserted.

## 2. The primitive-sixth polynomial has the same reduction as P_X

Let U=P1 minus the ten roots of F and infinity. The mu6 torsor over U
has characters chi^j, with values in Z[zeta6]=Z[zeta3]. For j=1,5 the
Euler L-functions are the two primitive-sixth factors. For j=2,4 they
are the two cubic factors. Every one of these four characters is
nontrivial on every omitted inertia group: at a finite root inertia is
mu6, and at infinity it is the subgroup mu3 generated by the exponent
-10 mod6. Thus all four local factors at omitted points are1.

Reduce their Euler factors modulo the prime(2) of Z[zeta3]. Its residue
field is F4. The sixth root of unity becomes a third root, so

                chi mod2 = chi^4 mod2,
                chi^5 mod2 = chi^2 mod2.

This equality holds pointwise on the Frobenius element at EVERY closed
point of U, not merely through a bounded number of point counts. The
Euler products therefore agree as formal power series over F4. Their
pairwise products are the reversed polynomials in (2), so

                         P_A mod2 = P_X mod2.              (3)

For completeness the L-function/cohomology identification here is the
usual rank-one finite-monodromy trace formula: the geometric character
is nontrivial, so H0 and H2 vanish; tameness and the eleven nontrivial
inertia groups give each relevant H1 character space dimension9. Thus
there are no unrecorded denominator factors in this comparison. This
can equivalently be read directly from the mu6 eigenspaces of D.
The general cohomological framework is the
[Stacks trace-formula chapter](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03UY).

Since (3) is irreducible of degree18, P_A itself is irreducible over Q.
This uses monicity and Gauss's lemma, not any irreducibility of a
character polynomial over Q(zeta3).

## 3. A norm solution would give an actual primitive-sixth map to E

Suppose (1) holds and set E:v^2=u^3+1, with its point at infinity as
origin. On D the functions

                         u=P/z^2, v=R/z^3

satisfy E's equation. They give a map D->E, extending to the smooth
projective models. It is nonconstant. If u were a nonzero constant,
then z^2=P/u would be in k(x), contradicting that X:k(x)(z^2) has
degree3. If u=0 then P=0 and R^2=F, contrary to F's simple zeros.

For z->zeta6*z, this map is equivariant for

                  (u,v)->(zeta6^(-2)u,zeta6^(-3)v).

That automorphism acts by -1 for its order2 element and has no
invariants for its order3 subgroup (the quotient of E has genus0).
Therefore the induced nonzero Jacobian homomorphism J(D)->E factors
through A up to isogeny. This argument preserves an ACTUAL curve map;
it is not an arbitrary representation-theoretic candidate.

All its coefficients descend to some finite extension F_(25^s).
E has6 points over F5: the five x-values contribute2,0,2,0,1 affine
points, respectively, and there is its point at infinity. Thus its
Frobenius5 polynomial is T^2+5 and Frobenius25 acts as the scalar -5
on its rational Tate module. A nonzero homomorphism A->E defined over
F_(25^s) forces an eigenvalue alpha of P_A to satisfy

                         alpha^s=(-5)^s.

Consequently alpha=5*zeta for a root of unity zeta. In particular this
step needs neither a computed zeta polynomial for D nor a general
Honda--Tate classification.

## 4. The binary order rules this out

Let zeta have exact order n. Since P_A is irreducible of degree18,

                    phi(n)=[Q(5*zeta):Q]=18.

At any prime above2, reduction of alpha=5*zeta is a root of the
irreducible polynomial in (3), hence has exact multiplicative order N.
Reduction cannot increase the order of a root of unity, so N divides n.
For divisibility of positive integers, phi(N)<=phi(n). This contradicts
the hypothesis phi(N)>18. This proves the criterion.
