# Regular companion points constrain all three Klein-four quotients

Version1, 26 September2026. Retain all hypotheses and notation of
[the simultaneous quotient theorem](klein_four_simultaneous_quotients.md).
Put j=j1+j2, d_i=10+c_i-h_i and
\[
T_i=(V_i-\epsilon t^7U_i)/E,\qquad
M_i=U_jT_k-U_kT_j\quad(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}).
\]
Then T_i and M_i are nonzero, deg T_i<=d_i, and
\[
\deg M_i\le e-14+d_j+d_k,\qquad J_i^{(1)}\mid M_i,
\]
where J_i^(1) is the reduced product of parameter values containing
exactly one triple common pole with inertia generated by sigma_i.
In particular
\[
2g+j_1\le3e+12+4j.
\]
Equality g=n+1 can occur only at n=26, and then the remaining
necessary profile is (g,s,a,j1,j2,e)=(27,14,14,0,0,14).
This does not assert its realization.

At n=91 the [forced profile](klein_four_grid_singularity_bound.md)
has j1=26,j2=0. The three inertia counts satisfy q_i<=13,
so every quadratic quotient genus is between22 and35. Its actual
character numerators also satisfy the first- and second-order
divisibilities in the proof, retaining all companion cancellations.

At a point ramified for both the cubic reconstruction and t, the two
finite P-root labels are distinct. Such incidence is empty if
epsilon^29=1 and is always disjoint from t^-1(mu29). Nonempty
incidence restricts epsilon^29 to a fixed squarefree degree90
spectrum and can occur at only one parameter value and at most
two points. The disjoint-incidence alternative imposes no such
finite scalar restriction.

No further whole degree in14..91 is excluded by these statements.
