# Complete repeated-\(u^{20}\) affine certificate

## Status and scope

This note is **proved-text** under the displayed normalized local
double-fiber hypotheses.  It closes the local gate described in file 100:
it recovers all nineteen new variables, gives every simple-branch Schur
correction and repeated-branch response column, proves the two required pivot
minors, and identifies the resulting sixteen-dimensional affine fibers.

The proof uses no finite sampling.  The archived programs were used only to
recover the coefficient convention and the missing variable list; the list is
derived again below from the degree filtration.

As with the surrounding layer certificates, this theorem is conditional on
the incoming graph having been constructed through repeated \(u^{15}\), and
on the normalized local double-fiber equation used in files 184 and 186.  It
does **not** prove the upstream specialization \(c=d=2\), the clean-cubic
condition, or any additional global coverage statement.

## Local coefficient convention

Work in characteristic \(5\).  Write the local curve equation at \(x=0\) as

\[
 \Phi(u,w)=\sum_i F_i(u)w^i,
 \qquad u=x.
\]

For the relevant tail coefficients, use the retained convention

\[
 F_{35-j}(u)=u(u-1)U_j(u),
 \qquad
 U_j(u)=\sum_{A=0}^3 v_{j,A}u^A.
\tag{187.1}
\]

Thus the variable denoted `uj_A` in the archived programs is \(v_{j,A}\),
and its perturbation of the curve equation is

\[
 \delta_{j,A}\Phi
 =u(u-1)u^Aw^B,
 \qquad B=35-j.
\tag{187.2}
\]

Assume that the degree-four tangent form and the normalized boundary jet are
those of files 184 and 186.  There is one simple branch and three repeated
branches.  Write

\[
 w_-=uq_-(u),\qquad q_-(0)=-1
\]

on the simple branch.  On the repeated branches, work in the clean etale
cubic algebra

\[
 A=R[s]/(h(s)),
\]

over the ring \(R\) of older coordinates, localized at the discriminant of
\(h\).  Then \(1,s,s^2\) is an \(R\)-basis and

\[
 w_+=uQ(u),\qquad Q=1+su+O(u^2),
\]

\[
 \Phi_w(u,w_+)=u^5\ell T(u),qquad
 p=\ell^{-1}\in A^\times,\qquad T(0)=1.
\tag{187.3}
\]

At the simple branch, write

\[
 \Phi(u,uq)=u^4G(u,q),\qquad
 G_q(u,q_-)=2aT_-(u),qquad T_-(0)=1.
\tag{187.4}
\]

Here \(a\) is a unit; in the normalized equation of files 184 and 186,
\(a=1\).  It is useful to abbreviate

\[
 R_{n,r}=[u^r]Q^nT^{-1},qquad
 S_{n,r}=[u^r]q_-^nT_-^{-1},qquad
 W=R_{26,2}.
\tag{187.5}
\]

## The nineteen variables are the complete new layer

The leading \(u\)-degree of (187.2) after substituting either
\(w=u+O(u^2)\) or \(w=-u+O(u^2)\) is

\[
 N(j,A)=1+A+B=36+A-j.
\tag{187.6}
\]

For a repeated branch, \(\Phi_w\) has order five.  The coefficient
\([u^{15}]\mathcal E\) uses the coefficients of \(z=w^{-1}\) through
degree nineteen, hence the branch through \(w_{21}u^{21}\), and therefore
the curve equation through degree twenty-six.  Similarly,
\([u^{20}]\mathcal E\) uses \(z\) through degree twenty-four, the branch
through \(w_{26}u^{26}\), and the curve equation through degree thirty-one.
Consequently the new band is exactly

\[
 27\leq N(j,A)\leq31.
\tag{187.7}
\]

The one variable \(v_{7,0}\) in this band is the pivot already solved by the
simple-\(u^{20}\) equation.  Removing it, (187.7) gives exactly

\[
\begin{gathered}
 v_{12,3},\\

 v_{10,1},v_{10,2},v_{10,3},\\

\end{gathered}
\tag{187.8}
\]

There are nineteen variables.  This proves both that every listed variable is

## Exact affineness at this layer

Let \(J\) be the ideal generated by the variables in (187.8) together with
the simple pivot \(v_{7,0}\), and compare a branch with its value at \(J=0\).
On a repeated branch, a perturbation of least degree twenty-seven changes
\(w\) first in degree \(27-5=22\).  Therefore

\[
 \delta w\in Ju^{22}A[[u]].
\tag{187.9}
\]

Since \(\Phi\) has order four, \(\Phi_{ww}\) has order at least two.  Hence
the quadratic Taylor term in the old equation has order at least

\[
 2+2\cdot22=46>31.
\]

The \(w\)-derivative of a new term has order at least twenty-six, so its
product with \(\delta w\) has order at least forty-eight.  It follows that
the repeated branch through degree twenty-six is affine-linear in all the
new variables.  Moreover,

\[
 (w+\delta w)^{-1}-w^{-1}
 =-w^{-2}\delta w+O(J^2u^{41}),
\]

so the repeated \(u^{20}\) residual, which only uses \(z\) through degree
twenty-four, is affine-linear as well.

On the simple branch, \(\Phi_w\) has order three, so the same argument starts
with \(\delta w\in Ju^{24}R[[u]]\) and is even stronger.  Thus the simple
\(u^{20}\) equation and its solution for \(v_{7,0}\) are affine-linear in
all nineteen variables.  This also shows directly that none of the lower
ODE residuals is changed.  Equivalently, one may use

\[
 D^5=D,
 \qquad D=(u-1)\frac d{du},
\]

to note that \(D^4z-z+z^5\) has only fifth-power degrees; the new
perturbations begin too late to alter the equations at degrees
\(0,5,10,15\).

This filtration argument promotes the differential response formulas in
files 182 and 185 to exact affine formulas at this truncated layer.

No unrecorded division occurs in (187.12): the common factor
\((2a)^{-1}\) cancels between the variable and the pivot.

## The two pivot minors

Retain the old repeated-\(u^5\) columns \(I,L,M,p\), and put

\[
 U_I=I/p,\qquad U_L=L/p,\qquad U_M=M/p.
\]

Files 182, 184, and 186 prove, under the present normalized local
hypotheses,

\[
 [s^2]U_I=4,qquad [s^2]U_L=0,qquad U_M-U_L=s.
\tag{187.15}
\]

Put

\[
 F=[u^2](Q^{14}+4Q^{16})T^{-1}.
\]

File 184 proves

\[
 [s^2]F=2.
\tag{187.16}
\]

The four columns used in the pivots reduce as follows:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{8,3}/p&=1,\\
 C_{7,1}/p&=U_L,\\
 C_{9,3}/p&=U_L+3s,\\
 C_{9,2}/p
  &=[u^2](Q^{24}+4Q^{26})T^{-1}=F.
\end{aligned}
\tag{187.17}
\]

Indeed, \(Q^{5r}\equiv1\pmod {u^5}\) for every integer \(r\) in
characteristic five.  This compares exponents differing by ten or fifteen
in (187.17).  The degree-one identity for \(C_{9,3}\) is also immediate
from \(Q=1+su+O(u^2)\).

Write

\[
 U_L=\alpha_0+\delta s,
\]

and let \(\nu=N_{A/R}(p)\in R^\times\).  With the determinant convention of
file 80, (187.15) gives

\[
 \rho_3=[I,L,p]=\nu\delta,
 \qquad
 \rho_4=[I,M,p]=\nu(\delta+1).
\tag{187.18}
\]

Using (187.16)--(187.18), one obtains the exact identities

\[
 \boxed{
 [C_{9,2},C_{8,3},C_{7,1}]=2\rho_3,}
\tag{187.19}
\]

and, modulo \(\rho_3\),

\[
 \boxed{
 [C_{9,2},C_{9,3},C_{8,3}]=4\rho_4.}
\tag{187.20}
\]

For completeness, after division by the common unit \(p\), the first
determinant is \([F,1,U_L]=2\delta\).  On \(\rho_3=0\), the unit \(\nu\)
forces \(\delta=0\), and the second is
\([F,U_L+3s,1]=4\), while \(\rho_4=\nu\).  Common-unit normalization
multiplies both normalized determinants by \(\nu\), proving
(187.19)--(187.20).

Notice that the distinguished first column is \(v_{9,2}\), not merely
\(v_{10,3}\).  The latter also has the valid expression

\[
 C_{10,3}/p=[u^2](Q^{23}+Q^{26})T^{-1},
\]

but the archived \(\rho_3\) and fallback \(\rho_4\) pivots are exactly the
triples in (187.19) and (187.20).

## Fiberwise surjectivity and the outgoing graph

The response table is valid over the entire older-coordinate ring \(R\),
not only at the zero section.  There are two independent reasons:

1. the filtration argument above proves exact affineness before any older
   coordinates are specialized; and
2. the selected columns use only \(Q\bmod u^3\) and \(T\bmod u^3\).
   A tail term \(F_iw^i\) with \(i\geq8\) begins in degree at least nine on
   the repeated branch.  It can first change \(Q\) in degree three, while
   its \(w\)-derivative can first change \(T\) in degree three.  Hence all
   older free tail coordinates are invisible in (187.17).  The universal
   identities from files 184 and 186 apply to the remaining low jet.

It follows from (187.19) that on

\[
 D(\Delta\rho_3)
\]

the repeated residual map from the nineteen new variables to the rank-three
algebra \(A\) is surjective.  One may solve uniquely for

\[
 v_{9,2},\quad v_{8,3},\quad v_{7,1}.
\]

Its other sixteen variables are free.  Explicitly, they are

\[
\begin{gathered}
v_{12,3},v_{11,2},v_{11,3},v_{10,1},v_{10,2},v_{10,3},
v_{9,0},v_{9,1},v_{9,3},\\
v_{8,0},v_{8,1},v_{8,2},v_{7,2},v_{6,0},v_{6,1},v_{5,0}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{187.21}
\]

On the fallback stratum

\[
 V(\rho_3)\cap D(\Delta\rho_4),
\]

equation (187.20) instead solves uniquely for

\[
 v_{9,2},\quad v_{9,3},\quad v_{8,3},
\]

and the free variables are

\[
\begin{gathered}
v_{12,3},v_{11,2},v_{11,3},v_{10,1},v_{10,2},v_{10,3},
v_{9,0},v_{9,1},\\
v_{8,0},v_{8,1},v_{8,2},v_{7,1},v_{7,2},v_{6,0},v_{6,1},v_{5,0}.
\end{gathered}
\tag{187.22}
\]

Thus on either stratum the repeated-\(u^{20}\) solution scheme is the graph
of three affine functions over \(\mathbb A_R^{16}\).  The previously solved
simple pivot \(v_{7,0}\) is then another affine function of these sixteen
variables and the older coordinates.  This is exactly the incoming affine
graph required by the simple-\(u^{25}\) layer.

The complete list of inverted factors is:

- the clean cubic discriminant \(\Delta\), equivalently the branch derivative
  \(\ell\) and hence the common column \(p=\ell^{-1}\);
- the simple tangent unit \(2a\), equivalently the simple pivot coefficient
  \((2a)^{-1}\); and
- \(\rho_3\) on the first stratum, or \(\rho_4\) after imposing
  \(\rho_3=0\) on the fallback stratum.

The constants \(2,3,4\) are units in characteristic five.  No sampled-field
nonvanishing and no additional hidden denominator is used.
