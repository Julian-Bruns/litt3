# The generic \(j=m=e=1\) row: formal escape and the full-grid target

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved structural bookkeeping and formal compatibility; self-check
complete.**  The formal model below is deliberately **not** an existence
claim for curves or covers.

This note isolates the all-degree row which survives the full-orbit and
sign-norm constructions.  Its main conclusions are:

1. in the row \(j=m=e=1\), every lower field and every ramification-defect
   invariant collapses tautologically;
2. all presently available genus, Prym, trace, conductor, discriminant, and
   pushed-incidence inequalities are compatible for unbounded \(M\);
3. even the \(2rs+4\) doubled hyperelliptic branch divisors have a formal
   escape: they can be packed into stationary *full-grid* fibers without
   spending any cyclic spectral conductor; and
4. the next genuinely geometric problem is therefore to control those
   full-grid fibers using the cyclic cover and the interpolation identity,
   not to sharpen another total genus or conductor count.

Throughout, \(r\) is an odd prime invertible in \(k\),

\[
 g(X)=s+1,\qquad g(Y)=rs+1,
\]

\(Y\to\mathbf P^1_t\) is hyperelliptic, and one has the prime-ratio
diamond

\[
 V\xrightarrow{a}Y,\qquad V\xrightarrow{p}C\xrightarrow{c}X,
 \qquad \deg p=r,\quad \deg a=\deg c=M,
\]

where all maps are finite etale, \(p\) is a \(C_r\)-torsor, and
\(a\beta\ne a\) for a generator \(\beta\).

## 1. Exact collapse of the generic row

Use the notation of files 81, 83, and 84.  Thus \(F=k(C)\), \(K=k(V)\),
\(B\) is generated by the coefficients of

\[
 P(T)=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(T-\beta^i t),
\]

\(D\) is generated by the coefficients of the interpolation polynomial
\(Q\) satisfying \(Q(\beta^i t)=\beta^i z\), and

\[
 e=[F:B],\qquad j=[D:B],\qquad m=[F:D].
\]

### Proposition 1 (generic-row identities)

If \(j=m=e=1\), then

\[
 B=D=F,\qquad E=E_*=L=K,
\]

and

\[
 N=M,\qquad d=2M,\qquad \nu=1,\qquad \eta=0.       \tag{1.1}
\]

In particular, the common-defect formulas of file 84 and its extra-orbit
Prym inequality add no condition in this row.  The square sign norm and
the coprime conclusion of file 83 are automatic.

#### Proof

The equalities \(j=m=e=1\) give \(B=D=F\).  File 81 gives
\(N=M/m=M\), \(jd=2N\), and \(j=1\Longleftrightarrow z\in E\), hence
\(d=2M\) and \(z\in E=F(t)\).  Since \(t\) is primitive for the cyclic
extension \(K/F\), this says \(E=E_*=K\).  The field \(K/F\) is already
Galois and contains all \(\beta^i(t),\beta^i(z)\), so its full signed
splitting field \(L\) is also \(K\).

The sign product belongs to \(B=F\), so \(\nu=1\).  Finally

\[
 \eta=Ns+1-g(D)=Ms+1-g(C)=0
\]

by etale Riemann--Hurwitz for \(c:C\to X\). \(\square\)

The coefficient line bundle has degree \(2M\), and its coefficient span
has dimension

\[
                         3\le w\le r+1.              \tag{1.2}
\]

The spectral incidence curve

\[
                  \Sigma:\ P(T)=0\subset C\times\mathbf P^1
\]

has normalization \(V\), and exact invariants

\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_a(\Sigma)&=r(Ms+1)+(r-1)(2M-1)\\
             &=Mrs+2M(r-1)+1,\\
 g(V)&=Mrs+1,\\
 \delta(\Sigma)&=2M(r-1),\\
 \deg\mathfrak C_\Sigma&=4M(r-1).                   \tag{1.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus spectral adjunction is an equality with positive linear slack, not an
asymptotic obstruction.

The projection \(\Sigma\to\mathbf P^1\) is finite flat of degree \(2M\).
Its discriminant degree and the different degree of its normalization are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \deg\operatorname{Disc}(\Sigma/\mathbf P^1)
     &=2rM(s+2),\\
 \deg\operatorname{Diff}(V/\mathbf P^1)
     &=(2rs+4)M=q_0M.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{1.4}
\]

Here and below \(q_0=2rs+4\) is the number of hyperelliptic branch
values.

Their difference is

\[
 2rM(s+2)-q_0M=4M(r-1)=2\delta(\Sigma).              \tag{1.5}
\]

When every \(D_\alpha\) is reduced, the marked fibers give exactly the
lower contribution \(q_0M\).  The entire remaining discriminant allowance
is again the conductor, so the discriminant count cannot improve (1.3).

If \(w\ge4\), a generic projection of the coefficient curve to
\(\mathbf P^3\) is birational and has degree \(2M\).  Castelnuovo gives

\[
                   Ms+1\le (M-1)^2,                  \tag{1.6}
\]

which is satisfied for every \(M\ge s+2\).  For \(w=3\), the plane genus
bound is weaker asymptotically.  File 83 gives only
\(2M=d\ge s+3\).  Consequently no coefficient-genus inequality grows fast
enough to eliminate this row for large \(M\).

## 2. The marked branch divisors and full grids

Let \(\mathcal A\subset\mathbf P^1\) be the branch set of the hyperelliptic
map.  Put

\[
                         q_0=|\mathcal A|=2rs+4.      \tag{2.1}
\]

For every \(\alpha\in\mathcal A\), evaluation of the norm polynomial gives

\[
                 \operatorname{div}P(\alpha)=2D_\alpha,
                 \qquad \deg D_\alpha=M.             \tag{2.2}
\]

Call \(x\in C\) a **simple full-grid point** if the \(r\) values of \(t\)
on \(p^{-1}(x)\) are distinct and all belong to \(\mathcal A\).

### Lemma 2 (full grids are stationary and conductor-free)

Let \(x\) be a simple full-grid point, and let
\(S_x\subset\mathcal A\) be its set of \(r\) root values.  Then:

1. \(x\in D_\alpha\) with multiplicity one exactly for
   \(\alpha\in S_x\);
2. the differential of the coefficient morphism vanishes at \(x\); and
3. \(x\) contributes zero to

   \[
       \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\sum_{y\in C}
           n_{\alpha,y}(n_{\alpha,y}-1),
       \qquad n_{\alpha,y}=\operatorname{mult}_yD_\alpha.            \tag{2.3}
   \]

#### Proof

Since \(p\) is etale, it splits over the completed local ring at \(x\).
Write its \(r\) local root functions as \(t_i\).  The map \(a\) is etale,
whereas the hyperelliptic function has ramification index two at every
point above \(\mathcal A\).  For a uniformizer \(u\) at \(x\),

\[
                         t_i-\alpha_i\in u^2k[[u]]^\times.
\]

If one of the displayed values is infinity, first make a projective
change of the \(t\)-coordinate whose pole avoids the finite set \(S_x\).
The norm-coefficient map changes by a projective linear transformation,
so stationarity and the asserted multiplicities are unchanged.

Because the \(\alpha_i\) are distinct, evaluation of
\(P(T)=\prod_i(T-t_i)\) at \(\alpha_i\) has valuation exactly two.  This
proves the first assertion and makes every term in (2.3) zero at \(x\).

Every coefficient of \(P\) is an elementary symmetric function of the
\(t_i\).  Since every \(dt_i\) vanishes at \(x\), all coefficient
differentials vanish there.  This is the second assertion. \(\square\)

Let \(\mathfrak R_\chi\) be the stationary divisor of the birational
coefficient morphism \(\chi:C\to\mathbf P(W^\vee)\).  Its Wronskians are
sections of \(L^2\otimes\omega_C\), so

\[
            \deg\mathfrak R_\chi
              \le 2\deg L+2g(C)-2=2M(s+2).           \tag{2.4}
\]

If all \(q_0M\) marked half-divisor incidences are reduced and packed into
simple full grids, their number is

\[
                         F_{\rm grid}={q_0M\over r}
                            =\left(2s+{4\over r}\right)M.             \tag{2.5}
\]

The stationary budget does not exclude this, because

\[
 2M(s+2)-F_{\rm grid}=4M\left(1-{1\over r}\right)>0. \tag{2.6}
\]

Moreover, the cyclic collision inequality of the plane-Gauss note is
blind to this packing: its left side in (2.3) is zero.  This is the exact
reason that total conductor plus total Wronskian degree does not close the
generic row.

## 3. An unbounded formal packing model

The preceding numerical escape is not merely a rounding artifact.

### Proposition 3 (balanced formal full-grid data)

Put

\[
                    P_0={q_0-1\choose r-1}.
\]

For every \(\lambda\ge1\), set \(M=\lambda P_0\).  There is formal marked
divisor data with the following properties:

1. every \(D_\alpha\) is reduced of degree \(M\);
2. every supporting point belongs to exactly \(r\) of the divisors;
3. every supporting point has the local numerical profile of Lemma 2;
4. there are exactly \(q_0M/r\) supporting points; and
5. the branch-collision cost (2.3) is zero.

#### Proof

For every \(r\)-element subset \(S\subset\mathcal A\), introduce
\(\lambda\) distinct formal points \(x_{S,1},\ldots,x_{S,\lambda}\), and
put

\[
             D_\alpha=\sum_{S\ni\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{\lambda}x_{S,i}.
\]

Each \(\alpha\) lies in exactly
\({q_0-1\choose r-1}\) such subsets, so \(D_\alpha\) is reduced of degree
\(M\).  Every point occurs in the \(r\) divisors indexed by its subset.
Finally

\[
 \lambda{q_0\choose r}
   ={q_0\over r}\lambda{q_0-1\choose r-1}
   ={q_0M\over r}.
\]

All multiplicities are one, proving the remaining assertions. \(\square\)

Geometrically, the coefficient value attached to \(x_{S,i}\) would be the
binary form with roots \(S\).  Repeated \(i\)'s would be distinct
normalization branches above the same coefficient point.  Proposition 3
asserts only that every numerical incidence, stationary, and conductor
budget allows this configuration.  Whether a single integral coefficient
curve coming from the cyclic spectral cover can realize it is precisely
the missing geometric question.

## 4. The trace and incidence inequalities also have an unbounded model

Assume now the extra cyclic-Honda hypotheses of file 69, and take \(M\)
larger than \(g(Y)=rs+1\).  For each \(1\le j<r\), assign the formal cross
data

\[
 e_j=1,\qquad d_j=M,\qquad w_j=0,\qquad I_j=2M.        \tag{4.1}
\]

Then the Rosati norm may be assigned

\[
                              s_0=[M].                 \tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 0<[M]<[rM],
\]

and the trace identity of file 69 becomes

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(s_0\mid H^1(Y))
 =2M(rs+1)
 =2M(rs+1+r-1)-(r-1)2M.                               \tag{4.3}
\]

Every individual cross-defect is nonnegative:

\[
 \delta(\Gamma_j)=M^2+rsM.                            \tag{4.4}
\]

The basic Prym packing is also compatible: the copy of \(J(Y)\), of
dimension \(rs+1\), fits in

\[
 \dim\operatorname{Prym}(C/X)=(M-1)s
\]

as soon as \(M\ge r+2\), exactly the already known lower bound.

The low-degree and Frobenius-lattice tests in file 69 do not apply to
\(d_j=M>g(Y)\).  The primitive-multiplicity condition there is a linear
lower bound on \(M\), and hence is also satisfied for all sufficiently
large choices in Proposition 3.

Likewise, the pushed-incidence construction has a compatible formal
choice: take generic image degree one and the birational divisor-family
alternative.  Its exact normalization defect

\[
                         rM(M+s)                       \tag{4.5}
\]

is positive and is not bounded above by any existing theorem.  The
weighted-grid theorems, and the scalar/non-scalar involution trace
theorems, do not apply: they require a nontrivial coefficient quotient
or a ramified coefficient involution, whereas \(C=B=D\) here.

Thus the assignments (1.1)--(4.5), for unbounded \(\lambda\), satisfy all
applicable numerical constraints in files 68, 69, 71, 75, and 81--84,
the cyclic collision and discriminant budgets, and the parameterized
pushed-incidence identities.  This proves only the insufficiency of those
numerical constraints; it does not construct a diamond.

## 5. Exact strategic boundary

The most concrete next target is a theorem about the *simultaneous
realizability* of the full-grid data.  In intrinsic terms one must exploit
all three facts at once:

1. \(K/F\) is a cyclic etale extension of degree \(r\);
2. \(t\in K\) is primitive and its norm coefficients generate \(F\), with
   a nonpencil coefficient system; and
3. the interpolation polynomial satisfies

   \[
                         Q(T)^2\equiv f(T)\pmod{P(T)}. \tag{5.1}
   \]

A useful result would bound the stationary full-grid divisor more sharply
than (2.4), or prove that near-saturation forces a differential/Frobenius
relation among \(t,\beta t,\ldots,\beta^{r-1}t\).  Such a relation could
then force a coefficient-field drop or a visible symmetry.  General
Castelnuovo, Pluecker, discriminant-degree, Hodge-index, Prym-dimension,
and total-conductor estimates cannot do this: Proposition 3 gives them an
unbounded formal escape.
