## Scope actually supplied

KZB Remark 5.9 (p.32) uses the full lower ramification filtration to compute
the canonical coefficient. Theorem 5.11 (pp.32–33) states generator/relation
bounds 3e/6e for arbitrary possibly wild separably rooted stacky curves;
e is the maximum reduced denominator. The improved bounds require
g + sum floor(c_i) + deg(Delta) >= 2. This is not a cyclic-inertia theorem.
Its proof uses floor divisors and pole-order induction from VZB 8.3.1.
Corollary 4.20 and its proof (pp.29–30) construct particular global modular
root stacks using specified Hasse-invariant and modular-form identities.

Proof-local predecessors checked:

- Kobin, [Artin–Schreier root stacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03146),
  Lemma 4.10 (PDF p.8), Proposition 7.1 and proof (PDF pp.27–28):
  the floor-section formula and canonical different formula allow arbitrary
  finite stabilizers. The proof is local quotient descent plus the ordinary
  different formula, with no commutativity requirement.
- Voight–Zureick-Brown,
  [The canonical ring of a stacky curve](https://jvoight.github.io/articles/stacky-canonical-rings-final-fixederrata.pdf),
  Definition 5.1.4 (p.49; PDF p.54): separably rooted means that a stacky point
  descends to a separable residue extension. It is automatic over our
  algebraically closed field. Lemma 5.4.7 (p.56) proves the floor identity
  by coarse-space factorization. Theorem 8.3.1 and proof (pp.83–85) count
  poles using nonspecial divisors and a degree-one section; they do not
  impose a fixed-atlas condition.
- Kobin, [Artin–Schreier–Witt theory for stacky curves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09161),
  Theorem 5.8 and proof (pp.20–21), Section 8 (pp.24–25): the intrinsic
  root-stack theorem assumes cyclic wild subgroup. Its proof uses the
  cyclic intermediate cover and Witt-vector equations. The elementary
  abelian discussion is local; Questions 8.1–8.2 explicitly ask for an
  extension to arbitrary automorphism groups, including quaternion inertia.

## Consequences for the two prospective signatures

These are our deductions from the section formula, conditional on an ACTUAL
orbifold with the stated signature. They do not show that either orbifold,
or an atlas by the fixed curve, exists.

Put the wild point at 0 and tame point at infinity, and take the coarse
canonical divisor to be -2[infinity]. The rational divisors are

    D1 = (1143/1000)[0] - (8/7)[infinity],
    D2 = (3143/3000)[0] - (22/21)[infinity].

Their positive-degree pieces have monomial bases indexed respectively by

    ceil(8m/7) <= a <= floor(1143m/1000),
    ceil(22m/21) <= a <= floor(3143m/3000).

Here the monomial is x^(-a) times the chosen rational canonical tensor to
the mth power. Thus each dimension is max(0, upper-lower+1). The first
semigroup has primitive boundary rays (7,8) and (1000,1143), determinant
-1, so it is freely generated by those rays: its graded ring is polynomial
with generator weights 7 and 1000. This stronger particular calculation
does not need KZB's general degree bound.

For the first signature KZB's stated bounds would be 3000/6000; for the
second, 9000/18000. In both cases the integer part sum is 1, coarse genus
and log degree are 0, so the improved bound does not apply. These are bounds
on the abstract orbifold section ring, not atlas degrees.

## Exact remaining boundary

The floor ring depends on D and its marked coarse support. Over P1 with
two marked points this loses all local action data beyond the two different
ratios. In particular, finite generation alone cannot recover a prescribed
nonabelian inertia action or tell whether the fixed X is an etale atlas.
Given an actual atlas, pullback supplies a differential-compatible graded
embedding into R(X), with local zero orders determined by the actual
different and ramification index. Reversing this implication needs a proof
that the graded embedding realizes the differential pullback and the
specified etale stack morphism. None of the checked ring bounds supplies it.

The closest extra construction, KZB Corollary 4.20, starts with an already
specified modular stack and explicit modular forms, then identifies root
data by identities. Extending that proof to our signatures would require
constructing the missing nonabelian root/action data and verifying descent;
the rational signature and generator degrees do not provide those inputs.
The ASW predecessor explicitly leaves the corresponding general intrinsic
description open. This is a substantive missing step, not a usable
near-matching atlas theorem.

KZB's general bound is recorded as its stated theorem, not independently
audited here. Its proof moves integral coefficient parts into a log divisor
and invokes the tame pole induction for the remaining fractional part.
That argument does not authorize replacing our actual nonabelian orbifold
by a global cyclic root stack. The safe inputs for the current computation
are the arbitrary-inertia different formula, floor-section identity, and
direct two-point semigroup calculation.
