# Torsion flexibility of a canonical root on a cyclic etale cover

**Status: author proof, 2026-09-05; not independently audited.**

This note isolates the Picard-theoretic content of the torsion class in the
oriented cyclic diamond.  It proves that the cyclic cover and the canonical
root identity alone do **not** force that class to be generated by the cover.
It does not construct a map from the covering curve to the prescribed
hyperelliptic curve.

## 1. Setup

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed, let \(r\ne\operatorname{char}k\) be an
odd prime, and let

\[
                         p:V\longrightarrow C
\]

be a connected cyclic etale cover of degree \(r\), with generator \(\beta\).
Assume \(g(C)\ge2\).  Let

\[
               \vartheta\in J(C)[r]
\]

be the order-\(r\) line bundle defining the torsor.  Thus

\[
 \ker\bigl(p^*:J(C)[r]\longrightarrow J(V)[r]\bigr)
                         =\langle\vartheta\rangle.       \tag{1.1}
\]

Consider line bundles \(Q\) satisfying

\[
                         Q^r\simeq p^*\omega_C.          \tag{1.2}
\]

Such bundles exist: after choosing any line bundle of degree
\(2g(C)-2\) on \(V\), the remaining degree-zero discrepancy has an
\(r\)-th root because multiplication by \(r\) on \(J(V)\) is surjective.
Define

\[
       \eta_Q:=\omega_C\otimes\operatorname{Nm}_{V/C}(Q)^{-1}.
                                                               \tag{1.3}
\]

Taking norms in (1.2) shows that \(\eta_Q\in J(C)[r]\).

## 2. The exact range of the norm class

### Theorem

As \(Q\) varies through all solutions of (1.2), the classes \(\eta_Q\)
form a coset of

\[
       \langle\vartheta\rangle^\perp
       \subset J(C)[r],                                  \tag{2.1}
\]

where orthogonality is for the Weil pairing.  This subgroup has dimension
\(2g(C)-1\) over \(\mathbf F_r\).  Consequently the set of possible
\(\eta_Q\) always contains classes outside
\(\langle\vartheta\rangle\).

Moreover, the possible pullbacks \(p^*\eta_Q\) form a coset of a subspace
of \(J(V)[r]\) of dimension \(2g(C)-2\).  In particular the identity
\(p^*\eta_Q=0\) is not a formal consequence of (1.2).

#### Proof

The solutions of (1.2) form a torsor under \(J(V)[r]\).  If
\(z\in J(V)[r]\), then

\[
 \eta_{Q\otimes z}
       =\eta_Q\otimes\operatorname{Nm}_{V/C}(z)^{-1}.    \tag{2.2}
\]

Pullback and norm are adjoint for the principal polarizations and hence for
the Weil pairings.  Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Nm}:J(V)[r]\to J(C)[r]\bigr)
       =\ker(p^*)^\perp
       =\langle\vartheta\rangle^\perp.                  \tag{2.3}
\]

This proves the coset assertion.  The space in (2.3) has dimension
\(2g(C)-1\).  Since it contains the one-dimensional isotropic line
\(\langle\vartheta\rangle\) and \(g(C)\ge2\), any one of its cosets either
misses that line or contains many points outside it.  Thus not every
\(\eta_Q\) belongs to the torsor line.

Write \(N=1+\beta+\cdots+\beta^{r-1}\).  From (1.2) and (1.3),

\[
                 p^*\eta_Q=(r-N)Q.                      \tag{2.4}
\]

Replacing \(Q\) by \(Q\otimes z\) changes (2.4) by \(-Nz\).  On
\(r\)-torsion,

\[
                       N=p^*\operatorname{Nm}.
\]

By (2.3), its image has dimension

\[
 (2g(C)-1)-1=2g(C)-2,                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

because the kernel of \(p^*\) inside
\(\langle\vartheta\rangle^\perp\) is precisely
\(\langle\vartheta\rangle\).  This proves the last assertion.  QED.

## 3. Relation with the unipotent formula

Put \(T=\beta-1\) and \(\delta=TQ\).  Equation (1.2) implies
\(\delta\in J(V)[r]\).  In \(\mathbf F_r[C_r]\),

\[
            N=T^{r-1},
\]

and the polynomial \(S=(N-r)/(\beta-1)\) reduces to \(T^{r-2}\).
Consequently the formula from the oriented diamond becomes

\[
             p^*\eta_Q=-S\delta=-T^{r-2}\delta.         \tag{3.1}
\]

The dimension calculation (2.5) shows directly that this top unipotent
class can be nonzero.  Thus nilpotence \(T^r=0\) does not supply the one
additional vanishing \(T^{r-2}\delta=0\).

If \(g(C)-1=Ms\), every such \(Q\) also has an abstract \(2s\)-th root
\(L\in\operatorname{Pic}^M(V)\): multiplication by \(2s\) on the
degree-zero Picard variety is surjective.  Hence merely writing
\(Q=L^{2s}\) adds no restriction.  What is not supplied by this argument is
an effective reduced divisor representing \(L\), much less an identity

\[
                  L=a^*\mathcal O_Y(P_\infty)
\]

for an etale map \(a:V\to Y\).  Any proof of vanishing in the actual
diamond must use this additional mapping geometry.

## 4. Prime-to-r refinements preserve the distinction

Let \(q:C'\to C\) be a connected finite etale cover of degree \(n\) with
\(r\nmid n\), and base-change \(p\) to \(p':V'\to C'\).  Norm followed by
pullback is multiplication by \(n\), so

\[
                 q^*:J(C)[r]\longrightarrow J(C')[r]
\]

is injective.  In particular \(q^*\vartheta\) still has exact order \(r\),
so \(V'\) is connected.  Furthermore,

\[
 q^*\eta_Q\in\langle q^*\vartheta\rangle
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \eta_Q\in\langle\vartheta\rangle.                    \tag{4.1}
\]

The corresponding map \(V'\to V\) also has degree \(n\), so it is injective
on \(r\)-torsion after pullback; nonvanishing of \(p^*\eta_Q\) is preserved
as well.  Thus the prime-avoiding etale refinements of file 109 can be chosen
to preserve both connectedness of the cyclic torsor and the exact independent
\(r\)-torsion obstruction.
