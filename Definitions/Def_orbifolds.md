# Effective orbifold curves and atlases

ID: `orbifolds`. Uses [base conventions](Def_base_conventions.md).

An *effective orbifold curve* is a smooth proper connected
Deligne--Mumford stack of dimension one with trivial generic stabilizer.
It has a coarse curve B and is a curve on a dense open subset. In wild
characteristic, effectivity means faithful action on formal germs,
NOT faithful action on tangent spaces.

A *curve atlas* C→S here is a representable finite etale surjection.
Its degree is the degree of the generic extension k(C)/k(B). It need
not be Galois and may be divisible by the characteristic. The word
etale describes the map to the stack; the coarse map C→B can ramify.

The canonical degree of S is the rational number deg(omega_S), with
etale normalization n deg(omega_S)=2g(C)-2 for an atlas of degree n.
Do not replace wild ramification contributions by the tame formula
1-1/e without a tameness hypothesis.

If a finite constant group G acts faithfully on a curve W, [W/G]
denotes its effective quotient stack. Stabilizer orders may be divisible
by p although G as a constant group scheme is etale. A *common orbifold*
for X,Y is an S with actual finite etale curve atlases from both curves.
Its existence for a cored span is a theorem; it is not assumed for a
general common cover.

For the coarse map S -> B, write b_i for its exceptional points,
e_i for their inertia orders, and delta_i for their local different
exponents on a curve atlas. A reduced divisor on S is a finite sum of
distinct residual gerbes, including ordinary points (e=1). Its degree
assigns weight 1/e_i to b_i and weight one to an ordinary point.

For a chosen common quotient S, a *compatible reduced marking* is a
reduced divisor D_S whose pullbacks give the two endpoint divisors.
For a cored span one must use the quotient supplied by the group
generated by BOTH endpoint deck groups to classify all its markings.
An arbitrary common orbifold need not already contain the entire relation.
