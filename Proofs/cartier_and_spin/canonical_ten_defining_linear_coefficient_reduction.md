## Removing every transvection-free case

Suppose K has no transvection. Its center has orderEIGHT. Every nonmonomial applicable row in the displayed specialization has center of order at mostFOUR, so none can be K. The only possible table row is therefore a monomial family (indeed only the second row can have scalar orderEIGHT).

A rank-FOUR monomial group is solvable: its diagonal subgroup is abelian and its permutation quotient is a subgroup of S₄, which is solvable. Consequently its projective image N is solvable. The quotient R/N has order ONE orFIVE, so R is solvable as well. A nontrivial finite solvable group has a nontrivial normal abelian subgroup: its last nontrivial derived subgroup is such a subgroup. This contradicts the accepted normal abelian exclusion for R. Therefore K contains a transvection.

This deletion requires neither a separate proof of primitivity nor a uniqueness theorem for a monomial system of lines. In particular a normalizer of a monomial group is not silently assumed monomial.

## Removing the possible index-five extension

It remains to show that R is N rather than an index-FIVE normalizer extension. We establish the relevant normalizer inside the actual k-linear projective group, without invoking abstract field or graph automorphisms.

Let A∈GL₄(k) represent an element normalizing N projectively. The full determinant-ONE inverse image of N is SL₄(F_q): its scalar kernel μ4 is already contained in F_q and in SL₄(F_q). Therefore A normalizes SL₄(F_q) honestly.

The F_q-linear span of SL₄(F_q) is M₄(F_q). Indeed it contains I and I+tE_ij for i≠j; their differences span the off-diagonal elementary matrices. The span of a group is an algebra, so E_ijE_ji supplies the diagonal elementary matrices too. Conjugation by A preserves this span and fixes every scalar from F_q. It is an F_q-algebra automorphism of M₄(F_q). The elementary matrix-algebra form of Skolem–Noether realizes it by conjugation with B∈GL₄(F_q). Then B⁻¹A centralizes M₄(F_q), so B⁻¹A is scalar in GL₄(k). We have proved
\[
N_{\operatorname{PGL}_4(k)}(\operatorname{PSL}_4(\mathbf F_q))
=\operatorname{PGL}_4(\mathbf F_q).
\]
Since R normalizes N, it lies in this normalizer. The quotient PGL₄(F_q)/PSL₄(F_q) is cyclic of ordergcd(FOUR,q−ONE)=FOUR. Thus [R:N] dividesFOUR. The elementary actual-inertia reduction gives [R:N] ONE orFIVE. It follows that R=N.
