# Proof: principal parts make restriction to the clump faithful

[Statement](../../Theorems/shared_tensors/clump_fiber_picard.md).
We use the [common-line extension spectrum](../deformations/shared_line_extension_spectrum.md),
[finite clump descent](clump_finite_descent.md), and the
[actual common Picard group](saturated_divisor_relations.md).
All line classes, cohomology maps and restrictions retain the given
identification on Z.

## Restriction and its canonical kernel element

Trivialize the line fibers at the vertices of the finite connected
graph $\Gamma_S$. The edge isomorphisms become nonzero scalars.
Changing vertex trivializations is a graph coboundary; this defines
$\operatorname{res}_S$. A nonzero compatible vertex section is
nonzero at every vertex by connectedness and trivializes this class.
Its space, when nonzero, is one-dimensional.

The conormal map
\[
\mathcal O(-S)|_S\xrightarrow{d}\Omega|_S
\]
is an isomorphism and commutes with both étale pullbacks. Hence
$H:=\Omega(S)$ has a canonical trivial restriction. Put
$K=\ker(\operatorname{res}_S)$. We first show that every member
of K has Y-degree divisible by $R+2=\deg H_Y$.

## There is no smaller degree direction in the restriction kernel

Let L belong to K. Multiply it by a suitable integral power of H
to obtain $L'$ of degree
\[
R+2< D'\le2R+4.
\]
Set $M=L'\Omega^{-1}$. The conormal trivialization shows
\[
M^{-1}(S)|_S=L'^{-1}H|_S\simeq\mathcal O_S
\]
as a common fiber triple. Since $\deg M_Y=D'-2>R$, the line
$M^{-1}(S)$ has negative degree on both endpoints and on Z.
Its principal-parts boundary therefore sends this common unit
injectively to a nonzero class in J(M).

The low-degree spectrum excludes J(M) whenever
$R<\deg M_Y\le2R$. Indeed a nonzero class in that range would
have $p^n\deg M_Y=2(R+1)$ for some $n\ge1$, while
$2(R+1)/p\le R$ for $p\ge3,R\ge2$. Thus D' can only be
$2R+3$ or $2R+4$. The degree residues of K modulo R+2 lie
in $\{-1,0\}$. They form a subgroup of $\mathbf Z/(R+2)$;
as R+2 is at least four, the only such subgroup is zero.
This proves the degree assertion.

Subtracting a power of H now shows
\[
K=\mathbf Z[H]+(K\cap T),
\]
where T is the finite degree-zero subgroup. Its order is prime to p,
by the absence of shared regular one-forms. We must still exclude
its possible nontrivial kernel; degree arithmetic alone does not do so.

## Finite étale descent detects every torsion line

Every element of T has order prime to p, since there is no shared
regular one-form. The finite clump-descent theorem applies directly:
a common cyclic torsor of exact order N has a connected component
diagram; trivial fiber transport would split its lifted clump into
N components, whereas that diagram has at most one. Therefore
$K\cap T=0$, and hence $K=\mathbf Z[\Omega(S)]$.
No Frobenius root calculation or exceptional first-Witt case is
needed for this step; prime-to-p detection holds in every genus.
The index formula in the statement follows from
$0\to T\to P\to e_0\mathbf Z\to0$. It is prime to p because
$R+2\equiv1\pmod p$ and $p\nmid|T|$.

## Exact tangent transport order

The cotangent edge identifications are exactly the derivatives of
the two actual étale local maps. Thus their loop multipliers describe
$\operatorname{res}_S(\Omega)$. They generate a finite cyclic
subgroup of $k^\times$: there are finitely many graph generators,
and each scalar lies in a finite field.

Suppose its order divides q, q positive. The kernel calculation
gives $\Omega^q=H^b$ for some integer b. Degrees imply
$2q=b(R+2)$, so b is positive, and this equality is equivalent to
\[
\Omega^{q-b}\simeq\mathcal O(bS),\qquad q-b=bR/2>0.
\]
The polynomial common section ring is generated by the primitive
tensor of divisor eS. Thus the last identity holds exactly when
e divides b. Its smallest possible b is e, for which the tensor
itself supplies the identity and $q=d+e$. This proves the exact
order, including the integer parity condition.

## Principal-part layers and the strengthened dimension bound

For $D=\deg L_Y>2R$, the all-degree extension reduction identifies
J(L) with matching principal parts of order
$b=\lfloor(D-1)/(2R)\rfloor$. Filter by actual pole order c.
The cth successive quotient injects into the common sections of
\[
L^{-1}(cS)|_S=(L\Omega^c)^{-1}|_S,
\]
where the conormal isomorphism is used again. Such a section space
has dimension one if $L\Omega^c\in\mathbf Z[H]$, and zero
otherwise. This proves (4). Two allowed orders differ by a multiple
of the exact order h of $\operatorname{res}_S(\Omega)$, proving
the stated congruence and counting bound. This argument does not
assume that an invariant leading coefficient lifts to a higher jet.

It also shows that nonzero J(L) in this range forces
$L\in\langle\Omega,H\rangle=\langle\Omega,\mathcal O(S)\rangle$.
In the low-degree range, $L^{p^n}=\Omega(2S)$ instead gives
$\operatorname{res}_S(L)^{p^n}=\operatorname{res}_S(\Omega)^{-1}$.
The finite restriction image has order prime to p, so taking pth
roots preserves its cyclic subgroup generated by this character.
This proves the same line-class restriction at every positive degree.

For a simple pole there is no further lifting obstruction at S:
the common fiber section space itself is the principal-parts space.
Its nonvanishing is equivalent to
$L\Omega=H^{j+1}$ for an integer j, or
$L=\Omega^j((j+1)S)$. The inequality D>R says j is positive.
The jth power of the canonical conormal unit gives its section.
Negative degree makes its boundary nonzero. For $2R<D\le4R$
the allowed pole order b is one, and the only degrees in this
interval are those with j=1 or with j=2 and R at least four.
This proves the final exact assertion.

The conclusion concerns fiber transport at a known finite component.
It does not prove that the formal holonomy is finite, algebraize an
element with this derivative, or create the required finite component
from an arbitrary span.
