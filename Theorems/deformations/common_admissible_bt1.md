# A common admissible oper has actual compatible BT1 realizations

Version1,20 September2026. Let $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5$ and let
$X\xleftarrow f Z\xrightarrow gY$ be the TWO ACTUAL finite etale
maps of a span of smooth proper connected hyperbolic curves.
Suppose admissible active projective opers $r_X,r_Y$ have equal
pullbacks on $Z$. Assume that $r_Y$ is indigenous-ordinary.

There are actual everywhere-versal height-two, dimension-one BT1
groups $H_X/X$, $H_Y/Y$, inducing the given projective opers, and a
flat line $D$ of order dividing four on $Z$ such that
\[
f^*H_X\simeq g^*H_Y\otimes\Lambda_D.
\tag{1}
\]
Here $\Lambda_D$ is the corresponding rank-one etale
$\mathbf F_5$-local system, with the convention dictated by
contravariant Dieudonne theory. Thus a connected CYCLIC finite etale
refinement $h:Z'\to Z$ of degree1,2 or4 makes the two groups
isomorphic. Neither endpoint is replaced. No ordinariness of $r_X$
or $r_Z$ and no bound on the original covering degrees is needed.

The group $H_Y$ can be chosen as the first truncation of its
determinant-normalized full group from
[ordinary oper effectivity](ordinary_oper_bt_effectivity.md).
The assertion for $H_X$ is actual finite-flat descent, not a claim
that an arbitrary special-fiber crystal is effective. It also does
not assert that $H_X$ extends globally to BT2 or that the full group
on $Y$ descends through $f$.

In the other direction, compatible everywhere-versal height-two,
dimension-one BT1 groups induce common admissible active projective
opers. Equality after any finite etale refinement implies equality
on the original source. Hence, in the ordinary-genus-two endpoint
setting, the existence of a common admissible active oper is
equivalent to the existence of compatible actual BT1 data after
finite etale source refinement.

For the selected two endpoints all85 active opers on $Y$ are
ordinary, so the equivalence covers their entire admissible active
branch. It supplies no common oper or group in the dormant or
no-common-connection branches. Both original common-cover problems
remain open.
