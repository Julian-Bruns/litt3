\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 V&\xrightarrow{a}&Y\\
 \downarrow p&&\\[-2mm]
 C&\xrightarrow{c}&X,
 \end{array}
 \qquad
 \deg a=\deg c=M,\qquad \deg p=7,
\tag{55.1}
\]

where \(p\) is a cyclic etale cover generated by \(\beta\), and

\[
 Y:z^2=1-t^{31},\qquad
 P(T)=\operatorname{Nm}_{V/C}(T-t),\qquad
 W=W_P\subseteq H^0(C,L).
\tag{55.2}
\]

For every hyperelliptic branch value

\[
                    \alpha\in\mathcal A=\mu_{31}\cup\{\infty\},
\]

file 44 constructs an effective degree-\(M\) divisor \(D_\alpha\) with

\[
                   \operatorname{div}(P(\alpha))=2D_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_\alpha=\mathcal O_C(D_\alpha-D_\infty)
                    \in J(C)[2].
\tag{55.3}
\]

This note gives two controls on these divisors which are independent of
the dimension-only argument of file 46.

First, if eight of the 32 labels \(\epsilon_\alpha\) coincide, then all
coefficient sections are quadratic expressions in sections of one
base-point-free degree-\(M\) line bundle.  This produces a new
coefficient-field coarsening whose degree divides both \(M\) and the degree
of the coarsening in file 47.  In particular, in the full-span case at
\(M=9\), no label can occur eight times.  Hence the 32 branch values give
at least five distinct labels, and the image of \(J(Y)[2]\) in \(J(C)[2]\)
has dimension at least three.

Second, the spectral curve in \(C\times\mathbf P^1_t\) has total delta
invariant \(12M\), and its conductor is exactly the sum of the six
pairwise root-collision divisors, each of degree \(4M\).  Collisions inside
the special branch fibers are tangent and therefore cost twice as much as
ordinary branch collisions.  This yields a uniform quantitative bound on
the nonreducedness of all 32 divisors \(D_\alpha\).

Neither result yet excludes a seven-diamond.  The exact remaining gap is
stated in Section 4.

## 1. Eight equal labels force a quadratic coarsening

For a line bundle \(A\) with \(A^2\simeq L\), put

\[
 U_A=\operatorname{im}\!\left(
       \operatorname{Sym}^2H^0(C,A)\longrightarrow H^0(C,L)
                              \right).
\tag{55.4}
\]

Let

\[
 \phi:C\longrightarrow\mathbf P(W^\vee),
 \qquad
 e=[k(C):k(B)]
\tag{55.5}
\]

be the coefficient map and its generic degree, as in Proposition 47.1.

### Theorem 55.1 (the eight-point threshold)

Suppose that a subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal A\) of eight distinct branch
values has constant label:

\[
                    \epsilon_\alpha=\epsilon_0
                    \qquad(\alpha\in S).
\tag{55.6}
\]

Then the common line bundle

\[
 A=\mathcal O_C(D_\infty)\otimes\epsilon_0,
 \qquad \deg A=M,
 \qquad A^2\simeq L,
\tag{55.7}
\]

is base-point free, and

\[
                              W\subseteq U_A.          \tag{55.8}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
 \psi_A:C\longrightarrow\mathbf P(H^0(C,A)^\vee)
\tag{55.9}
\]

is the complete morphism, and \(B_A\) is the normalization of its image,
then \(\phi\) factors through \(\psi_A\) by a linear system of quadrics.
Writing

\[
                   e_A=[k(C):k(B_A)],
\tag{55.10}
\]

one has

\[
                         e_A\mid e,qquad e_A\mid M.   \tag{55.11}
\]

#### Proof

Equality (55.6) identifies all line bundles
\(\mathcal O_C(D_\alpha)\), for \(\alpha\in S\), with \(A\).  Under any
such identifications, the canonical section with divisor \(D_\alpha\)
squares, up to a nonzero scalar, to the coefficient section \(P(\alpha)\).
Thus

\[
                         P(\alpha)\in U_A
                         \qquad(\alpha\in S).          \tag{55.12}
\]

The coefficient curve

\[
 \nu:\mathbf P^1\longrightarrow\mathbf P(W),
 \qquad \alpha\longmapsto[P(\alpha)]
\]

is nondegenerate and satisfies

\[
                         \nu^*\mathcal O(1)=\mathcal O(7)
\tag{55.13}
\]

by file 46.  Any eight distinct points of this curve span
\(\mathbf P(W)\).  Indeed, if their span were proper, a hyperplane
containing them would pull back to a nonzero section of
\(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(7)\) with eight distinct zeros.  A hyperplane
containing the whole curve is impossible because the curve is
nondegenerate.  Therefore the eight sections in (55.12) span \(W\),
which proves (55.8).

If \(A\) had a base point, every product of two sections of \(A\) would
vanish there.  Equation (55.8) would then give a base point of \(W\),
contrary to Proposition 44.3.  Hence \(A\) is base-point free.

Every element of \(U_A\) is the pullback of a quadratic form on the target
of (55.9).  Thus (55.8) gives the asserted factorization of \(\phi\).
On function fields this says

\[
                  k(B)\subseteq k(B_A)\subseteq k(C),
\]

so \(e_A\mid e\).  Finally, if \(A_A\) denotes the hyperplane bundle on
the normalization \(B_A\), then

\[
                   A\simeq\psi_A^*A_A,
 \qquad
                   M=\deg A=e_A\deg A_A,
\]

and hence \(e_A\mid M\).  This proves (55.11). \(\square\)

### Corollary 55.2 (five distinct labels at full span for \(M=9\))

Assume

\[
                         M=9,\qquad \dim W=8.
\tag{55.14}
\]

Then every fiber of the map

\[
 \mathcal A\longrightarrow J(C)[2],
 \qquad \alpha\longmapsto\epsilon_\alpha
\tag{55.15}
\]

has at most seven elements.  Consequently

\[
 \#\{\epsilon_\alpha:\alpha\in\mathcal A\}\geq5,
 \qquad
 \dim_{\mathbf F_2}h(J(Y)[2])\geq3,
\tag{55.16}
\]

where \(h=p_*a^*:J(Y)\to J(C)\).

#### Proof

Suppose that eight labels coincide.  Theorem 55.1 gives a
base-point-free degree-nine line bundle \(A\) and a morphism \(\psi_A\).
Theorem 47.3 gives \(e=2\).  Hence (55.11), together with
\(e_A\mid9\), forces \(e_A=1\): the morphism \(\psi_A\) is birational
onto its image.

Moreover, (55.8) and \(\dim W=8\) imply

\[
          \binom{h^0(A)+1}{2}\geq8,
 \qquad\text{so}\qquad h^0(A)\geq4.                  \tag{55.17}
\]

Thus \(\psi_A(C)\) is a nondegenerate degree-nine curve in projective
dimension at least three.  Castelnuovo's bound is largest in dimension
three, where

\[
             9-1=4(3-1)+0,
 \qquad
             \pi(9,3)=\binom42(3-1)=12.
\tag{55.18}
\]

This contradicts \(g(C)=19\).  Hence no label occurs eight times.  Since
there are 32 branch values, at least \(\lceil32/7\rceil=5\) distinct
labels occur.  A vector space over \(\mathbf F_2\) containing five
distinct elements has dimension at least three.  Equation (44.11) says
that all labels belong to \(h(J(Y)[2])\), proving (55.16). \(\square\)

## 2. The exact spectral conductor

Let

\[
 \Psi=(p,t):V\longrightarrow C\times\mathbf P^1_t,
\]

and let \(\Sigma\) be its reduced image.  For \(1\leq j\leq6\), define
the root-collision divisor

\[
 E_j^t=(t,t\beta^j)^*\Delta_{\mathbf P^1}
                         \quad\text{on }V.             \tag{55.19}
\]

The two maps in (55.19) are distinct by Proposition 44.3, so this is an
effective divisor.

### Theorem 55.3 (spectral normalization and conductor budget)

The map \(V\to\Sigma\) is the normalization and is birational.  The
spectral curve has

\[
 p_a(\Sigma)=26M+1,
 \qquad
 \delta(\Sigma)=12M.                                 \tag{55.20}
\]

If \(\mathfrak C_\Sigma\) is the conductor divisor on \(V\), then

\[
 \mathfrak C_\Sigma=\sum_{j=1}^6E_j^t,
 \qquad
 \deg E_j^t=4M,
 \qquad
 \deg\mathfrak C_\Sigma=24M.                         \tag{55.21}
\]

#### Proof

Proposition 44.3 says that \(t\) generates \(k(V)\) over \(k(C)\).
Therefore \(k(\Sigma)=k(C)(t)=k(V)\), which proves birationality and the
normalization assertion.

The curve \(\Sigma\) has bidegrees seven and \(2M\).  On
\(C\times\mathbf P^1\), one consequently has

\[
 \Sigma^2=2\cdot7\cdot2M=28M.                         \tag{55.22}
\]

The intersection with the canonical divisor can be computed on the
normalization:

\[
 \Sigma\cdot K_{C\times\mathbf P^1}
   =\deg p^*K_C+\deg t^*K_{\mathbf P^1}
   =7(4M)-2(2M)=24M.                                  \tag{55.23}
\]

Adjunction gives \(p_a(\Sigma)=26M+1\).  Since
\(g(V)=14M+1), this proves (55.20).

Etale-locally on \(C\), the seven branches of \(\Sigma\) are the smooth
graphs of the seven functions \(t\beta^i\).  Exactly as in Proposition
45.3, the conductor exponent on one normalized branch is the sum of its
intersection multiplicities with the other six branches.  This proves
the divisor identity in (55.21).

Finally, the diagonal on \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) has line
bundle \(\mathcal O(1,1)\).  Since both \(t\) and \(t\beta^j\) have
degree \(2M\),

\[
                         \deg E_j^t=2M+2M=4M.
\]

Summing over the six nontrivial translates gives conductor degree
\(24M=2\delta(\Sigma)\), as required. \(\square\)

## 3. Quantitative reducedness of the 32 special divisors

For \(\alpha\in\mathcal A\) and \(x\in C\), let

\[
 n_{\alpha,x}=\operatorname{mult}_x(D_\alpha),
 \qquad
 s_\alpha=|\operatorname{Supp}(D_\alpha)|.
\tag{55.24}
\]

Thus

\[
                 \sum_x n_{\alpha,x}=M,
 \qquad
                 \sum_x(n_{\alpha,x}-1)=M-s_\alpha.
\tag{55.25}
\]

### Corollary 55.4 (the branch-collision inequality)

One has

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}\sum_{x\in C}
          n_{\alpha,x}(n_{\alpha,x}-1)\leq12M
 }
\tag{55.26}
\]

and consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A}s_\alpha\geq26M.
 }
\tag{55.27}
\]

#### Proof

The divisor \(R_\alpha=a^*P_\alpha\) is reduced, because \(a\) is
etale.  Its points above \(x\) are exactly the
\(n_{\alpha,x}\) distinct branches of \(\Sigma\) over \(x\) whose
\(t\)-value is \(\alpha\).

On every such branch, \(t-\alpha\) has order two: these are precisely the
tame ramification points above the hyperelliptic branch value \(\alpha\).
Hence two distinct such branches have intersection multiplicity at least
two in \(C\times\mathbf P^1\).  In the conductor divisor, ordered pairs
of distinct branches are counted.  The contribution above
\((x,\alpha)\) is therefore at least

\[
                         2n_{\alpha,x}(n_{\alpha,x}-1).
\tag{55.28}
\]

Summing (55.28) and using the total conductor degree \(24M\) from
Theorem 55.3 proves (55.26).  Since

\[
                         n(n-1)\geq2(n-1)
                         \qquad(n\geq1),
\]

equations (55.25)--(55.26) give

\[
 \sum_\alpha(M-s_\alpha)\leq6M.
\]

There are 32 values of \(\alpha\), so this is exactly (55.27).
\(\square\)

## 4. Exact boundary of the result

Theorem 55.1 turns a large collision among square-root labels into a
genuine lower-degree linear series and a field coarsening.  It eliminates
such a collision in the most rigid case \(M=9,\dim W=8\).  What it does
not control is a distribution of the 32 labels in fibers of size at most
seven.  The bound (55.16) is therefore far from the injectivity on
\(J(Y)[2]\) that would settle the remaining norm-kernel question.

Theorem 55.3 and Corollary 55.4 show that geometric collisions among the
seven roots have a completely fixed global budget, and that collisions
inside a hyperelliptic branch fiber consume that budget with multiplicity
at least two.  They do not prevent all \(D_\alpha\) from being reduced;
indeed, reduced special divisors consume none of this conductor budget.

A further theorem on this route must therefore relate **linear-equivalence
collisions** \(\epsilon_\alpha=\epsilon_\beta\) to **geometric root
collisions** measured by the \(E_j^t\), or else show directly that the
quadratic coarsening forced by eight equal labels is impossible beyond the
single full-span case handled above.  Neither implication follows from
degree, genus, or conductor bookkeeping alone.
