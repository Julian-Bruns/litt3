# Automorphisms of the cyclic genus-fifteen atlas

## Status and scope

Proved. This note excludes the most direct degree-\(31\) source of a
non-visible correspondence: applying an unexpected automorphism to the
cyclic atlas does not produce one.

It does not classify every degree-\(31\), genus-\(15\) cover of
\(\mathbf P^1(31,31,31)\).

Let

\[
             Y:\quad y^{31}=x(x-1)
\]

over \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\).

## Theorem

\[
              \operatorname{Aut}_k(Y)\simeq C_{31}\times C_2.
                                                               \tag{20.1}
\]

The factor \(C_{31}\) sends \(y\mapsto\zeta y\), and the factor \(C_2\)
sends \(x\mapsto1-x\). Consequently, if

\[
 a:Y\longrightarrow S=\mathbf P^1(31,31,31)
\]

is the cyclic atlas and \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(Y)\), then the two
maps \(a\) and \(a\alpha\) differ by a permutation of the three stacky
points of \(S\). They therefore form a visible self-correspondence.

## The branch-set calculation

We first prove the elementary calculation responsible for (20.1).

### Lemma

In characteristic \(5\),

\[
 \operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)}
       \bigl(\mu_{31}\cup\{\infty\}\bigr)
 =\{t\mapsto\zeta t:\zeta^{31}=1\}\simeq C_{31}.          \tag{20.2}
\]

### Proof

Put \(B=\mu_{31}\cup\{\infty\}\), and suppose that a fractional linear
map \(\phi\) preserves \(B\).

We first show that \(\phi(\infty)=\infty\). Otherwise, by precomposing and
postcomposing with scalings by elements of \(\mu_{31}\), we may arrange

\[
                     \phi(\infty)=1,\qquad \phi(1)=\infty.
\]

After scaling its matrix, \(\phi\) then has the form

\[
                       \phi(t)=\frac{t+b}{t-1},
                       \qquad b\ne-1.                    \tag{20.3}
\]

For every \(t\in\mu_{31}\setminus\{1\}\), equation (20.3) and preservation
of \(B\) give \(\phi(t)^{31}=1\). Hence

\[
                  (t+b)^{31}-(t-1)^{31}
\]

vanishes at the thirty roots of

\[
                  \frac{t^{31}-1}{t-1}=1+t+\cdots+t^{30}.
\]

Its degree is at most thirty, so it is a scalar multiple of the latter
polynomial. Lucas's theorem, or the factorization

\[
                  (1+T)^{31}=(1+T)(1+T^5)(1+T^{25}),
\]

shows that the only nonzero binomial coefficients
\(\binom{31}{j}\) modulo \(5\) occur for

\[
                  j\in\{0,1,5,6,25,26,30,31\}.
\]

After the leading terms cancel, the polynomial on the left is supported
only in degrees

\[
                  30,26,25,6,5,1,0.
\]

A nonzero scalar multiple of \(1+t+\cdots+t^{30}\) has a nonzero
coefficient in every degree. The scalar must therefore be zero. Comparing
the coefficient of \(t^{30}\) in
\((t+b)^{31}=(t-1)^{31}\) gives \(b=-1\), contradicting the nonzero
determinant condition in (20.3). Thus \(\phi\) fixes \(\infty\).

Write now \(\phi(t)=at+b\), with \(a\ne0\). Since it permutes
\(\mu_{31}\), there is a nonzero scalar \(\lambda\) such that

\[
                  (at+b)^{31}-1=\lambda(t^{31}-1).
\]

The coefficient of \(t^{30}\) is \(a^{30}b\), so \(b=0\); comparison of
the leading and constant terms then gives \(a^{31}=1\). Conversely, every
such scaling preserves \(B\). This proves (20.2). \(\square\)

## Proof of the theorem

Set

\[
                         t=y,\qquad v=2x-1.
\]

Because \(4=-1\) in characteristic \(5\), the equation of \(Y\) becomes

\[
                         v^2=1-t^{31}.                    \tag{20.4}
\]

Thus \(Y\) is hyperelliptic of genus \(15\), and the branch set of its
hyperelliptic map \(t:Y\to\mathbf P^1_t\) is precisely

\[
                         \mu_{31}\cup\{\infty\}.
\]

For a hyperelliptic curve of genus at least two in odd characteristic, the
hyperelliptic involution is unique and central. Every automorphism descends
to a projective automorphism preserving the branch set, and the kernel of
this descent is generated by the hyperelliptic involution. The lemma
therefore gives

\[
 1\longrightarrow C_2\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(Y)
 \longrightarrow C_{31}\longrightarrow1.
\]

All \(31\) branch-set scalings lift explicitly as
\((t,v)\mapsto(\zeta t,v)\), and the hyperelliptic involution is
\((t,v)\mapsto(t,-v)\). These lifts commute, proving (20.1).

In the original coordinates, the \(C_{31}\)-factor fixes \(x\), while the
hyperelliptic involution sends \(x\) to \(1-x\). Hence \(a\alpha\) is
either \(a\) or the composition of \(a\) with the target transposition
\(0\leftrightarrow1\). This is precisely visibility. \(\square\)

## Computational audit

As an independent check, the \(32\cdot31\cdot30\) fractional linear maps
determined by ordered images of three points of \(B\) were enumerated over
\(\mathbf F_{125}\), which contains \(\mu_{31}\). Exactly \(31\) preserve
\(B\), all fix \(\infty\), and all are the scalings in (20.2). The proof
above, rather than this finite enumeration, establishes the result over
\(k\).
