## The actual field extension and finite groups

Choose a nonzero original section u0. Every spin ratio satisfies
\[

\]
The Γ field is generated by these ratios, and the primitive source hypothesis gives k(T)=E k(Γ). Thus T/E is generated by finitely many SIXTEENTH roots of elements of E. Since μ16⊂k and SIXTEEN is prime to FIVE, this extension is Galois with abelian group H embedded in a product of μ16 groups. Its exponent divides SIXTEEN. It is étale: E contains an original X-field and is an intermediate field of the ACTUAL étale map T→X; the corresponding normal-curve factor maps are étale.

The compositum E is G-stable. Therefore G normalizes its entire deck group H. The finite group HG acts on the SAME T. Put N=H∩G. It is normal in G, and it is normal in H because H is abelian. The quotient T0=T/N is a genuine smooth connected quotient retaining finite étale maps to Y and every original X, since N fixes both fields and acts freely on T.

Use the finite Galois extension k(T)/k(T)^(HG). The fixed fields of H and G are exactly E and k(Y). The standard compositum/fixed-intersection identity yields
\[
E\,k(Y)=k(T)^{H\cap G}=k(T_0).
\]
The action of G0=G/N on E is faithful, because its kernel upstairs is precisely G∩Aut(T/E)=N. The map T0→Y is its actual étale torsor, and T0→E is actual étale of degree e=|H/N|. The equivariant map of that torsor to E defines Y→[E/G0]. Its pullback along E→[E/G0] is exactly T0, by the definition of the quotient stack. Since the pulled-back map T0→E is finite étale of degree e, étale descent makes Y→[E/G0] representable finite étale of that degree. For every geometric inertia group I of [E/G0], representability and étaleness make I act freely on the e-element atlas fiber. Hence |I| divides e. The degree e is a power of TWO, so every inertia group is a TWO-group and the stack is tame.

## Complete degree and signature bound

Let B be the smooth coarse curve. Faithfulness gives trivial generic inertia, so the coarse Y→B map has degree e. Tameness and the étale atlas square make every coarse branch fiber complete and uniform, with its common index a power of TWO dividing e. Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
2=e\left(2g(B)-2+\sum_j(1-1/e_j)\right).
\]
It implies g(B)≤TWO. In genus TWO only e=ONE and no branch are possible. In genus ONE each branch contributes at least ONE HALF. Thus either e=TWO with TWO indices2, or e=FOUR with ONE index2.

In genus ZERO, order the indices and write r for their number. One has r≥THREE. If r≥SIX, the area is at least ONE, so e≤TWO and equality forces SIX indices2. For r=FIVE the area is at least ONE HALF; e≤FOUR, and equality forces FIVE indices2. For r=FOUR, the all-two area is ZERO; the first positive area is ONE QUARTER, obtained by (2,2,2,4). Since e is a power of TWO, e≤EIGHT. For e=EIGHT the area equation forces that signature. For e=FOUR every index is TWO orFOUR, and area ONE HALF forces exactly TWO indicesFOUR, giving(2,2,4,4). For e=TWO the only indices are TWO and the area is ZERO. These are all quadrilateral cases.

For r=THREE, if the smallest index is TWO, the second is at least FOUR. A second indexFOUR gives area ONE QUARTER minus1/e3, and its first positive value is ONE EIGHTH at e3=EIGHT, yielding e=SIXTEEN. If the smallest index is at least FOUR, the area is at least ONE QUARTER. Thus e≤EIGHT, and the only possible case is e=EIGHT with signature(4,4,4). These elementary inequalities give the complete displayed table and e≤SIXTEEN.
