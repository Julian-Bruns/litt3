## Actual quotients and the noncommutative scalar

For a normal subgroup K of P, and a free left R-module M, the map

    k[P/K] tensor_R M --> M^K,    1 tensor m |--> N_K m

is an isomorphism of k[P/K]-modules. This follows on each regular
summand by its basis of K-coset sums, even though the order of K is
zero in k. Normality makes N_K central and supplies the quotient action.
The actual negative-cohomology Cartan--Leray identification is compatible
with pullback from T/K. Applying these facts to BOTH free modules in
the Hodge presentation identifies the induced quotient matrix with
the actual Hodge matrix of T/K.

Frobenius fixes all group elements and the coefficients of N_K. Thus
this is compatible with the actual coefficient twist on the source;
it does not identify the source twist with the target by an unsupported
linear convention. Right exactness then specializes the cokernel.
The proof does not assert that taking invariants of the defect cokernel
is exact, which would be an invalid substitute for this argument.

A matrix block invertible modulo J is invertible over R: its remaining
part lies in the nilpotent matrix ideal generated by J. The usual
ordered block operations therefore give the scalar Schur entry over
the noncommutative ring itself. No determinant or commutativity is used.
On a rank-one free LEFT module this scalar map is right multiplication
by f, so its image is the LEFT ideal R f. The same invertible block
operations specialize to every group quotient.

## Vanishing of the linear term

On each actual cyclic-five quotient, the first two unipotent layers
give a rank-two subbundle A_2 of the actual pushed-forward structure
sheaf on C^(1), with an extension 0 -> O -> A_2 -> O -> 0.
For a generator s of H0(E), its connecting image pairs under Serre
duality with s as the cup product of the extension class and beta(s,s).
The latter section is zero because beta is alternating.

Both H0(E) and H1(E) have dimension one. Hence this connecting map
vanishes, the long exact sequence gives h0(E tensor A_2)=2, and the
actual cyclic cover has defect at least two. This argument does not
put an alternating scalar form on H0(E), and does not assume an
ordinary Jacobian.

If the specialized scalar in k[e]/(e^5) had nonzero linear coefficient,
it would be e times a unit and its cokernel would have dimension one.
Thus every nonzero F5-character of P kills the image of f in J/J^2.
The identification

    J/J^2 = k tensor_F5 (P/(P^5[P,P]))

shows that coordinate F5-characters already detect every coefficient
over k. Consequently f belongs to J^2. The trivial group also satisfies
the statement, since J=0.

## Width and actual Heisenberg filtration

The rank estimate follows directly by restricting right multiplication
by f to J^i and choosing a vector-space complement. Its two image
contributions have dimensions at most dim(R/J^i) and dim J^(i+2).
No equality of filtered and associated-graded ranks is required.

For the actual Heisenberg group, the binomial ordered-word basis is
invertible relative to the group basis. The displayed yx rewriting
identity reduces inversions and never decreases the weight with
wt(x)=wt(y)=1, wt(z)=2. The central z commutes with x and y, and the
relations x^5=y^5=z^5=0 introduce no lower-weight terms. Thus the
weight filtration is multiplicative. Together with z in J^2 and
F_1=J, this proves both inclusions J^n=F_n, rather than assuming a
PBW associated-graded model.

For completeness, the all-odd-prime adjacent-width calculation has
a short direct proof. If A_m(t)=1+...+t^(m-1), then

    (1+t) A_p(t)^2 A_p(t^2) = A_p(t)^2 A_(2p)(t).

The coefficients on the left are adjacent sums of the Hilbert
coefficients. A coefficient on the right sums a length-2p window of
the nonnegative coefficients of A_p(t)^2, whose full support has
length 2p-1. Its maximum is therefore the full sum p^2. In particular
it is 25 for p=5. The balanced abelian examples follow from the
ordinary truncated polynomial algebra and its symmetric unimodal
coefficient sequence; the two central rank-three coefficients are
(3q^2+1)/4 and (3q^2-3)/4.
