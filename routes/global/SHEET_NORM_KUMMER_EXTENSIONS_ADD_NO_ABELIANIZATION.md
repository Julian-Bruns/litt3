# Sheet-norm Kummer extensions add no abelianization

Date: 2026-09-05.
Author: /root/gluing_cohomology_rigidity.
Status: proved and author-checked; not independently audited.

This records an exact monodromy consequence of an actual second étale
leg. It does not prove properness or failure of restricted theta.

## 1. The actual maps and the statement

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of characteristic \(p>0\), and let
\[
                       X\xleftarrow f Z\xrightarrow g Y
\]
be finite étale maps of smooth projective connected hyperbolic curves.
Let \(\pi:W\to X\) be the connected Galois closure of the specified
map \(f\), with group \(G\). Fix \(r_0:W\to Z\), and put
\(H=\operatorname{Gal}(W/Z)\).

Assume
\[
                  f_*g^*=0\quad\text{in }
                     \operatorname{Hom}(J(Y),J(X)).          \tag{1.1}
\]
In particular this holds when
\(\operatorname{Hom}(J(X),J(Y))=0\): duality through the principal
polarizations also gives \(\operatorname{Hom}(J(Y),J(X))=0\).
Minimality of the original joint image is not required by the lemma.

Fix a prime
\[
                                  \ell\nmid p|G|             \tag{1.2}
\]
and \(M\in J(Y)[\ell^a](k)\), with \(a\ge1\).
Let \(P\subset J(W)[\ell^a](k)\) be the subgroup generated by the
pullbacks of \(M\) along all sheets \(W\to Z\to Y\).
Let \(U\to W\) be the connected Kummer cover specified by \(P\).

**Theorem.** The composite \(U\to X\) is finite étale Galois, with
an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad N=\operatorname{Hom}(P,\mu_{\ell^a}).                  \tag{1.3}
\]
The natural quotient induces an isomorphism
\[
                               E^{\rm ab}\xrightarrow{\sim}G^{\rm ab}.
                                                               \tag{1.4}
\]
In particular there are no new deck characters, and no nontrivial
\(\ell\)-primary character of \(E\).

The conclusion also holds for the subgroup generated by all sheet
pullbacks of any finite collection of \(\ell\)-primary classes on \(Y\).
Their orders can first be bounded by a common \(\ell^a\).

## 2. Sheet indexing, pullback conventions, and the exact norm

Use a left action of \(G\) on \(W\), and the associated left action
on its Picard group
\[
                              \gamma\cdot L=(\gamma^{-1})^*L.
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]
For a left coset \(\gamma H\in G/H\), put
\[
                   r_{\gamma H}=r_0\gamma^{-1},\qquad
                   h_{\gamma H}=g r_{\gamma H}.
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]
This is well-defined since \(r_0\) is fixed by \(H\). The sheets satisfy
\[
                \gamma\cdot h_{\delta H}^*M=h_{\gamma\delta H}^*M.
                                                               \tag{2.3}
\]
Thus their span is a cyclic \(G\)-module, with the possibility of
additional relations or even \(P=0\).

The split étale base change
\(W\times_X Z=\coprod_{G/H}W\), together with base change for norm,
gives the exact homomorphism identity
\[
                   \sum_{\gamma H\in G/H}h_{\gamma H}^*
                             =\pi^*f_*g^*:J(Y)\longrightarrow J(W).
                                                               \tag{2.4}
\]
In multiplicative notation, the pullback of the norm of a line bundle
is the tensor product of its pullbacks along these sheets. Hence
(1.1) gives
\[
                       \sum_{\gamma H}h_{\gamma H}^*M=0.     \tag{2.5}
\]
This is a sum over sheets, with each sheet counted once, not an
unjustified division of the sum over all of \(G\).

Generic distinctness of the homomorphisms \(h_i^*\), when known from
minimality, need not give distinct values on a prescribed finite
\(\ell\)-torsion subgroup. Nothing here assumes that \(P\) has the
maximal possible rank or that the sheet-sum is its only relation.

## 3. The finite-module lemma and the necessary duality step

Put \(R=\mathbf Z/\ell^a\). There is a surjection
\[
                         R[G/H]\longrightarrow P,\qquad
                         e_{\gamma H}\longmapsto h_{\gamma H}^*M,
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]
which kills \(\sum e_{\gamma H}\).
In the coinvariant quotient \(P_G\), all these generators become the
same element. Relation (2.5) multiplies that element by
\([G:H]\), a unit in \(R\). Therefore
\[
                                      P_G=0.                \tag{3.2}
\]
For several initial classes, apply the same argument to each orbit
generator.

Since \(|G|\) is a unit in \(R\), averaging
\[
                          e_G=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{\gamma\in G}\gamma
                                                               \tag{3.3}
\]
identifies invariants and coinvariants for every \(R[G]\)-module:
the map \(P^G\to P_G\) has inverse induced by \(e_G\).
Thus also
\[
                                      P^G=0.                \tag{3.4}
\]
For \(a=1\) this is the usual semisimple argument over
\(\mathbf F_\ell\). For \(a>1\), no semisimplicity over
\(\mathbf Z/\ell^a\) is asserted or needed; the averaging identity
is enough.

The Kummer kernel in (1.3) is the **dual** module. Its action is
\[
                      (\gamma\cdot n)(L)=n(\gamma^{-1}\cdot L).
                                                               \tag{3.5}
\]
Consequently the perfect finite Kummer pairing gives
\[
                                  (N_G)^\vee=P^G=0,
                      \qquad\text{so }N_G=0.                \tag{3.6}
\]
This duality distinction matters: \(P_G=0\) alone directly says
\(N^G=0\), whereas abelianization needs \(N_G=0\).
The averaging step (3.4) supplies the missing implication.

The weaker assumption \(\ell\nmid[G:H]\) already proves (3.2), but
is not enough for the averaging argument when \(\ell\mid|G|\).
No conclusion (1.4) is asserted in that omitted case.

## 4. Connected Kummer covers and the composite Galois group

Because \(k\) is algebraically closed, constants are
\(\ell^a\)-divisible, and the étale Kummer sequence gives
\[
                     H^1_{\rm et}(W,\mu_{\ell^a})
                                =\operatorname{Pic}(W)[\ell^a].
                                                               \tag{4.1}
\]
The actual subgroup \(P\) therefore defines a connected cover of \(W\):
take the common kernel of its characters of \(\pi_1(W)\).
Its deck group is precisely the dual \(N\), of order \(|P|\).
No free-module hypothesis on \(P\) is needed.

By (2.3), \(P\) is \(G\)-stable. Its common kernel in \(\pi_1(W)\)
is consequently invariant under the outer conjugation action from
\(\pi_1(X)\), and hence normal in \(\pi_1(X)\). This proves that
\(U\to X\) is Galois and gives (1.3). Conjugation on \(N\) is the
dual action (3.5); its definition is independent of the chosen lift
of \(\gamma\), since \(N\) is abelian.

Now \(N_G=0\) means \(N=[E,N]\), in additive notation for \(N\).
In particular \(N\subset[E,E]\). The surjection \(E\to G\)
therefore induces exactly the isomorphism (1.4).
No choice of a splitting of (1.3) is used.

If one instead takes the raw fiber product of the cyclic covers for
all sheet labels, it can be disconnected because of their relations.
Choosing a connected component gives the cover associated to \(P\).
The original sheet-permutation action of \(G\) need not preserve that
chosen component. The stabilizer of the component in the full
Kummer-deck-and-permutation group does preserve it, maps onto \(G\),
and is the group \(E\) in (1.3). Thus neither an unsupported connectedness
assumption nor a \(G\)-stable choice of component is being made.

## 5. The retained second leg and scope

Suppose \(M\) has exact order \(\ell^a\), and let \(Y_M\to Y\)
be its connected cyclic étale cover. Choose a connected component
\(Z_M\) of \(Z\times_Y Y_M\). Its maps to \(Z\) and \(Y_M\)
are actual finite étale maps. The Galois closure of \(Z_M\to X\)
first contains \(W\), and then all the conjugate Kummer classes
(2.3). It is exactly \(U\).

Equivalently, \(U\) dominates the selected sheet pullback over \(W\)
and therefore has actual finite étale maps
\[
                              X\longleftarrow U\longrightarrow Y_M.
                                                               \tag{5.1}
\]
If a pullback torsor is disconnected or trivial, its connected
components are treated as in §4; the statement remains valid.
No claim that the new joint image has normalization \(U\) is made.

Thus adding this genuine second-leg Kummer parameter can increase
the nonabelian monodromy while leaving the entire abelianization
unchanged. It supplies no new deck-character translations for the
character-orbit theta tests. This fact alone proves neither theta
properness nor theta failure.

The restriction \(\ell\nmid p|G|\) remains substantive. After
enlarging monodromy by an \(\ell\)-group, a new application with
that enlarged group does not automatically meet the same coprimality
hypothesis. The statement for all orders \(\ell^a\) above keeps the
original group \(G\) fixed.
