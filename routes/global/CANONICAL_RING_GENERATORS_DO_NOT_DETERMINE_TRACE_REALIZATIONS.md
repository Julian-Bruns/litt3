# Canonical ring generators do not determine trace realizations

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: elementary author proof with a checked primary
reference for the canonical-ring generation statements. This note concerns
actual characteristic-5 cycles, not an obstruction to their existence.

## 1. Higher differentials are sums of products

Let R(X)=direct_sum_m H^0(X,omega_X^m), for a smooth projective connected
curve over an algebraically closed field, with genus g>=2. In every
characteristic:

- If X is nonhyperelliptic, R(X) is generated by R_1. Equivalently,
  Sym^m(R_1)->R_m is surjective for every m.
- If X is hyperelliptic and g>=3, minimal generators consist of g
  elements of degree 1 and g-2 elements of degree 2.
- If g=2, minimal generators have degrees 1,1,3. The ring has a
  presentation k[u,v,w]/(w^2-h_3(u,v)w-f_6(u,v)), with the indicated
  weighted degrees. The term h_3 must be allowed in characteristic 2.

Thus generation in degrees at most 3 is uniform. These are algebra
generation statements: a section is a finite linear combination of
products, not necessarily a single product or a canonical decomposition.

Primary reference: John Voight and David Zureick-Brown,
[The canonical ring of a stacky curve, Chapter 2](https://jvoight.github.io/articles/stacky-canonical-rings-final-fixederrata.pdf),
Section 2.1 (arbitrary-field setup and Theorem 2.1.1), Section 2.5
(nonhyperelliptic), equation (2.3.2) (genus 2), Proposition 2.7.2 and
the polynomial P(R_{>=1};t)=gt+(g-2)t^2 (hyperelliptic genus >=3).

## 2. Pullback preserves products; summed trace loses branch correlations

Use the operators and actual k-linear cycle category from
[the joint-detection note](PLURICANONICAL_REALIZATIONS_JOINTLY_DETECT_ACTUAL_BIETALE_CYCLES.md).
For an actual span X <-f C ->g Y, set

    A_m(s)=(D_g^m)^(-1) D_f^m f^*s in H^0(C,g^*omega_Y^m).

Differential pullback is multiplicative:

    A_{a+b}(st)=A_a(s) A_b(t).

On an etale splitting of g, let A_{m,i} denote its ith branch. Then

    T_{a+b}(st)=sum_i A_{a,i}(s) A_{b,i}(t),
    T_a(s)T_b(t)=sum_{i,j} A_{a,i}(s) A_{b,j}(t).

The latter contains cross-branch terms. More generally, the image of a
product requires the mixed moment

    sum_i product_j A_{d_j,i}(s_j),

whereas separately known traces provide only the individual sums over i.
For a weighted cycle, its coefficient enters once in the mixed moment;
products of traces instead multiply coefficients. No multiplicative
extension from the values on ring generators is justified.

There is still the projection formula Tr_g(g^*u v)=u Tr_g(v). This applies
when a factor descends along g; it is not a product rule for two arbitrary
f-pulled factors. If all branch maps on generators are retained, their
products do determine every mixed moment. If g is an isomorphism, there
is one branch, so the operator really is a graded algebra map and its
values on algebra generators determine every degree.

## 3. Actual characteristic-5 cycle: T_0 through T_3 vanish but T_4 does not

Let k=Fbar_5 and let X be the smooth projective model of

    y^2=x^5-x.

The polynomial has derivative -1 and five distinct roots. Its separable
double cover of P^1 has six simple branch points including infinity,
so X has genus 2. There is one point infinity, where x and y have pole
orders 2 and 5. The differential eta=dx/y has divisor 2 infinity:
at a finite branch point y is a parameter and eta is a nonvanishing
multiple of dy; at infinity dx has order -3 and y has order -5.

Consequently, for m>=1 a basis of R_m is

    x^i eta^m,       0<=i<=m;
    x^j y eta^m,     0<=j<=m-3 (empty if m<3).

These elements are regular, independent in k(X)=k(x)+y k(x), and their
number is 2 for m=1 and 2m-1 for m>=2, as required by Riemann--Roch.

For a in F_5 the formula sigma_a(x,y)=(x+a,y) defines an automorphism
of X and fixes eta. Let Gamma_a be the actual span with C=X, f=sigma_a,
and g=id_X. Both legs are finite etale isomorphisms. Put

    P=sum_{a in F_5} Gamma_a.

The five joint images are distinct automorphism graphs, so P is a
nonzero k-linear minimal-image cycle. It is represented by a disconnected
actual span, the disjoint union of these five copies of X. No quotient
map by the translation group is being used as an etale leg; that
quotient is ramified at infinity and would not be an allowed leg.

We have T_m(P)=sum_a sigma_a^*. In F_5,

    sum_a a^r=0 for 0<=r<=3,
    sum_a a^4=-1.

Here r=0 means summing the constant polynomial 1. Expanding (x+a)^i
shows that the trace vanishes on all polynomial basis elements when
m<=3. The only extra basis element in these degrees is y eta^3,
which is fixed by every sigma_a and hence also sums to zero. Constants
sum to zero as well. Therefore

    T_0(P)=T_1(P)=T_2(P)=T_3(P)=0,
    T_4(P)(x^4 eta^4)=-eta^4 != 0.

The source ring is generated in degrees 1,1,3, yet the summed operator
vanishes throughout those degrees without vanishing on the ring.
Even knowing the complete multiplication law on the fixed endpoint
rings does not distinguish P from the zero cycle using T_0,...,T_3.

Equivalently, to compare two nonempty effective actual cycles of equal
total degree, compare P with five copies of the diagonal. The latter
has every T_m zero in characteristic 5, while P has the values above.
The latter cycle is zero after characteristic-5 coefficient reduction;
P is not, because its five graph basis elements are distinct.

## 4. Exact limit on finite reconstruction

Fix X,Y and a k-linear subspace V of the actual minimal-image cycle
space. By joint faithfulness, all weights together distinguish V.
Knowledge of a finite set of weights S determines every other weight
on V if and only if the map

    V -> direct_sum_{m in S} Hom_k(R_m(X),R_m(Y))

is injective. Necessity follows because its kernel must be annihilated
by every weight; joint faithfulness makes that intersection zero.
Sufficiency follows by recovering the unique cycle in V. Thus this is
a faithfulness question, not a canonical-ring generation consequence.

For a prescribed finite support Gamma_1,...,Gamma_r, let N be the sum
of their right degrees. The joint-detection theorem supplies one
sufficient weight: any m with (m-1)(2g(X)-2)>N is already injective on
their span, and so determines every weight there. A finite set cannot
be injective on an infinite-dimensional V, but this note does not
assert that any particular Hom space is infinite-dimensional. Nor
does the example prove that every fixed finite cutoff fails: it
specifically disproves the proposed cutoff at the ring-generator
degrees, already for genuine sums of degree-one etale spans.

The structural lesson is that products give a finite alphabet for
pluricanonical sections, while trace operators on their products encode
branch correlations of increasing order. None of this rules out a
common etale cover or proves Litt3.
