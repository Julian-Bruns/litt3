# Two coprime elliptic families for degree-five conjugate poles

Version1,3 October2026. New complete necessary coprime form, pending independent review. No stratum exclusion is claimed.

Suppose the actual degree-FIVE cubic coarse B is elliptic, and its TWO common simple infinity poles form a conjugate pair for z. In the notation of the [conjugate-pole form](actual_q0_tensor_degree_five_conjugate_pole_form.md), assume its odd numerator linears b,c are coprime. After necessary local-condition symmetries, L is linear and
\[
x_1=cL/[z(z-a)]+b y/[z^2(z-a)],\qquad
x_2=bL/(z-a)+c y/(z-a).
\]
Exactly TWO families suffice:
\[
\text{I: } b=2z+1,\quad c=z+2,\quad
\Phi=z[L^2+(z-a)^2],\quad a\ne0,\ a^3\ne1,\ L(a)\ne0;
\]
\[
\text{II: } b=vz+a,\quad c=z+4v^2+3v+3,\quad
a=4(v-1)^2,\quad v\ne\pm1,\quad
\Phi=z[L^2+z^2+z+1].
\]
In family II additionally a²+a+1≠0 and L(a)²+a²+a+1≠0. In each case the leading and constant coefficients of Φ/z are nonzero. The v=ZERO edge of family II is retained. Its first derivative numerator may lose two degrees at infinity.

The reversed first quadratic-norm numerator and the second quadratic-norm numerator are scalar quartic squares, giving EIGHT necessary equations in THREE parameters per family. The proportional case is retained separately by the earlier conjugate-pole theorem; no coprime hypothesis is imposed on that case.

Both actual maps and any original Y-leg remain on the SAME original source.
