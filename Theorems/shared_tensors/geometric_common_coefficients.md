# Arithmetic structure on one endpoint suffices for a common coefficient

Let $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_p$, let $\ell\ne p$, and let
$X\xleftarrow f Z\xrightarrow g Y$ be an actual finite étale span
of smooth projective connected curves over $k$. Let
$\mathcal L_X,\mathcal L_Y$ be semisimple geometric
$\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$-local systems with a specified isomorphism
\[
\eta:f^*\mathcal L_X\xrightarrow{\sim}g^*\mathcal L_Y.
\]
Suppose $\mathcal L_X$ has an arithmetic extension over a finite-field
model, semisimple with EVERY irreducible constituent of finite-order
determinant. An irreducible arithmetic extension of finite determinant
is one sufficient case.

After a finite extension of the constant field, $\mathcal L_Y$ has
such an arithmetic extension too, and the ORIGINAL $\eta$ is arithmetic.
The given arithmetic structure on $\mathcal L_X$ is retained by restriction.
If a normalized arithmetic extension on $\mathcal L_Y$ was already
specified, it can also be retained after a further constant extension.
No common arithmetic Frobenius identification must be supplied separately.

More generally, a finite diagram of geometric morphisms between
semisimple arithmetic local systems whose irreducible constituents all
have finite determinant descends after a finite extension of constants.
For any fixed finite collection, ALL its geometric Hom spaces descend
over one finite extension. The assertion also applies to geometric
direct summands of that collection.

The constituentwise determinant condition is essential. Finite
determinant of an entire reducible object does not suffice: constant
Frobenius eigenvalues $c,c^{-1}$ with $c$ not a root of unity have
product one, but their geometric identifications with a constant
trivial pair do not become arithmetic over any finite extension.
Nor does an arbitrary geometric local system automatically have an
arithmetic extension. The theorem does not construct a common
coefficient from a bare span.
