# Supported functions through pole twenty-eight are cubic-invariant

The later [exact relation-lattice theorem](marked_divisor_relation_lattice.md)
extends this numerical range to1,617,893 and proves the sharp threshold.
The independent low-degree certificates below are retained.

Version3, 29 September2026 (scope unchanged; later theorem linked).
On the fixed genus-nine curve X:y^3=P(x),
let O be its unique infinity and Z the twelve points over A(x)=0, with
P=(11,22,18,5,19,20,15,16,9,22,1) and A=(1,21,14,22,13) in the usual
ascending F25 coding. If a nonconstant rational function g has only
the pole O, of order at most28, and all its zeros in Z, then g lies
in k[x]. Consequently its pole order is a multiple of three, and its
zeros have equal multiplicities on all three sheets of each cubic fibre.

The coefficients of g are arbitrary geometric coefficients. This is
not a search restricted to functions defined over a finite field.
Two distinct exact Hermite algorithms exhaust the three-character
cases20 through28; the unbounded missing-character theorem covers
all smaller cases. Both computations have no nonpolynomial survivor.

The theorem excludes comparison poles22,23,25,26,28 in every covering
degree. Its combination with the actual multiple-of-three exclusions
is recorded separately in the
[degree23 recognition theorem](line_recognition_degree_twenty_three.md).
It does not itself exclude the invariant
comparison poles24 or27, nor produce a shared object on an arbitrary
unmarked common cover. Those original questions remain open.
