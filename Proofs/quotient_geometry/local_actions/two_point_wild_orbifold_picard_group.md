# Proof: equivariant rational sections and actual invariant divisors

Version1. [Statement](../../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/local_actions/two_point_wild_orbifold_picard_group.md). [Independent whole-scope review: PASS](../../../Research/audits/TWO_POINT_WILD_ORBIFOLD_PICARD_AUDIT_2026_10_03.md).

An invertible sheaf on S is an H-linearized invertible sheaf on Γ. Its generic fiber is a one-dimensional semilinear representation of H over k(Γ). Since H acts faithfully, k(Γ)/k(P¹) is the actual finite Galois extension with group H. Hilbert90 supplies a nonzero invariant rational section of this generic fiber. Its divisor on Γ is H-invariant.

Conversely an H-invariant integral divisor on Γ has its canonical linearization and defines a line on S. Two such invariant divisors represent the same line on S precisely when their difference is the divisor of an H-invariant rational function: an equivariant isomorphism compares the chosen invariant rational sections, so their ratio belongs to k(P¹)×. Thus Pic(S) is the group of invariant divisors on Γ modulo divisors of rational functions on the coarse curve, pulled back to Γ.

Over each coarse point the H-action is transitive on the geometric fiber. Hence the invariant divisor group is freely generated by its reduced orbit divisors. Over a branch value its pullback multiplicity is the inertia order, whether tame or wild; over every ordinary point it is one. On P¹ the divisor of a coordinate identifying two distinct points gives all relations between the pullback point divisors. Choose an ordinary point to represent U. The two exceptional orbit divisors therefore give exactly eD_1=U and mD_2=U, and there are no further relations.

The orbifold degree sends U to1, D_1 to1/e, and D_2 to1/m. Eliminating U leaves Z² modulo the single relation(e,−m). Its torsion subgroup is Z/gZ, g=gcd(e,m), and its free quotient injects into Q via degree. Therefore the degree-zero subgroup is exactly that torsion subgroup. This argument neither divides by |H| nor requires linear reductivity of wild inertia.
