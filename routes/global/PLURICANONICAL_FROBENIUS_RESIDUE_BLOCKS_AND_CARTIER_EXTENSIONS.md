# Pluricanonical Frobenius residue blocks and Cartier extensions

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: author derivation, not independently audited.
This describes actual finite-etale trace actions in characteristic 5;
it neither constructs nor excludes a common etale cover.

Let k=Fbar_5 and let X <-f C ->g Y be a finite etale span of smooth
projective connected curves of genus at least two. Use the unnormalized
pluricanonical operators T_m from
[the joint-detection note](PLURICANONICAL_REALIZATIONS_JOINTLY_DETECT_ACTUAL_BIETALE_CYCLES.md).
Write X'=X^(5), F_X:X->X' for relative Frobenius, and similarly for C,Y.
All tensor powers below are powers of line bundles, not exterior powers.

## 1. The precise Frobenius relation

For every m>=0 and s in H^0(X,omega_X^m),

    T_{5m}(Gamma)(s^5) = T_m(Gamma)(s)^5.                 (1)

Here s |-> s^5 is additive and 5-semilinear, and its image is a
k-vector subspace since k is perfect. It is injective. For clarity,
the k-linear twisted form of (1) is the commuting square

    H^0(X',omega_X'^m)  --T_m(Gamma')--> H^0(Y',omega_Y'^m)
             | F_X^*                             | F_Y^*
             v                                   v
    H^0(X,omega_X^{5m}) --T_{5m}(Gamma)--> H^0(Y,omega_Y^{5m}).

In this display use the canonical line-bundle identification
F_X^*omega_X' = omega_X^5. This identification comes from Frobenius
base change of a line bundle; it is NOT the differential of F_X,
which is zero. Equivalently, H^0(X',omega_X'^m) is the scalar
Frobenius twist of H^0(X,omega_X^m).

Proof of (1): after an etale base change splitting g, the trace is a
sum of local pullbacks with differential comparison factors. Raising
that sum to its fifth power raises each summand and comparison factor
to its fifth power. Faithfully flat descent proves the identity.
No division by a cover degree occurs.

For a general k-linear cycle alpha=sum c_i Gamma_i one must twist
coefficients: T_{5m}(alpha)(s^5)=T_m(sum c_i^(1/5) Gamma_i)(s)^5.
Formula (1) with alpha on both sides requires Frobenius-fixed
coefficients. The twisted square remains valid with coefficient
base change understood.

For genus h, Riemann--Roch gives the following codimension of the
fifth-power subspace in H^0(omega^{5m}):

    m=0: 0;     m=1: 8h-9;     m>=2: 8m(h-1).           (2)

Thus T_m=0 only forces T_{5m} to vanish on that subspace. It leaves
most of the next space untreated. Conversely, T_{5m}=0 implies T_m=0
for an actual span, by injectivity of fifth powers.

## 2. Five vector-bundle families cover every weight

For r=0,1,2,3,4 define on X'

    E_{X,r}=F_{X*}(omega_X^r).

These are vector bundles of rank 5 and degree (2r+4)(h-1). Finite
flatness of Frobenius on a smooth curve gives the rank; equality of
Euler characteristics under finite pushforward gives the degree.
For every n>=0, projection formula gives a canonical isomorphism

    F_{X*}omega_X^{5n+r} = omega_X'^n tensor E_{X,r},
    H^0(X,omega_X^{5n+r}) = H^0(X',omega_X'^n tensor E_{X,r}). (3)

This is a genuine identification, not a splitting into five summands
within any fixed weight. The direct sum of the entire graded section
space can of course be grouped by its five degree residues. That
grouping has infinitely many twists n in each residue family.

For any etale h:U->V, the Frobenius square is cartesian. Flat base
change and the etale differential identification give canonical maps

    h'^* E_{V,r} = E_{U,r}.

Consequently Gamma' acts by pullback, comparison, and ordinary trace
on H^0(omega'^n tensor E_r). Under (3) this action is exactly
T_{5n+r}(Gamma). Indeed pushforward by Frobenius commutes with the
two finite maps, and the trace identification can be verified after
an etale splitting. The factor of weight 5n is the pullback of the
weight-n differential comparison on the twisted span; the remaining
factor is the comparison for E_r.

Therefore five fixed vector bundles per curve, equipped with their
etale comparison maps, do package ALL weights. Computing their
actions at finitely many n does not automatically determine all n:
the correspondence operators are not asserted to be linear over
the canonical section ring, and their ranks and section dimensions
grow with n.

## 3. Cartier supplies extensions, including a lower-weight quotient

Let B_X be the image of d:F_{X*}O_X -> F_{X*}omega_X. The standard
Cartier sequences on X' are

    0 -> O_X' -> E_{X,0} -> B_X -> 0,                   (4)
    0 -> B_X -> E_{X,1} --Car--> omega_X' -> 0.           (5)

Their elementary local description uses an etale parameter t:
F_*O has basis 1,t,...,t^4 over O_X'; d has kernel generated by 1,
and image generated by dt,t dt,t^2 dt,t^3 dt. Cartier kills these
four generators and sends t^4 dt to dt', where F^*t'=t^5.
This is the usual coordinate-independent Cartier map. The sequences
and their maps commute with etale pullback and trace: pullback is
etale-local functoriality, and trace reduces to summation on a
splitting cover.

Twist (4) and (5) by omega_X'^n. The latter gives the canonical map

    Car_n:H^0(X,omega_X^{5n+1}) -> H^0(X',omega_X'^{n+1})

and the intertwining identity

    Car_{Y,n} T_{5n+1}(Gamma)
       = T_{n+1}(Gamma') Car_{X,n}.                     (6)

For n>=1 this is a surjective quotient, with exact sequence

    0 -> H^0(X',B_X tensor omega_X'^n)
      -> H^0(X,omega_X^{5n+1})
      -> H^0(X',omega_X'^{n+1}) -> 0.                   (7)

To prove surjectivity, (4) and cohomology on a curve imply that
H^1(B_X tensor omega_X'^n) is a quotient of H^1(omega_X^{5n}),
which vanishes for n>=1. Riemann--Roch then gives

    dim ker(Car_n) = 8n(h-1),   n>=1.                   (8)

For n=0 the Cartier map on global one-forms need not be surjective;
ordinary and nonordinary curves must not be conflated.

For n>=2, (4) likewise gives

    0 -> H^0(X',omega_X'^n) -> H^0(X,omega_X^{5n})
      -> H^0(X',B_X tensor omega_X'^n) -> 0.             (9)

For n=1, this sequence instead continues with a surjection onto
H^1(X',omega_X')=k, followed by zero. Its right-hand section space
has dimension 8(h-1), whereas the actual fifth-power quotient has
dimension 8h-9, explaining the exceptional value in (2).

The kernel in (7) and quotient in (9) carry the same twisted
B_X-correspondence action. For n>=2 this connects adjacent weights
5n and 5n+1, but that rank-four bundle action is additional data.

## 4. Which splittings exist, and what remains uncontrolled

Sun's Frobenius stability theorem says F_*L is stable for every line
bundle L on a smooth projective curve of genus at least two. Thus
each E_{X,r} is stable, in particular indecomposable, and (4),(5)
are nonsplit vector-bundle extensions. Twisting does not change this.
The nonsplitting of (5) also follows directly from adjunction:

    Hom_X'(omega_X',F_*omega_X)
       = H^0(X,omega_X^{1-5}) = 0.

A short exact sequence of finite-dimensional k-vector spaces such
as (7) or (9) admits noncanonical linear splittings. This provides
no splitting compatible with the span action. Even vanishing of
the actions induced on both pieces of a filtration would allow a
nonzero off-diagonal map between quotient and subspace.

The concrete gaps in an attempted deduction from T_1=0 are:

- At weight 5, only the g-dimensional fifth-power subspace is
  controlled; its complement has dimension 8g-9.
- Cartier is a quotient from weight 5n+1 to twisted weight n+1.
  Vanishing on that quotient still allows an image in the kernel
  of dimension 8n(g(Y)-1).
- The other residue bundles E_2,E_3,E_4 and the B-bundle actions
  remain independent pieces of the required computation; none of
  the displayed identities identifies their actions with T_1.
- The five-bundle packaging retains infinitely many canonical
  twists. Bundle stability concerns subbundle slopes, not
  semisimplicity of correspondence operators on global sections.

There is consequently no implication here from vanishing in weight
one (or finitely many low weights) to vanishing in all weights.
Indeed the joint-detection theorem proves that every nonzero
characteristic-5 actual cycle is detected in every sufficiently
large weight, with a bound depending on its finite support.
