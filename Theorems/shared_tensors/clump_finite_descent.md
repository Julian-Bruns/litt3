# A clump detects all common finite monodromy

Version1,20September2026. Let $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_p$ and retain
an ACTUAL coreless finite étale span $X\xleftarrow fZ\xrightarrow gY$
of smooth projective connected curves. Suppose it has a clump S.
No genus-two, ordinariness, prime-to-p covering-degree or Jacobian
Hom hypothesis is required.

## Connected common torsors have connected lifted clumps

Let G be a finite constant group. Take G-torsors $T_X\to X$ and
$T_Y\to Y$ with a specified G-equivariant identification of their
pullbacks over Z. Retain the resulting diagram
\[
T_X\longleftarrow T_Z\longrightarrow T_Y.
\]
The individual torsors may be disconnected. Assume that the finite
graph of their connected CURVE components is connected, equivalently
that their common global regular-function algebra is k.

Then the full inverse image of S is ONE connected finite physical
component. Equivalently, after choosing a basepoint, transport around
the actual clump graph surjects onto G.

In particular there is no nontrivial connected common finite torsor
that splits into separate copies over the clump. This is a conclusion
about the two compatible torsors, not about an arbitrary cover of
only one endpoint.

## Full faithfulness for finite coefficients

Restriction from compatible finite étale covers of the original
span to finite coverings of the clump graph is fully faithful.
Both the covers and their maps are retained; no marking is discarded.

For any collection of common finite local systems whose JOINT
monodromy is finite, restriction to the clump graph is fully faithful.
Here joint monodromy is the finite group generated by the endpoint
monodromy images after using the specified source identification.
Invariant subspaces and morphisms detected on the clump therefore
descend to BOTH original endpoints, with the original matching.

The joint-finiteness condition is automatic for common finite local
systems over $\overline{\mathbf F}_\ell$: the finitely many matrices
lie in one finite field. It is not automatic for characteristic-zero
coefficients merely because the two endpoint images are separately
finite.

## One field of definition works for every common finite cover

Choose $\mathbf F_q$ over which the span is defined and EVERY point
of S_X,S_Y,S_Z is rational. Every geometrically common finite étale
cover has a UNIQUE compatible $\mathbf F_q$ descent structure for
which every point over this clump is rational. Thus the same field
works for all common finite covers, including those of unbounded
degree. Endpoint covers considered separately need not have this
descent property.

Equivalently, the common profinite quotient is a quotient of the
maximal étale fundamental group with S completely split on either
endpoint over this fixed field. Its finiteness is not asserted:
unramified towers with prescribed splitting can be infinite.

## Prime-to-p Picard torsion is detected in every genus

Let P be the group of common line-bundle triples. For the restriction
map to the actual clump graph,
\[
\operatorname{res}_S:P\longrightarrow H^1(\Gamma_S,k^\times),
\]
its kernel has NO nontrivial torsion of order prime to p.
Thus prime-to-p common Picard torsion embeds into the graph character
group. If there is no shared regular one-form, all common torsion is
prime to p, so all of it is detected.

The prime-to-p qualification in this last assertion is essential:
a character of a graph with values in $k^\times$ has no p-torsion,
and a p-primary line torsor need not be étale. The finite GROUP
torsor theorem itself allows $p\mid|G|$.

The proof extends the one-clump argument to the disconnected finite
torsor diagram and then uses its ACTUAL deck action. It provides a
general replacement for the former first-Witt argument in
[clump-fiber Picard detection](clump_fiber_picard.md).
It does not construct a clump or a common finite coefficient.
