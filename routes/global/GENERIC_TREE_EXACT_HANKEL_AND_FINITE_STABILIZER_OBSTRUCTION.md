# Generic trees: exact Hankel growth and infinite finite-set stabilizers

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: new deductions below, not independently audited.
No Litt3 conclusion. This supplements the actual-cycle
[transcendence theorem](CORELESSNESS_AS_TRANSCENDENCE_IN_THE_POSITIVE_CORRESPONDENCE_CATEGORY.md).

## Primary inputs and their limits

Krishnamoorthy, [Correspondences without a core](https://msp.org/ant/2018/12-5/ant-v12-n5-p05-p.pdf),
uses minimal correspondences throughout the graph sections (Condition 5.2).
Corollary 5.16 gives colored edge transitivity. The field generated by
a finite ball is finite Galois over its central vertex field (Lemma 5.4).
The full graph field is the infinite simultaneous Galois closure in the
coreless hyperbolic case (Corollaries 5.7–5.8, Proposition 5.10).

Lemma 6.10 proves that a minimal symmetric coreless correspondence of
type (3,3), with hyperbolic source, has a tree as generic graph and an
action transitive on every length of nonbacktracking arcs. Its proof uses
Tutte's theorem and the impossibility of a trivial finite-arc stabilizer.
Étaleness is not needed for that lemma.

General acyclicity and general symmetric all-arc transitivity are posed,
not proved, in Questions 5.17 and 6.11. Lemma 7.2 gives graph coverings
under specialization for projective hyperbolic étale correspondences;
Question 7.5 asks about physical cycles and asymptotics. These are not
general growth bounds. Theorem 9.6 allows at most one finite étale orbit;
Corollary 9.2 excludes it in characteristic zero.

## An unconditional restriction on the graph action

Assume the setting of the transcendence theorem: an actual connected
joint-minimal coreless bi-etale span between projective hyperbolic curves.
Let E be its generic graph field and let v be an X-vertex. Then
G_v = Gal(E/k(X)) acts faithfully on the graph.

For every finite vertex or edge set S, the pointwise stabilizer of
S union {v} in G_v is infinite. Indeed, let F be the compositum of the
finitely many image fields in S together with k(X). It is a finite
extension of k(X), inside E. Since E/k(X) is infinite Galois,
Gal(E/F) is infinite and fixes all those embeddings. Faithfulness follows
because the full collection of image fields generates E.

Thus the faithful algebraic action is nondiscrete in the topology of
pointwise convergence. In particular, a graph whose full automorphism
group has a finite pointwise stabilizer of some finite set cannot be the
generic graph of this span. This restriction is stronger than local
finiteness, biregularity, and infinity alone.

An immediate degree exclusion is (a,b) = (2,2): a connected infinite
simple graph of degree two is a doubly infinite path, whose vertex
stabilizer has order two. Degree one on either side also gives a core
(one endpoint field contains the other). Consequently a coreless span
has a,b >= 2 and (a-1)(b-1) >= 2. This alone does not prove exponential
growth for general graphs.

## Exact quantitative conclusion assuming the generic graph is a tree

Write a = deg(C/X), b = deg(C/Y), q = (a-1)(b-1), and
P = Gamma* Gamma in the actual cycle algebra. For n >= 1 let R_n
be the reduced sum of all joint-minimal images of endpoint pairs at
generic graph distance 2n; set R_0 = 1.

These are actual allowable cycles: take the length-2n alternating path
fiber product and select the components whose generic walks have no
immediate reversal. This condition selects components because diagonals
in finite etale fiber products are open and closed. In a tree each such
walk is the unique geodesic between its endpoints. Thus its endpoint
map has generic degree one, and R_n is reduced. The component-orbit
identification in the transcendence theorem shows that different
distances have disjoint sets of joint-minimal images.

The number of X-vertices at distance 2n from a fixed X-vertex is

    s_n = a(b-1) q^(n-1),    n >= 1.

Therefore the total left degree of R_n is s_n. Reversal preserves
distance, so R_n* = R_n. The faithful trace formula yields

    tau(R_i* R_j) = 0               if i != j,
    tau(R_0* R_0) = 1,
    tau(R_n* R_n) = s_n             if n >= 1.

Counting length-2n walks on a biregular tree shows that the coefficient
of an endpoint depends only on its distance from the root. In particular,
P^n is R_n plus a linear combination of R_0,...,R_(n-1): the coefficient
at distance 2n is one by uniqueness of geodesics. (The distance-only
claim also follows by induction, since each nonroot vertex has one
neighbor toward the root and its remaining neighbors away from it.)

The change of ordered basis from (R_0,...,R_n) to (1,P,...,P^n)
is triangular with diagonal entries one. Hence its Gram determinant is

    H_n = det[tau(P^(i+j))]_(0 <= i,j <= n)
        = product_(j=1)^n s_j
        = [a(b-1)]^n q^[n(n-1)/2].

This does not require transitivity on spheres; R_n may be reducible.
It determines degree growth and moments, not exponential growth in the
number of distinct irreducible correspondence images.

## Unconditional cubic symmetric specialization

If the original actual span is additionally a minimal symmetric
self-correspondence of type (3,3), Lemma 6.10 supplies the missing tree
hypothesis. The formulas specialize to

    deg_X(R_n) = 6 * 4^(n-1),
    H_n = 6^n * 4^[n(n-1)/2].

Its all-arc transitivity makes G_v transitive on every sphere: any two
geodesics beginning at v can be carried to each other by an element
fixing v. Thus each R_n is a single joint-minimal image, and its
normalization D_n is finite etale over X of degree 6 * 4^(n-1).
Riemann--Hurwitz gives

    g(D_n) - 1 = 6 * 4^(n-1) * (g(X)-1).

This growth is compatible with unbounded finite etale covers; it supplies
no genus contradiction. A general span between different endpoints does
not meet the symmetric cubic hypothesis. In particular, replacing it by
Gamma* Gamma gives an effective, generally disconnected cycle, not the
connected symmetric type-(3,3) input required by Lemma 6.10.
