# A coreless generic graph forces unbounded primitive correspondences

**Status:** proved structural consequence, 2026-09-05.  This is conditional
in general on finiteness of the cored primitive images.  For an ordinary
genus-two endpoint, the existing uniform orbifold bound removes that
condition.  It does not prove that a first coreless correspondence exists.

## Theorem 1 (from one coreless correspondence to infinitely many primitive images)

Let `k` be an algebraically closed field and let `X,Y` be smooth projective
curves of genus at least two.  Suppose that there is a finite bi-etale
correspondence

\[
                         X\xleftarrow{}Z\xrightarrow{}Y                 \tag{1}
\]

without a core.  Then there are infinitely many distinct integral curves

\[
                            \Gamma_i\subset X\times Y                  \tag{2}
\]

such that the normalization `C_i` of `Gamma_i` is finite etale over both
`X` and `Y` and the resulting correspondence is minimal.  The degrees of
the two legs are unbounded.

If only finitely many minimal cored correspondence images occur in
`X times Y`, then infinitely many of the `Gamma_i` are coreless and their
degrees are unbounded.

### Proof

#### Step 1: make the initial correspondence minimal

Replace `Z` by the normalization `Z_0` of its image in `X times Y`.  On
function fields this replaces `k(Z)` by the compositum of `k(X)` and `k(Y)`
inside `k(Z)`.  It is an intermediate extension of each of the two
unramified extensions, so `Z_0 -> X,Y` remains finite etale.  The endpoint
intersection, and hence corelessness, is unchanged.  By construction the
new correspondence is minimal.

Fix the distinguished blue vertex `P`, red vertex `Q`, and edge `PQ` in
Krishnamoorthy's generic two-coloured graph `G_gen` for this minimal
correspondence.  Proposition 5.10 applies because `Z_0` is hyperbolic and
says that `G_gen` is infinite.

The graph is locally finite: a blue vertex and a red vertex have valencies
equal to the two finite separable degrees of (1).  Since it is connected,
bipartite, locally finite, and infinite, it has infinitely many red
vertices.  Every such vertex `q` is joined to the fixed `P` by a finite path
of odd length.

#### Step 2: an endpoint vertex gives an actual bi-etale image

Regard the vertices as the embeddings

\[
 P:k(X)\hookrightarrow\Omega,\qquad q:k(Y)\hookrightarrow\Omega
\]

used in the definition of the generic graph, and put

\[
                      F_q=P(k(X))\,q(k(Y))\subset\Omega.                \tag{3}
\]

Choose one path from `P` to `q`.  Successively fiber-product the edge
curves over the intermediate vertex curves and take the connected component
specified by the chosen embeddings.  Equivalently, its function field is
the compositum in `Omega` of the finitely many edge fields on the path.
Call that field `E_q`.

Every fiber product used here is a fiber product of finite etale maps.
Thus its connected components are smooth and finite etale, and induction
shows that the path component is finite etale over both endpoint curves.
In particular, `E_q` is finite unramified over both `P(k(X))` and
`q(k(Y))`.

The field `F_q` is intermediate between either endpoint field and `E_q`.
Ramification indices in a tower multiply, so an intermediate extension of
a finite unramified extension of smooth curves is again unramified.  Hence
the smooth projective curve `C_q` with function field `F_q` is finite etale
over both `X` and `Y`.  Since (3) is generated by the two endpoint fields,
the map

\[
                         C_q\longrightarrow X\times Y
\]

is birational onto its image `Gamma_q`; this is precisely minimality.
Notice that `F_q`, and hence `Gamma_q`, depends only on the endpoint vertex
`q`, not on the chosen path.

#### Step 3: infinitely many vertices give infinitely many images

Fix one integral minimal image `Gamma subset X times Y`, with function
field `F`.  A red vertex `q` for which `Gamma_q=Gamma` comes from an
embedding

\[
                   F\hookrightarrow\Omega
\]

extending the fixed embedding `P:k(X)->Omega`; its restriction to `k(Y)`
is `q`.  There are at most `[F:k(X)]` such embeddings because the extension
is finite separable.  Thus one fixed image accounts for only finitely many
red vertices.  Since there are infinitely many red vertices, the images
`Gamma_q` are infinite in number.

This count fixes the original `X` embedding and counts red endpoint
vertices, not paths.  Backtracking, multiple paths to one vertex, and
directed self-correspondence cycles therefore create no false multiplicity.

#### Step 4: bounded degrees give only finitely many images

For any `D`, a fixed smooth proper hyperbolic curve has only finitely many
connected finite etale covers of degree at most `D`.  One way to see this
is that its geometric etale fundamental group is topologically finitely
generated.  For each resulting source curve `C`, the scheme of separable
maps `C -> Y` of the forced bounded degree is zero-dimensional: its tangent
space at a map is

\[
                         H^0(C,h^*T_Y)=0
\]

because `T_Y` has negative degree.  The bounded-degree Hom scheme is of
finite type, hence has only finitely many such points.  Consequently only
finitely many bi-etale images in `X times Y` have either leg degree bounded
by `D`.

The infinitely many images already constructed therefore have unbounded
degrees.  The etale genus formula ties the two degrees by

\[
 (g(X)-1)\deg(C_q/X)=(g(Y)-1)\deg(C_q/Y),
\]

so both are unbounded.  Finally, if only finitely many minimal cored images
exist, removing them leaves infinitely many coreless images, still of
unbounded degree.  This proves the theorem. \(\square\)

[Krishnamoorthy, Definition 5.3 and Proposition 5.10](https://msp.org/ant/2018/12-5/ant-v12-n5-p05-p.pdf).

## Corollary 2 (ordinary genus two)

Assume `char(k)=5` and `Y` is ordinary of genus two.  Then the final
conclusion of Theorem 1 is unconditional: one coreless bi-etale
correspondence from `X` to `Y` produces infinitely many coreless minimal
images of unbounded degree.

Indeed, if a minimal image `C -> X,Y` is cored, the core-to-orbifold theorem
gives a common effective proper orbifold `S`.  On function fields,

\[
 k(C)=k(X)k(Y),\qquad k(S)=k(X)\cap k(Y).
\]

Therefore

\[
 \deg(C/X)=[k(X)k(Y):k(X)]
       \le [k(Y):k(S)]=\deg(Y/S)\le 42000.              \tag{4}
\]

The last inequality is the audited uniform ordinary-genus-two orbifold
bound.  Since the degrees in Theorem 1 are unbounded and there are only
finitely many images in the bounded range, every sufficiently high-degree
image in the constructed sequence is coreless.

[Core-to-orbifold bridge](CORED_BIETALE_CORRESPONDENCES_HAVE_COMMON_ORBIFOLDS.md).
[Uniform ordinary genus-two bound](ORDINARY_GENUS_TWO_UNIFORM_ORBIFOLD_DEGREE_BOUND.md).

This is a conditional answer to Krishnamoorthy's Question 3.21 in general,
and an unconditional answer to that question once one endpoint is ordinary
of genus two.  It is not an existence theorem for the first coreless
correspondence.

## Theorem 3 (uniform monodromy support retained by the tower)

Let `G_X` and `G_Y` be the geometric monodromy groups of the Galois closures
of the two legs of the primitive initial correspondence `Z_0`, and let

\[
              \Pi=\{\ell:\ell\text{ divides }|G_X|\,|G_Y|\}.
\]

Every path source used above, and every minimal image `C_q`, has the
following property on **both** legs: the order of its geometric Galois
closure group is divisible only by primes in `Pi`.

More precisely, build a path source one edge at a time.  At each step the
new map to the preceding source is a connected component of the base change
of one of the two original legs.  Its Galois-closure group is therefore a
subgroup of `G_X` or `G_Y`.  The Galois closure of a tower embeds in an
iterated wreath product of these step groups.  It follows inductively that
its order is `Pi`-supported.  The field `F_q` is an intermediate field of
the path source, so the Galois closure of `F_q` over either endpoint is
contained in the corresponding path Galois closure.  This proves the claim.

Two useful specializations are retained through every minimalization:

- if both initial Galois closures have order prime to five, so do both legs
  of every `C_q`;
- if both `G_X` and `G_Y` are solvable, both leg monodromy groups of every
  `C_q` are solvable.

This is genuine uniform information, but it does not yet meet the existing
bounded-Raynaud-quotient theorem.  Wreath iteration does not bound exponent:
even iterated regular wreath products built from one cyclic group of prime
order can contain elements of increasingly large prime-power order.  Nor
does it preserve the narrower abelian-by-cyclic or nilpotency-class-two
classes.  What the graph tower supplies is fixed prime support and a tower
of bounded one-step monodromy types, not a fixed exponent bound.  A Raynaud
statement stable under towers whose successive Galois factors lie in the
finite subgroup lists of `G_X` and `G_Y` would combine directly with this
construction; the present bounded-exponent statement does not.
