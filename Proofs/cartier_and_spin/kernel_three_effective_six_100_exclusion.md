# Proof: actual primitive cube field and two sixth-power fibers

Version1. [Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/kernel_three_effective_six_100_exclusion.md). Retain the original sourceT and both actual endpoint maps. Put U=T/K, H=G/K, and B=Γ/H. The actual U→Y is Galois étale, U→Γ has degree six, and the effective Y→B map has degree six with ordinary distinguished fiber2P+Q1+...+Q4.

The accepted tame different primitivity theorem gives k(T)=k(Γ)(a), with a of degree eighteen. The faithful cubic character ofK acts on a by multiplication. Consequently k(U)=k(Γ)(a³), and b=a³ is the primitive degree-six cube generator in a rational Nu frame. No simultaneous Galois closure is introduced.

## The invariant generator reconstructs the actual source field

For Nu weights(1,0,0) on cones(2,6,6), the coarse degree of Nu is zero. Choose its nonzero invariant section t. It has weight-one zero at the order-two cone and is a unit at every other coarse point. The actual function f=t/s³ descends toY and has exact divisor
\[
\operatorname{div}(f)=R_1+R_2+R_3-3P,
\]
where the three Ri lie over the order-two cone. Thus degf=3. In particular f is a finite nonzero unit at both order-six cone fibers.

The identity b=t/f gives k(Γ)(f)=k(U). If F=k(B)(f)⊂k(Y), then
\[
6=[\Gamma F:\Gamma]\le [F:B]\le [Y:B]=6.
\]
Hence F=k(Y). This uses the actual primitive cube field; coprimality of three and six is neither available nor needed.

Choose coarse coordinateβ with distinguished ordinary point zero and the order-two cone infinity. The invariant Nu^j spaces have coarse degrees floor(j/2), and ratios of their sections to t^j are polynomials inβ of that degree. The actual norm e18 has order three atβ=0. The coefficient e15 has order at least two there: the local ramified quadratic for b has trace of order at least two and norm of order three, while the four companion factors are units. Thus the actual primitive minimal polynomial of f overB is
\[
P_\beta(f)=\kappa_0\beta^3f^6-\lambda\beta^2f^5+L_2(\beta)f^4-L'_1(\beta)f^3+L_1(\beta)f^2-Af+1,
\]
whereκ0≠0, λ,A are constants, and Lj has degree at most j. All coefficients and fields come from the actual same-source maps.

## Two sixth powers force the cone positions equal

Let β=a,b be the distinct, finite, nonzero order-six cone positions. At either cone the actual Y fiber consists of one point of index six. Since f is a unit there, finite-flat specialization of its primitive norm gives a sixth power of a linear polynomial. Normalize its constant coefficient to one. Its linear coefficient is the coefficient of f in Pβ, namely v=−A, independent of the cone position. The coefficient v is nonzero because the specialized leading coefficientκ0β³ is nonzero.

In characteristic five,
\[
(1+vf)^6=1+vf+v^5f^5+v^6f^6.
\]
Comparison at the two cones therefore gives
\[
v^6=\kappa_0a^3=\kappa_0b^3,
\qquad v^5=-\lambda a^2=-\lambda b^2.
\]
Since v≠0, λ≠0. Thus a³=b³ and a²=b². The positions are nonzero, so a=b, contrary to distinct cone values.

The stated weight row is excluded without a computation or any replacement of the two actual endpoint maps.
