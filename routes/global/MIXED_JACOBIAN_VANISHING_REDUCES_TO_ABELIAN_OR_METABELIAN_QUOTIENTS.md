# Mixed Jacobian vanishing: perfect-factor descent and solvable residual quotients

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: /root.
Status: author proof; the orbit and derived-subgroup deductions were independently
checked by /root/gluing_cohomology_rigidity on this date. No novelty claim.
The earlier coprime-product theorem is retained.

## 1. Statement

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed, let \(W,T\) be smooth projective connected
curves with \(g(T)\ge2\), and let a finite group \(H_1\times H_2\) act on \(W\).
Let \(r:W\to T\) be nonconstant. Assume
\[
 r_*(a_*-1)(b_*-1)=0:J_W\longrightarrow J_T
                  \qquad(a\in H_1,\ b\in H_2).                \tag{1}
\]
For each factor put
\[
 D_i=[H_i,H_i],\qquad E_i=[D_i,D_i]D_i^2,                     \tag{2}
\]
where \(D_i^2\) denotes the subgroup generated by squares.

**Theorem.**

1. If either \(H_i\) is perfect, then \(r\) is fixed by one entire factor:
   it factors through \(W/H_1\) or \(W/H_2\).
2. Without any group restriction, either the same factorization holds, or:
   * if \(T\) is nonhyperelliptic, \(r\) factors through
     \(W/(D_1\times D_2)\);
   * if \(T\) is hyperelliptic, \(r\) factors through
     \(W/(E_1\times E_2)\).
3. In the latter hyperelliptic alternative, the orbit of \(r\) under each
   \(D_i\) has at most two elements. There are subgroups \(K_i\le D_i\)
   of index at most two such that \(r\) factors through
   \(W/(K_1\times K_2)\). The characteristic subgroups \(E_i\) give a
   canonical, possibly smaller, subgroup through which to descend.
4. If the original action is free and \(r\) is finite étale, every descended
   map just asserted is finite étale.

The groups \(H_i/D_i\) are abelian. The groups \(H_i/E_i\) are metabelian:
their derived subgroups \(D_i/E_i\) are elementary abelian \(2\)-groups.
Thus the non-axis alternative discards every nonsolvable composition
factor from both group directions.
There are no degree bounds or restrictions on group orders relative to
the characteristic.

## 2. A constant biadditive pairing

Choose \(t_0\in T(k)\), and write \(A=\operatorname{AJ}_{t_0}\circ r:W\to J_T\).
For \(a\in H_1,b\in H_2\), define
\[
 \delta_{a,b}(w)=A(abw)-A(aw)-A(bw)+A(w).                      \tag{3}
\]
Its induced homomorphism on \(J_W\) is (1). The universal property of the
Jacobian makes (3) constant; denote its value by \(c(a,b)\in J_T(k)\).

Since the two factors commute, for \(a,a'\in H_1\),
\[
 \delta_{aa',b}(w)=\delta_{a,b}(a'w)+\delta_{a',b}(w).
\]
Constancy gives \(c(aa',b)=c(a,b)+c(a',b)\). The same argument in the other
factor proves a well-defined biadditive pairing
\[
                 c:H_1^{\mathrm{ab}}\times H_2^{\mathrm{ab}}
                                      \longrightarrow J_T(k).     \tag{4}
\]
In particular,
\[
                 c(D_1,H_2)=0=c(H_1,D_2).                          \tag{5}
\]
Its values are killed by both abelianization exponents. This concerns actual
Jacobian points; it requires neither reduced torsion group schemes nor
division by group orders. When the group orders are coprime, (4) vanishes
identically and the earlier coprime proof applies. Here we instead use (5).

## 3. The zero-pairing lemma

Fix \(a,b\) with \(c(a,b)=0\). At every \(w\), (3) gives an equivalence of
effective degree-two divisors on \(T\):
\[
                    r(abw)+r(w)\sim r(aw)+r(bw).                  \tag{6}
\]
If \(T\) is nonhyperelliptic, effective degree-two divisors have no
nontrivial linear equivalences. Equality of the unordered pairs implies
pointwise either \(r(bw)=r(w)\) or \(r(aw)=r(w)\). These equalizer loci
are closed and cover irreducible \(W\); one is all of \(W\). Therefore
\[
                         r b=r\quad\hbox{or}\quad r a=r.          \tag{7}
\]

If \(T\) is hyperelliptic, with involution \(\iota\), two unequal
linearly equivalent effective degree-two divisors both belong to its
unique degree-two pencil. Thus \(W\) is covered by the three closed loci
\[
 r b=r,\qquad r a=r,\qquad
             \bigl(r ab=\iota r\ \hbox{and}\ r b=\iota r a\bigr).  \tag{8}
\]
One alternative is global. In particular, if neither element fixes \(r\),
\[
                               r a=\iota r b.                   \tag{9}
\]
The argument also applies in characteristic two: a degree-two pencil on
a positive-genus smooth curve over a perfect field cannot be purely
inseparable, since that would make its function field rational.

## 4. Orbits under the derived groups

Assume neither whole factor fixes \(r\), and choose \(b\in H_2\) with
\(r b\ne r\). For every \(a\in D_1\), (5) allows the zero-pairing lemma.

If \(T\) is nonhyperelliptic, (7) forces \(r a=r\). All of \(D_1\) fixes
\(r\), and interchanging the factors gives the same for \(D_2\).

If \(T\) is hyperelliptic, each \(a\in D_1\) either fixes \(r\) or
satisfies (9). Crucially, the latter map \(\iota r b\) is independent
of \(a\). The orbit \(\{r a:a\in D_1\}\) therefore has at most two
elements. Its stabilizer
\[
                         K_1=\{a\in D_1:r a=r\}
\]
has index at most two, hence is normal in \(D_1\) with quotient trivial
or cyclic of order two. This implies \(E_1\subset K_1\).
The same argument gives \(E_2\subset K_2\).
Invariance under the two commuting subgroups gives invariance under
their product, so quotient descent proves assertions 2 and 3.

If \(H_1\) is perfect, then \(D_1=H_1\). The nonhyperelliptic argument
already says \(H_1\) fixes \(r\), a contradiction. In the hyperelliptic
case a perfect group has no index-two subgroup, so again its orbit has
one element, a contradiction. This proves assertion 1; it need not
specify which factor fixes \(r\).

Finally \(E_i\) is characteristic in \(D_i\), which is characteristic
in \(H_i\). The quotient \(D_i/E_i\) is abelian of exponent at most two,
proving the claimed residual-group descriptions.

## 5. Retaining the étale maps

For any product subgroup \(Q\) used above, freeness makes \(q:W\to W/Q\)
finite étale. If \(r=\bar r q\) is finite étale, then \(\bar r\) is finite
and separable, and
\[
                       1=e(r,w)=e(q,w)e(\bar r,q(w)).
\]
Thus \(\bar r\) is unramified and hence finite étale. The conclusion
retains the actual map from the same curve; no unrelated covers enter.

## 6. Ordinary mixed contributions and the exact boundary

Let \(P_{\mathrm{mix}}\subset J_W\) be the abelian subvariety generated by
the images \((a_*-1)(b_*-1)J_W\). If
\[
                            \operatorname{Hom}(P_{\mathrm{mix}},J_T)=0,
\]
then (1) holds. In particular this is true if \(P_{\mathrm{mix}}\) is ordinary
and \(J_T\) has no ordinary simple isogeny factor.
For a free product action with either factor perfect, this forces every
actual étale map to such a target to descend to one axis, even when the
factor orders share arbitrarily many primes.

Eligible factors include \(A_n\) for \(n\ge5\) and every nonabelian finite
simple group. The descent mechanism therefore applies to independent
simple factors such as those in earlier core-quotient reductions, PROVIDED
mixed vanishing is established there. This note does not prove that input.

For abelian factor groups, assertion 2 can provide no reduction. The
pairing (4) remains an obstruction to extending axis descent to shared
primes; no nonzero pairing is realized here by an actual étale example.
An abstract Jacobian representation or a finite group alone does not
establish (1) for a common-cover tower. This is a structural consequence
of mixed vanishing, not a general vanishing theorem or a solution of Litt's
problem.
