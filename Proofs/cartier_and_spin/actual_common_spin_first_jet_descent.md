# Proof: raw first-jet image, actual descent, and exact critical divisor

Version1,2 October2026. Computation-free scoped proof; focused whole-argument review PASS. [Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/actual_common_spin_first_jet_descent.md).

## The raw image and its local determinant

Use the actual Galois étale spin refinement $q:T\to Y$ and the line $L$ and basepoint-free projectively invariant series $W$ of the sixteen-spin theorem. Distinct orbit sections have distinct reduced infinity zero divisors unless proportional. Since $\dim W\ge8$, some ratio is nonconstant and has a simple zero or pole; it is not a fifth power. Thus the first-jet evaluation has generic rank two.

Its RAW sheaf image $P\subset J^1L$ is a torsion-free rank-two sheaf on the smooth curve, hence a bundle. It is a quotient of the trivial bundle $W\otimes O_T$ and is globally generated. Do not saturate this image: its full-rank saturation is all of $J^1L$, which would erase the ramification divisor.

Locally choose a unit section $s_0\in W$, possible by basepoint freedom. Express all other sections as $s_i=f_i s_0$. In the jet basis given by $j^1s_0$ and a vertical differential, their column differences are $(0,s_0df_i)$. The ideal generated by their derivatives is $(t^r)$ for an integer $r\ge0$. Thus $P$ locally has one horizontal unit generator and one vertical generator multiplied by $t^r$. This $r$ is precisely the common determinant valuation. The local quotient $J^1L/P$ has length $r$.

These integers define the effective divisor $R_W$. Projection to $L$ is surjective, and the exact integral image sequence is
\[
0\to\omega_T\otimes L(-R_W)\to P\to L\to0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\det P=\omega_T\otimes L^2(-R_W),\qquad\deg P=18d-\deg R_W.
\]
Global generation makes $\det P$ generated by determinants of its generating sections, so $\deg P\ge0$.

## Scalar cancellation and genuine one-leg descent

Choose the projective deck linearization of $L$ with scalar cocycle $c$. Functoriality of first jets gives the same scalar cocycle on $J^1L$, since $c$ is a constant scalar and its differential is zero. The equivariant jet image $P$ has that cocycle too. Therefore $P\otimes L^{-1}$ has a genuine $G$ linearization: the scalar factors cancel. It descends through the ACTUAL étale torsor $q$ to a rank-two bundle $Q_Y$.

The common determinant divisor $R_W$ is $G$ invariant. Because $q$ is étale and Galois, invariant integral divisors descend uniquely, so $R_W=q^*D$ for an effective divisor $D$ on $Y$. The degree bound $\deg R_W\le18d$ gives $\deg D\le18d/(8d)=9/4$, hence $\deg D\le2$.

Twist the local exact sequence by $L^{-1}$ and use $\omega_T=q^*\omega_Y$. Its kernel becomes $q^*(\omega_Y(-D))$, with the natural linearization; its quotient is $O_T$ with trivial linearization. Descent is exact, yielding
\[
0\to\omega_Y(-D)\to Q_Y\to O_Y\to0.
\]
The upstairs degree is $16d-\deg R_W=8d(2-\deg D)$, as is also immediate from the exact sequence, and the downstairs degree is $2-\deg D$.

For clarity, $\deg(P\otimes L^{-1})=\deg P-2d=16d-\deg R_W$, since its rank is two. This equals $8d(2-\deg D)$. The raw image degree and the twisted bundle degree must not be confused.

## The canonical Atiyah lattice and the nonzero class

The normalized jet extension $J^1L\otimes L^{-1}$ has local extension transitions $d\log\ell_{ab}$ for the line transitions $\ell_{ab}$. The identification $L^{16}\simeq q^*\omega_Y$ multiplies these logarithmic transitions by sixteen. Hence this extension is canonically the pullback of the Atiyah extension of $\omega_Y$ with its extension class scaled by $1/16$. The claim is compatible with actual deck actions: the sixteenth powers of the chosen line isomorphisms differ from the natural canonical isomorphisms only by constant factors, whose logarithmic derivatives vanish.

The integral inclusion $P\otimes L^{-1}\subset J^1L\otimes L^{-1}$ therefore descends to an inclusion of $Q_Y$ in that scaled Atiyah extension. It is identity on the quotient $O_Y$, and its kernel inclusion is $\omega_Y(-D)\hookrightarrow\omega_Y$. Thus its extension class maps to $c_1(\omega_Y)/16$ in $H^1(\omega_Y)$.

On a smooth curve the canonical class has degree two, and the trace identification sends this Chern class to $2\in k$. It is nonzero in characteristic five. Sixteen equals one in this characteristic, so the displayed class is exactly the nonzero canonical Atiyah class. This downstairs conclusion uses the actual linearization and transition calculation, not injectivity of pullback on cohomology; such injectivity can fail when a covering degree is divisible by five.

## Contact eight fixes the whole common ramification divisor

Under the exact noncyclic contact-eight hypotheses, the spin-series two-jet lemma gives $u_i/u_j=\lambda+O(t^3)$ at every point of $q^{-1}(Q)$. Thus all first-jet determinants have valuation at least two there, and $R_W\ge2q^*Q$. Equivalently $D\ge2Q$.

Since $\deg D\le2$, one has $D=2Q$ EXACTLY. There is no other common critical fiber and no higher common multiplicity at $Q$. The descended bundle has degree zero and is the asserted extension of $O_Y$ by $\omega_Y(-2Q)$.

## What an étale trivialization would require

Suppose, conditionally, that a connected finite étale cover $r:Z\to Y$ trivializes this degree-zero $Q_Y$. Its pulled kernel $r^*(\omega_Y(-2Q))$ is a degree-zero subline of $O_Z^2$, so it is trivial and embedded by constants. The quotient map to $O_Z$ is also constant; therefore the pulled extension splits.

Its image in the pulled canonical Atiyah extension consequently has zero extension class. But that class is $c_1(r^*\omega_Y)/16$, with trace equal to $2\deg r/16$. This can vanish in characteristic five only if $5\mid\deg r$. Therefore no prime-to-five finite étale cover can trivialize $Q_Y$.

Nothing here forces $Q_Y$ to be finite or Frobenius-trivial. Its degree-zero kernel can have nontrivial class and its extension can have a nontrivial Frobenius orbit. Global generation of $P=L\otimes q^*Q_Y$ is not global generation of $q^*Q_Y$, and must not be used to infer a finite coefficient or a common object descending to both endpoints.
