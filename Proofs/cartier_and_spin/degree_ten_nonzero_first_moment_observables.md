# Clearing the center and retaining the actual pole budget

30 September2026.
[Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/degree_ten_nonzero_first_moment_observables.md).
The weighted moment identities give Tr(1/phi)=0 and E1=df*z.
Under a base translation c becomes c+az and z is unchanged.
Thus the center c/z translates by a. Expansion of the three
centered powers gives the displayed Rj, since Tr(1/phi)=0 removes
the highest denominator and E1=df*z removes one more power of z.
In the quartic formula binom(4,2)=1 in characteristic five.
The identities prove invariance after clearing denominators,
including at isolated zeros of z. If z is identically zero then
W is zero, and all three cleared expressions vanish identically.

Use a regular primitive frame at each finite point, including the
fixed translation B0/y at a cubic branch point. At a selected point
the moment cancellations in the all-degree proof give
ord(z),ord(c)>=-3, ord(W)>=-6, ord(E2)>=-2.
The direct estimates for E3 and E4 are both at least-3: every small
root is integral and phi has order three. The displayed formulas
therefore give
\[
\operatorname{ord}(R_2)\ge-5,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(R_3)\ge-12,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(R_4)\ge-18.
\]
All these points are simple zeros of t.

At any other finite point, integral roots with phi a unit contribute
regularly. A root pole of order m>=1 contributes to Ej with order
at least (5-j)m-j. Its contributions to z,c are positive-order
functions; other sheets contribute regular functions. Thus z,c,W
are regular in this frame. E2 is regular, E3 has at most a simple
pole, and E4 has at most a triple pole. Such points lie in the
support of h_*G, so multiplication by v and v3 respectively removes
these possible poles. The estimate uses only ord(v)>=1 there and
does not replace an individual G by a pullback divisor.

At infinity the already proved bounds are
ord(z)>=15, ord(c)>=13, ord(W)>=27, ord(E2)>=4.
The short primitive has small roots of pole at most one and large
roots of pole m>=2. Small roots contribute to Ej from order7-2j,
and large roots from order(5-j)m-j. Hence ord(E3)>=1 and
ord(E4)>=-2. Substitution gives
\[
\operatorname{ord}(R_2)\ge19,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(R_3)\ge46,\quad
\operatorname{ord}(R_4)\ge73.
\]
As pole(t)=9 and pole(v)<=10, the three differential pole bounds
after clearing the finite support are26,72,119. Dividing by
omega0^j adds16j to each, giving58,120,183. Riemann--Roch gives
the dimensions in the statement.

In the scale presentation z,c,W and df are independent of lambda.
The small-moment numerator theorem gives degrees at most4,8,12
for Delta E2, Delta2 E3, Delta3 E4 when deg(D)=3. Lower moments,
after further multiplication by Delta, have no higher degree.
The same bounds consequently hold for the cleared centered moments.
These numerator identities are computational tools, not a proof
that the coefficient locus or its actual etale subset is empty.
