# Defect-neutral Galois covers descend every given compatible Witt tower

Version1,2026-09-11.

Let k=bar(F5). Let h:T→C be an actual connected finite etale Galois
cover of smooth projective curves of genus at least two. Let r_C be
a regular active admissible nilpotent projective connection, with
its specified weight-one periodic data, including the actual flat
square-trivial line and projective graded identification. Put r_T=h*r_C
and assume equality of the actual indigenous defects

                         d(T,r_T)=d(C,r_C).

Every GIVEN full compatible marked Witt tower of (T,r_T) extending
the original pulled-back W2 data descends uniquely along the ORIGINAL
h to a compatible full tower on C. The resulting curves, finite etale
map, deck action and periodic tuple algebraize over W(k).

No bound on the group, map degree or common defect is required. The
conclusion descends an existing upper tower; it does not assert one
exists. It does not apply to a non-Galois cover unless its individual
Galois closure is also defect-neutral.

More precisely, for an actual CYCLIC degree-five h and every n>=2,
suppose h_n:T_n→C_n is the given marked map with a compatible lower
tuple. A GIVEN compatible T_(n+2) extending that upper tuple forces
its GIVEN T_(n+1) to descend compatibly to C_(n+1) extending C_n.
Only one extra upper digit is needed. There is no assertion that this
particular T_(n+2) already descends at its top digit.

For a general neutral Galois cover, a subnormal chain in an actual
Sylow-five subgroup supplies the cyclic steps; the original remaining
map has degree prime to five and uses the established neutral theorem.
All intermediates remain neutral by injective pullback of defect
sections. No simultaneous Galois closure is presumed in a two-leg use.

In particular, if BOTH legs of an actual same-source matched span are
Galois and defect-neutral and that source has a full compatible tower,
the SAME given tower descends along both original maps and algebraizes
to an actual two-leg span. Primitive/non-Galois(N5) and the original
unmarked common-cover problem remain open.
