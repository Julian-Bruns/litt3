## 2. Exactly one boundary and its residual intersections

If both boundaries were components, their two distinct infinity points would consume two of RF=3, leaving only one intersection for two distinct auxiliary points. There is no F∞ component. This is impossible.

Suppose exactly one boundary B occurs. Write B′ for the other and
\[
R=B+H+V,
\tag{3}
\]
where H is the remaining horizontal part of fiber degree2 and V is finite vertical. No horizontal component is multiple. Every component of H and V has nonnegative intersection with B′, so RB′=0 forces each intersection zero. Their intersections with B are nonnegative and RB=0 gives
\[
h=HB\in\{0,1,2,3\},\quad VB=3-h,\quad HB'=VB'=0.
\tag{4}
\]
At each end fiber the entire degree-two H intersection consists of the two distinct auxiliary points, each of multiplicity1. In particular H+V misses B at BOTH B₀,B∞. The horizontal H is either integral degree2 or two distinct degree-one sections; the latter are smooth, because their proper degree-one maps to the rational base are isomorphisms.

## 3. The boundary factors from BOTH actual extensions

If B is the opposite boundary, interchange v with−v in this local calculation; this merely changes the first nonzero constant. The ensuing divisor and degree contradiction is identical, or one can repeat the first-leg swap argument directly.

Use a common regular R frame along B, chosen to trivialize O(R)|B because RB=0. With this frame suppressed write
\[
\rho=w r(t)+O(w²),\quad
a=w^{-3}A(t)+O(w^{-2}),\quad

Here r is the restriction of the residual R−B, a section of O_B(3); A,B₁ are the restrictions of the actual a,b, sections of O_B(1); q is a nonzero constant because C avoids B and CB=0. The residual misses B∞ and B₀ by Section2, so r has EXACT polynomial degree3 and r(0)≠0. At infinity w=s³w∞. Thus the transition weights on B are precisely3 for r,1 for A,B₁, and0 for q. ## 4. The other horizontal factors

At a simple auxiliary zero-fiber point of an integral curve Z, with t a parameter, write v=f(t), u₀²=−d. If a|Z is nonzero and no other local R component is present, restriction of the whole cubic gives a²=−γ₂Q Dρ. Q is a unit because the ACTUAL C avoids the auxiliary point, and Dρ is a unit multiple of−3dt⁵−f'f. Its order must be even. Leading orders1,3,5 are odd except for
\[
f=ct³+O(t⁴),\quad c²=-d,
\quad\operatorname{ord}(u-u₀)\ge7.
\tag{9}
\]
The derivation of(9), including all characteristic-five leading-order cases, is in the whole-audited [integral-conductor proof](canonical_fifteen_split_integral_conductor_exclusion.md). The identical statement for b holds after the ACTUAL endpoint swap.

Suppose H in(3) is integral. Its degree2 and the two auxiliary points make all four end intersections simple. At each end there is no other local R component: B is a different point and V contains neither end fiber. If a|H is nonzero, (9) gives a zero of u−u₀ of order≥7. The only poles of u on its normalization are bounded by3F∞ plus H∩B. Each of the TWO auxiliary infinity points removes at least one from its degree, so
\[
\deg(u|\widetilde H)\le6+h-2=4+h.
\tag{10}
\]
The function is nonconstant, since H passes both zero-fiber auxiliary points with distinct u values. For h≤2, (10) contradicts(9), forcing H to divide a. The actual swap gives the same b factor.

For h=3, nonzero b|H forces U=c_i s³+O(s⁴) at BOTH simple infinity auxiliary points, by(9) in swapped coordinates. Thus all six infinity pole orders of u are removed and deg u≤h=3. This contradicts the order-seven zero if a|H is nonzero. At least one of a,b therefore contains H. But V=0 when h=3 by(4), because any vertical prime meets a boundary positively. The boundary factor from Section3 then gives a forbidden FULL ρ factor in that same extension, excluded by the [full single-cubic theorem](canonical_fifteen_split_full_cubic_extension_exclusion.md).

If H consists of two distinct sections Z₁,Z₂, each section is auxiliary at both ends, with opposite auxiliary values from the other. Put j=Z_iB≤3. Its u pole degree is bounded by3+j−1≤5, because the infinity auxiliary point removes at least one. Nonzero a|Z_i would give the order-seven zero(9), contradiction. Thus both sections divide a. Their u functions are nonconstant, since a constant auxiliary u would be one of the four excluded constant-q₀ sections; the [constant-q₀ conductor theorem](canonical_fifteen_split_constant_q0_conductor_exclusion.md) excludes those actual factors.

It follows that, outside the already impossible h=3 case, ALL horizontal R factors divide BOTH actual extensions, with their full multiplicities. The remaining pole divisor P of h=a/ρ is finite vertical and bounded by V. In particular
\[
P(D_++D_-)\le V B\le3.

## 5. Global critical section with these vertical poles

We prove the conditional vertical-pole assertion of the theorem, allowing P to meet both boundaries with total intersection≤3. Let h=a/ρ have poles bounded by3D+10F∞+P, with P finite vertical and no end-fiber component. Rational cancellation in(1) gives
\[

Set
\[
G=(h³-P₂)/(\gamma₂Q)=\mathcal Dh/\rho.
\tag{12}
\]
The numerator has global poles bounded by10D+30F∞+3P and vanishes at the generic point of C, because h has its ACTUAL restriction y₂. Prime Cartier division by Q therefore makes G a GLOBAL section of
\[
A+3P,\qquad A=4D+12F_\infty-R,

\tag{13}
\]
This argument uses the numerator pole bound and sheaf divisibility; no regularity across the vertical poles is presumed for Dh. Smoothness of S extends the divisorial regularity across codimension two.

The exact actual infinity ledger is unchanged because P is finite. In every finite, auxiliary or boundary third-point case of ρ∞ it gives
\[

Thus G is nonzero and E=div(G) has no boundary component or F∞ component. Vertical components of E are retained. Its horizontal part has

## 6. Every horizontal critical component contradicts genus

Differentiation of(12), using DP₂=0,D h=ρG and γ₂=3/λ₂, gives
\[
\mathcal DG/G=\lambda₂\rho h²/Q-\mathcal DQ/Q.
\tag{17}
\]
At the generic point of Z_j, the finite vertical P is absent, h is regular, and Q is a unit because f_j≤5<CF15. Common-frame changes add only regular logarithmic terms. Thus(17) is regular even when Z_j also occurs in R. If5∤μ_j, its nonzero logarithmic residue forces D to preserve Z_j. The induced derivation is nonzero: Dt=v cannot vanish identically on a horizontal invariant curve, since Dv=−3dt⁵ would then also have to vanish. Therefore t is separating and, over perfect k, the derivation kernel is the fifth-power field. Since Du=0,
\[
u=f₀⁵\quad\text{in }k(\widetilde Z_j).
\tag{18}
\]
On the critical component, (12) gives h³=P(u−1). Let P^{1/5} be coefficientwise inverse Frobenius. As in the full-extension proof, the Bézout calculation h=(P^{1/5}(f₀−1)²/h)⁵ gives h=h₀⁵, hence
\[
h₀³=P^{1/5}(f₀-1).
\tag{19}
\]
This supplies a genuine AUXILIARY map to the genus-nine inverse-Frobenius cubic, not an original endpoint leg. If its degree is e≥1, then deg f₀=3e and deg u=15e. By(15)–(16), deg u≤3f_j+9≤24. Thus e=1, deg f₀3 and g(Ẑ_j)=9.

The image coordinates generate k(Ẑ_j), and the conic is quadratic in v. Hence its index over k(t,u) is≤2. Since f₀ belongs to the field, its index over k(t,f₀) is≤2 and also divides deg f₀3. It equals one even if f_j is even. The separating generating functions t of degree f_j≤5 and f₀ of degree3 give the Castelnuovo–Severi bound
\[
g(\widetilde Z_j)\le(f_j-1)(3-1)\le8,
\]
contradiction. No claim that5 divides f_j is made; finite boundary poles would invalidate that shortcut.

If5|μ_j, the total fiber budget in(15) forces μ_j=5,f_j=1. Its normalization is P¹. The boundary budget gives Z_jD≤1, so3≤deg u≤4. Its auxiliary identity h³=P(u−1) forces deg u to be a positive multiple of the trigonal degree3 of X. Thus deg u3, and the auxiliary inclusion has degree one, impossible between rational and genus-nine fields.

All horizontal components contributing to the positive fiber degree5 have been excluded. This proves the conditional finite-vertical-pole assertion and, by Sections2–4 and(11), excludes every boundary component of R.
