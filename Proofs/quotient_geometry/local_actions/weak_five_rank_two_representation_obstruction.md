# Proof: the two-dimensional inverse classes have nonsquare companion volume

Version1,3 October2026. Pending focused review; see the [statement](../../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/local_actions/weak_five_rank_two_representation_obstruction.md). No computation or finite-group classification is used.

The prescribed cyclic local lift and global patching established in the [general weak cyclic reflection obstruction](weak_cyclic_reflection_representation_obstruction.md) depend only on the actual curve and inertia data, not on the representation dimension. They give generators a,b,c of G of orders FIVE,FIVE,TWO, with abc=ONE and b conjugate to a⁻¹. The chosen tame class is the original nonidentity inertia class. Suppose an irreducible projective rank-TWO representation exists.

Take the unique unipotent lifts A,B of its p-generator images and let C=(AB)⁻¹. The image of c cannot be trivial: if it were, B would be projectively A⁻¹ and the projective image generated by a,b would be cyclic and reducible. Thus C has two distinct eigenvalues λ,−λ. Its determinant is ONE, giving λ²=−ONE, so λ∈F5.

The elementary Scott inequality in the cited proof applies to End(V), of dimensionFOUR. Simultaneous invariant and dual invariant dimensions are ONE by irreducibility. The C-centralizer dimension isTWO, while each A,B centralizer has dimension at leastTWO. Their sum is bounded bySIX, so both A and B have minimal centralizer dimensionTWO and are regular J2 blocks. In particular neither order-FIVE image has been killed. The same inequality on opposite Hom spaces proves simultaneous rigidity of any irreducible product-ONE triple in these three classes. Since their precise eigenvalues lie in F5, Frobenius rigidity and GL₂ Lang descent place A,B,C in SL₂(F5).

The entire projective image R is generated by the images of A,B. Determinant modulo squares in F5× is a quotient of PGL₂(F5) of orderTWO and kills these order-FIVE generators. Hence R⊂PSL₂(F5). The inverse conjugacy in G supplies an SL₂(F5) conjugator between A⁻¹ and B: the projective scalar is ONE because both are unipotent.

Write AB=ρ(I−TWO E), where E is its rank-ONE simple-eigenspace projector. Here ρ²=−ONE, so ρ has orderFOUR in F5×. Put M=ρB⁻¹. Then M−A has rankONE, its characteristic polynomial is (x−ρ)², and the pair A,M is irreducible. The simultaneous companion-basis argument of the cited proof works with n=TWO: choose q in kerℓ with ℓAq≠ZERO for M−A=vℓ, and use q,Aq. Thus both A and M are companion matrices in one F5 basis.

If e is its first vector, the positive M-cyclic basis has determinantONE. The B-cyclic basis therefore has determinant
\[
\det(e,Be)=\rho\det(e,M^{-1}e)
=-\rho(\det M)^{-1}=\rho,
\]
because detM=ρ²=−ONE. Every other A-to-B conjugator changes this volume by a square: the centralizer of a regular J2 consists of invertible polynomials in it, with determinants squares.

For A⁻¹ the corresponding cyclic-basis determinant is −(detA)⁻¹=−ONE. Thus inverse conjugacy changes the A-to-B determinant class by −ONE. This is a SQUARE in F5, so it does not change the square class. The order-FOUR scalar ρ is NONSQUARE in F5×, whose square subgroup has orderTWO. Hence every conjugator between A⁻¹ and B has nonsquare determinant, whereas the element of R supplied one with determinantONE. This contradiction proves the claimed obstruction.

The sign −ONE in the TWO-dimensional inverse cyclic basis is retained explicitly; it differs from the dimension-FOUR argument but is harmless precisely because it is a square in F5. The result concerns an actual projective representation of the target group only. A further étale source cover trivializing a coefficient may change the group and destroy primitive spin-carrier degreeTEN, so that operation does not automatically put the coefficient inside this theorem.
