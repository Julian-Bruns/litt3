## Ordinary finite points and the marked cubic branch

The regular finite primitive frame makes q and all coefficients of
S integral at every affine point. Away from tv=0, the leading
coefficient is a unit, so all source roots are integral. Actual
etaleness splits them in the completed base ring. Because tau is a
unit, phi(w) is a unit at each integral root: otherwise F(w)=0 would
reduce to tau=0. Thus the source energy and both correction terms
are regular. This uses the roots themselves, not an inverse of the
critical discriminant or critical leading coefficient.

At the marked branch v=0 in the linear profile, the accepted finite
pole budget in the regular primitive frame has at most three poles,
each of order at most one. At an integral root phi(w) is again a
unit. At a root with pole one, phi(w) has pole five, whereas dw has
pole at most two. Its contribution dw^2/phi(w) therefore has positive
order at least one. The correction terms are regular since q,gamma
are integral and tau is a unit. Thus no pole occurs at the marked
branch, even on coefficient boundaries preserving the actual profile.

## All fixed endpoints, including critical content

At a simple zero of t, both q-q(0) and tau have order exactly three.
For q this follows also from dq=A^2 dx/y^2, since t divides A and
the selected points are not cubic branch points. A constant source
translation removes q(0). The leading coefficient is a unit and all
source roots split integrally. The
[cubic-endpoint energy lemma](split_source_cubic_endpoint_energy.md)
then gives at most a simple pole for the full corrected expression.
Multiplication by t removes it.

No primitive critical-polynomial hypothesis is used here. In particular,
this step does not need to decide whether an endpoint-content curve
is empty or whether an individual critical branch is regular.

## Infinity: two weighted moments improve the bound

Use the short source coordinate, replacing B0 by L0 of degree three.
This is a base translation, so the corrected energy is unchanged.
In this frame q has pole seven, tau has pole twenty-seven, and gamma
has pole at most thirteen. The latter follows from gamma=D2 in
L_X(14O), since fourteen is a gap in the semigroup generated by three
and ten. Consequently each of the two correction terms has order
at least five as a quadratic differential at O.

The five large root poles are (6,6,4,2,2) in the constant profile,
or (5,5,3,2,2) in the linear profile. The other five roots have pole
at most one. If a root has pole m>=2, its fifth power dominates q,
so dw^2/phi(w) has order at least3m-2, hence at least four.
For a small root the elementary bound is three. Its order-three
coefficient cancels after summation, as follows.

Choose a uniformizer u at O and write the five small roots as
w_i=c_i u^{-1}+O(1), and q=q_{-7}u^{-7}+O(u^{-6}). The universal
weighted moment identity is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(w^2/\phi(w))=0.
\]
Every large root contributes order at least six to this identity,
whereas the coefficient of u^5 from the small roots is
q_{-7}^{-1} sum c_i^2. Thus sum c_i^2=0. That same sum is the
coefficient of u^3(du)^2 in their source energy. Hence the energy
has order at least four. A second moment removes its order-four term.
Write w_i=c_i u^{-1}+d_i+O(u) for the small roots. The identity
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(w^2/\phi(w)^2)=2\gamma/\tau
\]
has right side of order at least fourteen. Its coefficient at order
thirteen is 2 q_{-7}^{-2} sum c_i d_i: the preceding sum c_i^2 is
zero, and the large roots contribute only from order sixteen. Thus
sum c_i d_i=0. If the roots of pole two have leading terms
r_j u^{-2}, the order-six coefficient of Tr(w^2/phi)=0 now gives
sum r_j^{-3}=0. These roots are the only possible order-four
contributors to the energy, and their total coefficient is
4 sum r_j^{-3}=0. The small roots have no order-four contribution,
since the constant d_i differentiates to zero and sum c_i^2=0.
Therefore the energy, and the corrected expression, have order at
least five. Multiplication by t leaves a pole of order at most four.

Together with finite regularity this proves membership in
H^0(omega_X^2(4O)). Since div(omega0^2)=32O, division by omega0^2
gives L_X(36O). The three displayed monomial ranges contain13+9+6=28
elements and give its standard cubic-character basis.

## Computational meaning and precise limitation

The scalar expression can be computed in the finite separable algebra
k(X)[W]/F by implicit differentiation and trace, before imposing a
square norm or constructing the critical double cover. An actual
etale source makes every affine principal part vanish and forces the
remaining rational function into the fixed space L_X(36O). These are
algebraic conditions on the original source coefficients.

This replaces some local hypotheses in the earlier divided-critical-
trace method. It does not assert that these new conditions are
sufficient, or independent of all previously used equations. Their
joint parameter locus has not been decided in this proof. The cubic-
derivative sector and the original unmarked extraction problem remain
outside its scope.
