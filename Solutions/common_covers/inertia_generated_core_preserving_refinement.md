## 1. The general criterion

For a connected Galois cover C'->C with group G, an intermediate
cover C'/H->C is etale iff every inertia group acts trivially on G/H.
Equivalently H contains the normal subgroup generated by ALL inertia
groups. Thus the inertia hypothesis means there is no nontrivial
etale intermediate cover, including NON-GALOIS intermediates.

This is the usual genuinely ramified condition. Its equivalence with
surjectivity on etale fundamental groups is
[Biswas--Das--Parameswaran, Theorem2.4(1)<->(2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03246).
That part of the proof uses the etale cover corresponding to the
finite-index image of pi1; it imposes no characteristic or degree
restriction. In our Galois curve situation the inertia argument above
also proves the precise disjointness needed below directly.

Write K=k(X), L=k(Y), M=k(Z), and choose the endpoint extensions
K'/K, L'/L in a separable closure of M. The field K' intersection M
is etale over K, because it is intermediate in the ORIGINAL etale
map Z->X. By the preceding paragraph it is K. As K'/K is Galois,
K' and M are linearly disjoint over K. The same holds on the Y side.

Set M'=M K' L' and H=Gal(M'/M). It preserves both K' and L'; restriction
is surjective onto each full endpoint Galois group. Thus

    (K')^H=K, (L')^H=L,
    (K' intersection L')^H=K intersection L=k.

Each element of K' intersection L' is algebraic over these finite-group
invariants, by its orbit polynomial. Since k is algebraically closed,
the intersection is k. This proves corelessness.

At a point of Z, the original etale maps identify the completed base
fields. By hypothesis the two selected endpoint completed extensions
are the SAME subfield of a separable closure. Their compositum adds
no ramification over either. The normalization W of M' therefore has
finite etale maps to X',Y'. This argument applies to every choice of
embeddings, hence every normalized component. For tame extensions over
k((t)), equality follows from equal index: the unique extension of
index n is k((t^(1/n))). Equal indices do NOT suffice in the wild case.

## 2. Perfect groups prevent any loss of primitive weight

The original s remains a nonzero regular shared tensor upstairs, so
the canonical-intersection theorem gives k[t] upstairs with
s=c t^m, d=m d'. Rescale t. Since d is prime to the characteristic,
so is m. Write s_C=a_C eta_C^d in a rational one-form frame downstairs.
Then u_C=t_C/(pi_C^*eta_C)^d' lies in k(C') and u_C^m=a_C.

Because k contains all m-th roots of unity, k(C)(u_C)/k(C) is cyclic
Galois. A perfect group has no nontrivial cyclic quotient; hence u_C
already belongs to k(C). Thus t_C descends to a rational weight-d'
tensor on C. Its m-th power is s_C, so this DESCENDED tensor is regular,
by valuations downstairs. The two descended roots have pullbacks on
Z differing only by a constant m-th root of unity. Rescale one.
If m>1 this contradicts original primitivity. Therefore m=1.

Notice that regularity upstairs across a ramified cover would not by
itself prove regularity downstairs. The POWER identity is essential.
Nor is any coprimality between d and168 used here.

## 3. A fixed finite group realizes all three needed inertia orders

Let G=SL2(F7)/{+I,-I}. All matrices below are taken modulo7 and sign;
(a,b,c,d) denotes the matrix with rows (a,b),(c,d). Set

    A=(1,1,0,1), B=(1,0,1,1),
    tau2=(0,1,6,0), tau3=(0,1,6,1), tau4=(0,1,6,3).

The group has168 elements and A,B generate it (elementary matrices).
The tau_n have exact order n. The normal closures of each tau_n,
and of [A,B], are G. The last assertion shows G is perfect. These
small facts can be verified without classification: enumerate the
determinant-one matrices modulo sign, then close the explicit
conjugates under multiplication. The attached checker does exactly
this; it does not trust a group-library identification.

For every n=2,3,4 and j modulo n the following C,D satisfy

    [A,B][C,D] tau_n^j=1.

| n | j | C | D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 0 | (0,1,6,2) | (0,1,6,1) |
| 2 | 1 | (0,2,3,3) | (1,3,6,5) |
| 3 | 0 | (0,1,6,2) | (0,1,6,1) |
| 3 | 1 | (0,1,6,1) | (1,3,3,3) |
| 3 | 2 | (0,3,2,0) | (0,2,3,3) |
| 4 | 0 | (0,1,6,2) | (0,1,6,1) |
| 4 | 1 | (0,1,6,0) | (0,1,6,0) |
| 4 | 2 | (0,1,6,1) | (0,1,6,2) |
| 4 | 3 | (0,1,6,3) | (1,5,1,6) |

For a punctured genus-g curve, use A,B on its first handle, this C,D
on its second, identities on all others, and tau_n at each of its r
punctures. Take j=r mod n. The surface relation holds and the images
generate G. Since168 is prime to5,
[SGA1, XIII, Corollary2.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0206203), printed
pages290--292 (PDF305--307), realizes precisely these generators,
relation AND inertia by a G-Galois cover of the punctured curve.
The smooth completion has exact index n at every puncture and no
other ramification. Normal generation of tau_n makes it genuinely
ramified. Riemann--Hurwitz gives the asserted genus formula.

The cited SGA theorem's proof uses prime-to-p specialization for a
SINGLE pointed curve. We do not lift the original two-map span or
assume that both maps lift. The actual upper span is constructed by
the characteristic-five compositum argument in Section1.

## 4. Applying the cover simultaneously, and its degree bound

For a clump, the reduced endpoint divisors pull back to the same
divisor on Z. Choose the preceding Galois covers independently, with
the SAME n. Their completed fields over Z agree, so Section1 produces
the required actual coreless span. Section2 preserves primitive weight.

Both MK'/M and ML'/M have degree168. Hence
168 divides [M':M] and [M':M]<=168^2. Since [K':K]=[L':L]=168,

    [M':K']/[M:K]=[M':M]/168<=168,
    [M':L']/[M:L]=[M':M]/168<=168.

No assertion of a simultaneous Galois closure of the ORIGINAL legs is
made. These are chosen ramified auxiliary covers, not those closures.

## 5. The Cartier-zero equation becomes regular and transverse

The local argument is the one in the retained audited
[transverse reduction](../../routes/global/CORE_PRESERVING_TAME_REDUCTION_TO_TRANSVERSE_DORMANT_MIURA_DATA.md),
but the new auxiliary construction removes its dependence on both leg
degrees and on coprimality with d.

Let E=e/d in F5. The leading Cartier term excludes E=4. For E=1,2,3,
choose n=3,2,4 respectively, so n(E+1)=1. Pullback changes the order to

    e'=n e+d(n-1)=n(e+d)-d=0 modulo5.

Eligible Cartier zero commutes with rational separable pullback, also
at ramified points. Locally write the pulled tensor as a(du)^d with
a=u^(5m)v, v a unit. Then ell=a'/(d a)=v'/(d v) is regular, and

    r_s=-ell'/2+ell^2/4

is a regular projective connection. Its p-curvature is zero by the
[explicit equation theorem](../../routes/global/EXPLICIT_SECOND_ORDER_EQUATION_FOR_CARTIER_ZERO_PLURIFORMS.md).
Choose j with2dj=-1mod5. The horizontal solution jet of a^j, after
removing its horizontal factor u^(5mj), is (v^j,(v^j)'). Its first
coordinate is a unit. Its saturated horizontal line is therefore
everywhere transverse to the oper line. All identifications commute
with BOTH actual upper etale maps, so these structures are shared.
If E=0 this argument already applies on the original endpoints.

## 6. Gain and boundary

The old solvable-cover construction permits auxiliary degrees avoiding
any prescribed finite set of primes. That feature is NOT supplied by
the fixed group here; its audited special case remains available.
The degree-four regularization remains a smaller option when preserving
corelessness is unnecessary. Neither historical result is deleted.

The new bound168 is independent of tensor weight and original cover
degrees. But its branch support, and thus endpoint genus, remains
unbounded. Known coreless Tango examples prevent concluding a core
from the resulting transverse dormant structure. Original endpoints
and the degree168 descent maps must remain part of any next argument.
