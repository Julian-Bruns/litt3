# Wild local atlas notation

ID: `wild_ramification`. Uses [orbifolds](Def_orbifolds.md).

For a local Galois inertia action, I_i are the lower-numbering
ramification groups, I_0=I, P=I_1, q=|P|, and |I|=q t with p∤t.
The different exponent is delta=Σ_(i≥0)(|I_i|-1). Put c=delta-q t.
The first positive lower break is b and its first deeper subgroup is
N=P_(b+1); write |P/N|=p^r. A bound on r is NOT a bound on |P| or |N|.

For a two-branch effective atlas C→S with coarse P^1, one branch wild
and one tame, let n=deg(C/S), kappa=2g(C)-2, t=g_0 t_0, and let the
other tame inertia order be d=g_0 m, with gcd(m,t_0)=1. In the low-
different case 1<delta/(qt)<2, the signature reductions use a positive
integer Delta dividing kappa, with

    c m - q t_0 = Delta,
    n=(kappa/Delta) q g_0 t_0 m.

Old solution records call this integer `D`; here Delta distinguishes
it from a marked divisor. The displayed identities are the hypotheses
supplied by the signature-reduction results, not assumptions about every
arbitrary curve map. Full tame order t and reduced order t_0 differ.

The auxiliary HKG curve H realizes the local P-action with one wild
branch and has 2g(H)=c-q+2. It is not automatically an etale refinement
of C and is not assumed ordinary. Upper jumps refer to Herbrand upper
numbering; nonabelian groups need not have integral upper jumps.
