# Tame common poles and concentrated endpoints on the quintic quotient

Version1, 27 September2026. Retain the actual pole-fifteen comparison
and [its simultaneous cubic quotient](pole_fifteen_cubic_descent.md).
Write u=x1,v=x2 on S, t:S->P1 of degree five, deg u=deg v=n,
and A(v)=epsilon^4t^-13A(u).

At a common pole, u and v have the same pole order e in{1,3}.
Its parameter value c has c^29=1. Put ell=epsilon c^4.
The complete common-pole table remains

| e | ell | ramification index of t |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | not1 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 | 4 |
| 3 | not1 | 2 or3 |
| 3 | 1 | 2 |

In particular t is tame at EVERY common pole. It may still be
wildly ramified elsewhere. For d_c the sum of common-pole orders
above c, one has0<=d_c<=7 and sum d_c=n-15. At the exceptional
value ell=1 the sharper list is d_c in{0,1,3,6}.

The first two and fourth-coefficient trace equations from the
quartic calculation continue to hold, now with FIVE endpoint
labels. They must not be combined with the completed FOUR-label
enumeration, whose cardinality hypothesis is essential.

If all five zero-endpoint branches share one label(alpha,phase),
and all five infinity-endpoint branches share another single
label, then
\[
g(S)\le4n-84,\qquad n\ge21.
\]
The two labels and phases need not agree. In this case all four
recorded low endpoint sums vanish. Nevertheless the common-pole
moments need NOT vanish: the homogeneous equations admit, for
example, epsilon=1 and S2=S6=1. Such moment data do not construct
an actual comparison.

No existence or nonexistence theorem for the remaining quintic
quotients is asserted.
