## Statement

Let \(G\) be a finite group generated by elements of order five, with a
surjection \(G\to Q=PSL_8(\mathbf F_5)\) and finite two-group kernel \(N\).
If \(N\) is noncentral, then
\[\dim_{\mathbf F_2}N/\Phi(N)\ge97,344,\qquad |N|\ge2^{97,344}.
\]
Here \(\Phi(N)=N^2[N,N]\) is the Frattini subgroup. In particular this
applies to the actual full-linear source sector if its projective kernel
is a two-group, because its noncentrality has already been audited.
It also applies to the audited abstract elementary abelian construction.
The result rules out all smaller two-kernels but does not give a global
bound on the original étale degree, which remains unrestricted.

The proof has two elementary parts: noncentrality forces a faithful
characteristic-two representation of \(Q\) on \(N/\Phi(N)\), and one
abelian unipotent subgroup of \(Q\), with its Levi normalizer, forces the
displayed dimension. No cross-characteristic classification or minimum-
degree table is used.

## Noncentrality forces faithful action on the Frattini quotient

Put \(V=N/\Phi(N)\) and \(r=\dim_{\mathbf F_2}V\). Conjugation by \(G\)
on \(V\) factors through \(Q\), since inner automorphisms of \(N\) act
trivially on this quotient. If that \(Q\)-action were trivial, the image
of \(G\to\operatorname{Aut}(N)\) would lie in
\[U=\ker(\operatorname{Aut}(N)\to GL(V)).
\]
This kernel is a two-group. For a direct proof, fix an ordered basis of
\(V\). Every tuple of lifts of this basis generates \(N\): otherwise it
would lie in a maximal subgroup of the finite two-group, whose index-two
quotient would contradict spanning \(V\). The set of lift tuples has
cardinality \(|\Phi(N)|^r\). The group \(U\) acts freely on that set, as
an automorphism fixing a generating tuple is identity. Thus \(|U|\)
divides a power of two.

An order-five element cannot have nontrivial image in \(U\); the given
generators would therefore centralize \(N\), contrary to its noncentrality.
The \(Q\)-action on \(V\) is consequently nontrivial. The standard
simplicity theorem for \(PSL_n(\mathbf F_q)\), outside its usual rank-one
exceptions, makes \(Q\) simple: Steinberg, [§4 Theorem 5](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/steinberg/steinberg-yale-notes.pdf), applied to type \(A_7\) over \(\mathbf F_5\).
Hence this action is faithful. Extend
scalars to \(\overline{\mathbf F}_2\); dimension remains \(r\).

## A sixteen-dimensional elementary abelian five-subgroup

In \(SL_8(\mathbf F_5)\) take
\[E=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_4&X\\0&I_4\end{pmatrix}:
X\in\operatorname{Mat}_4(\mathbf F_5)\right\}.
\]
It has trivial intersection with the scalar center and so embeds in \(Q\).
It is the additive group \(C_5^{16}\). Its normalizer contains the image
of \(L=SL_4(\mathbf F_5)\times SL_4(\mathbf F_5)\), acting on \(X\)
by \(X\mapsto PXR^{-1}\). Characteristic two is prime to \(|E|\), so
the representation \(V\otimes\overline{\mathbf F}_2\) decomposes into
weight spaces for \(E\). Faithfulness ensures a nontrivial weight occurs.
The actual Levi image in \(Q\) is the quotient by diagonal \(\mu_4\);
this kernel acts trivially on \(E\) and its characters, so the character
orbits computed using \(L\) are precisely the actual orbits.

Choose a primitive fifth root \(\zeta\in\overline{\mathbf F}_2\).
Every character of \(E\) has the form
\[\chi_Y(X)=\zeta^{\operatorname{tr}(YX)},\qquad
Y\in\operatorname{Mat}_4(\mathbf F_5).
\]
The trace here is the matrix trace in \(\mathbf F_5\), interpreted as an
exponent modulo five. The contragredient Levi action on \(Y\) is left/right
matrix multiplication by elements of \(SL_4\). It preserves matrix rank,
and preserves determinant when the rank is four.

For ranks \(s=1,2,3\), \(SL_4\times SL_4\) is transitive on all matrices
of rank \(s\). Indeed \(GL_4\times GL_4\) is transitive by row/column
reduction. Since \(s<4\), transformations on a complementary kernel or
cokernel coordinate can adjust either determinant without changing the
matrix. For full rank, the \(SL_4\times SL_4\)-orbits are the sets of
each fixed nonzero determinant: left multiplication already acts
transitively on such a set.

The number of rank-\(s\) four-by-four matrices is
\[m_s={4\brack s}_5^2\,|GL_s(\mathbf F_5)|\quad(1\le s<4).
\]
For rank one this is
\[m_1=\frac{(5^4-1)^2}{5-1}=\frac{624^2}{4}=97,344.
\]
For rank two it is \(806^2\cdot480>97,344\); for rank three it is
\(156^2\cdot(124\cdot120\cdot100)>97,344\). A full-rank determinant
orbit has size
\[|SL_4(\mathbf F_5)|=5^6(5^2-1)(5^3-1)(5^4-1)>97,344.
\]
Thus EVERY nontrivial \(E\)-character has a Levi orbit of size at least
97,344. Because the representation is \(L\)-invariant, every character
in the orbit of an occurring weight occurs with the same positive
multiplicity. Distinct weights have distinct direct-summand spaces.
Its total dimension \(r\) is therefore at least 97,344, proving the
bound. Finally \(|N|\ge|N/\Phi(N)|=2^r\).

## Relation to the actual source frontier

The accepted source theorem requires the actual full-SL8 projective
kernel to be even and noncentral, and the extension to be nonsplit.
The argument here requires the additional hypothesis that this actual
kernel is a two-group; it does not turn a general even kernel into one.
The abstract construction meets these conditions with an elementary
abelian two-kernel, so it must be correspondingly large. There is no
contradiction between that construction and this lower bound.

The original common-cover degree and carrier genus have no accepted
upper bound small enough to contradict this estimate. The bound does
not prove an original \(X\)-leg descends, does not generate the full
spin carrier from the eight-dimensional row, and does not provide the
missing invariant square root on the quotient. BOTH actual finite étale
maps from the SAME source remain indispensable.
