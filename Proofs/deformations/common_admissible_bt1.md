# Proof: realize both endpoints, then use the finite-character torsor

[Statement](../../Theorems/deformations/common_admissible_bt1.md).

The later [first-periodic effectivity theorem](admissible_periodic_bt_effectivity.md)
realizes every admissible active oper by an actual everywhere-versal
BT1, after the permitted flat fourth-root correction. Apply it
separately on $X$ and $Y$ to obtain $H_X,H_Y$. It requires no
indigenous ordinariness.

Their actual pullbacks to the same proper connected $Z$ induce the
same projective oper. The [finite-character comparison](ordinary_oper_bt_effectivity.md#comparing-two-bt1-realizations-of-the-same-projective-oper)
identifies the realizations of a fixed projective oper as a torsor
under $H^1_{\rm et}(Z,\mathbf F_5^\times)$. That comparison explicitly
uses only the existence of the two groups. Hence their discrepancy
is a rank-one etale character $\Lambda_D$ of order dividing four,
and gives (1), including the connection, Hodge line and both
Frobenius arrows. No effectivity of candidate matrices is needed.

The connected cover belonging to the character's image has degree
equal to its order, hence1,2 or4. It trivializes the discrepancy,
so $(fh)^*H_X\simeq(gh)^*H_Y$ on that same $Z'$. Both original
endpoint maps are retained through this refinement.

Conversely, the [Cartier-rigidity calculation](versal_bt_cartier_realization.md)
associates an admissible active projective oper to every actual
everywhere-versal BT1. It commutes with etale pullback. Thus a
compatible group comparison gives equal pulled-back projective
opers; faithful flatness of a source refinement descends their
equality to $Z$.

If $r_Y$ is indigenous-ordinary, [ordinary effectivity](ordinary_oper_bt_effectivity.md)
supplies a normalized full group on $Y$ whose first truncation can
be used in the construction. Its higher comparisons are additional
data: the finite-character torsor above compares only BT1s and
does not produce them.
