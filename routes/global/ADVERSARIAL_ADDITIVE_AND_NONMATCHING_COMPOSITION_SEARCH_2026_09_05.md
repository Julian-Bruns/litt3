# Additive moduli and nonmatching composition: adversarial search

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: /root/adversarial_singleton_counterexample.
Status: two bounded obstructions and a demonstrated failure of a proposed
norm argument. This bounded search itself found no singleton example.
Its follow-on algebraic investigation did produce the
[projective etale singleton counterexample](CORELESS_PROJECTIVE_ETALE_SINGLETON_TANGO_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md).
The obstructions below apply only to their stated recipes.

## 1. A concrete additive family fails corelessness

The Moret–Bailly recipe starts with a supersingular elliptic curve E and
the family A_t = E²/H_t, where H_t is the alpha_p subgroup corresponding
to the line [1:t] in alpha_p². This is a genuinely global projective
moduli family, so it is a more concrete additive replacement than a
formal Serre–Tate coordinate. The construction is described in
[Katsura–Oort, section 1](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1987__62_2_107_0.pdf).

Its prime-to-p polarized isogeny correspondences cannot be coreless:
the prime-to-p Hecke orbit of every supersingular principally polarized
abelian variety is finite. This statement applies after passing to an
algebraic closure of the generic field as well. See
[Chai–Oort, Remark 2.4(b), p. 8](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~chai/papers_pdf/HOC_v5.pdf).
If a curve maps generically finitely to this moduli locus, each generic
correspondence-graph class is therefore finite, even after any fixed
finite framing cover. Finite generic graph classes give a nonconstant
core: symmetric functions of a separating coordinate over the finite
class are invariant, and cannot all be constant since the coordinate
is a root of their monic polynomial.

This excludes this supersingular additive recipe even if one manages
to construct a regular exact differential with p-divisible divisor.
It does not exclude mixed-slope, non-supersingular higher-dimensional
moduli constructions. Such a construction would still need an actual
Hecke-stable curve, global additive torsor, compactification, and two
étale maps from the same connected source; none was supplied here.

## 2. Nonmatching composition on a connected full fiber product

Let X <- Z1 -> Y and Y <- Z2 -> T be finite étale spans of smooth
projective connected curves. Suppose W = Z1 ×_Y Z2 is connected.
Every actually shared outer regular d-pluriform descends to a regular
d-pluriform on Y, shared across each of the two original spans.

Indeed, put B=k(Y), L1=k(Z1), L2=k(Z2). Connectedness of the full
fiber product says L1 tensor_B L2 is a field, so L1 and L2 are linearly
disjoint and their intersection in k(W) is B. Divide the shared tensor
by the pullback of any nonzero rational d-pluriform on Y. Its coefficient
belongs to L1 intersect L2, hence B. The descended tensor is regular
because its pullback under the surjective étale map Z1 -> Y is regular.

Consequently, if both original spans are coreless with invariant rings
k[eta1] and k[eta2], generated by one-forms whose lines on Y differ,
the outer span has no positive canonical intersection at all. For a
positive weight d the descended tensor would make eta1^d proportional
to eta2^d; their rational ratio would then be constant. Thus changing
the intermediate witness cannot create a singleton via a connected
FULL fiber product.

This is not a claim about an arbitrary component of a disconnected
fiber product. On such a component L1 intersect L2 can properly contain
B, and the displayed descent argument stops there.

## 3. Why the obvious norm repair is invalid

Separability and étaleness alone do not make a norm of an exact
differential Cartier-zero in the corresponding pluriform sense.
In characteristic five take t=u² and alpha=d(u+u²). On the open t-line
obtained by removing 0, 1/4 and infinity this is an étale double cover,
and alpha is exact and nowhere vanishing upstairs. Its norm is

    N(alpha) = (1 - 1/(4t)) (dt)^2.

The eligible power for weight two is the cube, of weight six.
The coefficient of t^(-1) in (1-1/(4t))³ is -3/4, which is nonzero
in characteristic five, and the other occurring exponents are
0,-2,-3. Thus C_1(N(alpha)^3) is nonzero.

This is an affine algebraic counterexample to the proposed norm
identity, not a projective counterexample to the singleton branch.
It also has zero divisor zero on the indicated open curves, so merely
imposing p-divisibility away from the compactification boundary does
not fix the argument. A proof for arbitrary projective components
would have to use additional global input. No such proof or actual
disconnected-component singleton construction was obtained here.
