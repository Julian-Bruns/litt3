# The Rosati norm in the seven-diamond and its polarization limit

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved.**  [Self-check recorded; independent audit pending](audits/42_ROSATI_POLARIZATION_AUDIT.md).

In the equal-degree diamond of files 38 and 40, this
note computes the positive endomorphism

\[
             (p_*a^*)^\dagger(p_*a^*)\in\operatorname{End}(J(Y))
\]

and records all consequences which follow just from Rosati positivity,
the degrees of the maps, and the induced Prym polarizations.  The
calculation gives a strict interval and an exact coincidence formula.

It does not improve the bound \(M\geq9\).  This failure is sharp at the
level of polarized abelian varieties: Section 3 constructs, for \(M=9\),
a principally polarized nineteen-fold satisfying all the norm,
orthogonality, polarization-type, and dimension identities forced on
\(J(C)\).  Section 4 gives a compatible rational \(C_7\)-module in the
Honda isogeny category.  These are compatibility models, not Jacobians of
curves.  They show exactly that a further argument must use the Jacobian
locus, effectivity of curve correspondences, or integral information
specific to the actual cyclic cover.

Throughout, \(J=J(Y)\), and all daggers are Rosati adjoints for the
canonical principal polarizations unless another polarization is
displayed explicitly.

## 1. The exact norm endomorphism

Assume that the reduction of file 38 gives

\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 V&\xrightarrow{a}&Y\\
 \downarrow p&&\\[-2mm]
 C&\xrightarrow{c}&X,
 \end{array}
 \qquad
 \deg a=\deg c=M,
 \qquad
 \deg p=7,
\tag{42.1}
\]

where \(p\) is a cyclic etale cover generated by \(\beta\), and
\(a\beta\ne a\).  Put

\[
 e=a^*:J\longrightarrow J(V),\qquad
 T=\beta^*,\qquad
 N=1+T+\cdots+T^6=p^*p_*,
\tag{42.2}
\]

and

\[
                  h=p_*a^*:J\longrightarrow J(C),
 \qquad s=h^\dagger h.
\tag{42.3}
\]

### Proposition 42.1 (norm, positivity, and coincidences)

In the situation above:

1. one has the exact identities
   \[
       e^\dagger e=[M],\qquad N^\dagger=N,\qquad N^2=7N,
       \qquad
       s=e^\dagger Ne
        =\sum_{j=0}^6 a_*\beta^{j*}a^*;
   \tag{42.4}
   \]
2. both \(s\) and \([7M]-s\) are nonzero positive Rosati-symmetric
   endomorphisms of the absolutely simple variety \(J\).  Equivalently,
   \[
                         0<s<[7M]
   \tag{42.5}
   \]
   in the Rosati positive cone;
3. for \(1\leq j\leq6\), let
   \[
     I_j=\deg (a,a\beta^j)^*\Delta_Y,
   \tag{42.6}
   \]
   with local intersection multiplicities.  Then \(I_j\geq0\),
   \(I_j=I_{7-j}\), and
   \[
      \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(s\mid H^1_{\mathrm{et}}
          (Y,\mathbf Q_\ell)\bigr)
              =42M-\sum_{j=1}^6 I_j .
   \tag{42.7}
   \]

#### Proof

Pull-push for the degree-\(M\) map \(a\) gives
\(a_*a^*=[M]\), which is the first identity in (42.4).  An automorphism
of a curve preserves its canonical principal polarization, so
\(T^\dagger=T^{-1}\).  The standard trace identity for the cyclic cover
gives

\[
                   p^*p_*=\sum_{j=0}^6T^j=N.
\]

It follows at once that \(N^\dagger=N\), \(N^2=7N\), and

\[
 h^\dagger h=a_*p^*p_*a^*=e^\dagger Ne,
\]

which proves (42.4).

The endomorphism \(N=p_*^\dagger p_*\) is positive.  The self-adjoint
idempotent \(N/7\) is the projector onto the invariant summand, so
\(7-N\) is positive as well.  Lemma 40.2 says that \(h\ne0\).  Since
\(J\) is absolutely simple, \(h^\dagger h=s\) is therefore positive
definite.  On the other hand,

\[
 [7M]-s=e^\dagger(7-N)e.                         \tag{42.8}
\]

If (42.8) vanished, the image of \(e\) would lie in the invariant
summand, and hence \(Te=e\).  Thus
\(\beta^*a^*=a^*\).  The map-rigidity argument in Theorem 40.3 then gives
\(a\beta=a\), contrary to (42.1).  Hence (42.8) is also positive
definite, proving (42.5).

For \(j\ne0\), the two maps \(a\) and \(a\beta^j\) are distinct by
Corollary 38.4.  Their graphs therefore meet properly, so (42.6) is a
nonnegative integer.  The Lefschetz trace formula for the correspondence

\[
                    a_*\beta^{j*}a^*:J\longrightarrow J
\]

gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(a_*\beta^{j*}a^*\mid H^1(Y,\mathbf Q_\ell)\bigr)
                       =2M-I_j.                    \tag{42.9}
\]

Indeed, its traces on \(H^0\) and \(H^2\) are both \(M\), while its
Lefschetz number is the intersection with the diagonal.  The term
\(j=0\) is \([M]\), whose trace on \(H^1(Y)\) is \(30M\).  Summing
(42.9) proves (42.7).  Finally, the change of variable
\(v\mapsto\beta^jv\) identifies the coincidence divisors for \(j\) and
\(-j\), including their local multiplicities.  Thus
\(I_j=I_{7-j}\). \(\square\)

## 2. What the Prym polarization actually bounds

Factor \(h\) as

\[
                 J\xrightarrow{\varpi}A
                   \xhookrightarrow{\ i\ }J(C),
 \qquad A=\operatorname{im}(h).
\tag{42.10}
\]

The variety \(A\) has dimension fifteen.  Let
\(\lambda_A=i^*\lambda_C\) be its induced polarization.

### Proposition 42.2 (the exact polarization constraints)

With the preceding notation,

\[
             \varpi^*\lambda_A=\lambda_J\circ s,
 \qquad
             \deg s=(\deg\varpi)^2\deg\lambda_A.       \tag{42.11}
\]

For every prime \(\ell\ne5\), the \(\ell\)-primary kernel of
\(\lambda_A\) can be generated by at most

\[
                         4M-28                            \tag{42.12}
\]

elements.

More precisely, put \(K_c=\ker(c^*:J(X)\to J(C))\) and
\(\kappa=|K_c|\).  If

\[
               P_c=\ker(c_*:J(C)\to J(X))^0,
\]

then the polarization induced on \(P_c\) has

\[
                         \deg\lambda_{P_c}
                           =\frac{M^6}{\kappa^2},         \tag{42.13}
\]

and its kernel has at most six elementary generators at every
prime different from five.  There is no resulting divisibility condition
on \(M\), because the finite kernel of \(\varpi\) in (42.10) can absorb
many elementary divisors of the pullback polarization in
(42.11).

#### Proof

The first identity in (42.11) is the definition of the Rosati norm
\(h^\dagger h\); taking degrees gives the second.

For an abelian subvariety of a principally polarized variety,

\[
                   \ker\lambda_A=A\cap A^\perp.
\]

Here \(\dim J(C)=2M+1\) and \(\dim A=15\), so
\(\dim A^\perp=2M-14\).  A finite subgroup of an abelian variety of
dimension \(2M-14\) has an etale \(\ell\)-primary part requiring at most
\(2(2M-14)=4M-28\) generators.  This proves (42.12).

Let \(B=c^*J(X)\).  The map \(c^*:J(X)\to B\) has kernel \(K_c\), and

\[
                       c_*c^*=[M].                       \tag{42.14}
\]

Thus the pullback to \(J(X)\) of the polarization induced on \(B\) is
\(M\lambda_X\).  Since \(g(X)=3\), taking degrees gives

\[
                  \kappa^2\deg\lambda_B=M^6.
\]

The complementary subvarieties \(B\) and \(P_c\) in the principally
polarized variety \(J(C)\) have anti-isometric polarization kernels.
Consequently \(\deg\lambda_{P_c}=\deg\lambda_B\), proving (42.13), and
the kernel needs at most \(2\dim B=6\) generators.

The final warning follows already from (42.11): it controls the pullback
polarization on \(J\), not the induced polarization on its quotient
\(A=J/\ker\varpi\).  Quotienting by an isotropic subgroup of
\(\ker s\) removes two elementary generators for every generator of the
subgroup.  The following construction shows that this freedom is large
enough in the extremal case \(M=9\). \(\square\)

## 3. An exact polarized compatibility model at \(M=9\)

This section works in the category of polarized abelian varieties over
\(k\).  It does not assert that the principally polarized variety
constructed below is a Jacobian.

Choose a direct summand of the underlying symplectic module

\[
                       H_0\simeq(\mathbf Z/9)^{13}
                              \subset J[9]
\tag{42.15}
\]

which is isotropic for the commutator pairing of \(9\lambda_J\).  The
polarization \(9\lambda_J\) descends through

\[
                     \varpi:J\longrightarrow A=J/H_0
\]

to a polarization \(\lambda_A\) satisfying

\[
        \varpi^*\lambda_A=9\lambda_J,
 \qquad
        \operatorname{type}(\lambda_A)=(1^{13},9,9).    \tag{42.16}
\]

Indeed,
\(\ker\lambda_A=H_0^\perp/H_0\simeq(\mathbf Z/9)^4\).

Give \(J(X)\) the polarization \(9\lambda_X\), of type
\((9,9,9)\), and choose an elliptic curve \(E_0\) with polarization
\(9\lambda_{E_0}\), of type \((9)\).  The two symplectic modules

\[
 \ker(9\lambda_X)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \ker\lambda_A\oplus\ker(9\lambda_{E_0})
\]

are both isomorphic to \((\mathbf Z/9)^6\).  Choose an anti-isometry
between them.  Quotient

\[
          A\times J(X)\times E_0
\]

by the graph of that anti-isometry.  The product polarization descends to
a principal polarization on a nineteen-dimensional abelian variety
\(B\).  Because the graph meets each factor trivially, all three factors
embed in \(B\), with their displayed induced polarizations, and the
images of \(A\) and \(J(X)\) are orthogonal.

Set

\[
 h=i_A\varpi:J\longrightarrow B,
 \qquad
 u=i_X:J(X)\longrightarrow B.
\]

Then

\[
             h^\dagger h=[9],\qquad
             u^\dagger u=[9],\qquad
             u^\dagger h=0.                             \tag{42.17}
\]

Moreover, the connected orthogonal complement of \(u(J(X))\) has
dimension sixteen and contains \(A\).  These are exactly the dimension,
norm, orthogonality, and Prym-polarization requirements imposed on
\(J(C)\) by a putative diamond with \(M=9\).

Thus the fact that the Prym has only one dimension left after accommodating
the fifteen-fold \(J\) is not a contradiction: the large kernel of
\(h\) changes the induced polarization on its image from type
\((9^{15})\) to type \((1^{13},9,9)\), which fits exactly.

## 4. Compatibility with the order-seven Honda module

The preceding value \(s=[9]\) also satisfies all rational order-seven
norm identities in \(\operatorname{End}^0(J)\); the noncommutative part
of the Honda algebra creates no extra condition.

Let

\[
                         W=J^7
\]

with product polarization \(\operatorname{diag}(9\lambda_J)\), let
\(T\) cyclically permute the seven factors, and let
\(e:J\to W\) be the first coordinate inclusion.  Then

\[
 e^\dagger e=[9],\qquad
 e^\dagger T^je=0\quad(1\leq j\leq6),
 \qquad
 e^\dagger(1+T+\cdots+T^6)e=[9].                       \tag{42.18}
\]

Let \(\Delta:J\to W\) be the diagonal map.  In the rational polarized
isogeny category define

\[
                    P^*=\Delta\varpi^{-1}:A\longrightarrow W,
 \qquad P_*=(P^*)^\dagger .
\tag{42.19}
\]

Using \(\varpi^\dagger\varpi=[9]\), one obtains

\[
                       P_*P^*=[7],
 \qquad
                       P_*e=\varpi,                     \tag{42.20}
\]

and \(P^*P_*=1+T+\cdots+T^6\).  Hence (42.18)--(42.20)
simultaneously realize

\[
 a_*a^*=[9],\qquad p_*p^*=[7],\qquad
 p^*p_*=\sum T^j,qquad h=p_*a^*,qquad h^\dagger h=[9].
\]

The module \(W\) has dimension \(105\), invariant dimension \(15\), and
primitive dimension \(90\).  Adding a fixed four-fold and a pure
\(\Phi_7\)-module of abelian dimension eighteen gives total dimension
\(127\), invariant dimension \(19\), and primitive dimension \(108\),
which are precisely

\[
                g(V),\quad g(C),\quad g(V)-g(C)
\]

when \(M=9\).  Such pure polarized \(\Phi_7\)-modules exist, for example
from products of tame genus-three Jacobians with an order-seven action
and genus-zero quotient.

Finally, in (42.18) all six cross endomorphisms vanish.  Formula (42.9)
would then require

\[
                           I_j=2M=18
                  \qquad(1\leq j\leq6),                 \tag{42.21}
\]

so that (42.7) reads \(270=378-108\).  Thus even the exact bidegree and
coincidence-trace constraints are numerically compatible.

## 5. Consequence for the proof strategy

The strongest unconditional conclusion of the Rosati calculation is the
strict positive decomposition

\[
                   [7M]=s+([7M]-s)                       \tag{42.22}
\]

together with (42.7), (42.11), and (42.13).  None forces a divisibility
condition on \(M\).  The model at \(M=9\) satisfies every one of these
relations while also attaining the lower endpoint from file 40.

Accordingly, an improvement of \(M\geq9\) cannot follow from the Honda
division algebra, Rosati positivity, polarization types, and bidegrees
alone.  It must distinguish Jacobians from general principally polarized
abelian varieties, distinguish homomorphisms induced by actual maps of
curves from abstract homomorphisms, or use the integral \(7\)-adic lattice
specific to a connected cyclic etale cover.
