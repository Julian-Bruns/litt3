# Two remaining profiles in the trace-zero degree-ten sector

Version1, 25 September2026. Use the actual problem and conventions of
[the trace-zero theorem](admissible_degree_ten_trace_zero.md), so a_1=0,
a_2!=0 and all divisor and everywhere-etale conditions are retained.
Every quadratic polynomial v is excluded over the algebraic closure.
The only surviving possibilities are:

| v, up to nonzero scalar | positive G-multiplicities above O | large b-poles above O |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | (4,4,2) | (6,6,4,2,2) |
| x-r, P(r)=0 | (3,3,1) | (5,5,3,2,2) |

In the linear case the finite G-partition above (r,0) is (1,1,1).
For d=deg v, pole(a_3)=16-3d and pole(a_4)<=17-3d.
The exact forced third coefficient is certified on the whole
trace-zero linear kernel, before restricting v to be polynomial.

If the required rho with div(rho)=E-E^c exists, then
\[
k(S)=k(X)(\rho)=k(x,\rho).
\]
Thus it cannot come from an intermediate quadratic cover.

For each support, the survivors lie in eleven affine seven-dimensional
coefficient families: one constant and ten linear. Everywhere-etaleness
requires a specified residual polynomial R_0 of degree at most144 to
be a nonzero square. Each family has a nonempty dense open subset on
which R_0 has degree144 and is squarefree, so that subset is excluded.
The exceptional closed square loci remain open problems. A square
norm alone would still not prove etaleness or the nontrivial order-five
class. The a_1!=0 cubic-derivative sector is outside this statement.
