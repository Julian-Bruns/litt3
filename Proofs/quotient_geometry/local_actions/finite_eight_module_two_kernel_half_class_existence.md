This proves the [statement](../../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/local_actions/finite_eight_module_two_kernel_half_class_existence.md).

No source-geometry result is a dependency. The uses of central closure below
are finite-group arguments with their primary input stated explicitly.

## Exact finite input and result

Let $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5$, let
$H_0=\operatorname{SL}_8(\mathbf F_5)$, let $Z_0=\mu_4$ be its scalar
center, and let $Q=H_0/Z_0$. Use the accepted two generators $A,B$ with
\[A^5=B^5=1,\qquad \langle A,B\rangle=H_0,
\qquad (AB)^2=3I_8.\]
Their projective images have exact orders $5,5$, their product has
exact order two, and the image of $B$ is conjugate to the image of
$A^{-1}$ inside $Q$. These are settled finite-tuple inputs; they were
not recomputed. Write $\alpha\in H^2(Q,k^*)$ for the natural multiplier,
of exact order four.

For the last kernel restrictions, use the accepted PRIMARY input that
$H_0$ is centrally closed. This is Steinberg's
[§7 Corollary 2 after Theorem 14](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/steinberg/steinberg-yale-notes.pdf#page=102),
printed page 95, PDF index 101. Elementary commutators give perfectness
of $H_0$; central closure and its central quotient by $\mu_4$ then give
$H^2(Q,k^*)=\operatorname{Hom}(\mu_4,k^*)$, of exponent four.
No full multiplier table or classification is used. The finite
construction and half-class do not require a new group-classification
input.

**Constructive existence theorem.** There are a finite group $G$, a
surjection $G\to Q$ with finite two-group kernel $N$, and generators
$a,b$ of $G$ such that:

- $a,b$ have exact order five, and $c=(ab)^{-1}$ has exact order two;
- $b$ is conjugate to $a^{-1}$ INSIDE $G$;
- the inflated natural multiplier still has exact order four, and there
  is $\beta\in H^2(G,k^*)$ of exact order eight satisfying
  $2\beta=-\operatorname{inf}\alpha$;
- the original labeled pullback
  $H=G\times_Q H_0$ is generated by its unique order-five lifts;
- there is a nonsplit central extension
  $1\to\mu_2\to\overline E\to H\to1$, also generated by those
  order-five lifts, and
  $1\to\mu_8\to\overline E\to G\to1$ represents the half-class;
- $N$ is nontrivial, even and noncentral, and the extension $G\to Q$
  is nonsplit.

Since $8\beta=0$, the same constructed class also satisfies
$10\beta=-\operatorname{inf}\alpha$. The optional refinement below
permits $N$ to be elementary abelian of exponent two. In particular a
group-only obstruction excluding ALL finite two-kernels is false for
these stated data.

## The auxiliary triangle group has a full central eight-cover

Put
\[\Delta=\langle x,y\mid x^5=y^5=(xy)^2=1\rangle.\]
This is the orientation-preserving hyperbolic triangle group of signature
$(5,5,2)$, realized faithfully in $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf R)$.
Let $\widetilde P\to\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf R)$ be the universal
cover. Its kernel is an infinite cyclic center $\langle z_0\rangle$.
Form the connected eightfold cover
$P_8=\widetilde P/\langle z_0^8\rangle$, with central kernel $C_8$.

Choose lifts of $x,y$ to $P_8$. Their fifth powers are central. Fifth
powers are an automorphism of $C_8$, so scalar multiplication gives
unique lifts $\widehat a,\widehat b$ of exact order five.
Set $z=(\widehat a\widehat b)^2$. It is central because $xy$ is an
involution in $\Delta$.

Here is an explicit proof that $z$ has order EIGHT, without a sign choice
in an orbifold lift presentation. There is a quotient
$P_8\to\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbf R)$ over
$\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbf R)$, obtained by further dividing the
universal center by $\langle z_0^2\rangle$. Let $M$ be the image of
$\widehat a\widehat b$. Its projective image is a nontrivial involution.
Thus $M^2$ is scalar. It cannot equal $+I$: a real determinant-one
matrix with $M^2=I$ is diagonalizable with eigenvalues $\pm1$, and
determinant one forces it to be $I$ or $-I$, both projectively trivial.
Consequently $M^2=-I$. The image of $z$ in the central $C_2$ is nontrivial,
so $z$ is an ODD power of $z_0$ modulo eight, and has order eight.

Let $E=\langle\widehat a,\widehat b\rangle\subset P_8$. It contains
the full $C_8=\langle z\rangle$ and maps onto $\Delta$, so it is the
full preimage of $\Delta$. It has the exact presentation
\[E=\langle \widehat a,\widehat b,z\mid
\widehat a^5=\widehat b^5=1,\quad
(\widehat a\widehat b)^2=z,\quad z^8=1,\quad z\text{ central}\rangle.\]
To check exactness, the presented group modulo its central $z$ is
$\Delta$, so its kernel over $\Delta$ is cyclic of order at most eight.
Its evident map to the realized $E$ sends $z$ to an element of exact
order eight. This map is therefore injective on the kernel and induces
the identity on the quotient; it is an isomorphism.

The triangle-group facts used here are the presentation and hyperbolic
realization, together with the fact that every torsion element is conjugate
to a power of one of the three vertex generators. These are given in
Milnor's original [triangle-group discussion, §2](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf#page=4),
especially Corollary 2.5 and Remark 2.6. His
[§3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf#page=11)
identifies the full inverse image in the universal cover and its cyclic
center. The finite eight-cover argument above is explicit.

## A surface kernel and a finite two-quotient detecting the center

The finite tuple defines a surjection
\[f:E\longrightarrow H_0,\qquad
\widehat a\mapsto A,\quad\widehat b\mapsto B,\quad z\mapsto3I_8.\]
All presentation relations hold. Modulo centers this gives
$\Delta\to Q$. Put
\[K=\ker(\Delta\to Q),\qquad R=\ker(f),\qquad t=z^4.\]
The exact orders of the three images $5,5,2$ imply that $K$ is torsion
free. Its action on the hyperbolic plane is free and cocompact; thus
$K$ is the fundamental group of a closed orientable surface of genus
$g\ge2$. The triangle area, or orbifold Euler characteristic, gives
\[2g-2=|Q|/10.\]
This auxiliary real surface is used to construct finite abstract groups,
and is not a claimed characteristic-five source or carrier.

The restriction $R\to K$ is surjective. For a lift in $E$ of an element
of $K$, its matrix image is scalar in $Z_0$. A suitable power of $z$
cancels that image. Also
$R\cap\langle z\rangle=\langle z^4\rangle=\langle t\rangle$.
Hence
\[1\longrightarrow\langle t\rangle\simeq C_2
\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow1\]
is a central extension.

Choose a standard surface presentation
$K=\langle u_i,v_i\mid\prod_{i=1}^g[u_i,v_i]=1\rangle$ and lifts
$U_i,V_i$ in $R$. For some $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}$, $R$ has presentation
\[R=\langle U_i,V_i,t\mid t^2=1,\quad t\text{ central},
\quad \prod_{i=1}^g[U_i,V_i]=t^\epsilon\rangle.\]
As above, exactness follows from the quotient presentation for $K$ and
the nontrivial known kernel $\langle t\rangle$.

If $\epsilon=0$, send $t$ to the generator of $C_2$ and all $U_i,V_i$
to the identity. If $\epsilon=1$, use
$D_8=\langle r,s\mid r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle$, send
$t\mapsto r^2$, $U_1\mapsto r$, $V_1\mapsto s$, and all other handle
generators to the identity. Since $[r,s]=r^2$, every relation holds.
In either case there is a homomorphism $R\to P$ to a finite two-group
$P$ of order at most eight with $t$ nontrivial. Write $S$ for its kernel.

The subgroup $S$ is normal in $R$ and of two-power index. There are
only finitely many conjugates of $S$ under $E$, since $E/R=H_0$ is
finite. Form the exact finite intersection
\[S_* =\bigcap_{e\in E/R}eSe^{-1}.\]
The notation means one representative of each coset; it is independent
of the choices since $S$ is normal in $R$. This is normal in $E$.
The group $R/S_*$ embeds in a finite direct product of copies of $P$,
so it is a finite two-group. Because $t$ is central in $E$ and is outside
$S$, it is outside every conjugate and outside $S_*$.

Consequently
\[\overline E=E/S_*\]
is finite, still maps onto $H_0$, and retains $z$ of exact order eight:
an element of order dividing eight whose fourth power is nontrivial has
order eight. Its two order-five generators still generate the group.
This gives a finite construction with
$|R/S_*|\le8^{|H_0|}$, without any appeal to a general residual-finiteness
theorem.

## Original quotient, labeled pullback and exact half-class

Let $Z=\langle z\rangle\subset\overline E$ and define
\[G=\overline E/Z,\qquad H=\overline E/\langle z^4\rangle.\]
The quotient map $G\to Q$ has kernel
\[N=(\overline R Z)/Z\simeq\overline R/\langle z^4\rangle,\qquad
\overline R=R/S_*.\]
Thus its kernel is a finite two-group. The images $a,b$ generate $G$,
have exact order five because their projective images do, and satisfy
$(ab)^2=1$. Their product projects to the specified nontrivial
involution in $Q$, so $c=(ab)^{-1}$ has exact order two.

The map from $H$ to $G\times_Q H_0$ records the quotient label and the
matrix image. If an element is in its kernel, its representative lies
in $Z$ and has trivial matrix image, hence belongs to
$\langle z^4\rangle$. The map is injective. Both finite groups have
order $4|G|$, so it is an isomorphism. This proves the exact ORIGINAL
LABELED identification, rather than identifying $H$ with its matrix image.
It also proves that the labeled pullback is generated by the two unique
order-five lifts.

Every character $\overline E\to k^*$ is trivial: its two generators
have order five, and $k^*$ has no nontrivial element of order five.
For the central extension $Z\to\overline E\to G$, transgression from
$\operatorname{Hom}(Z,k^*)$ is therefore injective.
Choose a faithful character $\theta:Z\to\mu_8(k)$ with
\[\theta(z)^2=3^{-1}.\]
This is possible and faithful because $3$ has exact order four.
Let $\beta$ be its transgression. Then $\beta$ has exact order eight.
The matrix scalar character on $Z$ is $z\mapsto3$, whose transgression
is the inflation of the natural multiplier, with the same convention.
Thus
\[2\beta=-\operatorname{inf}\alpha.\]
The injected character $z\mapsto3$ has order four, so the inflated
multiplier retains its exact order four. The resulting central double
$\overline E\to H$ is nonsplit: a split product $C_2\times H$ cannot
be generated by elements of order five. Hence $\overline E$ supplies
exactly the required abstract $H_L$.

## Internal inverse conjugacy and escaping-kernel restrictions

Choose a conjugator in $Q$ between the images of $a^{-1}$ and $b$,
and lift it to $G$. Its conjugate of $a^{-1}$ and $b$ are two
order-five lifts of the SAME element of $Q$. In the preimage of that
cyclic subgroup, their cyclic groups are complements to the normal
two-group $N$. Schur–Zassenhaus conjugacy, with the solvable cyclic
quotient, conjugates these complements by an element of $N$. Since
conjugation by $N$ preserves the original quotient label, it conjugates
the element itself to $b$, not to an unspecified power of $b$.
Therefore $b$ is conjugate to $a^{-1}$ INSIDE $G$.

The accepted group arguments now show $N$ is nontrivial and noncentral.
If the labeled matrix kernel were odd, its preimage in $\overline E$
would have a unique normal odd complement; dividing by it would give
a central double of the centrally closed $H_0$, impossible for an
order-five-generated group. If $N$ were central, the labeled matrix
kernel would be central by the labeled-pair commutator argument;
central closure would split the corresponding extension, forcing its
central factor to be generated by elements of order five. A two-group
cannot be such a nontrivial factor. The resulting trivial-kernel case
would again contradict the compulsory double.

Finally $G\to Q$ cannot split. A section would restrict the equation
$2\beta=-\operatorname{inf}\alpha$ to a half-class for $\alpha$ on
$Q$, whereas $H^2(Q,k^*)$ has exponent four. Twice any such class has
order at most two and cannot equal an exact order-four class.
Thus this finite two-kernel extension is even, noncentral and nonsplit,
as required.

## Separate refinement: an elementary abelian two-kernel

This refinement is not needed for the finite-two-kernel theorem.
It replaces the possible $D_8$ quotient by $C_2$ for this exact $Q$.

Let $\widetilde R$ be the preimage of $R$ in the full inverse image of
$\Delta$ in $\widetilde P$. Its kernel over $K$ is
$\langle z_0^4\rangle$: the primitive-eight generator $z$ is an odd
power of $z_0$ modulo eight and maps to a scalar of order four in $H_0$.
The full inverse image of $K$ in $\widetilde P$ is the fundamental
group of the unit tangent bundle of the hyperbolic surface of genus $g$.
Its central relation for any lifted standard handle generators is
\[\prod_{i=1}^g[U_i,V_i]=z_0^{\pm(2g-2)}.\]
The sign depends on the central orientation and is irrelevant here.
This follows from the Euler number $\pm(2g-2)$ of the oriented tangent
circle bundle. The identification with the universal-cover preimage
and its canonical Euler class is also stated in the primary
[Stover–Toledo paper, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.12772#page=16).

The order formula
\[|Q|=\frac{5^{28}}4\prod_{i=2}^8(5^i-1)\]
shows $80\mid|Q|$: already the factors $5^2-1=24$ and $5^4-1=624$,
after division by four, retain more than the required factor sixteen,
and $5^{28}$ supplies the factor five. Hence
$8\mid(2g-2)=|Q|/10$.
The lifted handles can be chosen in $\widetilde R$ since it maps onto
$K$, and changing a lift by a central element leaves its commutators
unchanged. Dividing by $\langle z_0^8\rangle$ makes the displayed
relation trivial. Thus the central $C_2$ extension $R\to K$ has
$\epsilon=0$ and splits.

Use only its $C_2$ character detecting $t$ in the core construction.
Then $\overline R$ embeds in a product of copies of $C_2$ and is
elementary abelian. So is
$N=\overline R/\langle t\rangle$. Internal inverse conjugacy can
then be verified directly: if two order-five lifts differ by $n\in N$
and $T$ is their order-five conjugation action on this vector space,
their fifth-power relation says
$(1+T+T^2+T^3+T^4)n=0$. Coprime averaging gives
$n\in\operatorname{im}(1-T)$, exactly the condition for conjugacy
by an element of $N$.

## Exact remaining limitation

The construction gives a concrete finite quotient recipe, with all
relations and central orders proved. It does not calculate a small
matrix model, the kernel's exact dimension, or a minimal extension;
none is needed for the existence assertion and no such bounded
computation is claimed. The main finite-two-kernel conclusion is
independent of the elementary-abelian refinement.

No weak cyclic characteristic-five curve with this group is constructed.
No invariant original $L$ with its canonical identity, actual section
module, selected carrier, or either finite étale endpoint map is
constructed. In particular the auxiliary hyperbolic surface and its
topological covers do not replace the original geometric problem.
BOTH original finite étale maps from the SAME smooth projective source
remain necessary geometric inputs. The unmarked common-cover problem
remains UNSOLVED; the new output is that this finite-group bottleneck
cannot be closed using only the stated half-class and tuple relations.
