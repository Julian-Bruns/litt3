## 1. The full-orbit field already is the Abel-image field

Use file 81's cyclic etale diamond

\[
 V\xrightarrow[p]{r}C\xrightarrow[c]{M}X,
 \qquad a:V\xrightarrow{M}Y,
 \qquad Y:\ z^2=f(t),
\]

where the characteristic is different from two, \(r\) is an odd prime
different from the characteristic, \(a\beta\ne a\), and
\(g(Y)=rs+1\). Put \(F=k(C)\), and let \(D\subset F\) be the field
generated by the coefficients of both

\[
 P(T)=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(T-t_i),\qquad Q(t_i)=z_i,
 \qquad \deg Q<r.
\]

Here \((t_i,z_i)=a(\beta^iv)\). The norm divisor gives a morphism

\[
 \phi:C\longrightarrow Y^{(r)},\qquad
 c\longmapsto \sum_{v\in p^{-1}(c)}a(v).
\]

**Identification.** The normalized image of \(\phi\), and the
normalized image of its Abel map to \(\operatorname{Pic}^r(Y)\), both
have function field \(D\). The map from \(C\) to either normalization
has the separable degree \(m=[F:D]\) of file 81.

**Proof.** Generically the \(t_i\) are distinct. The unordered divisor
then determines \(P,Q\) by symmetric functions and interpolation.
Conversely the equations \(P(t)=0\), \(z=Q(t)\) recover that divisor.
These are inverse rational constructions on the open locus of distinct
finite \(t\)-values, proving the first field identification.

On the further open locus away from the hyperelliptic branch points,
the first \(r\) differentials in the canonical basis

\[
 dt/z,\ t\,dt/z,\ldots,t^{g(Y)-1}dt/z
\]

evaluate at these \(r\) points with rank \(r\): their evaluation
matrix is a Vandermonde matrix multiplied by invertible diagonal
factors. Riemann--Roch therefore gives \(h^0(Y,\mathcal O_Y(E))=1\)
for the generic divisor \(E\). The Abel map has a single reduced
point as its fiber there, and its differential is injective (dually,
the same evaluation map is surjective). Thus it induces a birational,
separable map on this image. Equivalently, one can use the standard
isomorphism of the Abel map over the locus of line bundles with exactly
one effective divisor. Finally, separability of \(F/D\) is proved in
file 81. \(\square\)

In particular there is no extra generic degree between the full-orbit
curve and the Abel image. Do not describe a divisor of degree less
than the genus as "nonspecial" here: the relevant assertion is
\(h^0(E)=1\), not \(h^1(E)=0\).

### Consequence for the literature comparison

For the current pair, \(r=3\), \(g(Y)=25\), file 81 already gives

\[
 N=M/m\ge3,\qquad m\mid M,\qquad m\le M/3.
\]

Thus the initially suggested literature reduction \(m\le5\) at
\(M=9\) adds nothing: the existing bound is \(m\le3\).

Debarre's Theorem 6.6 is a genuine low-theta-degree rigidity theorem,
but not an all-degree replacement for this argument. Its proof uses
small traces of totally positive algebraic integers to force a
principal induced polarization; it does not give that conclusion
when the trace is unbounded. The orbit degree and genus bounds do not
put the Abel image in its small-trace range. The general genus bound
in Theorem 5.1 is quadratic in theta degree and likewise leaves the
large-degree regime open.

Primary source, including the proofs of Theorems 5.1 and 6.6:
[Debarre, Degrees of curves in abelian varieties](https://arxiv.org/html/alg-geom/9210005v1).

## 2. Direct coefficient zeros do not improve the full-grid budget

For the current cubic orbit, write \(Q=q_0+q_1T+q_2T^2\). Lagrange
interpolation gives

\[
 Q(T)=\sum_i z_i\frac{(T-t_j)(T-t_k)}
 {(t_i-t_j)(t_i-t_k)},\qquad \{i,j,k\}=\{0,1,2\}.
\]

At a simple finite full grid, the \(t_i\) approach three distinct
hyperelliptic branch values \(\alpha_i\), and in a parameter \(x\) on
\(C\),

\[
 t_i=\alpha_i+a_i^2x^2+O(x^3),\qquad
 z_i=b_ix+O(x^2),\qquad a_i b_i\ne0.
\]

The Vandermonde matrix is invertible at \(x=0\). Therefore
\(Q=xA(T)+O(x^2)\), where \(A(\alpha_i)=b_i\) and \(A\ne0\).
All coefficients vanish, but their common ideal has order exactly one.
Projectivizing the coefficient vector removes this common factor.

At a simple infinity incidence with only sheet zero above infinity,
\(v(t_0)=-2\) and \(v(z_0)=-51\). If the other two \(t\)-values
are finite and distinct, then

\[
 Q=\frac{z_0}{(t_0-t_1)(t_0-t_2)}(T-t_1)(T-t_2)+R(T),
 \qquad R\in k[[x]][T].
\]

The prefactor has valuation \(-47\), so \(q_2\) has a pole of exact
order 47. The same pole bound is sharp for the other two coefficients.
At an opposite-sign collision away from the branch set, with
\(v(t_1-t_2)=u\), \(z_1(0)=-z_2(0)\ne0\), and the third
\(t\)-value distinct, \(q_2\) instead has a pole of exact order \(u\).
Thus Vandermonde denominators cannot be discarded.

In the balanced simple packing from the older full-grid note, there
are \(49M/3\) full grids avoiding infinity, whereas the simple infinity
incidences alone contribute \(47M\) to the pole divisor of \(q_2\).
This test configuration already shows that the direct coefficient-zero
budget is weaker than the old \(20M\) stationary bound. It is not a
global theorem that every configuration has precisely these poles:
multiple infinity incidences and additional collisions require their
own local accounting.

## 3. The marked labels do not bound tangent orders locally

Take two formal points of \(C\) over the same point of \(X\), using
the same local parameter \(x\), and split the cubic etale torsor over
each formal disc. Choose three distinct finite branch values
\(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2\). On sheet \((\epsilon,j)\), set

\[
 t=\alpha_j+q_{\epsilon j}(x)^2,\qquad
 z=q_{\epsilon j}(x)h_j(q_{\epsilon j}(x)^2),
\]

where \(h_j(0)\ne0\), \(q_{\epsilon j}(0)=0\), and
\(q'_{\epsilon j}(0)\ne0\). These are actual etale formal maps to
the three Weierstrass points of \(Y\). The cubic norm evaluated at
\(\alpha_j\) is \(-q_{\epsilon j}^2\) times a unit. Every unit of
\(k[[x]]\) is a square here, so each marked norm is a square of
valuation two, exactly the reduced full-grid profile.

Nevertheless the two branches over the same \((x_0,P_{\alpha_j})\)
have differential ratio

\[
 q'_{2j}(0)/q'_{1j}(0),
\]

which can be any element of \(\overline{\mathbb F}_5^\times\).
Its prime-to-five order is unbounded. This is only a formal local
feasibility statement, not a connected global common cover. A useful
bound must therefore use global compatibility beyond the marked
divisors and their local square identities.
