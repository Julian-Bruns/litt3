# Proof: a Cartier jet replaces the pole-twelve support enumeration

24 September 2026. All rational functions here are actual functions
on X, not freely chosen endpoint divisors.

## The conceptual argument

By [the infinity criterion](uniform_admissible_norm.md), a Cartier-fixed
differential in omega_X(R_X) having no constant dt coefficient, where
t=x^3/y, belongs to k(x)dx. This criterion is a separate scalar lemma;
it does not require the differential to arise from an admissible cover.

Suppose g has only its pole at O, supported finite zeros as stated,
and pole order less than forty. Write uniquely
\[
g=a(x)+b(x)y+c(x)y^2.
\]
The degree bounds give deg b<=9 and deg c<=6. At every root r of P,
g(r,0)=a(r) is nonzero, since no such point is an allowed zero.
Thus gcd(a,P)=1. If dlog g lies in k(x)dx, comparison of the three
cubic characters in dg=g*dlog g yields
\[
3P(ab'-ba')+P'ab=0,\qquad
3P(ac'-ca')+2P'ac=0.
\]
Since P is squarefree and coprime to a, these force P|b and P|c.
Their degrees are less than deg P=10, so b=c=0. We have proved
\[
\deg(g)<40,\quad [t^0dt]d\log g=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad g\in k(x).
\tag{1}
\]
Cartier fixity is automatic for a logarithmic differential, and its
poles lie in R_X regardless of zero multiplicities divisible by five.

For pole degree d in {3,6,9,12,15,18}, the monomial basis of L(dO)
contains only x-powers and x-powers times y. Its leading term is a
nonzero multiple of x^(d/3). Every other term is at least two orders
less singular: d-1 is a Weierstrass gap, and the cubic character forces
the expansion of x^(d/3) to have exponent differences divisible by three.
Thus g=t^-d(u_0+O(t^2)), with u_0 nonzero. It follows that
dlog g=-d dt/t+O(t)dt has no constant coefficient, also when 5|d.
Apply (1). This proves the asserted polynomial restriction in all six
degrees, with no multiplicity-pattern calculation.

## The remaining order-ten calculation

The Weierstrass semigroup at O is generated by3 and10. An order-ten
function has form a(x)+b y, deg a<=3 and b!=0; scale b=1. Its norm
to k(x) is a^3+P. Since all zeros lie above A, this norm is
\[
S=\prod_{i=0}^3(x-\alpha_i)^{m_i},\quad m_i\ge0,\quad\sum m_i=10.
\]
There are286 integer compositions. For each, S-P has degree nine.
Normalize its leading coefficient. A monic cubic whose cube equals
the normalized polynomial is uniquely determined successively by the
coefficients of x^8,x^7,x^6, since3 is invertible. In every case the
remaining polynomial is nonzero. Leading-coefficient cube roots are
allowed over k, so this tests all geometric functions, not only
functions with coefficients in a finite field.

agree on every case in the
[exact certificate](../../../litt3-computation-data/cubic_full_return_boundary_replies_20260924/pole_ten_norm_support.json).
For provenance, the earlier independent455-case classification at
order twelve is retained in
[its data](../../../litt3-computation-data/cubic_full_return_boundary_replies_20260924/pole_twelve_norm_support.json),

It is not needed in the new proof at order twelve, fifteen or eighteen.

## Consequence for actual tensor comparisons

Use the exact normal form of the two actual maps h_1,h_2:T->X,
with div z=D_2-D_1 and D_i=h_i^*O. Cancelling their common part gives
a pole divisor of degree delta on T. Under h_1, its poles push to
delta O; its zeros push to the finite part of R_X, since the common
second-fundamental divisor contains D_2. Thus Norm_h1(z) has precisely
the supported norm form above and pole degree delta.

The earlier complete exclusions give delta!=3,6,9. The present
calculation gives delta!=10; eleven is a Weierstrass gap. Therefore
delta>=12, and delta<=deg h_i gives conditional recognition through
covering degree eleven. Polynomial norms at delta12,15,18 do not
imply that an intermediate quotient has degree three, or that z
descends. No higher-degree realization is asserted.
