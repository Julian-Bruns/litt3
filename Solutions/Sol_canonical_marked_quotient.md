# Simple-root tensors have a canonical commensurability quotient

## Main theorem

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic p>=5. Let C be smooth,
projective and connected of genus g>=2. Let s be a nonzero regular
weight-d canonical tensor, with ANY positive integer d, such that

    div(s)=d D,                    D reduced.                  (1)

There is a canonical smooth proper effective DM curve S_s and a
representable finite etale atlas q:C -> S_s with these properties.

1. The tensor descends to beta on S_s. The components of C x_(S_s) C
   are EXACTLY the normalized jointly minimal bi-etale self-images
   satisfying f^*s=g^*s. Thus the quotient controls all such
   correspondences at once, not just those generated by a chosen seed.
2. Its degree, equivalently the sum of the degrees of all those distinct
   self-images over C, satisfies

       deg(C/S_s) <= (4p/(p-4)) d (g-1).                      (2)

3. If a pair (Y,t) satisfies the same condition in weight d, then an
   actual tensor-preserving bi-etale span between (C,s) and (Y,t)
   identifies their canonical quotients (S_s,beta) and (S_t,beta_t).
   Its jointly minimal images are components of the corresponding
   fiber product over this quotient.
4. Even allowing f^*s=lambda g^*s with an arbitrary nonzero constant,
   there are only finitely many jointly minimal self-images. They are
   the components of twisted fiber products indexed by the finite group

       G=Aut(S_s,k beta).

   There may be several components for one group element. The multiplier
   character G -> k^* is injective. Hence G is cyclic
   of order prime to p. A second canonical quotient [S_s/G] governs
   all of these line-preserving self-images.

There is no Galois, ordinary, Jacobian, or finite-field hypothesis;
the weight may be divisible by p.
In particular (1), not merely the existence of a shared Tango line,
is the essential differential hypothesis here.

## 0. The canonical connection handles p-divisible weights

Suppose p divides d. On omega_C^d use the canonical Frobenius
connection and put eta=(nabla s)/s. This is a GLOBAL regular one-form:
at a point of D the local coefficient of s is x^d u, with u a unit,
and its logarithmic derivative is du/u. Changes of differential frame
contribute d-th powers, whose derivatives vanish. This construction
commutes with actual etale pullback.

If eta=0, the field identity ker(d)=k(C)^p gives a unique regular
weight-d/p tensor s_0 with s_0^p=s and div(s_0)=(d/p)D. Equality of
tensor pullbacks is unchanged by this extraction. Repeat until the
weight is prime to p or eta is nonzero.

In the latter case the rational function t=s/eta^d satisfies

    dt/t=eta != 0,                deg(t)<=d(2g-2).             (*)

Indeed, logarithmic differentiation in a differential frame proves
the identity since p|d. The function is thus nonconstant and separating.
Writing E=div(eta), effective of degree 2g-2, gives div(t)=d(D-E),
which proves the degree bound.

Every exact s-preserving self-image preserves eta and hence t. It is
a component of the generically reduced fiber product C x_(P1,t) C.
The SUM of the degrees of all such etale self-images is therefore
at most deg(t). This bounds the whole relation, not only one image.

For two fixed endpoint tensors with equal pullbacks, their eta forms
also have equal pullbacks. Either both vanish, when roots can be
extracted simultaneously, or both are nonzero and the same argument
places all the joint images in the fiber product of the two separating
functions. Thus it also proves finiteness between two different
endpoints in this branch. A fixed scalar multiplier makes no change
to the argument after rescaling one endpoint tensor.

The bound (*) uses the reduced weight if horizontal p-th powers were
first removed. Otherwise the prime-to-p contact argument below applies.
Both bounds are at most (4p/(p-4))d(g-1). No inseparable root torsor
has been treated as etale.

## 1. Finiteness supplies an actual groupoid

The [audited contact/root bound](Sol_etale_root_contact_bound.md),
with the general-characteristic constant from the
[contact theorem](Sol_contact_degree_bound.md),
bounds each normalized self-image preserving s by the right side of (2)
when p does not divide its weight. Section0 handles all other weights.
A fixed hyperbolic curve has finitely many finite etale covers of bounded
degree. For each such source W, its maps to C of this degree form a finite
set: the bounded-degree Hom scheme is of finite type, and its tangent
space H^0(W,h^*T_C) vanishes. Therefore the set F_s of these joint images
is finite. The same finiteness holds between two fixed endpoint pairs
with equal tensors, or with one tensor rescaled by a fixed constant.

The set F_s contains the diagonal and is closed under transpose and
normalized composition. For composition, both pullbacks agree with the
intermediate tensor; after joint minimalization their equality persists
by injectivity of separable tensor pullback. Both new endpoint maps
remain etale, since intermediate extensions of unramified curve
extensions are unramified.

Apply the [finite-groupoid theorem](Sol_finite_correspondence_groupoid.md).
It produces S_s and q with exactly the specified self-relation. The
equality of the two tensor pullbacks is effective descent data, so s
descends to beta. The same reasoning descends D to a reduced Cartier
divisor D_S on S_s, and div(beta)=d D_S.

In particular any effective orbifold atlas C -> T to which s descends
factors canonically as C -> T -> S_s. Its self-relation is a subunion
of F_s: effectivity identifies the relation with the normalization of
its reduced image in C x C, also in wild characteristic.

## 2. The bound is on the WHOLE relation

The nonhorizontal case of Section0 already bounds the whole relation.
Horizontal p-th powers do not change it. It remains to prove the bound
when p does not divide d.

Write n=deg(C/S_s) and L=omega_(S_s)(-D_S). The tensor beta trivializes
L^d. Take its finite etale mu_d root torsor and a connected component
T -> S_s of degree h, with h dividing d. Choose a connected component
C' of C x_(S_s) T, and put c=deg(C'/C). Then

    deg(C'/T)=n c/h,                  g(C')-1=c(g-1).          (3)

The tautological one-form on T pulls back to a form on C' having only
simple zeros. The stack T is still effective. On its coarse curve the
tautological form is a rational differential, so the general atlas
contact bound, proved using the whole reduced self-fiber product in
[the contact theorem's atlas corollary](Sol_contact_degree_bound.md),
applies:

    n c/h <= (4p/(p-4)) c(g-1).

Cancel c and use h|d. This proves (2). Disconnected root torsors cause
no problem; (3) records the component degrees instead of assuming
connectedness or making incompatible root choices.

## 3. Different endpoints have the same quotient

Given one tensor-preserving span between (C,s) and (Y,t), take on
C disjoint_union Y ALL normalized tensor-preserving images between
each ordered pair of its components. Each of the four sets is finite
by Section1. Together they form a composition-closed groupoid. Its
quotient is connected, since the given cross-correspondence is
surjective onto both endpoints.

Each endpoint alone is a surjective atlas of this quotient. Its
self-relation is respectively F_s or F_t. Thus the quotient is both
S_s and S_t, with the descended tensors identified. The cross-relation
is precisely C x_S Y. Its components have distinct joint images,
because generic inertia is trivial. This proves assertion3 and gives
a direct structural strengthening of the earlier single-span core
theorem, without a choice of a Galois leg.

## 4. Preservation up to a scalar is only a finite cyclic extension

For a fixed lambda for which a span f^*s=lambda g^*s exists, apply
Section3 to the two copies (C,s) and (C,lambda s). Multiplying s by
a nonzero constant does not change its exact self-relation. Their
canonical quotients are therefore the same underlying S_s. The
result is an automorphism carrying beta to a scalar multiple of beta,
with multiplier lambda or lambda^(-1), according to endpoint order.
The corresponding cross-images are exactly the components of the
fiber product formed using this automorphism.

Conversely, any such automorphism gives these actual bi-etale
fiber-product correspondences. Distinct multiplier-preserving
automorphisms cannot remain invisible: if phi^*beta=beta, every
component of C x_(S_s,phi) C belongs to F_s. Its quotient relation
already identifies the two endpoints, so phi is the identity.
Equivalently compare the exact two-object quotient in Section3. To
justify the generic-to-global step explicitly, the Isom space between
two orbifold automorphisms is finite. A generic isomorphism extends
uniquely over each normal curve atlas by properness and the valuative
criterion; these extensions agree on overlaps. Thus generic identity
of an automorphism of this effective smooth orbifold implies identity.

Thus the multiplier homomorphism on G is injective. The finite-groupoid
note proves Aut(S_s) finite for any effective orbifold with a
hyperbolic curve atlas, so G is finite. A finite subgroup of k^* is
cyclic of order prime to p. Quotienting by G produces an effective
proper smooth DM curve: nonidentity automorphisms act nontrivially
on the coarse function field, since a generically trivial automorphism
of an effective smooth orbifold is trivial. Its self-relation over C
is the union of the twisted fiber products just described.

## 5. Stability under the operations used in the investigation

These are identities of the canonical quotients, not only persistence
of a yes/no core conclusion.

**Etale endpoint refinements.** If a:C' -> C is any connected finite
etale cover and s'=a^*s, then

    (S_(s'),beta_(s')) = (S_s,beta).

Apply Section3 to the actual span C <- C' -> C' with its second leg
the identity. In particular, arbitrarily large or non-Galois endpoint
refinements do not change the canonical object.

**Taking powers.** Let G=Aut(S_s,k beta), of order m. For ANY r>=1,
including p-divisible r, the exact self-images preserving s^r form
the finite groupoid whose quotient is

    S_(s^r) = [S_s/G[r]],        |G[r]|=gcd(m,r).                (4)

To prove this without applying the root bound in a p-divisible weight,
the equality (f^*s)^r=(g^*s)^r makes the rational ratio of the two
nonzero tensors an r-th root of unity, hence a constant. Its multiplier
belongs exactly to G[r]. Section4 then identifies the full relation
and its quotient. Conversely every such multiplier fixes s^r.
In particular S_(s^(p^a))=S_s for every a>=0.

**Forgetting the chosen scalar and power.** Define

    S_[s]=[S_s/G].

This quotient is unchanged by multiplying s by a nonzero constant,
by taking ANY positive power, and by connected finite etale endpoint
refinement. The reason is the exact equivalence

    f^*(s^r) is a constant multiple of g^*(s^r)
       iff f^*s is a constant multiple of g^*s.

The refinement assertion follows either from Section3 or by composing
the actual etale maps before taking their normalized joint image.
Consequently, if alpha is an actual tensor root of a^*s on an etale
cover, S_[alpha]=S_[s] as well. Roots on arbitrarily RAMIFIED covers
are not included in this assertion.

Thus the line-preserving quotient is a stable invariant of this
tensor-decorated etale commensurability class. The exact quotient records
only an additional, explicitly finite cyclic multiplier choice.

## 6. A terminating closure procedure and a genuine Galois envelope

Put B=floor((4p/(p-4))d(g-1)). Given any finite list of actual
s-preserving self-spans, first replace each source by its normalized
joint image, discard duplicates, and add the diagonal and transposes.
Repeatedly form connected fiber-product components, normalize their
joint images, and add any new images.

Every intermediate list is contained in F_s. The SUM of the endpoint
degrees of all images that can ever be added is at most B, by (2).
Thus if the initial sum is b_0, there are at most B-b_0 successful
single-image additions. Equivalently the procedure terminates after
at most B rounds in which a new image appears. A round with no new
image certifies closure: the finite-groupoid theorem then constructs
the common orbifold for the given seeds.

This is a mathematical termination certificate for closure under actual
composition. It does not require enumeration of all etale covers of C
and does not assert that the resulting quotient equals S_s when the
seeds do not generate the full tensor-preserving relation.

There is also a single connected finite etale W -> C that dominates
ALL normalized s-preserving self-images and is Galois over both copies
of C in each one. Namely, take the Galois closure of C -> S_s in the
finite-etale covering category. If n=deg(C/S_s), its monodromy is a
transitive subgroup G_0<=S_n, and

    deg(W/C)<= (n-1)! <= (B-1)! .                             (5)

The curve W is a scheme since its map to C is finite etale. Write
C=W/A. Each component of C x_(S_s) C is the quotient of W by an
intersection A intersect h A h^(-1), for some h in G_0. Consequently
W dominates it, and its two composites to C are Galois and etale.

The logical order is essential: the contact bound proves a finite
groupoid, the groupoid supplies S_s, and ONLY THEN is this Galois
closure taken. No simultaneous Galois closure of an arbitrary
coreless span has been presumed. For line-preserving self-images
the same construction uses S_[s], with finite atlas degree n|G|;
the sharper bound (5) concerns exact tensor preservation.

## 7. A weight-free formulation over an algebraic closure of a finite field

Let k=Fbar_p with p>=5, and let D be ANY reduced effective divisor of
degree 2g(C)-2. Then omega_C(-D) has finite order: its degree-zero
Jacobian point is defined over a finite field. If its order is d,
a trivialization of omega_C^d(-dD) gives a regular tensor s with
div(s)=dD. Section0 permits p-primary factors in d.

Preserving D under a self-span is equivalent to preserving the tensor
line k s: the quotient of the two tensor pullbacks has divisor zero
and is constant. Consequently the canonical orbifold

    S_(C,D)=S_[s]

governs ALL finite bi-etale self-correspondences preserving D. It is
independent of the tensor, trivialization, weight, and etale endpoint
refinement. For independence of the weight, take a common power of
two chosen tensors; their divisors agree, so they differ by a constant,
and Section5 applies.

For two marked curves (C,D_C),(Y,D_Y) with
deg D_C=2g(C)-2 and deg D_Y=2g(Y)-2, choose a common multiple of their
two torsion orders. If an actual bi-etale span pulls these reduced
divisors back to the SAME divisor, its two tensor pullbacks differ by
a constant. Their canonical line quotients are therefore identified.
In particular, the span has a core.

Over a general algebraically closed field, the same conclusion holds
if the degree-zero line bundles are torsion. Reducedness is essential:
replacing D by a nonreduced divisor would incorrectly include known
coreless examples with higher-order zeros.

**Finite partners in every fixed genus.** Fix (C,D), and let S=S_(C,D).
The marking descends to a reduced divisor D_S on S. Every marked partner
(Y,D_Y) admits a representable finite etale atlas Y -> S and necessarily
D_Y is the inverse image of D_S. Conversely any such curve atlas is
a partner, by taking a connected component of C x_S Y.

For fixed genus g(Y)=h>=2 the atlas degree must be

    deg(Y/S)=(h-1)deg(C/S)/(g(C)-1).

The group pi_1(S) is finitely generated, as proved in the finite-groupoid
note. It has only finitely many open subgroups of this fixed index.
There are therefore only finitely many marked partners of genus h,
up to isomorphism. This settles the corresponding marked variant in
the negative: varying genus-h marked curves gives infinitely many
objects, only finitely many compatible with one fixed (C,D).

This conclusion concerns the SAME pulled-back marking on the common
source. An unmarked common cover need not provide it, so the marked
variant is not substituted for Litt's original problem.

## Why this is more stable than a single-correspondence statement

The conclusion is preserved when several allowed correspondences are
combined: they already lie in one finite groupoid. Non-Galois covers,
wild inertia, extra source refinements, and multiplication of the
tensor by constants have explicit places in the theorem. The result
therefore gives a canonical object on which later arguments can be
built, not only a degree cutoff for one pair of maps.

The zero hypothesis remains restrictive. A tensor with uniform zero
multiplicity e different from d is not covered. Known coreless Igusa
and Tango correspondences cannot be excluded by this theorem merely
because they preserve a line or because a root exists after some cover.
Nor does the theorem produce a shared tensor for an arbitrary common
cover. Those are still separate open gates in the Litt3 investigation.
