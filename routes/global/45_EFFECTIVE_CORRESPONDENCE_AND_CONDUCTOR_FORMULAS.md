# Effective correspondences in the seven-diamond

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved.**  This note supplies the curve-level information missing
from the polarized compatibility model of file 42.  For the actual image

\[
                  D=(p,a)(V)\subset C\times Y
\]

it computes the Neron--Severi class, self-intersection, and conductor
exactly.  These formulas introduce no new inequality: the conductor is
precisely the sum of the already known coincidence intersections.

There is nevertheless one useful effectivity obstruction.  On the
genus-fifteen hyperelliptic curve \(Y\), an effective bidegree-\((n,n)\)
correspondence with zero action on \(J(Y)\), for \(n\leq15\), is pulled
back from \(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) through the two hyperelliptic
maps.  In particular, such a correspondence cannot have odd degree.
Thus the cross-zero polarized model at \(M=9\) in file 42 cannot be the
class of an actual effective degree-nine correspondence.

Only non-breaking clarifications were suggested (the odd image-degree cases,
torsion-freeness of Hom, and the separable/different wording).

Throughout, the maps in the exact diamond are

\[
 \begin{array}{ccc}
 V&\xrightarrow{a}&Y\\
 \downarrow p&&\\[-2mm]
 C&\xrightarrow{c}&X,
 \end{array}
 \qquad
 \deg a=\deg c=M,\qquad \deg p=7,
\tag{45.1}
\]

and \(p\) is generated by the fixed-point-free automorphism \(\beta\).

## 1. The image is birational and has graph branches

### Lemma 45.1 (normalization of the image)

The map

\[
                    \nu=(p,a):V\longrightarrow C\times Y
\tag{45.2}
\]

is birational onto its reduced image \(D\).  Thus \(V\) is the
normalization of \(D\).  The two projections of the normalization are the
etale maps \(p\) and \(a\), of degrees seven and \(M\).

At every point of \(D\), all analytic branches are smooth.  Etale-locally
on \(C\), they are graphs of etale maps to \(Y\).  All singularities of
\(D\) therefore arise from intersections among distinct graph branches;
there are no singularities intrinsic to an individual branch.

#### Proof

Let \(\widetilde D\) be the normalization of \(D\), and let
\(e=\deg(V/\widetilde D)\).  Since \(p\) factors through \(\widetilde D\)
and has prime degree seven, \(e\) is either one or seven.  If \(e=7\),
then \(\widetilde D\to C\) has degree one, so the map \(a\) factors through
\(p\).  This would give \(a\beta=a\), contrary to the defining
nonvisibility property in file 38.  Hence \(e=1\).

Both \(p\) and \(a\) are etale.  An etale coordinate on \(C\) is therefore
a parameter on each point of the normalization above a given point of
\(D\), while \(a\) is an etale function of that parameter.  This gives
the asserted graph description. \(\square\)

## 2. Neron--Severi and self-intersection

Let

\[
            h=p_*a^*:J(Y)\longrightarrow J(C),
            \qquad s=h^\dagger h
\tag{45.3}
\]

as in file 42.  Write

\[
 F_C=C\times\{y_0\},\qquad F_Y=\{c_0\}\times Y.
\]

Under the standard correspondence summand in
\(\operatorname{NS}(C\times Y)\), let \(\gamma_h\) denote the class
corresponding to \(h\).

### Proposition 45.2 (the exact Neron--Severi class)

One has

\[
 [D]=7F_C+MF_Y+\gamma_h,                               \tag{45.4}
\]

where the three displayed summands are mutually orthogonal except for
\(F_C\cdot F_Y=1\), and

\[
 \gamma_h^2
  =-\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
       s\mid H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(Y,\mathbf Q_\ell)\right).
\tag{45.5}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D^2
   &=14M-\operatorname{Tr}(s\mid H^1(Y))\\
   &=\sum_{j=1}^6 I_j-28M\\
   &=2(I_1+I_2+I_3)-28M.                              \tag{45.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

#### Proof

The intersections of \(D\) with the two fiber classes are

\[
                         D\cdot F_Y=7,\qquad
                         D\cdot F_C=M,
\]

because these are the degrees of \(p\) and \(a\).  The correspondence
homomorphism represented by \(D\) is \(p_*a^*=h\), which gives (45.4).

The standard intersection pairing on the correspondence summand is the
negative of the cohomological Rosati pairing.  Applied to a class and
itself, this is (45.5).  The first equality of (45.6) follows.  Proposition
42.1 gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(s\mid H^1(Y))
      =42M-\sum_{j=1}^6 I_j.
\]

Finally \(I_j=I_{7-j}\), proving the remaining equalities. \(\square\)

## 3. The conductor is exactly the coincidence divisor

For \(1\leq j\leq6\), put

\[
 E_j=(a,a\beta^j)^*\Delta_Y,\qquad \deg E_j=I_j.
\tag{45.7}
\]

Let \(\mathfrak C_D\) be the conductor divisor on the normalization
\(V\) of the Cartier curve \(D\).

### Proposition 45.3 (local and global conductor formulas)

The conductor divisor and total delta invariant are

\[
              \mathfrak C_D=\sum_{j=1}^6E_j,
       \qquad
              \deg\mathfrak C_D=\sum_{j=1}^6 I_j,
\tag{45.8}
\]

and

\[
 \delta(D)
   =p_a(D)-g(V)
   =\frac12\sum_{j=1}^6I_j
   =I_1+I_2+I_3.                                      \tag{45.9}
\]

In particular,

\[
 p_a(D)=14M+1+I_1+I_2+I_3.                            \tag{45.10}
\]

#### Proof

Etale-locally at \(c\in C\), label the seven points of \(p^{-1}(c)\) and
write the corresponding branches of \(D\) as

\[
                         y=f_i(t).
\]

Each branch is smooth.  For a reduced plane curve which is a union of
smooth branches, its delta invariant is the sum of the pairwise local
intersection multiplicities:

\[
 \delta_c(D)=\sum_{i<k}
       \operatorname{ord}_t(f_i-f_k).                  \tag{45.11}
\]

On the \(i\)-th normalized branch, the conductor exponent is the sum of
its intersection multiplicities with all the other branches.  The divisor
\(E_j\) records the ordered intersection of branch \(i\) with branch
\(i+j\).  Thus summing the six \(E_j\)'s gives the conductor divisor on
every normalized branch.  Every unordered pair occurs twice in the
ordered sum, proving (45.8) and (45.9).

For an independent global check, the canonical class of the product has

\[
 D\cdot K_{C\times Y}
  =\deg p^*K_C+\deg a^*K_Y
  =28M+28M=56M.                                       \tag{45.12}
\]

Adjunction and (45.6) give

\[
 p_a(D)-1
  =\frac{D^2+D\cdot K_{C\times Y}}2
  =14M+\frac12\sum_jI_j.
\]

Since \(g(V)=14M+1\), this reproduces (45.9)--(45.10).
\(\square\)

The local graph model also shows why (45.9) supplies no hidden positivity
beyond \(I_j\geq0\).  Two etale graph branches may have any prescribed
positive contact order: in suitable parameters they may be

\[
                         y=t,\qquad y=t+t^r
\]

for arbitrary \(r\geq1\).  Thus smooth normalization and etaleness impose
no parity or congruence on an individual local contribution.

## 4. Zero-action effective correspondences on a hyperelliptic curve

The following statement applies to any hyperelliptic curve and is
independent of the particular seven-diamond.

### Theorem 45.4 (low-degree zero correspondences are hyperelliptic)

Let \(Y\) be a hyperelliptic curve of genus \(g\geq2\), with hyperelliptic
map

\[
                         \pi:Y\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1
\]

and hyperelliptic line bundle \(H=\pi^*\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(1)\).
Let \(\Gamma\subset Y\times Y\) be a reduced irreducible effective
Cartier divisor, finite and dominant of degree \(n\) over each factor.
Suppose its induced homomorphism

\[
                         u_\Gamma:J(Y)\longrightarrow J(Y)
\]

is zero.  If \(n\leq g\), then \(n\) is even and there is a divisor
\(\Gamma_0\) of bidegree \((n/2,n/2)\) on
\(\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1\) such that

\[
                    \Gamma=(\pi\times\pi)^*\Gamma_0
\tag{45.13}
\]

scheme-theoretically.

In particular, no such zero-action correspondence exists in odd degree
\(n\leq g\).

If, in addition, the normalization \(Z\) of \(\Gamma\) has etale
projections to both factors, then the two hyperelliptic involutions lift
to free involutions on \(Z\).  Writing \(n=2r\), quotienting by the
involution from either target factor produces an etale degree-\(r\)
cover of the other copy of \(Y\).  The remaining map of that quotient to
\(\mathbf P^1\) has degree \(2r\), is simply ramified over every one of
the \(2g+2\) hyperelliptic branch values, with fiber type \(2^r\), and is
unramified elsewhere.

#### Proof

The standard decomposition of the Picard group of a product says that a
line bundle on \(Y\times Y\) is an exterior product, up to its
correspondence component in
\(\operatorname{Hom}(J(Y),J(Y))\).  Since \(u_\Gamma=0\), there are line
bundles \(A,B\in\operatorname{Pic}^n(Y)\) with

\[
                      \mathcal O(\Gamma)\simeq A\boxtimes B.
\tag{45.14}
\]

The defining section of \(\Gamma\) lies in

\[
 H^0(Y,A)\otimes H^0(Y,B).
\]

The complete systems \(|A|\) and \(|B|\) are base-point-free.  Indeed, a
base point of \(A\) would make the entire corresponding vertical fiber a
component of \(\Gamma\), while a base point of \(B\) would make a
horizontal fiber a component.  Either conclusion contradicts
irreducibility and dominance.  In particular both spaces of sections
have dimension at least two.

We use the following elementary hyperelliptic fact.  If \(L\) is a
base-point-free line bundle of degree \(d\leq g\), then

\[
                         d=2r,\qquad L\simeq H^r.
\tag{45.15}
\]

To prove it, choose a base-point-free pencil inside \(H^0(Y,L)\), which is
possible over the infinite field \(k\).  It defines a degree-\(d\) map
\(\varphi:Y\to\mathbf P^1\).  First remove its possible inseparable part,
which matters in characteristic five.  In a coordinate on the target,
write

\[
                         \varphi=\psi^{5^e}
\]

with \(e\) maximal.  Then \(\psi:Y\to\mathbf P^1\) is separable and
\(\deg\varphi=5^e\deg\psi\).  If \(\psi\) does not factor through
\(\pi\), the degree-two extension \(k(Y)/k(\pi)\) shows that the two
maps generate \(k(Y)\).  Castelnuovo--Severi, now applied to the separable
map \(\psi\), gives

\[
                  g\leq\deg\psi-1\leq d-1,
\]

contrary to \(d\leq g\).  Hence \(\psi\), and therefore \(\varphi\),
factors through \(\pi\).  Write \(\varphi=r_0\pi\), where \(r_0\) has
degree \(r=d/2\).  Pulling back \(\mathcal O(1)\) gives (45.15).

Apply (45.15) to both \(A\) and \(B\).  Thus \(n=2r\) and

\[
                         A\simeq B\simeq H^r.
\]

Since \(r\leq g/2<g+1\), the standard decomposition

\[
 \pi_*\mathcal O_Y
    \simeq\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}
       \oplus\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(-g-1)
\]

shows that every section of \(H^r\) is pulled back from
\(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1}(r)\).  The defining section in (45.14) is
therefore the pullback of a section of
\(\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1}(r,r)\).  This proves
(45.13).

Suppose now that the normalization projections are etale.  The
involutions

\[
             \iota\times1,\qquad1\times\iota
\]

preserve (45.13), hence lift to \(Z\).  The second one is a nontrivial
deck transformation of \(Z\to Y\) through the first projection.  It is
free because that projection is etale.  Put

\[
                         Z_2=Z/\langle1\times\iota\rangle.
\]

Then \(Z_2\to Y\) through the first projection is etale of degree \(r\),
so

\[
                         g(Z_2)=r(g-1)+1.
\]

The function \(\pi\) on the second factor descends to a map

\[
                         q_2:Z_2\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1
\]

of degree \(2r\), and \(Z\) is the normalization of the pullback of the
hyperelliptic double cover along \(q_2\).  Since \(Z\to Z_2\) is etale,
the ramification index of \(q_2\) above each hyperelliptic branch value
must be even.  Above one such value, its contribution to the ramification
divisor is therefore at least \(r\): a partition of \(2r\) into even
indices has at most \(r\) parts.

Riemann--Hurwitz gives the total ramification

\[
 \deg R_{q_2}
   =2g(Z_2)-2+2\deg(q_2)
   =2r(g-1)+4r
   =2r(g+1).
\]

The \(2g+2\) hyperelliptic branch values already contribute at least
\((2g+2)r=2r(g+1)\).  Equality is forced throughout.  Thus every index is
two and there is no other ramification.  The argument with the two
factors reversed proves the symmetric assertion. \(\square\)

### Corollary 45.5 (the cross-zero \(M=9\) model is not effective)

For the particular curve \(Y\) of genus fifteen, an effective
bidegree-\((n,n)\) correspondence with zero Jacobian action and
\(n\leq15\) must have even degree and factor through the hyperelliptic
line.  In particular, an effective degree-nine correspondence cannot have
zero action on \(J(Y)\).

Hence the choice

\[
              a_*\beta^{j*}a^*=0\qquad(1\leq j\leq6)
\]

in the \(M=9\) polarized compatibility model of file 42 cannot be realized
by six actual birational image correspondences
\((a,a\beta^j)(V)\subset Y\times Y\).

If one of the maps \((a,a\beta^j)\) has generic degree \(e_j>1\) onto its
image, the same conclusion applies to the normalized image in degree
\(n=M/e_j\): the induced correspondence is still zero, because
\[
 a_*\beta^{j*}a^*
       =[e_j]\,u_{\Gamma_j}.
\]

## 5. Exact boundary of the effectivity argument

For a general effective correspondence \(\Gamma\) as in Theorem 45.4,
with normalization \(Z\) and etale projections of degree \(n\), the same
intersection calculation gives

\[
 \Gamma^2
   =2n^2-\operatorname{Tr}(u_\Gamma^\dagger u_\Gamma\mid H^1(Y)),
\tag{45.16}
\]

and

\[
 \delta(\Gamma)
   =n^2+14n
      -\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
          (u_\Gamma^\dagger u_\Gamma\mid H^1(Y)).
\tag{45.17}
\]

If \(u_\Gamma=0\), these reduce to

\[
            \Gamma^2=2n^2,\qquad
            \delta(\Gamma)=n^2+14n,\qquad
            \Gamma\cdot\Delta_Y=2n.                    \tag{45.18}
\]

Thus zero action requires a very singular image, even though its
normalization and both normalization projections are smooth and etale.
The graph-branch formula shows that these singularities are numerically
possible; it supplies no contradiction by itself.

The threshold \(n\leq g\) in Theorem 45.4 is the exact boundary of this
argument.  Starting in degree \(g+1\), a hyperelliptic curve can have
base-point-free pencils which do not factor through its hyperelliptic map,
so the Castelnuovo--Severi step no longer applies.  Consequently the
present effectivity argument does not prove that a zero-action
correspondence of arbitrary degree is visible.  What it does prove is the concrete
low-degree obstruction above, which is absent from the abstract polarized
model.
