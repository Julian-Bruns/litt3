# Proof: the invariant tensor controls the lower break and different

Version1,3 October2026. Independent whole review: [PASS](../../../Research/audits/Q0_COINCIDENT_CLOSURE_AND_CUBIC_WILD_AREA_AUDIT_2026_10_03.md). See the [statement](../../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/local_actions/cubic_tensor_small_area_tame_action.md).

At a wild point of C write the inertia order as e=wa, where w is a positive power of FIVE and a is prime to FIVE. For its lower groups put
\[
D=\sum_{j\ge1}(|I_j|-1),\qquad \delta=wa-1+D.
\]
Every nontrivial lower wild group has FIVE-power order, so FOUR divides D. A tame complement of order a exists and can be linearized in a source parameter t. If u is a quotient parameter, its power series is invariant under t→ζt. The nonzero series u′ therefore satisfies u′(ζt)ζ=u′(t). Its order is the actual different exponent δ, so ζ^(δ+ONE)=ONE. Hence a divides δ+ONE and thus a divides D. This is why the completed Galois extension, rather than only a numerical local index, is required.

Let m be the order of T at the source point. Tame invariance of its leading term t^m(dt)³ gives a dividing m+THREE. The allowed m therefore give a=ONE, except possibly (m,a)=(ZERO,THREE) or(EIGHT,ELEVEN). The invariant tensor descends generically to a meromorphic cubic on C/H: the generic differential line for a separating extension is pulled from the quotient field. If n is its integer local order at the image, exact pullback gives
\[
m+3=wa(n+3)+3D.
\]
For either exceptional pair m+THREE=a. Write D=ak. Since a is odd, FOUR divides k. The formula implies 3k−ONE=rw for a positive integer r: k is a positive multiple of FOUR. Modulo TWELVE the left side is ELEVEN. A power of FIVE is ONE orFIVE modulo TWELVE, so r is ELEVEN orSEVEN modulo TWELVE, respectively. Thus k≥(SEVEN w+ONE)/THREE, and
\[
\delta/(wa)=1-1/(wa)+k/w\ge10/3
\]
for a=THREE, with a strict inequality for a=ELEVEN.

For a=ONE, let b be the first lower break. Some element of the wild group has σ(t)=t+c t^(b+ONE)+higher terms, with c nonzero. The first change of the leading term t^m(dt)³ under this substitution has coefficient c[m+THREE(b+ONE)] at relative order b. Higher unit coefficients change only at larger relative orders. Exact invariance forces
\[
m+3(b+1)=0\pmod5.
\]
For m=minusTWO orEIGHT, b≡THREE modulo FIVE, hence b≥THREE. For m=ZERO, b≡FOUR, hence b≥FOUR. The groups I1 through Ib have order w, so D≥b(w−ONE). Therefore δ/w≥FOUR−FOUR/w≥SIXTEEN/FIVE in the first case, and δ/w≥FIVE−FIVE/w≥FOUR in the second. Every wild orbit has the claimed lower bound.

Finally Hurwitz for the actual finite Galois action gives
\[
\sum_v\delta_v/e_v=2(g(C)-1)/|H|-2g(C/H)+2.
\]
If the normalized area is smaller than SIX/FIVE, this nonnegative sum is smaller than SIXTEEN/FIVE because g(C/H)≥ZERO. Even ONE wild orbit is then impossible. The global order of H may be divisible by FIVE; tameness here means that every point stabilizer is prime to FIVE.
