# Finite-flat Dirac obstruction using duality trace

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: proved. This is a new, standalone extension of
`DIRAC_TRACE_POLARIZATION_OBSTRUCTION_2026_09_05.md`; that file is unchanged.
No ordinarity or reducedness hypothesis occurs in the theorem below.

## 1. Statement and the scheme-theoretic Dirac condition

Let k be algebraically closed, and let v:A -> B be an isogeny of abelian
varieties of dimension g >= 1. Write K=v^{-1}(0), and decompose its finite
coordinate algebra into local factors:

\[
C=H^0(K,\mathcal O_K)=\prod_{h\in K(k)}C_h.
\]

Each C_h is Artinian local Gorenstein, with residue field k. In particular
its socle Soc(C_h)=Ann_{C_h}(\mathfrak m_h) is one-dimensional. The
identity component has algebra C_0. If T is a line bundle on A, define a
**socle-Dirac section** to be s in H^0(A,T) such that its restriction to
every component K_h with h != 0 is zero, and its restriction to K_0 is a
nonzero element of

\[
\operatorname{Soc}(T|_{K_0})
=\{z\in H^0(K_0,T|_{K_0}):\mathfrak m_0z=0\}.
\]

This is intrinsic: changing a generator of the free rank-one C_0-module
T|_{K_0} multiplies by a unit and preserves its nonzero socle. When K is
reduced this is precisely the ordinary pointwise Dirac condition. When
K_0 is nonreduced, a socle-Dirac section has s(0)=0 in its ordinary point
fiber. Thus it must not be confused with a section nonzero at the reduced
identity point.

**Theorem.** Let N be a line bundle on B such that every global section
of N vanishes at 0. If R=v^*N tensor T^{-1} is generated by global sections
at the reduced point 0 of A, then T has no socle-Dirac section on K.

The proof uses the Grothendieck-duality counit, not the algebra trace.

## 2. Global duality trivialization: why it exists even for inseparable v

An isogeny is finite faithfully flat. Since A is smooth, hence
Gorenstein, v is a Gorenstein morphism and its fibers are Gorenstein.
One may also check a fiber directly: the pullbacks of a regular system
of g parameters on B form a system of parameters in the regular local
ring of A, hence a regular sequence. The resulting zero-dimensional
complete intersection is Gorenstein. For the general flat criterion see
[Stacks, Lemma 48.25.7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C02), and for
the invertible relative dualizing module see
[Stacks, Lemma 49.16.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C14).

Put E=v_*O_A. Finite flat duality identifies v_*omega_v with
Hom_{O_B}(E,O_B), with the E-module action

\[
(a\varphi)(b)=\varphi(ab).
\]

Its counit epsilon:v_*omega_v -> O_B sends phi to phi(1).
This is the elementary finite-morphism description of duality and trace;
see [Stacks, Section 48.11, especially Remark 48.11.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AWZ).

Compatibility of dualizing sheaves with composition gives

\[
\omega_A\simeq v^*\omega_B\otimes\omega_v,
\qquad
\omega_v\simeq\omega_A\otimes(v^*\omega_B)^{-1}.
\tag{1}
\]

No generic separability is needed here: apply
[Stacks, Remark 48.23.4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C10) to the
finite flat Cohen--Macaulay morphism v, of relative dimension zero.

Choose nonzero invariant top forms eta_A and eta_B. Invariant
differentials trivialize the cotangent bundles of abelian varieties, so
these forms are nowhere vanishing. In (1), the section

\[
\sigma=\eta_A\otimes(v^*\eta_B)^{-1}
\]

is therefore a nowhere-vanishing global generator of omega_v. Here
v^*eta_B is the pullback section of the *line bundle* v^*omega_B. We do
not apply the differential map v^*omega_B -> omega_A, which may be zero.
In particular, no division by a zero pulled-back differential occurs.

Multiplication by sigma defines an O_A-module isomorphism O_A -> omega_v.
Compose its pushforward with epsilon to obtain

\[
\tau_\sigma:E\longrightarrow O_B,
\qquad a\longmapsto\epsilon(a\sigma).
\tag{2}
\]

Under the Hom description, sigma itself is the functional tau_sigma;
the isomorphism induced by sigma is

\[
E\xrightarrow{\sim} E^\vee,
\qquad a\longmapsto[b\mapsto\tau_\sigma(ab)].
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently the sheaf pairing is perfect. This also proves tau_sigma
is a nonzero morphism, although tau_sigma(1) can be zero. The latter is
a statement about one global constant, and says nothing against (3).
Changing the two invariant forms rescales this construction by a
nonzero scalar. Nonvanishing conclusions are independent of that choice.
Equivalently, (2) is obtained from the usual duality map
v_*omega_A -> omega_B by these two trivializations.

## 3. Base change and nonvanishing on a single socle

There is no subtle duality base-change assumption left to check. On an
affine open Spec S of B, let D be the finite locally free S-algebra of
its inverse image. For every S-algebra S', finite projectivity gives

\[
\operatorname{Hom}_S(D,S)\otimes_SS'
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{S'}(D\otimes_SS',S').
\tag{4}
\]

Evaluation at 1 commutes with this isomorphism. The isomorphism (3)
remains an isomorphism after tensoring with S'. Applying (4) with
S'=k(0) gives the functional

\[
\lambda:C\longrightarrow k,
\qquad (a,b)\longmapsto\lambda(ab)\text{ a perfect pairing}.
\tag{5}
\]

Restricting this pairing to each product factor C_h is perfect: distinct
factors multiply to zero. Let 0 != z in Soc(C_0). If lambda(z)=0, then
for every b in C_0 we have bz=b(0)z, so lambda(bz)=0. Products with
every other factor are also zero. This contradicts perfection of (5).
Therefore

\[
\lambda(z)\ne0
\quad\text{for every nonzero socle element supported on }K_0.
\tag{6}
\]

This short argument also explains why mere set-theoretic support at 0
is insufficient: a general element of C_0 need not satisfy (6).

## 4. Proof of the theorem

Suppose s is socle-Dirac. Choose t in H^0(A,R) with t(0) != 0. On K_0
this section is a generator of its invertible module: its coefficient
in any local trivialization is a unit. Thus st restricts to a nonzero
socle element on K_0 and is zero on every other component.

The projection formula and (2) give a global section

\[
u=(\mathrm{id}_N\otimes\tau_\sigma)(st)\in H^0(B,N).
\]

Identify (v^*N)|_K canonically with N_0 tensor O_K. The base change in
(4) then says u(0)=(id_{N_0} tensor lambda)((st)|_K), which is nonzero
by (6). But all global sections of N vanish at 0. Contradiction.

## 5. Principal polarizations and the numerical obstruction

Now assume char(k)=p>0, (A,L) is principally polarized, K is a finite
flat maximal isotropic subgroup of A[p], and B=A/K has a principal
ample line bundle M with v^*M numerically equivalent to L^p. The
compatibility of the polarizations is an explicit hypothesis; the
trace theorem itself works for every isogeny.

The unique theta section of M has a nonempty divisor. Translating M
by a point on that divisor produces a principal representative M_0
whose unique theta section vanishes at 0. Also v^*M_0 is numerically
equivalent to L^p. Thus the theorem yields

\[
0\notin\operatorname{Bs}(v^*M_0\otimes T^{-1})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T\text{ has no socle-Dirac section on }K.
\tag{7}
\]

In particular, if T is numerically equivalent to L^m for an integer
1 <= m <= p-2, then its residual R is numerically equivalent to L^{p-m}
with p-m >= 2. Numerical triviality on an abelian variety equals
membership in Pic^0, so R=L^{p-m} tensor P. Every such twist is a
translate of L^{p-m}, because its polarization homomorphism is an
isogeny, surjective on algebraically closed points even if inseparable.
The powers L^n with n >= 2 are globally generated, so R is globally
generated and (7) applies. These standard polarization and power facts
are the same arbitrary-characteristic inputs as in the preceding
ordinary proof; see
[Genestier--Ngo, Theorem 2.2.3](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~ngo/Shimura.pdf).

**Conclusion:** the bound m <= p-2 excludes socle-Dirac sections without
ordinarity and without reducedness of K. For p=2 this positive range is
empty. The same argument applies whenever the residual is generated at
0, including the two-ample-factor criterion of the preceding note.

## 6. Frobenius test and the sharp boundary

Take v=F_{E/k}:E -> E^{(p)} for a supersingular elliptic curve. Its fiber
over 0 is supported at 0 and has local algebra k[x]/(x^p): in completed
parameters, the relative Frobenius is k[[y]] -> k[[x]], y -> x^p.
The k[[y]]-linear functional

\[
\psi\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}a_i(y)x^i\Bigr)=a_{p-1}(y)
\]

generates Hom_{k[[y]]}(k[[x]],k[[y]]) over k[[x]]. Indeed its pairing
matrix in the displayed basis is anti-diagonal with entries 1. On the
fiber it extracts the coefficient of x^{p-1}, so psi(x^{p-1})=1 while
psi(1)=0. Every other dualizing generator is w psi for a unit w; its
fiber value at x^{p-1} is w(0) != 0. The generator obtained from invariant
forms is of this kind. It need not be literally the coefficient
functional in an arbitrarily preselected parameter.

In contrast, multiplication by any element of k[x]/(x^p) has trace
p times its constant coefficient, hence zero. This explicitly checks
why algebra trace fails and duality trace succeeds.

The boundary m=p-1 really admits examples with nonreduced K: take
T=O_E((p-1)[0]). Its canonical divisor section, written in a regular
local frame, is a unit times x^{p-1}; its restriction to K is a nonzero
socle element. This T is ample and numerically L^{p-1}, where
L=O_E([0]), and v^*O_{E^{(p)}}([0])=O_E(p[0]). Products of these examples
give every dimension, with socle generator
product_i x_i^{p-1} in k[x_1,...,x_g]/(x_1^p,...,x_g^p). The product
Frobenius kernels are maximal isotropic for the product principal
polarization: on each elliptic factor the descended principal
polarization has pullback p times the source polarization.

## 7. The residual Theta + q^*D remains an evaluation question

Let q:A -> C be a quotient by an abelian subvariety, and set

\[
T=L^{p-1}\otimes q^*O_C(-D).
\]

For the permitted M_0 the actual residual is

\[
R=L\otimes q^*O_C(D)\otimes P_0,
\qquad P_0=v^*M_0\otimes L^{-p}\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(A).
\tag{8}
\]

If any permitted M_0 makes (8) generated at 0, socle-Dirac is impossible.
Existence therefore forces 0 to remain a base point for every permitted
M_0. Duality changes none of this quantifier or the constrained twist.
The expression Theta+q^*D alone need not imply global generation: on a
product E_1 x E_2, with q=pr_1 and the product theta line bundle,
L tensor q^*O(D) has an O_{E_2}([0]) factor, so all its sections vanish
along E_1 x {0}, even when D is ample on E_1. The exact evaluation and
fiber tests in Section 6 of the preceding note consequently carry over
unchanged. No universal exclusion at (8) or converse existence claim
is made here.

The only replacement required in the original trace argument is thus
the global dualizing generator and the intrinsic socle-Dirac condition.
Inseparability supplies no obstruction to this replacement.
