# The critical quadratic package is blind to additive annihilator translation

Version2,3 October2026. The local scalar scope below is now explicit;
the algebraic translation and all original source hypotheses are unchanged.
On an actual degree-ten admissible source,
write $K=k(X)$, $F(W)=0$, $F'=\phi D$, $U(W)=uD(W)$,
$\deg F=10$, $\deg D=3$, and $\deg U\le5$. The source polynomial
$F$ is separable. Keep formal degrees in all critical resultants.

After extending scalars to $K(z)$, put $u_z=u-z$ and $U_z=U-zD$.
The five traces
\[
\rho=\operatorname{Tr}(uW^4/\phi),\quad
m_5=\operatorname{Tr}u,\quad
\mu_j=\operatorname{Tr}(u^2W^j/\phi)\ (0\le j\le2)
\]
are unchanged. Consequently the critical quadratic $Q$ and the
formal resultants $\operatorname{Res}_{10,3}(F,D)$,
$\operatorname{Res}_{3,5}(D,U)$ and
$\operatorname{Res}_{3,2}(D,Q)$ are unchanged. The critical identity
becomes
\[
U_z^2-FQ=D(V_2-2zU+z^2D).
\]
These conclusions retain repeated critical roots and numerator degree
drops. No critical discriminant is inverted.

For a nonzero constant scalar $z\in k^\times$ on the original source,
however, $u_z$ has order zero at every old
finite selected point where $u$ had order two. This translation is
therefore not an admissible deformation. Differentiating the critical
quadratic or parity identities along it gives no reciprocal-trace
equation by itself. The full selected-divisor conditions can still
obstruct trace zero; they are not invariant under this operation.
The algebraic identities above hold for every scalar in $K(z)$, including
the independent indeterminate. The local unit conclusion on the original
source does not apply to an arbitrary nonzero rational function in $K$.
