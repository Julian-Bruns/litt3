# Every nonpolynomial supported function uses all three cubic characters

Version3,3 October2026. On the fixed genus-nine curve X:y^3=P(x),
let O be its unique infinity and Z the twelve points over A=0.
For a nonzero function regular outside O with every finite zero in Z,
write
\[
g=U(x)+V(x)y+W(x)y^2.
\]
If g is not in k(x), ALL THREE coefficients are nonzero.
Equivalently its three cubic conjugates span dimension one or three,
never two. There is no bound on pole degree, multiplicities or
geometric coefficients.

The structural input is general. Let C have genus G>=2 over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic different from three,
with cyclic cubic quotient C/gamma=P1, a totally ramified point O,
and Z a union of s unramified cubic fibres. If a noninvariant function
regular outside O, with zeros in Z, omits a cubic character, there
is such a function of pole degree
\[
\delta\le2G-2+3s.
\]
A minimal witness has disjoint conjugate zero divisors, and their
ratio is separating. If w occupied fibres have multiplicity divisible
by the positive characteristic, the bound improves by 3w.
This is a reduction on the endpoint, preserving all allowed support.

For the fixed X, G=9 and s=4 give delta<=28. The later
[exact marked relation lattice](marked_divisor_relation_lattice.md)
places the least noninvariant supported pole at1,617,894.
Thus it rules out the reduced witness and proves the all-degree
assertion. The older separate bound and bounded Hermite tests
are subsumed.

Supported three-character functions exist at high poles.
No polynomial-norm conclusion or common-cover existence/nonexistence
follows. The auxiliary quotient map replaces neither actual étale leg.
