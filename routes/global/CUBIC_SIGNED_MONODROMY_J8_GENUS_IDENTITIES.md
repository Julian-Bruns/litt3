# Cubic signed monodromy at eight sign choices

## Status and scope

**Status: proved; self-check complete.**

This note treats the \(j=8\) case in the cubic full-orbit construction.
It classifies the three possible signed monodromy groups, gives the exact
genus characters of every allowed tame inertia class, and extracts the
one uniform inequality supplied by the cubic spectral-incidence bound.

The group classification is characteristic-free. The inertia and genus
statements are proved in characteristic zero or in characteristic
\(p\nmid48\); in particular they apply in characteristic \(5\). They are
not asserted in characteristics \(2\) or \(3\), where the signed-root
model or tame-inertia argument fails.

## 1. Setup and the three groups

Let

\[
 {\mathcal W}=C_2\wr S_3=(C_2)^3\rtimes S_3
\]

act on the six signed letters

\[
                         1,2,3,\bar1,\bar2,\bar3
\]

and on the eight sign selections, identified with the vertices of the
binary cube. Let

\[
              \beta=(1\,2\,3)(\bar1\,\bar2\,\bar3).
\]

Suppose \(H\leq{\mathcal W}\) contains \(\beta\), and the orbit of the
selection \(\{1,2,3\}\) has size

\[
                              j=8.                     \tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(H_0\) be its stabilizer. In the full-orbit field construction, let

\[
 L/B\quad\text{be the }H\text{-Galois cover},\qquad
 D^*=L^{H_0}.
\]

Let \(E\) be the degree-three quotient obtained by forgetting signs, and
let \(E'\) be the degree-six quotient obtained by choosing one signed
letter. Assume

\[
 L\longrightarrow E'\quad\text{is etale}.              \tag{1.2}
\]

Write

\[
 g(B)=b,\qquad g(E')=3ds+1,                             \tag{1.3}
\]

and assume that the cubic spectral incidence gives

\[
                         g(E)\leq3b+2d-2.               \tag{1.4}
\]

Finally, assume the norm construction injects \(J(Y)\) into \(J(D^*)\),
where \(g(Y)=3s+1\). Thus

\[
                         g(D^*)\geq3s+1.                \tag{1.5}
\]

### Proposition 1 (classification at \(j=8\))

Exactly the following groups can occur:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
H&|H|&|H_0|\\ \hline
C_2\times A_4&24&3\\
S_4^{\mathrm{tw}}&24&3\\
C_2\times S_4={\mathcal W}&48&6.
\end{array}                                             \tag{1.6}
\]

Here \(S_4^{\mathrm{tw}}\) is the index-two subgroup

\[
 \ker\!\left((C_2)^3\rtimes S_3
       \xrightarrow{\;\text{sign parity}+\operatorname{sgn}\;}C_2\right).
\]

The other index-two copy

\[
 \ker(\text{sign parity})\simeq S_4
\]

preserves the parity of cube vertices and has two orbits of size four;
it is the \(j=4\), not the \(j=8\), group.

#### Proof

The stabilizer \(H_0\) embeds in the permutations of the three selected
letters and contains \(\beta\), so \(|H_0|=3\) or \(6\). Since its orbit
has size eight, \(|H|=24\) or \(48\).

The wreath product has order \(48\) and is isomorphic to
\(C_2\times S_4\). Its three index-two subgroups are the kernels of the
three nonzero characters generated by sign parity and
\(\operatorname{sgn}:S_3\to C_2\). The kernel of sign parity has the two
parity orbits just described. The other two kernels are
\(C_2\times A_4\) and \(S_4^{\mathrm{tw}}\), and both are transitive on
the cube. If \(|H|=48\), then \(H={\mathcal W}\). \(\square\)

## 2. Exact tame inertia characters

Because of (1.2), every inertia subgroup acts freely on all six signed
letters. Under the characteristic hypothesis, inertia is tame and cyclic.
A cyclic group acting freely on six letters has order \(2,3\), or \(6\).

Use the following branch counts:

- \(u\): the central sign involution
  \[
                  c=(1\,\bar1)(2\,\bar2)(3\,\bar3);
  \]
- \(v\): a noncentral free involution, inducing a transposition on the
  three unsigned pairs;
- \(a\): an order-three inertia subgroup;
- \(h\): an order-six inertia subgroup.

For a permutation action, its different contribution at a tame branch
value is the degree minus the number of cycles. Directly on the six
signed letters, the three unsigned pairs, and the eight cube vertices,
one obtains:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
\text{type}&\text{six signed letters}&E'/B&E/B&D^*/B\\ \hline
u&(2,2,2)&3&0&4\\
v&(2,2,2)&3&1&4\\
a&(3,3)&4&2&4\\
h&(6)&5&2&6.
\end{array}                                             \tag{2.1}
\]

The allowed columns in the three groups are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
H&\text{allowed inertia types}\\ \hline
C_2\times A_4&u,a,h\\
S_4^{\mathrm{tw}}&v,a\\
C_2\times S_4&u,v,a,h.
\end{array}                                             \tag{2.2}
\]

For example, an order-three element has cycles
\((3,3,1,1)\) on the cube, while an order-six element has cycles
\((6,2)\). Both free involution classes have cube cycle type
\((2,2,2,2)\). The central involution fixes all three unsigned pairs;
the other free involution has pair cycle type \((2,1)\). These
observations prove (2.1). The index-two descriptions in Proposition 1
then give (2.2).

### Theorem 2 (the three exact genus identities)

With a disallowed branch count set equal to zero according to (2.2),

\[
\boxed{
6ds=6(2b-2)+3(u+v)+4a+5h,
}                                                        \tag{2.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{
2g(E)-2=3(2b-2)+v+2a+2h,
}                                                        \tag{2.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
2g(D^*)-2=8(2b-2)+4(u+v+a)+6h.
}                                                        \tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently (1.4) and (1.5) give

\[
                       v+2a+2h\leq4d,                  \tag{2.6}
\]

\[
             8(2b-2)+4(u+v+a)+6h\geq6s.               \tag{2.7}
\]

#### Proof

Apply tame Riemann--Hurwitz to the degree-six, degree-three, and
degree-eight permutation quotients using the three contribution columns
of (2.1). Equation (1.3) turns the left side of the degree-six formula
into \(6ds\). Comparing (2.4) with twice (1.4), minus two, gives (2.6).
Comparing (2.5) with (1.5) gives (2.7). \(\square\)

## 3. The clean twisted-\(S_4\) inequality

### Corollary 3

If \(H=S_4^{\mathrm{tw}}\), then

\[
\boxed{\qquad
                  b\geq {d(s-2)\over2}+1.
\qquad}                                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

One also has the usually weaker inequality

\[
                  b\geq {3s\over8}+1-d.                \tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Here \(u=h=0\). From (2.6),

\[
                         v+2a\leq4d.
\]

Therefore

\[
                 3v+4a\leq3(v+2a)\leq12d.
\]

Using this in (2.3) gives

\[
             6ds\leq12b-12+12d,
\]

which is (3.1). Also \(v+a\leq4d\); substituting this upper bound into
(2.7) gives (3.2). \(\square\)

## 4. The exact obstruction in the two central groups

For \(C_2\times A_4\) and \(C_2\times S_4\), the central inertia count
\(u\) occurs in (2.3), with coefficient three, but is invisible in the
cubic quotient and hence absent from (2.6). It also occurs with a positive
coefficient in the lower bound (2.7). Thus increasing \(u\) simultaneously
supplies the degree-six genus required by (2.3) and makes the Jacobian
lower bound easier to satisfy.

Consequently, the identities (2.3)--(2.7) alone give no analogue of
(3.1) for either central group. A uniform argument must supply an
independent upper bound on central sign-inertia branch values.

There is a useful refinement, but it has the same limitation. In either
central group, \(c\) induces an involution on \(D^*\). Its quotient
\(\overline D\) is the degree-four action on antipodal pairs of cube
vertices. The image of \(J(Y)\) is anti-invariant because \(c\) restricts
to the hyperelliptic involution on \(Y\). Hence

\[
 \dim\operatorname{Prym}(D^*/\overline D)\geq3s.
\]

The quotient action has different contributions \(0,2,2,2\) for
\(u,v,a,h\), respectively. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
 \dim\operatorname{Prym}(D^*/\overline D)
       =4b-4+2u+v+a+2h\geq3s.
}                                                        \tag{4.1}
\]

Again the uncontrolled \(u\) enters positively. Even the Prym refinement
cannot close the central cases without geometric control of the points
where all three square roots acquire the common central sign monodromy.

## 5. Self-check

The subgroup classification was checked both from the three index-two
characters of \(C_2\wr S_3\) and by exhaustive enumeration of its
\(98\) subgroups: exactly \(10\) contain \(\beta\), and exactly the three
groups in (1.6) have sign-selection orbit size eight.

For each of those groups, conjugacy classes were enumerated and retained
only when every nonidentity power was fixed-point-free on the six signed
letters. Their cycle contributions are exactly (2.1), and substitution
back into the three Riemann--Hurwitz formulas reproduces
(2.3)--(2.5).
