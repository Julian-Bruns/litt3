# Exact canonical subsystems can be birational in unbounded genus

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: /root/exact_canonical_subsystem_unbounded_test.
Status: explicit counterexample to an abstract Cartier-subsystem genus bound;
no common-etale-cover construction or nonexistence claim.

## Statement

Over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic 5, there are smooth
projective curves of unbounded genus possessing a four-dimensional subspace

\[
 V\subseteq\ker\bigl(C:H^0(C,\omega_C)\longrightarrow H^0(C,\omega_C)\bigr)
\]

which generates the canonical line bundle and defines a birational morphism
onto its image in projective three-space. Here C on differential spaces
denotes the absolute, inverse-Frobenius-semilinear Cartier operator.

Thus basepoint freeness and birationality of a Cartier-zero canonical
subsystem do not imply a bound on the genus in fixed characteristic, and
do not force Cartier to vanish on the whole space of holomorphic forms.

## The curves and the canonical divisor

Take

\[
 m=5r+4,\qquad r\geq0,\qquad r\not\equiv3\pmod5,
 \qquad C_m:\ y^5-y=x^m,
\]

where C_m means the smooth projective model. The affine equation is smooth
and finite etale over the affine x-line, because its derivative in y is
-1. It is irreducible: if x^m=h^5-h for h in k(x), then any pole of h
produces a pole of h^5-h of order divisible by 5. The pole of x^m has
order m prime to 5, a contradiction. The Artin-Schreier extension
k(C_m)/k(x) consequently has degree 5.

There is exactly one point P at infinity, and

\[
 v_P(x)=-5,\qquad v_P(y)=-m.
\]

Indeed, at any point above infinity of ramification index e, the equation
gives 5v(y)=-em, so 5 divides e; since the extension has degree 5, this
forces total ramification and uniqueness. A convenient uniformizer is

\[
 t=y/x^{r+1},\qquad v_P(t)=1.
\]

For each nontrivial automorphism sigma_c: y -> y+c, c in F_5^*,

\[
 v_P(\sigma_c(t)-t)=v_P(c/x^{r+1})=5(r+1)=m+1.
\]

The different exponent is therefore 4(m+1). Riemann-Hurwitz and the
different formula for the pullback of dx give

\[
 2g(C_m)-2=-10+4(m+1)=4m-6,
 \qquad g(C_m)=2(m-1)=10r+6,
\]

\[
 \operatorname{div}_{C_m}(dx)=(4m-6)P.
\]

In particular, dx has no zero or pole at any affine point. These genus
and differential conventions agree with the standard Artin-Schreier
formulas in Farnell--Pries, Sections 2.2 and 2.4,
[*Families of Artin-Schreier curves with Cartier-Manin matrix of constant
rank*](https://www.math.colostate.edu/~pries/Preprints/13ASfarnellpries313archive.pdf).
The valuation calculation above proves the formulas needed here directly.

## Elementary Cartier calculation and exactness

Put K=k(C_m). Since K/k(x) is finite separable, x is a separating
parameter and {1,x,x^2,x^3,x^4} is a basis of K over K^5. Uniquely write

\[
 f=\sum_{j=0}^4 a_j^5x^j,\qquad C(f\,dx)=a_4\,dx.
\]

This also proves the exactness criterion without a global primitive
assumption: if a_4=0, then

\[
 f\,dx=d\left(\sum_{j=0}^3\frac{a_j^5x^{j+1}}{j+1}\right).
\]

Conversely, expanding any rational primitive in the same p-basis shows
that its derivative has no x^4 component. Thus Cartier zero means exact
as a rational differential. It does not mean the existence of a global
regular primitive on the projective curve.

For every integer n>=0 this formula gives

\[
 C(x^n dx)=
 \begin{cases}
 x^{(n-4)/5}dx,&n\equiv4\pmod5,\\
 0,&n\not\equiv4\pmod5.
 \end{cases}
\]

Set i=(4m-6)/5=4r+2 and take

\[
 V_m=\langle dx,\ x\,dx,\ xy\,dx,\ x^i dx\rangle_k.
\]

The first two forms have Cartier zero. The sign in the equation gives
y=y^5-x^m, so semilinearity over fifth powers implies

\[
 C(xy\,dx)=yC(x\,dx)-C(x^{m+1}dx)=0,
\]

because m+1 is divisible by 5. Finally i is congruent to 4 modulo 5
exactly when r is congruent to 3 modulo 5, which was excluded. Hence
C(V_m)=0. Explicit rational primitives for the four generators are

\[
 x,\quad x^2/2,\quad x^2y^5/2-x^{m+2}/(m+2),\quad x^{i+1}/(i+1).
\]

All denominators are nonzero in k under the stated hypotheses.

## Regularity, basepoint freeness, and birationality

The four forms are regular on the affine curve. Their orders at P are,
respectively,

\[
 4m-6,\qquad4m-11,\qquad3m-11,\qquad0.
\]

These orders are nonnegative for m>=4. Thus V_m consists of holomorphic
forms. The forms are linearly independent: i>=2, the three monomials
1,x,x^i are independent in k(x), and xy is not in k(x) because y is not.
This includes r=0: then m=4, i=2, g=6, and the orders are 10,5,1,0.
There is no exceptional dependence at the initial member.

On the affine curve dx never vanishes. At P the section x^i dx never
vanishes. Therefore the evaluation V_m tensor O_{C_m} -> omega_{C_m}
is surjective and the associated morphism is everywhere defined.
On its dx chart it has coordinates

\[
 \phi_m=[1:x:xy:x^i].
\]

The field generated by the coordinate ratios contains x and xy, hence
y=(xy)/x. It is exactly K. Thus phi_m is birational onto its image;
in particular, this is not a merely inseparable parametrization.

Taking, for example, r=5s for s>=0 gives genera 50s+6 tending to infinity
while dim(V_m)=4 and the characteristic remains 5.

## Why this does not force superspeciality or solve the two-leg problem

In fact Cartier is nonzero on every C_m in this family. The form y dx
is holomorphic, with infinity order 3m-6, and

\[
 C(y\,dx)=yC(dx)-C(x^m dx)=-x^r dx\ne0.
\]

Consequently a(C_m)<g(C_m). The construction does not satisfy the
full-Cartier-zero hypothesis to which a superspecial genus bound would
apply. Generating the canonical line bundle as an O_C-module does not
make these four sections a basis of all global sections, and Cartier is
not O_C-linear, so sheaf generation cannot extend Cartier vanishing to
all holomorphic forms.

The example refutes only the proposed abstract implication from an
exact, basepoint-free birational canonical subsystem to bounded genus.
It supplies no second etale leg, no common etale cover of prescribed
curves, and no map to a fixed superspecial curve. The displayed map to
the x-line is ramified at infinity and has genus-zero target. The
birational projective image varies with m and has normalization C_m.

Any stronger route retaining specified canonical-ring relations,
Poisson polynomial identities, or the compatibility equations for two
prescribed etale pullbacks still requires its own analysis. This example
does not show that those stronger equations have solutions, and gives
no reason to discard them.
