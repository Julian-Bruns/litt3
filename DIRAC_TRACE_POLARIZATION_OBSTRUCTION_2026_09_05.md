# A trace obstruction to a Dirac section on ordinary p-torsion

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: proved elementary lemma and stated consequences.
This is a standalone verification; it makes no assertion about any Litt3
application or existence on an arbitrary principally polarized abelian variety.

## 1. Setup and the quotient polarization

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic p > 0. Let (A,L) be an
ordinary principally polarized abelian variety of dimension g >= 1, and put

\[
H=A[p](k),\qquad v:A\longrightarrow B=A/H.
\]

Here H also denotes the constant reduced subgroup scheme represented by
these points. It has order p^g, and v is a finite étale isogeny with
scheme-theoretic fiber v^{-1}(0)=H. It is not the quotient by the full,
nonreduced group scheme A[p].

Fix a principal ample line bundle M on B such that

\[
v^*M\equiv L^{\otimes p}.
\tag{1}
\]

This compatible polarization exists. Indeed, H is totally isotropic for the
pairing of p lambda_L: its restricted pairing takes values in mu_p, and a
map from the reduced constant scheme H x H to mu_p is trivial. The
polarization therefore descends over v. Its degree is
p^{2g}/(p^g)^2=1, so the descended polarization is principal. Over k it has
an ample line bundle representative M. This uses polarization descent,
not an unproved choice of linearization of the particular line bundle L^p.
See van der Geer--Moonen, [Proposition 11.25](https://www.math.ru.nl/~bmoonen/BookAV/PolWp.pdf).

For any line bundle T on A, call s in H^0(A,T) a Dirac section on H if

\[
s(0)\ne0,\qquad s(h)=0\quad(h\in H\setminus\{0\}).
\tag{2}
\]

These are statements in the respective one-dimensional fibers; no
trivialization of T on H is part of the definition. Ampleness of T is
unnecessary for the trace lemma itself.

## 2. Trace lemma, with fiber conventions

**Lemma.** Let N be any line bundle on B such that every global section of
N vanishes at 0. If

\[
R=v^*N\otimes T^{-1}
\tag{3}
\]

is generated by global sections at 0, then T has no Dirac section on H.

**Proof.** If s were such a section, choose t in H^0(A,R) with t(0) nonzero.
The tensor product s t is a section of v^*N. The algebra trace, followed by
the projection formula, gives

\[
v_*v^*N\simeq N\otimes v_*\mathcal O_A
\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_N\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_{A/B}}N.
\tag{4}
\]

Thus u=Tr_v(s t) is a global section of N and u(0)=0 by hypothesis.
The algebra trace of a finite locally free algebra commutes with base
change. The fiber algebra here is

\[
(v_*\mathcal O_A)\otimes k(0)\simeq\prod_{h\in H} k.
\]

The trace of multiplication by (a_h)_h on this algebra is sum_h a_h.
Moreover, each fiber (v^*N)_h has the canonical identification with N_0
coming from v(h)=0. Consequently

\[
0=u(0)=\sum_{h\in H}(s t)(h)=s(0)t(0)\ne0,
\]

a contradiction. The last product is nonzero under
T_0 tensor R_0 = N_0. This also explains why summing the values of s
alone would not be justified. QED.

There is no division by |H|. In characteristic p the trace of the unit is
|H|=0, but the trace functional on the split fiber algebra is still the
nonzero sum functional. In particular, it takes value 1 on the vector
supported at a single point. The construction in (4) is the algebra trace
on coherent sheaves; see the [Stacks Project, Section 49.4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BSY)
and [Exercise 111.22.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02DU).

The same proof works for any finite étale morphism and a distinguished
point in a split fiber, assuming its target line bundle has a base point
at the image. Ordinarity and principal polarizations enter the present
application only through the specified quotient and the available N.

## 3. Choosing the principal theta representative

An ample principal line bundle M has h^0(B,M)=1. Its nonzero section has a
nonempty zero divisor Delta, since dim B >= 1 and M is nontrivial ample.
Choose b in Delta(k) and set

\[
M_0=t_b^*M.
\]

The unique theta section of M_0 vanishes at 0, because t_b(0)=b. Translation
preserves the principal polarization and numerical class, so
v^*M_0 remains numerically equivalent to L^p. The statement h^0(M)=1
follows from Riemann--Roch and vanishing for ample line bundles; see
van der Geer--Moonen, [Theorems 9.11 and 9.14](https://www.math.ru.nl/~bmoonen/BookAV/CohomLB.pdf).

Therefore, for any such M_0,

\[
0\notin\operatorname{Bs}(v^*M_0\otimes T^{-1})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
T\text{ has no Dirac section on }H.
\tag{5}
\]

Only one choice of M_0 satisfying the left-hand condition is needed.
Conversely, existence of a Dirac section forces 0 to be a base point of
the residual line bundle for every principal representative whose theta
section vanishes at 0.

## 4. The numerical proportional bound and its sharpness

**Corollary.** If T is ample and T is numerically equivalent to L^m for an
integer 1 <= m <= p-2, then T has no Dirac section on H.

Indeed, the residual R for any M_0 chosen above satisfies

\[
R\equiv L^{p-m},\qquad p-m\ge2.
\]

On an abelian variety numerical triviality of a line bundle is equivalent
to membership in Pic^0. Thus R=L^{p-m} tensor P for some P in Pic^0(A).
For an ample line bundle K, the isogeny
a -> t_a^*K tensor K^{-1} is surjective, so every Pic^0 twist of K is a
translate of K, including in inseparable characteristic. All powers L^n
with n >= 2 are globally generated, hence so is R. Apply (5). The power
theorem is valid over a field of arbitrary characteristic; see
Genestier--Ngo, [Theorem 2.2.3](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~ngo/Shimura.pdf).
For p=2 the displayed range of positive m is empty.

The bound is sharp as a statement uniform over ordinary ppav's, in every
dimension. For ordinary elliptic curves E_1,...,E_g set

\[
A=\prod_i E_i,\qquad
L=\boxtimes_i\mathcal O_{E_i}([0]),\qquad
D_i=\sum_{0\ne h\in E_i[p](k)}[h].
\]

The canonical section of O(D_i) is nonzero at 0 and vanishes at every
nonzero p-torsion point. Their exterior product is a Dirac section of

\[
T=\boxtimes_i\mathcal O_{E_i}(D_i)\equiv L^{p-1}.
\]

Each factor has degree p-1 > 0, so T is ample. This explicit product
example proves sharpness without asserting Dirac existence on an
arbitrary ordinary ppav or invoking a Raynaud construction.

## 5. Nonproportional positivity and the exact cohomological test

The trace lemma depends on R itself and does not require a proportional
polarization. Here are useful sufficient tests.

**Two ample factors.** If the numerical class p[L]-[T] is a sum of two
ample integral line bundle classes, then every residual R in (5) is
globally generated, and no Dirac section exists.

To prove the global-generation assertion, first write R=N_1 tensor N_2
with both N_i ample, absorbing a Pic^0 twist into one factor if needed.
For a fixed point x, the set

\[
U_i=\{P\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(A):
N_i\otimes P\text{ has a section nonzero at }x\}
\]

is nonempty and open. Nonemptiness follows by translating an effective
divisor of N_i away from x, and openness follows from cohomology and base
change because ample line bundles have no higher cohomology after any
Pic^0 twist. The irreducible variety Pic^0(A) has a point P in
U_1 intersect (-U_2). Multiply sections of N_1 tensor P and
N_2 tensor P^{-1} that are nonzero at x. Their product is a section of R
nonzero at x. Since x was arbitrary, R is globally generated.

For example, it suffices that p[L]-[T]=2[N]+[Q] with N ample and Q nef:
use the ample factors N and N tensor Q.

**Evaluation cohomology.** The sequence

\[
0\to\mathcal I_0\otimes R\to R\to R|_0\to0
\]

shows exactly that R is generated at 0 if and only if

\[
H^1(A,\mathcal I_0\otimes R)\longrightarrow H^1(A,R)
\quad\text{is injective}.
\tag{6}
\]

In particular, if R is ample, or more generally H^1(A,R)=0, this is
equivalent to H^1(A,I_0 tensor R)=0.

Recall that IT_0 means H^i(A,F tensor P)=0 for every i>0 and every
P in Pic^0(A). IT_0 for I_0 tensor R is a sufficient condition for (5).
When R is ample, IT_0 for I_0 tensor R is equivalent to global generation
of R: the evaluation sequences give the assertion for every twist, and
every twist of an ample R is a translate. IT_0 for R alone is insufficient.
For example, a principal theta line bundle whose theta divisor contains
0 is ample and IT_0 but has no section nonzero at 0.

## 6. The residual class Theta + q^*D needs a separate check

Suppose q:A -> C is a quotient by an abelian subvariety, write L=O_A(Theta),
and let

\[
T=L^{p-1}\otimes q^*\mathcal O_C(-D).
\]

For a permitted M_0 the actual residual is

\[
R=L\otimes q^*\mathcal O_C(D)\otimes P_0,
\qquad P_0=v^*M_0\otimes L^{-p}\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(A).
\tag{7}
\]

The twist P_0 must be retained. Its source M_0 is constrained to have a
theta section vanishing at 0. Even if D is ample on C, q^*D need only be
nef on A; it cannot be substituted for a second ample factor in the
preceding test.

For instance, take A=E_1 x E_2,
L=O_{E_1}([0]) boxtimes O_{E_2}([0]), q=pr_1, and D of positive degree
on E_1. Then

\[
L\otimes q^*\mathcal O(D)
=\bigl(\mathcal O_{E_1}([0])\otimes\mathcal O(D)\bigr)
 \boxtimes\mathcal O_{E_2}([0]).
\]

All its sections vanish on E_1 x {0}. It is ample and IT_0 but fails
generation at 0. Thus the numerical expression Theta + q^*D alone does
not prove the needed evaluation property for a chosen residual.

A useful fiber test keeps the issue explicit. Put
K=ker(q), L'=L tensor P_0, and G=q_*L'. The higher direct images of L'
vanish, G is locally free, and its fiber at 0 is H^0(K,L'|_K). These facts
follow from ampleness on every fiber and cohomology and base change.
Evaluation of the residual at 0 factors as

\[
H^0(C,G\otimes\mathcal O_C(D))
\longrightarrow
H^0(K,L'|_K)\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)_0
\longrightarrow R_0.
\tag{8}
\]

If L'|_K has a base point at 0, then R has a base point there for every D.
If L'|_K is generated at 0 and G tensor O_C(D) is generated at 0, then R
is generated at 0 and the trace obstruction applies. In general, the
exact condition is that the composite (8) be nonzero.

Equivalently, define

\[
Z=\{Q\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)(k):
0\text{ lies on the theta divisor of }M\otimes Q\}.
\]

The exact sufficient criterion supplied by this method is the existence
of Q in Z for which

\[
0\notin\operatorname{Bs}
\bigl(v^*(M\otimes Q)\otimes T^{-1}\bigr).
\tag{9}
\]

A Dirac section forces the opposite base-point condition for every Q in
Z. This is a necessary condition for existence, not a converse existence
theorem. No automatic conclusion at (7) is asserted without an evaluation
or global-generation argument.
