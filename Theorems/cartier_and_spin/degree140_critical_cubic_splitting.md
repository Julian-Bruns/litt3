# A local cubic test at a colliding critical pair

Version1,30 September2026. Work in the actual primitive constant
degree140 chart and let p be an affine point where the two finite
critical coordinates coalesce, with phi a unit there. Put
\[
m=\operatorname{ord}_p(g_3^2+2g_2g_4)>0.
\]
The quadratic leading coefficient g2 is a unit. Let lambda be the
common critical value at p, assumed nonzero, and let
nu=ord_p(E_lambda), where E is the primitive scale quadratic on X.
The three source roots reducing to that critical coordinate split
over the completed base k[[r]] if and only if
\[
\bigl(\nu<3m\ \text{and}\ 6\mid\nu\bigr)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\bigl(\nu\ge3m,\ 2\mid m,\ 2\mid\nu\bigr).
\]
This is a statement about this cubic root cluster, not about every
other root or every point of the source.

In particular, when m is odd and lambda is an actual everywhere-etale
scale, the unique point of the critical normalization over p has
\[
e(\Lambda)=\nu=6r,\qquad 1\le r\le(m-1)/2.
\]
Thus a SIMPLE zero of the critical discriminant cannot lie above any
actual nonzero etale scale. Normalized ramification alone had allowed
that value. More generally these local splitting tests give finitely
many forbidden scale values, computable once the ratio is fixed.

No global emptiness or unmarked common-cover conclusion is asserted.
