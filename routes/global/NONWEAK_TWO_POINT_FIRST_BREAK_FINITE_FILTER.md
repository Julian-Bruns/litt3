# Residual nonweak two-point first-break filter

**Status:** exact finite integer calculation, 2026-09-05.
**Author:** `/root/canonical_trace_algebra`.
**Boundary:** formal necessary-condition rows only. The tail inequality
below is an input supplied by the proposed maximum-break argument; this
calculation does not prove that argument, realize any local action, or
construct an orbifold or atlas. No fixed curves are replaced.

## Input and finite reduction

Use the supplied normalized parameters

    D | 16,  1 <= a < D,  gcd(a,D)=1,  5 does not divide a,
    h >= 2,  h*c=a+D,  gcd(a,c)=1,  5 does not divide c,
    P=5^q,  L=(P+h)/a,  epsilon=c*L,
    m=c*t,  N=a*t,  t | L,  5 does not divide t.

Let b be the first lower break, with 5 not dividing b, and let
s=dim_F5(P/G_(b+1)). For a family with unbounded q, enforce

    1 <= b <= c/a,
    5^ceil(s/2) | a(b+1)+D,
    c/a-b >= 5^(1-s),
    c*t | b(5^s-1).

The third condition is the leading-coefficient consequence of the supplied
tail bound epsilon-b(P-1) >= 5(5^(q-s)-1) when q>s. The exact integrality
condition is also imposed:

    5^q == -h mod(a*t).

This is an actual power-of-five congruence, not an assertion based only on
coprimality.

Here the enumeration is finite without bounding q. Since h>=2,

    a*b <= c <= (a+D)/2,
    0 < a(b+1)+D <= 3(a+D)/2 < 48.

Thus v5(a(b+1)+D)<=2 and s<=4. Also b<=8. For each retained (b,s,c),
t divides the explicitly bounded integer b(5^s-1)/c. As a*t is prime to
five, the exact congruence has an unbounded set of solutions precisely
when -h belongs to the finite cyclic subgroup generated by 5 modulo a*t.

The Swan congruence itself follows from the exact identity

    a*(epsilon-b(P-1))=(c-a*b)*P+a(b+1)+D.

Because s<=q, the P term vanishes modulo 5^ceil(s/2). No cancellation
assumption or approximation is used at this step.

## Exhaustive result

Even before imposing the tail and tame constraints, the only tuples
(D,a,h,c,b) satisfying the base parameter conditions, b<=c/a, and
5 | a(b+1)+D are

| D | a | h | c | b | a(b+1)+D | Possible s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 10 | 1,2 |
| 8 | 1 | 9 | 1 | 1 | 10 | 1,2 |

The second row has zero leading tail coefficient and fails the supplied
positive tail bound. In the first row, s=1 fails tame divisibility because
3 does not divide 5-1. With s=2, tame divisibility becomes 3*t | 24.

Consequently **exactly four formal rows remain under the literal listed
integer filters**:

| D | a | h | c | b | s | t | m | N | Unbounded q |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 3 | 1 | all q>=3 |
| 8 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 6 | 2 | all q>=3 |
| 8 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 12 | 4 | all q>=3 |
| 8 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 8 | 24 | 8 | odd q>=3 |

If N is the order of an actual tame stacky point, one must additionally
require N>=2. Then the first row is deleted, and **exactly three genuine
tame-point rows remain**, with t=2,4,8. Independently, the ordinary-atlas
inequality d<2e is epsilon<mP+1 and also deletes exactly the first row:
for t=1 it reads 3P+9<3P+1, which is false. It holds for the other three
rows once q>=3. This distinction avoids treating identity inertia as a
second stacky point.

For all four,

    L=P+3,  epsilon=3P+9,
    epsilon-b(P-1)=2P+10.

For q>=3 the first quotient has order 25<P, as required for the residual
nonweak case. The exact tail bound holds:

    2P+10 >= 5(P/25-1).

The Swan divisibility has exactly v5(2P+10)=1. Finally, P+3 is always
divisible by 4, and is divisible by 8 exactly when q is odd. Thus these
are genuine infinite sequences of integer solutions to the stated filters,
not just surviving leading coefficients.

No later-break filtration, group presentation, local realization, global
orbifold realization, or ordinary-atlas existence has been checked.
