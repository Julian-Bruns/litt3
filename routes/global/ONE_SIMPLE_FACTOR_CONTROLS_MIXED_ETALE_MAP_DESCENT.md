# One simple factor controls descent in a product of étale covers

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: /root.
Status: author proof; no novelty claim. The dimension restriction below is
essential to this argument, not a claim about the optimal theorem.

## 1. Statement

Fix a smooth projective connected hyperbolic curve \(T/k\), where \(k\) is
algebraically closed. Suppose \(J_T\) has a simple isogeny factor \(A\) with
\(\dim A\ge3\). Fix a surjective homomorphism \(\rho:J_T\to A\).

**Theorem.** There is \(M=M(T,\rho)\) such that the following holds.
Let \(H=H_1\times H_2\) act on a smooth projective connected curve \(W\), and
let \(r:W\to T\) be nonconstant. If
\[
 \rho r_*(h_{1*}-1)(h_{2*}-1)=0:J_W\longrightarrow A
                      \qquad(h_i\in H_i),                       \tag{1}
\]
then at least one factor has a bounded orbit on the actual map:
\[
       [H_i:\{h\in H_i:r h=r\}]\le M\quad\hbox{for some }i.        \tag{2}
\]
Neither the group orders nor the degree of \(r\) enter \(M\).

If the action is free and \(r\) is finite étale, then for this \(i\) the
normalization \(C_i\) of the joint image of
\[
                        W\longrightarrow (W/H_i)\times T
\]
has finite étale maps to both factors, with
\[
                             \deg(C_i/(W/H_i))\le M.              \tag{3}
\]
No Galoisness of \(r\) or of the map in (3) is asserted or required.

In particular, let \(P_{\rm mix}\subset J_W\) be generated by all
\((h_{1*}-1)(h_{2*}-1)J_W\). The hypothesis
\(\operatorname{Hom}(P_{\rm mix},A)=0\) implies (1).
If \(A\) is nonordinary and \(P_{\rm mix}\) is ordinary, this orthogonality
holds. Other simple factors of \(J_T\) may be ordinary.

## 2. Fixed target geometry

Choose an Abel base point on \(T\), and put
\[
 a=\rho\operatorname{AJ}_T:T\to A,\quad
 D=a(T)_{\rm red},\quad S=D-D\subset A.
\]
The curve \(D\) generates \(A\). Let
\(e=[k(T):k(D)]_{\rm sep}\); the generic geometric fibers of \(a\)
have \(e\) points, whether or not \(a\) has an inseparable part.

Both translation stabilizers
\[
       K_D=\{c:D+c=D\},\qquad K_S=\{c:S+c=S\}
\]
are finite on geometric points. Indeed, their reduced identity components
are abelian subvarieties of simple \(A\). A positive-dimensional one would
be all of \(A\), impossible for \(D\) or \(S\). Here \(S\) has dimension two:
dimension one would make \(D\) a translate of an elliptic subvariety, whereas
\(D\) generates \(A\) of dimension at least three. Thus \(S\) is proper.

Fix a very ample line bundle on \(A\), giving a projective embedding.
Write \(d=\deg D\), \(s=\deg S\), and choose
\[
                   B=\max\left(1,\binom{s^2-1}{2}\right).
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
We shall use the following elementary bound:

**Translation-curve bound.** If a finite group \(K\subset A(k)\) translates
an integral curve \(R\subset S\) onto itself, then
\[
                           |K|\le\max(|K_S|,B).                    \tag{5}
\]

If \(K\subset K_S\), this is immediate. Otherwise choose \(c\in K\setminus K_S\).
The two distinct irreducible surfaces \(S,S+c\) both contain \(R\), so \(R\)
is a component of their intersection. The projective Bézout inequality for
intersection components gives \(\deg R\le s^2\).
The normalization \(\widetilde R\) therefore has genus at most \(B\), using
the plane degree-genus bound after a general birational projection.

It has genus at least two. Abelian varieties contain no rational curves;
a nonconstant map from a smooth genus-one curve has image an elliptic
translate, which simplicity of \(A\) excludes. Translations lift to
\(\widetilde R\) and act freely: a fixed point upstairs would give a fixed
point of a nonzero translation downstairs. The quotient is consequently
étale, even if the characteristic divides \(|K|\). Riemann–Hurwitz gives
\[
       g(\widetilde R)-1=|K|(g(\widetilde R/K)-1),\qquad
       |K|\le g(\widetilde R)-1<B.
 \]
This proves (5).

We also use a fixed fiber bound for the difference map \(D\times D\to S\).
For \(\eta\notin K_D\), its fiber is finite and has at most \(d^2\)
geometric points: identifying the second coordinate identifies this
fiber with \(D\cap(D-\eta)\), and Bézout applies to these distinct curves.

## 3. The projected mixed pairing

Set \(u=a r:W\to A\). Hypothesis (1) and the Albanese universal property
make
\[
             u(h_1h_2w)-u(h_1w)-u(h_2w)+u(w)=c(h_1,h_2)            \tag{6}
\]
constant. The telescoping identity in each variable shows that \(c\) is
biadditive, hence factors through \(H_1^{\rm ab}\times H_2^{\rm ab}\).
Its image is a finite torsion group of geometric points.

For \(b\in H_2\), put \(\delta_b(w)=u(bw)-u(w)\in S\).
There are two cases.

### 3.1. All differences in one direction are constant

Suppose every \(\delta_b\) is constant. Since \(u\) and \(u b\) both
surject onto \(D\), each constant belongs to \(K_D\). At a geometric
generic point \(w\), the values \(u(bw)\), \(b\in H_2\), therefore number
at most \(|K_D|\). Each such value has at most \(e\) preimages under \(a\).
Thus the distinct maps \(r b\) number at most \(e|K_D|\):
\[
                      [H_2:\operatorname{Stab}_{H_2}(r)]
                                      \le e|K_D|.                 \tag{7}
\]

### 3.2. A nonconstant difference in that direction

Otherwise choose \(b\in H_2\) with \(\delta_b\) nonconstant, and let
\(R=\delta_b(W)_{\rm red}\subset S\). Equation (6) gives
\[
                    \delta_b(h_1w)=\delta_b(w)+c(h_1,b).
\]
The finite group \(K=\operatorname{im}c(-,b)\) preserves \(R\). Bound (5)
therefore applies.

At a geometric generic point \(w\), the values \(\delta_b(h_1w)\) number
at most \(|K|\). They are generic points of \(R\), hence avoid the finite
set \(K_D\). For each such value, the pair
\[
                          (u(bh_1w),u(h_1w))
\]
belongs to a difference fiber with at most \(d^2\) geometric points.
Consequently \(u(h_1w)\) has at most \(|K|d^2\) possible values. The
actual values are generic on \(D\), since each \(u h_1\) is surjective,
so the generic fiber bound \(e\) for \(a\) applies. We obtain
\[
              [H_1:\operatorname{Stab}_{H_1}(r)]
                        \le e d^2\max(|K_S|,B).                  \tag{8}
\]
Distinct maps are distinguished at the geometric generic point, which
justifies identifying these value counts with orbit sizes.

Combining (7) and (8), one may take
\[
             M=\max\{e|K_D|,\ e d^2\max(|K_S|,B)\}.               \tag{9}
\]

## 4. Actual intermediate covers

For the factor satisfying (2), set \(Q=\operatorname{Stab}_{H_i}(r)\).
Its invariant field is
\[
                   k(W)^Q=k(W)^{H_i}\,r^*k(T).
\]
Indeed, \(k(W)/k(W)^{H_i}\) is Galois with group given by the effective
action, and the subgroup fixing the displayed compositum is precisely
\(Q\). Thus \(W/Q\) is the normalization \(C_i\) of the joint image and
has degree \([H_i:Q]\) over \(W/H_i\).

Freeness makes the quotient maps out of \(W\) étale. The map \(C_i\to T\)
is an intermediate factor of the finite étale map \(r\), and is likewise
finite étale. This proves (3), retaining both actual maps.

## 5. Tower consequence and limitation

Suppose a two-parameter tower has, beyond a fixed corner \((a,b)\), full
product tail groups
\[
        (\mathbf Z/\ell^{m-a})\times(\mathbf Z/\ell^{n-b})
\]
and mixed contribution with no factor isogenous to \(A\), for every
\(m\ge a,n\ge b\). Choose \(c\ge0\) with \(\ell^c\ge M\).
Every actual étale map from level \((m,n)\) to \(T\), for
\(m\ge a+c,n\ge b+c\), then descends to level
\[
                             (a+c,n)\quad\hbox{or}\quad(m,b+c).
\]
Indeed a subgroup of either cyclic tail group of index at most \(M\)
contains its \(\ell^c\)-multiple. This needs only the one factor \(A\),
not orthogonality of the whole target Jacobian.

The proof does not cover simple factors of dimension one or two: in
those cases \(D-D\) can be the whole abelian variety, and the intersection
argument for (5) no longer applies. It also does not prove the mixed
orthogonality assumption for arbitrary towers.
