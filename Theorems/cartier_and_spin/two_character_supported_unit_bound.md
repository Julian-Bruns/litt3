# A missing cubic character reduces the supported-unit problem to pole28

Version1, 27 September2026. On the fixed X:y^3=P(x), let Z be the twelve
points over the quartic A=0 and O its unique infinity. Suppose a function
g has only a pole at O, zeros supported on Z, is not in k(x), and omits
one cubic character in its unique expression
g=U(x)+V(x)y+W(x)y^2.

Then there is another such function, primitive with respect to common
polynomial content and not a fifth power, whose pole degree delta satisfies
\[
\delta\le16+3s\le28,
\]
where s<=4 is its number of occupied marked x-fibres. If w of the occupied
fibre multiplicities are divisible by five, the sharper bound is
delta<=16+3s-3w. All geometric coefficients are allowed.

The reduced witness must in fact have the form U+Vy. The alternative
U+Vy^2 contributes at least ten further different exponents at the
finite cubic branch points; it would require both delta<=18 and
delta>=20. The finite pole list is therefore
10,12,13,15,16,18,19,21,22,24,25,27,28, using only the characters0,1.

The bound is on the reduced witness, not every original function: common
polynomial factors and fifth powers can make the original pole arbitrarily
large. Thus a complete exclusion of the two-character witnesses through28
would exclude them in ALL degrees. The bound itself does not certify that
finite exclusion and does not concern functions using all three characters.
