# An explicit genus-five cubical cover and asymptotic Raynaud-avoiding lines

## Status and scope

**Proved; exact algebra certificate included; self-check complete.**

This note verifies the explicit characteristic-five family suggested by
the Pro computation and isolates exactly what its torsion-line argument
provides. It constructs cyclic covers of fixed \(a\)-number one with a
compatible dihedral action. It does **not** construct an independent
second map to a prescribed curve: the dihedral quotient below is the
already visible quotient of the same tower.

## 1. The cubical curve and its free all-sign quotient

Over \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\), put

\[
 q_1=t^2+2,\qquad q_2=t^2+3,\qquad q_3=t^2+t+1.         \tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(T\) be the smooth normalization of

\[
 r_i^2=q_i(t)\qquad(1\leq i\leq3).                     \tag{1.2}
\]

The three polynomials are square-free and pairwise coprime. Their
square classes are independent, since a root of \(q_i\) has odd
valuation only in the \(i\)-th class. Thus \(T/\mathbf P^1\) is a
connected \(C_2^3\)-cover. It is branched at the six finite roots of
the \(q_i\), with inertia generated by the corresponding coordinate
sign. Riemann--Hurwitz gives

\[
 2g(T)-2=8(-2)+6\cdot4=8,\qquad g(T)=5.                \tag{1.3}
\]

Let

\[
 \tau(r_1,r_2,r_3)=(-r_1,-r_2,-r_3).
\]

It belongs to none of the six inertia groups, so it acts freely. Hence

\[
                 X_0=T/\langle\tau\rangle,\qquad g(X_0)=3.          \tag{1.4}
\]

The three \(\tau\)-invariant canonical differentials pulled back to \(T\)
are

\[
 {dt\over r_2r_3},\qquad {dt\over r_1r_3},\qquad
 {dt\over r_1r_2}.
\]

Writing their canonical coordinates as \(x,y,z\), respectively, gives

\[
 [x^2:y^2:z^2]=[q_1:q_2:q_3].
\]

Eliminating \(t\) yields

\[
 2x^4+x^2y^2+x^2z^2+3y^4+2y^2z^2+z^4=0.              \tag{1.5}
\]

The three projective singularity ideals obtained by adjoining
\(x-1,y-1,z-1\), respectively, to (1.5) and its three partial
derivatives are all the unit ideal. Thus (1.5) is a smooth plane
quartic. It has genus three, so the canonical map above is birational
and identifies it with \(X_0\).

## 2. Exact Cartier data and the prime-to-five decomposition

For \(i<j\), let

\[
 E_{ij}:s^2=q_iq_j,\qquad
 C:s^2=q_1q_2q_3.                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

For an elliptic quartic \(s^2=f(t)\) in characteristic five, its Hasse
invariant is the coefficient of \(t^4\) in \(f^2\). For
\((E_{12},E_{13},E_{23})\), these coefficients are

\[
                              2,\quad2,\quad3,          \tag{2.2}
\]

so all three elliptic curves are ordinary.

For the genus-two curve \(C\), the Hasse--Witt matrix is

\[
 H_C=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_C^2=H_C,\qquad \operatorname {rank}H_C=1.            \tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
                         f(C)=1,\qquad a(C)=1.          \tag{2.4}
\]

The seven nontrivial characters of \(C_2^3\) give three rational
quotients from the one-element subsets, the three elliptic curves in
(2.1) from the two-element subsets, and \(C\) from the three-element
subset. The group-algebra isogeny therefore gives

\[
 JT\ \sim\ JC\times E_{12}\times E_{13}\times E_{23}.  \tag{2.5}
\]

Its kernel is killed by a power of \(2\). This point is essential:
because the isogeny degree is prime to the characteristic, it identifies
the 5-divisible groups, and not merely their rational isocrystals.
Hence

\[
                       f(T)=4,\qquad a(T)=1.            \tag{2.6}
\]

The involution \(\tau\) acts by the parity of a character subset. Its
invariant factors are precisely the three ordinary elliptic factors, so

\[
 JX_0\sim E_{12}\times E_{13}\times E_{23};             \tag{2.7}
\]

in particular \(X_0\) is ordinary. If

\[
 P=(\ker(\operatorname {Nm}_{T/X_0}))^0,
\]

then \(P\) is an abelian surface, \(P\sim JC\), and \(JT/P\) is ordinary.

## 3. Raynaud-avoiding torsion lines

Let \(\Theta_T\subset J(T^{(1)})\) be the Raynaud theta divisor and put

\[
                         D=\Theta_T|_{P^{(1)}}.          \tag{3.1}
\]

The ordinary-quotient criterion from
RESTRICTED_RAYNAUD_THETA_SUFFICIENT_CONDITIONS_AND_STABILITY_BOUNDARY.md
gives

\[
                         P^{(1)}\not\subset\Theta_T.
\]

Thus \(D\) is a proper effective divisor on the abelian surface
\(P^{(1)}\). It is nonempty and contains the origin, because

\[
              h^0(T^{(1)},\mathcal B_T)=a(T)=1.         \tag{3.2}
\]

Fix a very ample divisor \(H\) on \(P^{(1)}\). For every prime
\(\ell\ne5\), choose a member \(H_\ell\in|H|\) through the origin which
contains no component of \([\ell](D)\). Every \(\ell\)-torsion point of
\(D\) belongs to \(D\cap[\ell]^{-1}H_\ell\). Therefore

\[
 \#\bigl(D(k)\cap P^{(1)}[\ell]\bigr)
       \leq D\cdot[\ell]^*H
       =\ell^2(D\cdot H).                               \tag{3.3}
\]

On the other hand, the four-dimensional \(\mathbf F_\ell\)-space
\(P^{(1)}[\ell]\) has

\[
            \ell^3+\ell^2+\ell+1                       \tag{3.4}
\]

one-dimensional subspaces. A line is bad if one of its nonzero points
lies in \(D\). Each bad line contains at least one of the points counted
in (3.3), so for all sufficiently large primes \(\ell\ne2,5\), (3.4)
is strictly larger than the number of bad lines. There is consequently
a line

\[
 \Lambda\subset P^{(1)}[\ell],\qquad
                         \Lambda\cap D=\{0\}.           \tag{3.5}
\]

Choose a generator \(L\) of \(\Lambda\). It defines a connected cyclic
etale cover \(Z^{(1)}\to T^{(1)}\) of degree \(\ell\), and hence, by
invariance of the finite-etale site under relative Frobenius, a connected
cyclic etale cover \(Z\to T\). Etale Frobenius base change and the
character decomposition give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 a(Z)
 &=h^0(Z^{(1)},\mathcal B_Z)\\
 &=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}
   h^0(T^{(1)},\mathcal B_T\otimes L^i)
 =a(T)=1,                                               \tag{3.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

where (3.5) kills every nontrivial summand.

Finally, \(\tau\) acts as \([-1]\) on \(P\), so it preserves
\(\Lambda\) and sends its Kummer character to its inverse. The resulting
lift to \(Z\) conjugates the cyclic deck generator to its inverse.
Its square is a deck transformation fixed by inversion, hence is trivial
because \(\ell\) is odd. Thus the lift is an involution and

\[
                         D_{2\ell}\subset\operatorname {Aut}(Z).     \tag{3.7}
\]

Every nonidentity element of this dihedral group acts freely: rotations
are deck transformations of \(Z/T\), while reflections lie over the free
involution \(\tau\) of \(T\). Hence the composite

\[
                         Z\longrightarrow X_0
\]

is an etale Galois cover with group \(D_{2\ell}\).

## 4. Exact limitation

This proves that a nonordinary genus-five curve can have cyclic etale
covers of arbitrarily large prime degree whose \(a\)-number remains
exactly one, while the covers retain a free dihedral symmetry. It does
not produce a coreless correspondence or a map to an unrelated second
target: the map \(Z\to X_0\) is simply the composite through the fixed
quotient \(T\to X_0\). Accordingly, this family is a rigorous stress test
for restricted-theta arguments, not progress by itself on the missing
two-leg construction.
