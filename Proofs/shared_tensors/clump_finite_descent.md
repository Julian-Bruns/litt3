# Proof: lift the clump through the actual common finite torsor

[Statement](../../Theorems/shared_tensors/clump_finite_descent.md).
The important points are that the lifted diagram can be disconnected
as curves, and that both endpoint torsors carry the SAME G-action on
their specified common pullback. Neither endpoint torsor alone suffices.

## One-clump uniqueness for a finite connected diagram

Consider finitely many smooth projective connected curves as vertices
of a diagram, with finite étale correspondences as edges. Its graph
of curve components is assumed connected. A common rational function
means a tuple of rational functions on the vertex curves whose two
pullbacks agree on every edge curve. Assume this algebra is k.

Any nonempty finite clump meets every vertex curve: across an edge,
surjectivity supplies a preimage of each clump point, and saturation
then supplies a clump point on the other vertex curve. Let S and T
be two disjoint clumps. For an edge of degrees a and b the numbers
of their points satisfy
\[
a|S_A|=b|S_B|,\qquad a|T_A|=b|T_B|.
\]
Thus $|S_A|/|T_A|$ is constant throughout the connected diagram.
Choose positive integers u,v so $uS_A-vT_A$ has degree zero on
EVERY vertex curve.

These divisors define an actual common degree-zero line. It is
torsion as a line on the whole diagram. Indeed, each individual
degree-zero line on a curve over $\overline{\mathbf F}_p$ is torsion:
it is defined over a finite field and its rational Jacobian group
is finite. After taking a common power, trivialize all vertex lines.
The remaining edge identifications are nonzero constants, since
the edge curves are connected and projective. Finitely many such
constants are roots of unity; a further common power kills all of
them. This also kills the gluing, not just the individual line classes.

Consequently powers of the canonical sections of S and T become
sections of the SAME common line. Their ratio is a common rational
function, nonconstant because its divisor is a nonzero multiple of
$uS-vT$. This contradicts the hypothesis. The diagram therefore
has at most one connected finite physical component.

This proof deliberately uses the finite-field algebraic closure.
It does not assume that the compatible Picard group of a disconnected
diagram is finite; edge gluing can contribute a torus.

## A connected common finite torsor remains coreless as a diagram

Now take the common G-torsor in the theorem. Let a be a common
rational-function tuple on its lifted diagram. For $g\in G$,
deck translation gives another such tuple, denoted $g(a)$.
The coefficients of the monic polynomial
\[
\prod_{g\in G}(T-g(a))
\]
are G-invariant common rational functions. They descend to the
ORIGINAL X and Y and agree on the ORIGINAL Z. Corelessness makes
all of them constants in k.

On every connected component curve upstairs, a therefore satisfies
a monic polynomial over the algebraically closed constant field k.
It is constant on that component, hence regular. Connectedness of
the graph of curve components makes these constants equal. Thus
the common rational-function algebra upstairs is again exactly k.
This argument works even when $p$ divides $|G|$; no averaging or
trace division is used.

The inverse image of S is a finite covering graph of $\Gamma_S$.
Every connected component of it is a clump for the lifted diagram,
because the torsors and both maps are finite étale. The preceding
one-clump lemma shows that there can be only one. For a principal
G-cover of a connected graph, the number of components is the index
of its based monodromy image in G. Hence that image is all of G.

## Recovering morphisms and invariant subspaces

For compatible finite étale covers, use their finite fibers as sets.
The endpoint actions generate a finite subgroup of the permutation
group of the common fiber. Applied to this group, the torsor theorem
says the clump graph has the full joint image. Graph-equivariant set
maps are therefore exactly the maps descending to both endpoint
covers with their given source matching. This proves full faithfulness
for finite covers, including disconnected ones.

Choose a point of S_Z and use the specified source identification
to identify the two coefficient fibers. For finitely many systems,
let G be the finite group generated by their endpoint monodromy
images in the corresponding product of general linear groups.
The associated principal G-torsors form the actual compatible
diagram. Its component graph is connected: the endpoint images
generate G, while the source identification identifies their fibers.
Equivalently, the common functions on the finite G-sets are the
functions invariant under these generating subgroups, hence constants.

The clump graph has the same full image G by the theorem. A fiber
linear map is therefore invariant under the clump transport exactly
when it intertwines G, equivalently when it descends to both endpoint
local systems and agrees on Z. The same argument treats invariant
subspaces and proves full faithfulness, without semisimplicity.

Over $\overline{\mathbf F}_\ell$, both finite endpoint images and
the specified identification involve finitely many matrix entries.
They lie in a common finite field, so their generated group is finite.
This explains the stated sufficient condition and does not extend
it to arbitrary characteristic-zero gluing matrices.

## Uniform arithmetic descent with the clump split

Let $\sigma$ be arithmetic q-Frobenius acting on geometric objects.
Since every vertex and edge of the clump is rational over $\mathbf F_q$,
the restriction of a compatible cover and that of its $\sigma$-twist
have a canonical identification as coverings of the same finite graph.
One can describe it by relative Frobenius on their finite étale fibers;
it is functorial in every vertex and edge map.

Full faithfulness lifts this graph identification to a UNIQUE
isomorphism of the entire compatible covers. The resulting isomorphism
is an effective descent datum. To check continuity and the cocycle,
choose r so the cover, its given matching and ALL its clump fiber
points are defined over $\mathbf F_{q^r}$. The rfold product of the
lifted isomorphism restricts to the identity on the clump under this
field model. Faithfulness makes it the identity globally. Finite
Galois descent now gives the required $\mathbf F_q$ model.

In this descent structure Frobenius fixes each geometric point above
the clump, so these fibers split completely. Any other such structure
has the same graph identification and is equal to it by faithfulness.
The assertion fixes the specified geometric cover and its matching;
it does not assert that every pre-existing arithmetic form is split.

## The Picard corollary and exact-order connectedness

Let $\tau\in P$ have exact order N prime to p, and choose a common
trivialization $\tau^N\simeq\mathcal O$. Its cyclic torsors on X,Y,Z
form a common principal $\mu_N$ diagram. Its common regular-function
algebra decomposes into the character spaces
\[
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{N-1}H^0_{\rm common}(\tau^{-j})=k.
\]
For $0<j<N$, a nonzero section would have no zeros, since its
endpoint lines have degree zero, and would trivialize the common
line $\tau^j$, contrary to exact order. Thus the component graph
is connected even when individual torsor curves are not.

Suppose $\operatorname{res}_S(\tau)=0$. Choose a nonzero compatible
section of its fibers over the clump vertices. Its Nth power divided
by the chosen common trivialization is constant across the connected
clump graph. Rescale the section so this constant is one. Its N
multiples by $\mu_N$ are then N disjoint sections of the lifted
principal graph, making N disjoint connected clumps. The finite-torsor
theorem forces N=1. This proves injectivity on prime-to-p torsion.

Finally, absence of a shared regular one-form excludes p-primary
common torsion by the established
[logarithmic torsion criterion](saturated_divisor_relations.md).
This extends the detection to the whole torsion group in that case.
