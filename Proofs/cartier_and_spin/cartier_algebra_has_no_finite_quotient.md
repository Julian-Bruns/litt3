# Reduced jets normally generate the full infinitesimal coordinate group

1 October2026.
[Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/cartier_algebra_has_no_finite_quotient.md).
The only geometric input is the
[actual full Cartier monodromy identification](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/first_cartier_full_monodromy.md).
The group calculation below is functorial over arbitrary $k$-algebras;
it does not discard nonreduced translation directions or assume a
simultaneous Galois source.

## Full coordinate groups

Let $q=p^r\ge2$. For a $k$-algebra $R$, an element of $\mathcal G_q(R)$
is the substitution
\[
h(t)=b+a_1t+\cdots+a_{q-1}t^{q-1},\qquad b^q=0,\quad a_1\in R^\times.
\]
It has the unique factorization $h=\tau_b\circ j$, where
$\tau_b(t)=t+b$ and $j(t)=h(t)-b$ belongs to $J_q(R)$.
Translation is an automorphism because $b^q=0$ and $q$ is a
characteristic power. This establishes the scheme factorization
$\alpha_q\times J_q\simeq\mathcal G_q$; it is not asserted to be
a semidirect product. The reduced subgroup
$J_q\simeq\mathbf G_m\times\mathbf A^{q-2}$ is connected.

Choose $c\in k^\times$ with $c\ne1$, and let $s_c(t)=ct$.
It belongs to $J_q(k)$. For every nilpotent translation over every
$R$, direct composition gives
\[
s_c\tau_b s_c^{-1}\tau_b^{-1}=\tau_{(c-1)b}.
\]
Let $K$ be any normal closed subgroup scheme of $\mathcal G_q$
containing $J_q$. This commutator lies in $K(R)$. Since $c-1$ is
a unit, every element of $\alpha_q(R)$ is of the form $(c-1)b$.
Thus $K$ contains all translations, as a subgroup functor, and the
factorization proves $K=\mathcal G_q$. This proves normal generation
without a Lie-algebra argument or a claim about only geometric points.

Now let $f:\mathcal G_q\to Q$ be a homomorphism to a finite group
scheme. Its restriction to $J_q$ is trivial. Indeed a map from a
reduced scheme factors through the reduction of a finite scheme;
over the perfect algebraically closed field that reduction is a
finite étale group scheme. A connected source has constant image,
and the identity forces the constant to be the group identity.
The scheme-theoretic kernel of $f$ is a normal closed subgroup
containing $J_q$, and normal generation gives $\ker f=\mathcal G_q$.
Thus $f$ is trivial, including when $Q$ is nonreduced or noncommutative.

## The projective normalizer branch

Use the fractional-linear realization of $\mathcal N_p$ from the
actual monodromy theorem. Fppf locally an element has the form
\[
h(t)=\frac{at+b}{ct+d},\qquad b^p=0,
\]
with invertible determinant. Since $b$ is nilpotent, $a,d$ are units
locally, and $\beta=h(0)=b/d$ satisfies $\beta^p=0$.
Then $\tau_{-\beta}\circ h$ fixes zero and belongs to the Borel
$B$. The value $\beta$ and the factorization are independent of
the matrix representative and agree on overlaps. Equivalently
$\alpha_p\times B\to\mathcal N_p$, $(\beta,j)\mapsto\tau_\beta j$,
is a scheme isomorphism. Again this is a factorization, not a
semidirect-product assertion.

The Borel is smooth and connected, so a homomorphism to any finite
group scheme kills it. The same constant scalings $s_c\in B(k)$
and nilpotent translations lie in $\mathcal N_p$, and their
commutator is $\tau_{(c-1)\beta}$. Thus the normal closure of $B$
contains all of $\alpha_p$ and then the whole normalizer.
Every finite homomorphism from $\mathcal N_p$ is consequently trivial.

## Exact categorical consequence

For the original actual source comparisons, the full neutral tensor
subcategory generated by the algebra has Tannaka group equal to
$\mathcal G_p$ or $\mathcal N_p$, by the geometric input. Under
neutral Tannaka equivalence an object, including any tensor
subquotient, is a representation of this actual image group. If its
common monodromy is finite, its representation factors through its
finite scheme-theoretic image. The group result makes that image
trivial. Hence the object is a direct sum of the unit common bundle,
with the trivial comparison representation.

The fact that the object is a SUBQUOTIENT of an algebra representation
does not turn a subgroup of monodromy into its quotient. Its monodromy
is a quotient image of the WHOLE actual group. In particular the
finite normal subgroup $H[F]$ cannot simply be substituted for that
whole group.

In the no-oper branch the actual monodromy at each height is the
full group $\mathcal G_{p^r}$, with compatible Frobenius subalgebras.
Every object constructed by finitely many sums, tensors, duals and
subquotients from the tower lies at some largest height, since each
smaller algebra is a subalgebra of that height. The same argument
therefore makes every finite-common-monodromy object in the entire
tower-generated category trivial.

## Strong semistability inside the generated category

Now assume $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_p$ and let $E$ be a common
strongly semistable degree-zero object in the category. The finite
field boundedness and Frobenius-periodicity argument used in
[common coefficient rigidity](../../Theorems/shared_tensors/common_finite_coefficients.md)
gives one exponent $r$ for which both endpoint bundles of
$F_{\mathrm{abs}}^{r*}E$ are finite-étale-trivial. Their comparison
on the ACTUAL source is retained by functorial absolute Frobenius.
This does not identify distinct relative twists as $k$-curves.

The Frobenius pullback stays in the generated tensor category:
$F_{\mathrm{abs}}^*E$ is the subbundle of $\operatorname{Sym}^pE$
locally spanned by the $p$th powers of a basis. The transition matrices
are the entrywise $p$th powers, with their scalar Frobenius retained.
Iteration proves the same at height $r$.

Choose endpoint finite étale trivializations and any connected
component of their product over the given source. On this smooth
proper connected refinement the comparison is a matrix of global
regular functions, hence a constant invertible matrix. Equivalently
identify the two finite local-system fibers at one source point.
The finitely many endpoint monodromy matrices and this constant
comparison have entries in one finite field. Their jointly generated
matrix group is finite, and the specified source restrictions agree.
Thus $F_{\mathrm{abs}}^{r*}E$ has finite COMMON monodromy, not merely
two separate finite endpoint presentations.

By the already proved finite-quotient result this Frobenius object
is trivial in the common category. For the original representation
of the actual generated Tannaka group, all entrywise $p^r$ powers
are therefore the identity representation. Its scheme-theoretic
image lies in the finite group scheme $\mathrm{GL}(E)[F^r]$.
Applying the finite-quotient result once more makes $E$ itself
trivial, including any possible infinitesimal monodromy.

If $E$ has arbitrary slope and is common strongly semistable, its
dual and tensor product are strongly semistable on both endpoints;
in particular $\operatorname{End}E$ is degree zero. It belongs to
the same tensor category and hence is the trivial common object.
The conjugation representation on $\operatorname{End}(E)$ is
therefore trivial scheme-theoretically. The centralizer of the full
matrix algebra in $\mathrm{GL}(E)$ is its scalar $\mathbf G_m$,
also over arbitrary coefficient algebras. The original representation
is consequently one character acting on every basis vector. This
proves the scalar-isotypic assertion.

In the full coordinate case the
[exact character calculation](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/truncated_coordinate_algebra_model.md)
is $X^*(\mathcal G_q)=\mathbf Z\chi_q$, $\chi_q=a_1^q$.
Section1 of the actual monodromy proof identifies the transition
$a_1^q$ with the derivative of the coordinate change on the
Frobenius-twisted endpoint. Thus its associated cotangent line is
the actual canonical line (or its dual if all frame actions are
reversed). Its character powers are exactly the integral canonical
powers. This uses the full coordinate-jet torsor identification;
the determinant relation alone would leave a torsion ambiguity.

## Scheme-theoretic independence of common finite coefficients

Take the neutral tensor category jointly generated by the actual
Cartier object and an independently given common finite coefficient
$V$. Let its group be $G$, let $H$ be the Cartier image and let $Q$
be the finite image of $V$. The scheme-theoretic joint image
$K\subset H\times Q$ has faithfully flat projections onto both
factors: both are quotient images of the same actual group $G$.
No replacement of the two source comparisons has been made.

Put $N=K\cap(1\times Q)$, identified with a closed subgroup of $Q$.
It is the kernel of $K\to H$. It is normal in $Q$: fppf locally
every element of $Q$ lifts to an element of $K$, and conjugation
by that lift preserves this kernel. The finite group-scheme quotient
$Q/N$ exists. The homomorphism $K\to Q/N$ is trivial on $N$.
Faithfully flat descent across $K\to H$ therefore gives a unique
homomorphism $\psi:H\to Q/N$.

Explicitly, for an arbitrary base algebra and a point $h$ of $H$,
choose an fppf local lift $(h,q)$ in $K$ and send it to the class
of $q$. Two lifts differ by $N$, so the class is independent of
the lift, agrees on overlaps, and descends. Multiplication of lifts
proves that this is a group homomorphism, also on nonreduced test
algebras. Since $K\to Q$ is faithfully flat, $\psi$ has full
scheme-theoretic image $Q/N$.

The no-finite-quotient theorem for $H$ makes $\psi$ trivial.
It follows that $Q/N$ is trivial and $N=Q$. Thus $K$ contains
$1\times Q$. Fppf locally lift an arbitrary $h$ to $(h,q)\in K$
and multiply by $(1,q^{-1})\in K$. This puts $(h,1)$ in $K$.
Descent, or equality of the resulting subgroup functors, proves
$K=H\times Q$. This proof needs neither geometric-point arguments
nor semisimplicity of representations of $Q$.

Representations of this product act on $E,F$ through $H$ and on $V$
through $Q$. Taking invariants of
$V^*\otimes\operatorname{Hom}_k(E,F)$ first under $Q$ and then
under $H$ gives exactly the Hom formula in the statement. Every
resulting map factors through the maximal trivial quotient of $V$.
The formula still allows maps from a nontrivial extension possessing
such a quotient.

The kernel of $G\to Q$ maps faithfully flatly onto $H$, because
the joint image is the product. Accordingly the exact common
$Q$-torsor restriction keeps the Cartier monodromy image equal to
$H$. This is the categorical persistence assertion. When representing
it geometrically, one must retain the entire compatible comparison
diagram implementing that restriction. Selecting a single connected
source component and omitting other edges is not, without further
argument, the restriction to this kernel. Arbitrary one-leg refinements
are likewise outside the assertion.

## The finite kernel remains a different torsor

An actual algebra-frame torsor $\mathscr P\to C$ is an $H$-torsor.
The quotient $\mathscr P/H[F]$ is the coordinate-jet torsor of a
Frobenius twist, and $\mathscr P\to\mathscr P/H[F]$ is the finite
kernel torsor. Its base is the jet torsor, not $C$. Reducing to a
finite torsor on $C$ would require additional compatible descent
or a finite quotient, which is not supplied by the kernel's existence.
The connected positive-dimensional reduced coordinate group is
essential to the actual monodromy identification.

This categorical exclusion applies exactly to the Cartier-generated
subcategory. It does not forbid an independently constructed common
finite coefficient outside it and does not settle the original
two-map common-cover problem.
