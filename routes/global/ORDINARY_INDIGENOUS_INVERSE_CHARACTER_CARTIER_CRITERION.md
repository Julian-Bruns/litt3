# Ordinary indigenous bundles and the inverse Cartier character

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: `/root/ordinary_indigenous_inverse_character_check`.
Status: source-checked deduction with direct regularity and descent proof.

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic 5, let C/k be smooth,
projective and connected of genus g>=2, and let d be 2 or 4. Suppose

\[
0\ne s\in H^0(C,\omega_C^d),\qquad
C_{d-1}(s^{5-4/d})=s,\qquad
\operatorname{div}(s)=(d/2)D
\]

with D reduced. Let pi:P->C be a connected component of the normalized
canonical root cover, with tautological differential eta satisfying
eta^d=pi^*s. Its actual cyclic deck group G has order dividing d. Write
chi for the character of eta.

**Theorem.** The indigenous projective bundle associated to this
deformation datum is unmarked, active, nilpotent, and admissible. It is
ordinary in Mochizuki's sense if and only if the inverse-Frobenius-
semilinear endomorphism

\[
T_s:H^0(C,\omega_C^2)\longrightarrow H^0(C,\omega_C^2),
\qquad t\longmapsto C_1(s^{4/d}t)
\tag{1}
\]

is bijective. Equivalently, it is ordinary if and only if

\[
C_P:H^0(P,\omega_P)_{\chi^{-1}}
\longrightarrow H^0(P,\omega_P)_{\chi^{-1}}
\tag{2}
\]

is bijective. This eigenspace has dimension 3g-3. The isomorphism
intertwining (1) and (2) is k-linear and is given by

\[
I(t)=\frac{\pi^*t}{\eta}.
\tag{3}
\]

## 1. The indigenous object and its square Hasse invariant

The [deformation-data translation](CARTIER_EIGENFORMS_AS_MULTIPLICATIVE_DEFORMATION_DATA.md)
shows that eta is Cartier-fixed. The local invariant is 3/2 at D and
1 elsewhere. Thus the associated bundle has no markings and its spike
divisor is zero. Its p-curvature is nowhere zero, so its dual is
surjective: this is admissibility in Mochizuki's Chapter II,
Definition 2.4 (PDF p.67).

For the construction and local formulas used below, see Bouw–Wewers,
[*Indigenous bundles with nilpotent p-curvature*](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0505275v2),
Section 4.3, equation (6), Proposition 4.8, and Theorem 4.11.

Choose a separating rational parameter z, put theta=dz and
partial=partial/partial z, and write s=a theta^d. Set v=a^(-4/d).
The generic Hodge line is generated by e0, and in the normalized
rank-two bundle the other basis vector is partial^{tensor 5}. We have

\[
\nabla_{\partial}e_0=v\partial^{\otimes5},\qquad
\Psi(\partial^{\otimes5})(e_0)=-\partial^{\otimes5}.
\]

The Hodge projection from Ad(E) to the tangent line uses the inverse
Kodaira–Spencer map. Since Kodaira–Spencer sends partial to the map
e0 |-> v partial^{tensor 5}, the projected p-curvature sends
partial^{tensor 5} to -v^{-1} partial. Consequently its square Hasse
invariant is

\[
H_E=-s^{4/d}\in H^0(C,\omega_C^4)
=\operatorname{Hom}(\tau_C^{\otimes5},\tau_C).
\tag{4}
\]

The identity is computed rationally, hence determines the global
morphism. Its divisor is 2D, as required for a double supersingular
divisor. The p-curvature sign is the convention nabla_partial^5 minus
nabla_(partial^5); the minus sign has no effect on bijectivity below.

There is no determinant-convention obstruction. The normalized
rank-two bundle has determinant tau_C^{tensor 5}. Choose a theta
characteristic kappa with kappa^2=omega_C and tensor by kappa^5 with
its canonical zero-p-curvature connection. This gives trivial
determinant with connection and preserves the projective bundle,
adjoint connection, p-curvature, and the computation (4).

## 2. Mochizuki's ordinary condition

In Mochizuki,
[*A Theory of Ordinary p-adic Curves*](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/A%20Theory%20of%20Ordinary%20p-adic%20Curves.pdf),
Chapter II, Proposition 2.6(1), PDF p.69, the square Hasse invariant is
precisely the Hodge projection of the p-curvature. On PDF p.73, its
dual is tensored with omega_C and composed with the Frobenius trace.
Lemma 2.11 identifies that trace with Cartier; Proposition 2.12,
pp.73–74, identifies the resulting operator on quadratic differentials
with the dual of the induced Frobenius on H^1(C,tau_C).

Using (4), this operator is -C_1(s^{4/d}t). Chapter II, Definition 3.1,
PDF p.80, defines ordinary by invertibility of that induced Frobenius.
Duality therefore proves the equivalence with (1). Relative Frobenius
gives linear maps to or from Frobenius twists; over the perfect field k,
the displayed absolute Cartier notation expresses the same test as an
inverse-Frobenius-semilinear endomorphism.

## 3. Regularity, descent, and the exact character

The root cover has ramification index 2 over D, even if the full root
cover is disconnected. At Q over x in D, tame differential pullback
gives ord_Q(eta)=2. If t is a rational quadratic differential with
ord_x(t)=h, then

\[
\operatorname{ord}_Q\left(\frac{\pi^*t}{\eta}\right)
=\bigl(2h+2(2-1)\bigr)-2=2h.
\tag{5}
\]

Away from D the cover is etale and eta is nonvanishing. Thus (3) maps
regular quadratic differentials to regular differentials, of character
chi^{-1}. Conversely, if alpha is a regular chi^{-1}-differential,
eta alpha is invariant and descends to a rational quadratic differential
t on C. Formula (5) forces t to be regular at D, and etale local descent
gives regularity elsewhere. This proves that (3) is a k-linear
isomorphism. Invariant rational tensors descend through the separable
function-field extension; regular descent is justified by (5), not by
an assumption about ramified descent.

Write eta=b theta and t=c theta^2, so b^d=a and b^4=a^{4/d}.
Cartier commutes with separable pullback and satisfies
C(f^5 beta)=f C(beta). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi^*T_s(t)
 &=\theta C_P(b^4c\theta)\\
 &=b\theta C_P((c/b)\theta)\\
 &=\eta C_P(I(t)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

Division by eta proves the intertwining assertion. Since G has order
dividing 4, its characters take values in F_5^times, so Cartier
preserves this particular character space.

## 4. Polarization compares the two character kernels

**Lemma.** For any smooth projective curve P/k with a finite cyclic
automorphism group of order dividing 4, and any character rho of that
group, one has

\[
\dim\ker(C_P|H^0(P,\omega_P)_\rho)
=\dim\ker(C_P|H^0(P,\omega_P)_{\rho^{-1}}).
\tag{7}
\]

Here is a proof which does not identify the two Cartier matrices.
Let H=H^1_dR(P/k), with its Frobenius F, Verschiebung V, and perfect
alternating cup-product pairing. The exact conventions are recorded in
Cais–Ulmer,
[*p-torsion for unramified Artin–Schreier covers of curves*](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16346v2),
Section 2, equation (2.1), and Section 4, equation (4.2) and the following
paragraph. In these conventions

\[
\operatorname{im}V=\ker F=H^0(P,\omega_P),\qquad
\operatorname{im}F=\ker V,\qquad
V|H^0(P,\omega_P)=C_P,
\]

and F and V are adjoint up to Frobenius on the pairing. All of these
identifications are compatible with automorphisms of P. Since the
characters are F_5-valued, F and V preserve each H_rho. Set
A_rho=ker(F|H_rho), B_rho=ker(V|H_rho), and
a_rho=dim(A_rho intersect B_rho). This last integer is the left side
of (7).

The BT1 identities and rank-nullity give
dim A_rho + dim B_rho = dim H_rho. Consequently

\[
a_\rho=\dim H_\rho/(A_\rho+B_\rho).
\]

The G-invariant perfect pairing pairs H_rho perfectly with
H_(rho^{-1}). Adjointness and the BT1 identities give, for orthogonals
taken in H_(rho^{-1}),

\[
A_\rho^\perp=A_{\rho^{-1}},\qquad
B_\rho^\perp=B_{\rho^{-1}}.
\]

Thus the dual of H_rho/(A_rho+B_rho) is
A_(rho^{-1}) intersect B_(rho^{-1}), proving (7). This is a direct
linear-algebra deduction from the source identities, not a claim in
the cited paper about the eigenform schemes considered here.

**Corollary.** For s in the theorem, its indigenous bundle is ordinary
if and only if the finite equimultiple eigenform intersection
E_d(C) intersect S_(d/2) is reduced at s. More precisely, the dimension
of its tangent space equals dim ker T_s.

Indeed, the [equimultiple tangent criterion](CARTIER_EIGENFORM_EQUIMULTIPLE_TANGENT_CHARACTER_CRITERION.md)
identifies that tangent space with the chi Cartier kernel; (7)
equates its dimension with the chi^{-1} kernel tested in (2).
A finite local k-scheme is reduced at its k-point exactly when its
tangent space vanishes. For d=2 the stratum is open, so the conclusion
also tests reducedness of E_2(C) itself. For d=4 it concerns the
equimultiple intersection, not the whole quartic pool.

## Consequences and boundaries

The two direct constructions use opposite characters. Their Cartier
kernels have equal dimensions by the separate polarization argument
above; no canonical identification of those kernels is asserted.

The theorem supplies a concrete additional hypothesis for the
[simultaneous etale lifting criterion](ORDINARY_INDIGENOUS_DATA_SIMULTANEOUS_ETALE_LIFTING.md):
for a compatible normalized eigenform on each of the two targets and
their specified common etale source, require the corresponding operator
(1), or equivalently block (2), to be bijective on all three curves.
The resulting ordinary indigenous projective bundles are compatible
with etale pullback: their rational connection construction and its
unique extension commute with that pullback. The conditional lifting
criterion then applies.

Neither reducedness of D nor ordinariness on a target is claimed to
force ordinariness on an etale source. This is a condition on a specified
character block, not a test of ordinary Cartier on C and not an assertion
that all of P is ordinary.
