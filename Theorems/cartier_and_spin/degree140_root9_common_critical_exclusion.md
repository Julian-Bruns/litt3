# A root-nine square has no common critical point

Version1,29 September2026. Use the fixed-r=[9] degree140 family on
its original ratio open and arbitrary nonzero scale, as in the
[critical-cover theorem](degree140_root9_critical_scale_budget.md).
After the actual primitive change Z=yW-B0, let
\(\bar D=3g_2W^2+2g_3W+g_4\).

No geometric square residual has an affine point of X where
\(g_2=g_3=g_4=0\). This includes all cubic branch points, all nine
points above t=0, arbitrary geometric ratio fields and all scales.
The corresponding square/common-critical incidence scheme is empty.

The complete marked common-critical incidence off P*t is reduced
of length304. Its eight residue degrees over K are
\(2,3,4,12,13,21,110,139\). At each of the three t-endpoints the
complete marked incidence is reduced of length36. Their residue
degrees are respectively
\((1,14,21),(12,24),(4,6,6,9,11)\).
All412 marked points have all-scale polynomial Bezout exclusions.
At the ten cubic branch points the incidence is empty even before
imposing any square equation.

Consequently the homogeneous critical quadratic defines a finite flat
degree-two scheme over affine X at every hypothetical square ratio.
It is generically connected by the critical-discriminant theorem; it
need not be etale. The fixed coefficient content of the residual scale
quadratic is supported on the nine points over t=0 and the marked
cubic branch ([9],0). The latter support is retained because the
leading coefficient of the actual degree-ten polynomial contains x-[9].

This is not a proof of zero fixed content, square-locus emptiness,
or the original common-cover problem.
