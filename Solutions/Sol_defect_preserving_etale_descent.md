## 1. Trace and the two relevant linear operators

Write d=deg h, and use normalized trace d^(-1)Tr_h. Since h is etale,
T_T=h*T_C and omega_T=h*omega_C. On quadratics and on V=H1(Tangent),
pullback followed by normalized trace is the identity. This works
without Galoisness. We obtain direct decompositions into pulled-back
and trace-zero summands.

For the infinitesimal determinant-of-p-curvature map L_r, trace
commutes with L_r. One can check this etale locally on C, where the
cover is a disjoint union and trace is summation: linearizing the same
local differential polynomial on each sheet commutes with summation.
Equivalently, in a separating coordinate, its differential operator
has coefficients on C and field trace commutes with differentiation.

The target is the space of horizontal regular10-differentials. It has
dimension3g-3 and is the span of the fifth powers of a quadratic basis,
by [the scalar model](Sol_nilpotent_scalar_model.md), Sections1--2.
Trace preserves this space: for any tensor q, Tr(q^5)=Tr(q)^5. Thus
the derivative is block diagonal for these decompositions. Equal
defects and equal source/target dimensions make its trace-zero block
invertible.

The identification of the nilpotent tangent defect with dim ker(Psi)
is the infinitesimal Verschiebung duality recorded in
[forced_canonical_witt_endpoint](Sol_forced_canonical_witt_endpoint.md),
Section1. The same conclusion holds for the semilinear Hodge variation operator
Psi on V. In relative notation it is Frobenius followed by multiplication
by the pulled-back square-Hasse tensor. Relative Frobenius commutes
with trace for finite etale maps, and the projection formula handles
that tensor. Normalize by d in F5, so the normalization is unchanged
by Frobenius. The trace projector therefore commutes with Psi, with
all relative twists retained. Its trace-zero summand has no kernel
by(D), and hence is bijective over the perfect field k. In particular

    ker(Psi_T)=h*ker(Psi_C),
    h*:coker(Psi_C)→coker(Psi_T) is an isomorphism.        (1)

The assertion about cokernels already follows from trace and equal
dimensions; it does not use a simultaneous Galois closure.

## 2. The entire fixed-curve germ

Let x be coordinates in the pulled-back quadratic space and y in
its trace-zero complement. Center the connection spaces at r and h*r.
Let F_C and F_T be the polynomial determinant maps to horizontal
regular10-forms from the scalar model. Their scheme-theoretic zero
fibers are precisely the nilpotent schemes, including nilpotents.

Project F_T(x,y) to the trace-zero target and call the result G(x,y).
Etale naturality gives G(x,0)=0 IDENTICALLY as a polynomial, not just
on nilpotent points. Therefore

                            G(x,y)=A(x,y)y

for a square polynomial matrix A. Its constant matrix is the invertible
trace-zero derivative from Section1. In the completed local ring it
is a unit matrix, so the ideal generated by G is exactly the ideal(y).
The remaining equations modulo(y) are the original equations F_C(x).
This proves the scheme isomorphism in Part1. In fact the same argument
works on the open set det A!=0 and in formal equicharacteristic families
of the specified etale cover.

In particular, the length-three germ of
[the explicit bad double](Sol_bad_double_cubic_defect.md) remains
k[[w]]/(w³) on EVERY prime-to5 cover satisfying(D). No large-genus
nonlinear equation calculation is required to determine that germ.

## 3. Descending one next Witt step

Use the higher inverse-Cartier lifting fact in
[forced_canonical_witt_endpoint](Sol_forced_canonical_witt_endpoint.md),
Sections1--2, including its full input tuple and square-trivial twist.
The inputs are: above an already compatible C_n, a curve C_(n+1)
has Hodge obstruction rho_C in V_C; variation by xi changes it by
-Psi_C(xi); the construction is etale-functorial. Hodge lifts, when
they exist, and projective graded identifications are unique because
H0(T_C)=0 and maximal Higgs graded automorphisms are scalar.

Suppose a compatible upper lift T_(n+1) has been given, and its W_n
reduction, with full previous data, has already descended to C_n and
the original h. Choose ANY curve lift C_(n+1)^0. It exists because
smooth projective curves have no H2 tangent obstruction. Lift the
original cover to this curve; call the resulting reference source
T_(n+1)^0. Its Hodge obstruction is h*rho_C(C_(n+1)^0).

Let xi_T be the actual difference between the given compatible upper
curve and this reference, in the marked curve-lift torsor V_T. Since
the given upper Hodge obstruction is zero, the variation formula gives

                 Psi_T(xi_T)=h*rho_C(C_(n+1)^0).          (2)

Project to the trace-zero summand of V_T. The right side is zero;
bijectivity from Section1 forces the trace-zero part of xi_T to be
zero. Thus xi_T=h*xi_C for a unique xi_C. Change the lower reference
curve by xi_C. Its induced cover now equals the GIVEN upper curve,
with its marking. Equation(2), or injectivity of pullback applied to
the new Hodge obstruction, proves that the new lower lift is compatible.

The Hodge line and projective graded identification descend as well:
the lower ones exist by this vanishing, and their pullbacks are the
unique upper ones. The previous periodicity twist is the unique lift
of the initial two-torsion line, so it is retained. This supplies the
complete input tuple at the next level, not only an underlying bundle.

Uniqueness of the lower curve and map follows from uniqueness of xi_C
and the marked deformation monomorphism for finite etale covers of
hyperbolic curves; see
[etale_refinement_deformations](Sol_etale_refinement_deformations.md).
No averaging of a nonlinear family or of group actions is used.

## 4. The full tower and its algebraization

At W2 the canonical marked lift and initial flow are already compatible
with h, by admissible_two_leg_w2_lifts. Apply Section3 inductively.
The descended lifts and maps are unique, so they form a compatible
full tower whenever the supplied upper tower does. Conversely, any
lower compatible tower pulls back, by the same etale naturality.

The formal lower curve is smooth and proper, with an ample canonical
bundle; Grothendieck existence algebraizes it over W(k). The resulting
algebraic etale cover is the unique cover lifting h, by the proper
henselian finite-etale equivalence. Its truncations identify it with
the supplied upper curve. These are the same algebraization steps
already used in etale_refinement_deformations. This establishes Part2.

In particular it is NOT necessary that C be ordinary. The trace-zero
complement, not the entire space V_T, is what must have invertible Psi.

## 5. A finite nonordinary-source partner branch

Take a coreless actual X←Z→Y_t as in the statement. The character and
two-leg strictness results in
[symplectic_p_cover_section_growth](Sol_symplectic_p_cover_section_growth.md),
Sections1--7, show the following from the Galois Y-leg and defect1:

* r_X is ordinary;
* g factors as Z−h→C−pi→Y_t, with pi one of the ten known bad doubles;
* g(C)=3, d(C)=d(Z)=1, and deg h is prime to5.

Both original maps remain in this setup. Lift the original f-cover to
the full canonical ordinary lift X^can. This gives a full smooth proper
Z_f^can with its pulled-back compatible filtered projective flow. Its
W2 reduction agrees with the one induced from Y_t and C, by the
canonical first-lift dictionary. Part2 therefore descends this ENTIRE
given tower along the original h. We obtain

                      X^can ← Z_f^can → C^lift            (3)

with both maps finite etale. This constructs the second actual map in
(3), rather than invoking ordinary-source canonical compatibility.

For a fixed r_X, the curve X^can is fixed. Characteristic-zero
fixed-genus partner finiteness bounds the isomorphism classes of
C^lift with genus3, with no bound on the degree of h or f. We use
exactly the source theorem and specialization argument already recorded
in [ordinary_source_partner_finiteness](Sol_ordinary_source_partner_finiteness.md),
Section2; its ordinary-source lifting argument is NOT being used.
Uniqueness of stable models makes distinct special-fiber C classes
give distinct geometric generic classes. Hence only finitely many C
occur. Each has finitely many degree-two etale quotients, since these
are quotients by involutions in its finite automorphism group. The
established bound120 on family parameters per Y-isomorphism class
then proves finiteness of t. Finally, there are only finitely many
r_X, by the fixed-curve nilpotent scalar count; take their finite union.

The original pi:C→Y_t need not lift to C^lift. Thus(3) is not a full
lift of the original span and does not repair its W3 mismatch. The
gain is to place a bounded-genus intermediate curve in a fixed
characteristic-zero common-cover class. It gives a new finiteness
mechanism precisely in a branch excluded from the ordinary-source
theorem. Section6 makes that count effective without changing the
parameter.

## 6. Effective exclusion for the original main choice

This section is new in Version2 and passed a separate scoped review
after the initial Sections1--5 audit. The explicit extension of
[the arithmetic count](Sol_arithmetic_genus_two_reduction_bound.md),
Section5, proves that ANY fixed characteristic-zero curve has fewer
than M=2^80000000 genus-three common-etale-cover partners. The bound
is uniform in the fixed curve: arithmetic partners are counted using
genus129 homology covers; nonarithmetic partners are subgroups of one
commensurator, giving the smaller bound2^17000.

For each of the at most5^(3g(X)-3) nilpotent connections on X,
Section5 thus yields fewer than M possible geometric special-fiber
classes of C. Counting all nilpotent points rather than only the
ordinary active ones only enlarges the bound. Different lower lifts
with isomorphic geometric generic fibers have the same geometric
special fiber, by stable-model uniqueness; hence counting lifts does
not undercount possible C.

Every etale double of a genus-two curve is hyperelliptic of genus3.
For clarity, a nonzero two-torsion class is represented by two of the
six hyperelliptic branch points. Its double has field k(u,v,sqrt R),
where R is the product of their linear factors, or one factor if one
point is infinity. The intermediate k(u,sqrt R) has genus0, so this
field exhibits the hyperelliptic map of C. The involution is unique
in genus>=2. Thus Aut(C) modulo this involution acts faithfully on
the eight branch points on P1. A projectivity is determined by three
ordered distinct points, giving

                   |Aut(C)| <=2*8*7*6=672.

This argument is valid in characteristic5, including possible wild
automorphisms; it does not apply the characteristic-zero Hurwitz bound
to C. Every degree-two etale quotient C→Y is determined, up to target
isomorphism, by its deck involution. Thus there are at most672 such
Y classes. Each allows at most120 parameters in the displayed genus-two
family. At most80640 parameters can therefore give the same C class.

Combining these three counts yields exactly(B). If X/F_q is fixed,
coefficientwise q-Frobenius preserves the property of admitting the
specified kind of span: both maps, their matched connections, Galoisness
and defect are transported together. Therefore all [F_q(t):F_q]
distinct parameter conjugates belong to the finite set counted by(B).
This proves the parameter-degree bound without any prime-degree
assumption or need to identify the field of moduli of C.

For the current main X,5^24<2^56 and80640<2^17, so(B)<2^80000073.
The EXISTING selection is a prime degree greater than K, with
D=335999!, G=1+8D, L=336000² and K>=3^(4G²L). Since
D>=2^335998, we have D²>80000073, and

                        K>2^(D²)>2^80000073.

The originally chosen parameter is already outside(B). Thus the
Galois Y-leg, defect-one active branch cannot occur for this SAME
pair. The preceding no-full-lift theorem already excluded defect zero.
The minimum remaining active defect for a Galois Y-leg is consequently2.

This is an all-degree stratum exclusion, not a full common-cover proof.
In particular the argument does not assert that a non-Galois source
of defect1 retains that defect after its Y-leg Galois closure. Nor does
it make the original pi:C→Y lift: that map may still be the obstruction
for other endpoints or smaller parameters. The descent to C alone is
what permits the finite-partner argument.
