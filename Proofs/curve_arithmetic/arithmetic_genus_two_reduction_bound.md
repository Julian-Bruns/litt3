# Proof: count square-subgroup covers, then all geometric reductions

[Statement](../../Theorems/curve_arithmetic/arithmetic_genus_two_reduction_bound.md).
User-supplied Pro argument, integrated and source-checked by /root,
2026-09-09. Arithmetic audit PASS, /root/audit_arithmetic_reduction_bound,

## 1. A bounded cover reaches a norm-one group

For an arithmetic genus-two surface group Gamma, its subgroup Delta
generated by squares is the kernel of abelianization modulo2. Thus
Gamma/Delta=(Z/2)^4, and C=Delta\H has genus17 and area64pi.
[Takeuchi, Section3, final remark, printed p609](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/27/4/27_4_600/_pdf/-char/en)
says the square subgroup of an arithmetic Fuchsian group is derived
from a quaternion algebra. Apply this to Gamma's inverse image in
SL_2(R); its square subgroup projects exactly to Delta. Enlarging its
order to a maximal order gives Delta contained in Lambda=P O^1.
Therefore Lambda is cocompact and has area at most64pi.

Let F be the totally real center, n=[F:Q], d=disc(F)>0, and
P=product_(p|D_B)(Np-1) for the finite quaternion discriminant.
The norm-one covolume formula in
[Macasieb, Section2.2, equation(3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1519)
gives

    area(Lambda)=8pi*d^(3/2)*zeta_F(2)*P/(4pi²)^n,
    d^(3/2)*P <= 8(4pi²)^n.                         (1)

[Odlyzko, Theorem1(5), printed p210](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj1949/29/2/29_2_209/_pdf/-char/en)
gives d>=58.6^n exp(f_2-70), with f_2>=0, unconditionally.
In particular d>=50^n exp(-70). Since
50^(3/2)/(4pi²)>8 and e<3, equation(1) implies

    8^n < 8e^105 < 8*3^105 < 2^171.

The last inequality follows from 3^5<2^8. It is enough to retain

    n<=64,       d<=2^258,       P<=2^387.           (2)

Indeed 4pi²<64, so the right side of(1) is below2^387; also P>=1.

## 2. Count fields, quaternion algebras, and order types

The real-embedding lattice O_F has determinant sqrt(d). Minkowski's
second theorem for the sup-norm cube gives product lambda_i<=sqrt(d).
Every lambda_i>=1 by integrality of the nonzero algebraic norm. Hence
there are n independent integral a_i, all conjugates bounded by sqrt(d).

Set N=binom(n,2). For each pair of distinct embeddings, equality of
their values on sum b_i a_i is a proper hyperplane. Each hyperplane
contains at most(N+1)^(n-1) grid points in {0,...,N}^n. Their union does
not cover the grid. Thus a primitive integral alpha can be chosen with
all conjugates at most nN sqrt(d)<=n³ sqrt(d)<=2^147. For n=1 the field
is Q and needs no avoidance argument.

Every coefficient of its monic minimal polynomial is at most
2^64*(2^147)^64=2^9472<2^10000. Consequently the number of fields is

    <64*(2*2^10000+1)^64 <2^700000.                 (3)

For any such F, the number A_F(M) of integral ideals with norm<=M obeys

    A_F(M)<=M²*zeta_F(2)<=M²*zeta(2)^n<2^n M².      (4)

There are at most n primes of norm2; for q>=3, q<=(q-1)². Thus
N(D_B)<=2^n P²<=2^838. Equation(4) gives fewer than2^1800 finite
discriminants (the sharper exponent is1740). A quaternion algebra is
determined by its ramified places. Allow also64 choices of the one
split real place. These are upper bounds; including forbidden parity
sets or duplicate presentations only enlarges the count.

The type number of this indefinite quaternion algebra is at most the
restricted class number, and hence at most h_F^+<=2^n h_F; see
Macasieb Section2.3, equations(4)-(5). Minkowski's ideal bound gives
each ideal class an integral representative of norm<=sqrt(d): apply
the first convex-body theorem to an ideal a and use (alpha)a^(-1).
By(4), h_F<2^n d<=2^322. The number of maximal-order types is therefore
less than2^386<2^400. No trace-field/field-of-moduli identification is
used here.

For fixed data the real realization is unique up to GL_2(R)-conjugacy;
allow a factor2 for orientation. The total number of PSL_2(R)-conjugacy
classes of relevant norm-one groups satisfies

    N_Lambda <2^700000*2^1800*64*2^400*2 <2^710000. (5)

## 3. Count covers and recover every original curve

The orientable hyperbolic orbifold minimum area pi/21 gives
[Lambda:Delta]<=1344. If Lambda has compact signature(h;m_1,...,m_s),
the area bound implies 2h-2+s/2<=32, hence4h+s<=68. Its usual
presentation has at most2h+s<=68 generators. An r-generated group
has at most(j!)^r subgroups of index j, by its coset actions on j labels.
Therefore each Lambda contributes at most

    1344*(1344!)^68 <2^1010000                     (6)

possible Delta. For the numerical bound, use1344<2^11; the resulting
exponent is11+68*1344*11=1005323<1010000.

Retain only torsion-free Delta of genus17. Its curve C has
|Aut(C)|<=84*16=1344. The original curve is C/(Gamma/Delta).
There are at most2^1344<2^2000 possible subgroups of Aut(C), even
counting every subset. This handles lattices Gamma not contained in
Lambda, and avoids restricting Gamma to be derived or congruence.
Equations(5)-(6) now give

    |S_2|<2^(710000+1010000+2000)
         =2^1722000 <2^2000000.                    (7)

## 4. One fixed p-adic place counts all reductions

First S_2 is Galois-stable. An arbitrary arithmetic curve has an actual
finite etale span with a torsion-free congruence Shimura curve T, using
the core in Gamma of its intersection with a commensurable congruence
lattice. The cover and its finite deck group descend to bar(Q), and
their quotient recovers the original curve. Finite etale proper-cover
descent is the proper base-extension theorem in
[Stacks, Lemma58.9.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A49).

Galois conjugates of T are again quaternionic congruence Shimura curves:
[Kucharczyk, Proposition5.3, Theorem5.4 and its finite-level formulation,
printed pp228-229](https://content.algebraicgeometry.nl/2018-2/2018-2-007.pdf).
Conjugating the span makes the original curve's conjugate commensurable
with such a T, hence arithmetic of the same genus. This proves stability
for the noncongruence classes too.

Fix iota:bar(Q)->bar(Q_p) and its geometric residue identification.
Stable-model uniqueness gives a partially defined geometric good
reduction map red_iota on S_2. Any place of any number-field model can
be realized by iota composed with a global Galois automorphism. Its
geometric good reduction is thus red_iota of a member of S_2.
Residue conjugates add no classes: automorphisms of bar(F_p) lift through
the unramified local Galois quotient to Aut(bar(Q_p)/Q_p); they preserve
the algebraic closure of Q inside bar(Q_p) and restrict to global
Galois automorphisms there. Twists and the extensions needed for good
reduction give the same geometric special fiber by
[stable-model uniqueness, Stacks109.24.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0E8C).

Thus E_(2,p) is contained in the image of this one partial map on the
Galois-stable set S_2. This proves |E_(2,p)|<=|S_2| and the theorem.
Nothing depends on p=5 in this counting argument. The role of p=5
in Litt3 is in the separate application, not the arithmetic bound.

## 5. Version2: genus three and a uniform partner bound

New extension,2026-09-10, separately reviewed PASS by
/root/audit_defect_preserving_descent; the preceding genus-two audit
retains its own scope. Reuse exactly the primary inputs and counting
arguments of Sections1--4, changing the homology-cover genus as follows.

For a compact arithmetic genus-three Gamma, the square subgroup has
index2^6=64. Its curve has genus129 and area512pi. Thus it lies in a
maximal-order norm-one Lambda of area at most512pi and

                     d^(3/2)P <=64(4pi²)^n.

Odlyzko's same inequality gives8^n<64e^105<2^174; hence n<=64 still.
The convenient resulting bounds are

          d<=2^260, P<=2^390, N(D_B)<=2^844.

The primitive integral generator has conjugates bounded by2^148 and
minimal-polynomial coefficients bounded by2^9536<2^10000. Thus the
field count remains below2^700000. The finite discriminant count is
below2^(64+2*844)=2^1752<2^1800, and the maximal-order type count is
below2^(128+260)=2^388<2^400. Allowing the split real place and
orientation as before gives the SAME norm-one group bound2^710000.

The orbifold minimum area pi/21 now gives [Lambda:Delta]<=10752.
If Lambda has signature(h;m_1,...,m_s), then4h+s<=516, so516
generators suffice. The number of candidate subgroups is therefore

    <=10752*(10752!)^516
     <2^(14+516*10752*14)=2^77672462<2^78000000.

The genus129 homology-cover curve has at most84*128=10752
automorphisms, so at most2^10752<2^11000 subgroups. Recovering the
original genus-three curve as its quotient gives

          |S_3|<2^(710000+78000000+11000)
               =2^78721000<2^80000000.                  (8)

The Galois-stability and fixed-place reduction argument in Section4
is genus-independent, so |E_(3,p)|<=|S_3| for every p.

Now fix ANY compact hyperbolic complex curve A. If A is arithmetic,
every curve sharing a finite etale cover with it is arithmetic, so(8)
bounds all its genus-three partners. Otherwise its commensurator
Lambda is discrete, and every partner lattice lies inside this SAME
Lambda, as proved in [Mochizuki, Sections2--3, Proposition3.2](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~motizuki/Correspondences%20on%20Hyperbolic%20Curves.pdf).
If there is a genus-three partner at all, its area8pi makes the area
of Lambda at most8pi. Its signature consequently satisfies4h+s<=12,
so it has at most12 generators. Each genus-three partner corresponds
to a subgroup of index at most168. Hence their number is at most

             168*(168!)^12 <2^(8+12*168*8)
                            =2^16136<2^17000.          (9)

Distinct subgroups can give the same curve, which only decreases the
count. The larger universal bound2^80000000 thus applies regardless
of A's genus or arithmeticity. For any other algebraically closed
characteristic-zero field, descend any finite collection of proposed
partners together with its witnessing maps and A to a finitely generated
field and embed it in C. The complex bound applies to every such finite
collection, so applies to the whole set. This is the finite-subset
argument of Mochizuki Theorem4.2, now with an explicit uniform bound.
