## 1. The full orbit square

Let k be algebraically closed, r an odd prime, char(k)!=2,r, and

    g(X)=s+1, g(Y)=rs+1, k(Y)=k(t,z), z^2=f(t), s>=1.

Assume ACTUAL finite etale maps from the same source

    V --a--> Y,     V --p--> C --c--> X,
    deg(p)=r,      deg(a)=deg(c)=M,

with p a cyclic torsor generated by beta and a beta!=a. Put

    F=k(C), K=k(V), t_i=beta^i(t), z_i=beta^i(z),
    P(T)=product_i(T-t_i), B=k(coefficients of P),
    E=B(t), e=[F:B], d=[E:k(t)]=2M/e.

Here B also denotes its smooth projective curve. The t_i are distinct:
otherwise the prime-order action fixes t, and oddness forces it to fix z.
The normalized coefficient image has a birational projective model of
degree d. Interpolate the UNIQUE polynomial Q(T) of degree<r with

    Q(t_i)=z_i,   D=B(coefficients of Q),   E_*=D(t).

Cyclic invariance puts Q in F[T] and D inside F.

### Theorem81.1: the actual full-orbit square

For j=[D:B] and m=[F:D],

    e=mj, [E_*:D]=r, [K:E_*]=m, F tensor_D E_*=K,
    V=normalization of C x_D E_*.

The maps V->E_*->Y are etale. Writing N=deg(E_*/Y),

    m divides M,  N=M/m=jd/2,  1<=j<=2^r.

All displayed extensions are separable. Also j=1 iff z belongs to E;
otherwise j is even.

**Proof.** P is irreducible over F and hence over D. As Q(t)=z,

    E_*=D(t,z), FE_*=K,
    [E_*:D]=[FE_*:F]=r.

This DEGREE EQUALITY proves linear disjointness over D and the asserted
square; an intersection formula is not substituted for it. The intermediate
maps of V/Y are etale and give N=M/m and jd=2N.

Over B, the algebra A=B[T]/P is the field E. Its element f(T) is invertible.
The solution scheme of q^2=f(T) in A is finite etale of degree2^r:
over a separable splitting field it is a product of r two-element schemes.
The coefficients of Q give a point with residue field D. Thus D/B is
separable and j<=2^r. If z=Q_0(t) with Q_0 in B[T], apply beta^i and
interpolation uniqueness to get Q=Q_0; this proves the j=1 equivalence.
Otherwise the quadratic E(z)/E lies inside E_*/E, whose degree is j.
Finally F/D is separable by faithful scalar extension to the already
separable K/E_*; E_*/D is separable because P has distinct roots. QED.

### Theorem81.2: norm and genus bounds

If JY is absolutely simple, then

    rs+1<=g(D)<=Ns+1,  N>=r,
    eta=Ns+1-g(D)>=0,
    deg Diff(C/D)=2m eta,  deg Diff(E_*/D)=2r eta.

If JX is also absolutely simple, then

    m>=gon(X)  OR  N>=r+2.                            (A)

**Proof.** Put h_D=(E_*/D)_*(E_*/Y)^*. If h_D=0, its Rosati-dual
moving degree-r divisors on Y lie in one complete linear system. They
have no common base point: parameters whose divisor contains a given
y form a finite set. Thus they yield a degree-r pencil. Together with
the hyperelliptic pencil it generates k(Y), so Castelnuovo--Severi gives
g(Y)<=r-1, impossible. Simplicity now gives dim im(h_D)=rs+1.
Etale E_*/Y and Riemann--Hurwitz for both lower maps prove the genus
bounds and different formulas.

The actual square gives p_*a^*=(C/D)^*h_D; pushing to JX gives zero
since JY is simple of larger dimension. If (C/D)_*c^*=0, the same
moving-divisor argument gives a degree-m pencil on X. Otherwise its
image has dimension s+1 and is orthogonal to im(h_D) in JD. Hence
g(D)>=(r+1)s+2, and comparison with Ns+1 gives N>=r+2. QED.

## 2. The finite signed-permutation group

The field L=B(t_i,z_i:0<=i<r) inside K is Galois over B. Its group H
acts faithfully on the2r signed letters (t_i,+/-z_i), giving

    H <= W_r=(F2)^r semidirect S_r.

The stabilizer H_0 of the chosen selection {(t_i,z_i)} fixes exactly D,
by interpolation. It contains beta and acts faithfully on r letters;
thus j=[H:H_0] and H_0<=S_r. For r=3, both the permutation image of H
and H_0 have order3 or6, while the sign kernel is a2-group. Therefore

    r=3 ==> j in {1,2,4,8}.                           (B)

### Theorem82.1: orbit congruence and equality geometry

More generally let ANY H<=W_r contain beta=(0,(0 1 ... r-1)), and put
O=H.0 in F2^r, j=|O|. Then

    j=1 mod r  if all-ones is not in O,
    j=2 mod r  if all-ones is in O.

If every nontrivial j under consideration is even, these sharpen to

    j=1+(2a+1)r  when all-ones is absent and j>1,
    j=2+2ar      when all-ones is present,             a>=0.

Consequently j belongs to{1,2,r+1} or j>=2r+2. Transitivity of H on
the signed letters is one sufficient condition for every cube orbit
to be even.

If j=r+1, then r=3 mod4 and, after cyclically labelling the coordinates,

    O={0} union {1_(A+i):i in Z/r},
    |A|=(r+1)/2, |A intersect(A+i)|=(r+1)/4 for i!=0.

All distinct orbit vertices have distance(r+1)/2. Prepending +1 to
their sign vectors gives a normalized Hadamard matrix of order r+1,
with beta cyclic on the other r rows and columns.

**Proof.** Beta has exactly two fixed cube vertices,0 and all-ones;
the other orbits have length r. This gives the congruences, and parity
gives the two displayed forms. For signed-letter transitivity, the sum
of a signed coordinate over any H-orbit is independent of the coordinate
but negates under changing its sign, so is zero; the orbit has even size.

When j=r+1, O minus{0} is one cyclic orbit of a subset A of weight w.
Cube isometries act transitively on O, so every pair of distinct vertices
has distance w. Thus |A intersect(A+i)|=w/2. Summing over i!=0 gives

    (r-1)w/2=w(w-1), hence w=(r+1)/2.

The intersection number is integral, so r=3 mod4. Distinct prepended
sign rows have inner product1+r-2w=0, proving the Hadamard assertion.
QED.

### Lemma82.2: an involution fixes a signed letter

If j=r+1 in Theorem82.1, every involution of H fixes a signed letter.

**Proof.** Write it as x |-> a+pi x, where pi^2=1 and pi(a)=a.
If a=0, pi has a fixed coordinate since r is odd. If a!=0, a lies
in O and has even weight(r+1)/2. To fix no signed letter, the involution
would have to flip every pi-fixed coordinate, an odd number of them.
All remaining support comes in two-cycles, making |a| odd: contradiction.
QED.

## 3. The all-prime Hadamard genus bound

Return to the actual diamond and put E'=B(t,z). Assume dim W_P>=3;
in the simple-JY application this follows from the nonpencil theorem68.4a.

### Theorem82.3

If j=r+1, every nontrivial inertia group of L/B has order r. The
quadratic E'/E is etale and, for b=g(B),

    g(E)=drs/2+1,
    b>=ds/2-(r-1)d/r+1,  d>=s+2.                     (C)

If r does not divide m, then L/B is etale and

    b=ds/2+1,  d>=s+3.                               (D)

**Proof.** Here z not in E. The2r signed letters are the conjugates
of the primitive pair(t,z) of E'/B. Their stabilizers are conjugates
of Gal(L/E'), and L/E' is an intermediate etale cover of V/Y.
Inertia therefore acts FREELY on the2r signed letters, so its order
divides2r before any tameness claim. Since char(k)!=2,r, it is then
tame and cyclic. Lemma82.2 rules out even inertia, leaving orders1,r.
In particular the quadratic E'/E is etale. Since E'/Y is etale of
degree d, its genus gives g(E)=drs/2+1.

The spectral curve in B x P1 has arithmetic genus

    r b+(r-1)(d-1).

Its normalization is E, so comparison gives(C)'s bound on b. The
nondegenerate birational degree-d coefficient model gives

    b<=(d-1)(d-2)/2.                                 (E)

Combining the bounds yields d>=s+1+2/r, hence d>=s+2.

For the stronger assertion, H_0<=S_r and j=r+1 imply v_r(|H|)=1;
all order-r subgroups are conjugate to <beta>. Also [K:L] divides m.
If beta fixed a point of L, it would act freely on the fiber of the
etale V->L, forcing r|[K:L]|m. Thus beta fixes no point when r does
not divide m; neither does any conjugate, so L/B has no inertia.

The intermediate E_*/B is now etale of degree rj. As
g(E_*)-1=Nrs and N=jd/2, its genus gives b=ds/2+1. Combining with(E)
gives d>=s+3, proving(D). QED.

### Theorem81.3: the cubic four-choice case

For r=3, char(k)=5, j=4, one has H=A4 or S4. Inertia has order1 or3,
E'/E is etale, and g(E)=3ds/2+1. If dim W_P>=3, then

    b>=ds/2-2d/3+1,  d>=s+2.

If3 does not divide m, L/B is etale and b=ds/2+1; with dim W_P>=3,
one has d>=s+3.

**Proof.** Theorem82.1 makes the orbit the four even-parity vertices

The transitive subgroup H contains a3-cycle, so it is A4 or S4.
The inertia and genus proof of Theorem82.3 applies with r=3; the
coefficient-dimension hypothesis is used only for(E) and its d-bounds.
This replaces the separate cubic calculation by its audited all-prime
proof, with the same hypotheses on each conclusion. QED.

## 4. The repaired short-degree sieve

### Corollary82.4

Assume JY absolutely simple. Then

    M<2r+2 ==> j in {1,2}.
    r+2<=M<2r ==> m=1 and e=j in {1,2}.

**Proof.** Theorem81.2 gives m|M and N=M/m>=r. Nonpencilness gives
d>=2, so j<=N<=M. Theorem82.1 leaves j=1,2,r+1 in the indicated
short range. If j=r+1, then(C) gives

    N=(r+1)d/2 >= (r+1)(s+2)/2.

For s>=2 this is already at least2r+2. For s=1, M=mN<2r+2 forces
m=1, so(D) gives d>=4 and again N>=2r+2. Thus j=r+1 is impossible.
Finally M<2r and N>=r force m=1. No intersection-degree shortcut is used.
QED.

For the [fixed explicit pair](../../Definitions/Def_fixed_pair.md),
M=6 reduces to e=1 or2, both excluded by the surface and cubic-closure

**Proof.** Here r=3,s=8, both Jacobians are simple and gon(X)=3.

If m=2, then N=3 contradicts(A), as m<gon(X). Thus m=1,e=j.
At e=2, E_*=K, so the quadratic setup used by the proof in78 is
valid directly. Its two final exclusions apply. QED.

## Scope

The orbit bound is independent of M, not an all-degree exclusion.
The Hadamard alternative is not empty as a finite-group problem:
cyclic difference sets can realize its displayed parameters. What
excludes it in the short geometric range is signed-root etaleness
together with the coefficient genus. Cubic eight-choice cases and
large reduced degrees remain open. Both actual original etale legs
are retained throughout; the original common-cover problem is unsolved.
