# The genus-one full-span coarsening is hyperelliptic

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved.**  **Audit: PASS** -- `/root/audit_m9_hyperelliptic`,
2026-09-04.  No breaking objection; one optional projection-formula
clarification is recorded.  [Audit record](audits/53_M9_GENUS_ONE_COARSENING_AUDIT.md).

Assume the exact seven-diamond of files 38 and 44 has (M=9), and that
the norm-coefficient space has its full possible dimension
(dim W_P=8).  Theorem 47.3 then gives a double cover

\[
                         q:C\longrightarrow B,
\]

where (g(C)=19) and (g(B)) is one or two.  Write \(\delta\) for its
deck involution, and retain the finite etale map

\[
                         c:C\longrightarrow X,
       \qquad X:\ v^2=x^7-x+1,qquad \deg c=9.
\tag{53.1}
\]

This note settles the geometry of both genus-one rows of Theorem 47.3.
They are not eliminated, but they are forced into a much narrower form:
the involution \(\delta\) lies over the hyperelliptic involution of \(X\).
Equivalently, \(c\) is the normalized pullback of a degree-nine map from
the elliptic curve \(B\) to the hyperelliptic line of \(X\).

## 1. The small endomorphism algebra of (J(X))

### Lemma 53.1

Over \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\),

\[
 \operatorname{End}^0(J(X))
 \simeq
 \mathbf Q(\sqrt{-11})\times
 \mathbf Q(\sqrt{-19})\times
 \mathbf Q(i).
\tag{53.2}
\]

The Rosati-fixed subalgebra is \(\mathbf Q^3\).  Moreover the only
automorphisms of \(X\) are the identity and its hyperelliptic involution
\(\iota_X\).

#### Proof

The Frobenius polynomial of the displayed \(\mathbf F _5\)-model is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_X(T)
  &=(T^2-3T+5)(T^2+T+5)(T^2+4T+5)\\
  &=T^6+2T^5+4T^4+8T^3+20T^2+50T+125.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{53.3}
\]

Thus (J(X)) is isogenous to three ordinary elliptic curves.  Their
geometric endomorphism fields have discriminants (-11,-19,-4), so the
three curves remain pairwise nonisogenous over (k).  This proves (53.2).
Rosati acts by complex conjugation on every factor, and hence fixes exactly
\(\mathbf Q^3\).

Every automorphism of a genus-three hyperelliptic curve descends to a
projective transformation preserving its eight branch points, with kernel
generated by the hyperelliptic involution.  For

\[
             \mathcal A_X=\{x^7-x+1=0\}\cup\{\infty\},
\tag{53.4}
\]

the stabilizer in \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\) is trivial.  This is an
exact finite computation over the splitting field \(\mathbf F _{5^6}\):
choose three branch points, enumerate their (8\cdot7\cdot6=336) ordered
images, form the unique projective transformation for each choice, and
test the full branch set.  Only the identity passes.  The accompanying
certificate `53_X_AUTOMORPHISM_CERTIFICATE.sage` also verifies (53.3) from
the point counts over \(\mathbf F _5,\mathbf F _{25},\mathbf F _{125}\).
This proves the automorphism assertion. \(\square\)

## 2. Compression of the involution

Put

\[
                    u=c_*\delta^*c^*\in\operatorname{End}(J(X)).
\tag{53.5}
\]

### Lemma 53.2

The endomorphism (u) acts on the three elliptic isogeny factors of
(J(X)) by integers

\[
                         \lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,
             \qquad -9\leq\lambda_i\leq9.
\tag{53.6}
\]

If (g(B)=1), at least two of the three integers equal (-9).

#### Proof

The involution \(\delta\) is Rosati self-adjoint, so (u) is as well.
Lemma 53.1 puts it in \(\mathbf Q^3\).  Each rational coordinate is an
algebraic integer because (u) is an actual endomorphism, and hence is an
integer.

The identities

\[
  9\operatorname{id}\mathbin{\pm}u
   =c_*(1\mathbin{\pm}\delta^*)c^*
\tag{53.7}
\]

show that both sides are Rosati-positive semidefinite.  This gives the
bounds (53.6).  Finally, if (h=q_*c^*:J(X)\to J(B)), then

\[
                         h^\dagger h=9\operatorname{id}+u.
\tag{53.8}
\]

For (g(B)=1), its action on first cohomology has rank at most two.  Every
nonzero scalar (9+\lambda_i) contributes rank two, so at most one such
scalar is nonzero. \(\square\)

## 3. The forced hyperelliptic factorization

### Theorem 53.3

In either row of Theorem 47.3 having (g(B)=1),

\[
                         c\delta=\iota_Xc.             \tag{53.9}
\]

Consequently there is a degree-nine map

\[
                         r:B\longrightarrow\mathbf P^1_x              \tag{53.10}
\]

such that (C) is the normalization of

\[
                  B\mathbin{\times}_{\mathbf P^1_x}X,                 \tag{53.11}
\]

and (c) is its second projection.  The map (r) is unramified away
from \(\mathcal A_X\), and every ramification index over \(\mathcal A_X\)
is one or two.  If its fiber over \(a\in\mathcal A_X\) has profile

\[
                           1^{a_a}2^{b_a},
\]

then

\[
 a_a+2b_a=9,qquad
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_X}a_a=36,qquad
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal A_X}b_a=18.                       \tag{53.12}
\]

#### Proof

Let

\[
             Z=(c,c\delta)_*[C]
\tag{53.13}
\]

be the effective pushforward cycle on (X\times X).  It has bidegree
((9,9)) and induces (u).  The 36 branch points of (q) are fixed by
(\delta), and each contributes exactly one to (Z\cdot\Delta_X): in a
tame local parameter (t), one has \(\delta(t)=-t\), while the etaleness
of (c) makes the linear term of (c(t)-c(-t)) nonzero.  Therefore

\[
 36\leq Z\cdot\Delta_X
       =18-\operatorname{Tr}(u\mid H^1(X))
       =18-2(\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3).           \tag{53.14}
\]

Suppose first that the reduced image of (53.13) is not the graph of
\(\iota_X\).  Positivity of its proper intersection with that graph gives

\[
 0\leq Z\cdot\operatorname{Graph}(\iota_X)
       =18+2(\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3).           \tag{53.15}
\]

Equations (53.14)--(53.15) force \(\sum_i\lambda_i=-9\).  Lemma 53.2
then forces, after reordering,

\[
                       (\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)=(-9,-9,9).
\tag{53.16}
\]

Let \(\Gamma\) be the reduced image of (53.13), and let (e) be the
generic degree of (C\to\Gamma\).  Since both projections factor the
degree-nine etale map (c), one has (e\mid9), both maps in the
factorization are etale, and the normalization of \(\Gamma\) has genus

\[
                              1+18/e.                  \tag{53.17}
\]

The cycle equality is (Z=e\Gamma), so the correspondence induced by
\(\Gamma\) is (u/e).  From (53.16),

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}((u/e)^\dagger(u/e)\mid H^1(X))=486/e^2.
\]

Adjunction on (X\times X) now gives

\[
 p_a(\Gamma)
  =1+\frac12\left(\frac{162-486}{e^2}+\frac{72}{e}\right)
  =1-\frac{162}{e^2}+\frac{36}{e}.                    \tag{53.18}
\]

For (e=1) and (e=3), this is respectively (-125) and (-5), which
is impossible.  Hence (e=9).  Both projections of \(\Gamma\) have
degree one, so \(\Gamma\) is the graph of an automorphism \(\tau\) of
(X).  Its action on cohomology has eigenvalues ((-1,-1,1)), each with
multiplicity two.  Lemma 53.1 says that \(\tau\) is either the identity or
\(\iota_X\), whose actions are respectively (+1) and (-1) on all of
(H^1(X)).  This contradiction proves that the supposition was false.
Thus the reduced image is the graph of \(\iota_X\), proving (53.9).

The invariant function (x\circ c) now descends through (q), giving
(53.10).  The anti-invariant function (v\circ c) generates the quadratic
extension (k(C)/k(B)) and satisfies

\[
                    (v\circ c)^2=r^7-r+1.

\]

This proves the normalized fiber-product assertion.  The elementary local
calculation for a normalized pullback of a tame double cover shows that
the projection to (X) is etale precisely when (r) is unramified away
from \(\mathcal A_X\) and has indices one or two above it.  Index-one
points over \(\mathcal A_X\) are exactly the branch points of (C/B), so
their total number is 36.  Riemann--Hurwitz for the degree-nine map from
the elliptic curve (B) gives total different 18; all ramification is
simple and lies over \(\mathcal A_X\), giving the final equality in
(53.12). \(\square\)

## 4. Boundary of the result

The theorem does not exclude the two genus-one rows.  It shows that any
such row must simultaneously realize two rigid pullback descriptions:
the degree-fourteen (C_{14}) or (D_{14}) description of Theorem 47.3,
and the degree-nine elliptic hyperelliptic pullback (53.10)--(53.12).
The remaining problem in these rows is therefore an explicit compatibility
problem between these two covers.
