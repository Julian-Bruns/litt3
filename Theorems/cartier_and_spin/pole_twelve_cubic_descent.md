# Cubic descent for every actual pole-twelve comparison

Version 1, 24 September 2026. Use the fixed curve and actual two-map
normal form of [low-pole reconstruction](new_line_low_pole_reconstruction.md).
If the comparison parameter has pole degree twelve, then
\[
[k(T):k(x_1,x_2)]=3.
\]
Both primitive minimal polynomials have zero cubic-character term.
This excludes the entire index-one branch for every covering degree
12<=n<=186, all35 norm patterns, repeated labels and geometric scalars.

The surviving simultaneous quotient S satisfies
\[
k(S)=k(u,v)=k(u,t)=k(v,t),\quad
\deg t=4,\quad\deg u=\deg v=n,\quad g(S)\le3n-21.
\]
Its exact reconstruction problem remains open. Require P(u) noncube,
r^3=P(v)/P(u), and both u,v unramified outside the roots of P and
infinity, with indices1 or3 above those values. Together with
\[
A(v)=\epsilon^4t^{-13}A(u),\qquad
(dv/du)^3P(u)^2=\epsilon^{-17}t^{48}P(v)^2,
\]
these conditions reconstruct the original two everywhere-etale maps
on y^3=P(u), with y_2=r y. The normal closure of k(T)/k(t) has
solvable group of order dividing1944, prime to five. This is a
parameter-map closure, not an etale common Galois refinement.

The proof's uniform positive norm multiplicities are conditional on
a nonzero character term and must not be retained in the surviving
quartic case. This theorem does not exclude pole twelve or supply a
tensor on an arbitrary unmarked common cover.
