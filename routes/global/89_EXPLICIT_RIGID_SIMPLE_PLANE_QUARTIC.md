# An explicit simple-Jacobian curve with no geometric automorphisms

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved, with an exact arithmetic certificate; independent audit
pending.** This is a nonempty explicit test case for the parameterized
theorem in file 87, not a claim to settle arbitrary common covers and not
a replacement for the pair in file 76.

Throughout, let \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\). Define the plane curve

\[
 X_0:\quad x^4+y^4+z^4+x^3y+y^3z+z^3x+xyz^2=0.                 \tag{89.1}
\]

### Theorem 89.1

The curve \(X_0\) is smooth, geometrically connected, nonhyperelliptic,
and of genus three. Its Jacobian is ordinary and absolutely simple, and

\[
                         \operatorname{Aut}_k(X_0)=1.             \tag{89.2}
\]

Consequently, for every prime \(\ell\) and every connected finite etale
Galois cover \(D\to X_0\) with abelian \(\ell\)-group deck group \(H\),

\[
                         \operatorname{Aut}_k(D)=H.               \tag{89.3}
\]

If \(Y/k\) is any hyperelliptic curve of genus at least two, there is no
common cover whose map to \(X_0\) is Galois with abelian \(\ell\)-group
deck group and whose map to \(Y\) is Galois with abelian deck group.
The trivial deck group is allowed in these statements.

#### Proof

Write \(F\) for the homogeneous polynomial in (89.1). On each of the
three affine charts, the ideal generated by \(F\) and its three partial
derivatives is the unit ideal. The linked certificate verifies these
three exact ideal equalities over \(\mathbf F_5\); they remain true after
base extension to \(k\). Thus the projective curve is smooth.

A reducible plane curve of positive degree has intersecting geometric
components by Bezout, and is not smooth at such an intersection. Hence
\(X_0\) is geometrically integral. The smooth plane quartic genus formula
gives genus three. Its plane embedding is canonical, so it is
nonhyperelliptic.

An exhaustive point count gives

\[
 \#X_0(\mathbf F_5)=4,\qquad
 \#X_0(\mathbf F_{25})=18,\qquad
 \#X_0(\mathbf F_{125})=160.                                     \tag{89.4}
\]

Newton identities and the functional equation recover the complete
characteristic polynomial of Frobenius on the Jacobian:

\[
 P(T)=T^6-2T^5-2T^4+18T^3-10T^2-50T+125.                       \tag{89.5}
\]

Its reduction modulo 17 is irreducible, so \(P\) is irreducible over
\(\mathbf Q\) and \(J(X_0)\) is simple over \(\mathbf F_5\). Its middle
coefficient is prime to 5, so the Jacobian is ordinary.

Let \(\pi\) be a root of \(P\), and put \(K=\mathbf Q(\pi)\). Exact
maximal-order arithmetic gives

\[
                [K:\mathbf Q]=6,\qquad
                \operatorname{disc}(K)=-11^5\,13^2.              \tag{89.6}
\]

The only roots of unity in \(K\) are \(1,-1\). Indeed, if a primitive
\(n\)-th root lies in \(K\), then \(\varphi(n)\mid6\), so

\[
                  n\in\{1,2,3,4,6,7,9,14,18\}.
\]

Every entry other than 1 and 2 would introduce a ramified prime 2, 3, or
7 in the cyclotomic subfield, hence in \(K\), contrary to (89.6).
Furthermore, direct arithmetic in \(K\) gives

\[
 \operatorname{minpol}(\pi^2)
       =T^6-8T^5+56T^4-234T^3+1400T^2-5000T+15625,              \tag{89.7}
\]

so \(\mathbf Q(\pi^2)=K\). The nonzero \(T^5\) coefficient excludes
\(P\in\mathbf Z[T^n]\) for every \(n>1\). Howe--Zhu, Proposition 3,
now implies \(\mathbf Q(\pi^n)=K\) for all \(n\ge1\) and absolute
simplicity. The criterion's root-of-unity alternative has only \(n=2\)
to check here, and (89.7) excludes it. See
[Howe--Zhu, section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0002205).

Over every finite extension, the ordinary simple Jacobian consequently
has endomorphism algebra \(K\). Since each geometric endomorphism is
defined over a finite extension,

\[
                  \operatorname{End}^0_k J(X_0)=K.               \tag{89.8}
\]

The action on the Jacobian faithfully embeds the finite group
\(\operatorname{Aut}_k(X_0)\) into \(K^\times\). Its image therefore
consists of roots of unity, and is contained in \(\{1,-1\}\). The
automorphism inducing \([-1]\), if it exists, is the hyperelliptic
involution. Since \(X_0\) is nonhyperelliptic, only the identity occurs.
This proves (89.2).

Equation (89.3) is now Theorem 87.3, with the trivial-cover case immediate.
The common-cover exclusion is Theorem 87.8. \(\square\)

## Certificate and scope

[`89_RIGID_PLANE_QUARTIC_CERTIFICATE.sage`](89_RIGID_PLANE_QUARTIC_CERTIFICATE.sage)
checks smoothness by exact ideals, counts every projective point over the
three stated fields, reconstructs (89.5), and checks irreducibility,
(89.6), and (89.7). No sampling over the prime field is used as a claim
about geometric smoothness or geometric automorphisms.

The resulting obstruction applies in unbounded \(\ell\)-power degrees
and to every hyperelliptic second curve. It does **not** imply that an
arbitrary common cover can be made simultaneously abelian Galois. That
missing implication is not being asserted.
