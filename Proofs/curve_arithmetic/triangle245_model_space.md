[Statement](../../Theorems/curve_arithmetic/triangle245_model_space.md).
The complete permutation list is generated by the
[triangle enumerator](../../scripts/orbifolds/enumerate_uniform_triangles.cpp)

## From an actual cover to the equations

For the given degree-forty cover, divide by its actual hyperelliptic
deck involution. The resulting rational degree-twenty map has
passport $(1^4 2^8;2^2 4^4;5^4)$. The six Weierstrass points are
the four simple points over the first branch value and the two
index-two points over the second. Normalize the branch values as
$0,1,\infty$, and put the latter two source points at $0,\infty$.
There are monic quartics $B,C$ and a nonzero $s$ with
\[
q=1+s x^2B^4/C^5.
\]
The zeros of $B$ are the four index-four points, and those of $C$
are the four poles of index five. They are disjoint from each other
and from $0,\infty$. Scaling $x$ makes $C(0)=1$.

Direct differentiation gives
\[
q'=s x B^3 A/C^6,\qquad A=(2B+4xB')C-5xBC'.
\]
The eight remaining critical points are the double zeros over zero.
Thus $C^5+s x^2B^4=A^2F$ for a quartic $F$. Since $A$ has degree
eight and leading coefficient $-2$, the leading coefficient of
$F$ is $1/4$. The six branch points of the double cover are exactly
$0,\infty$ and the roots of $F$, giving $y^2=xF$ over the algebraic
closure. This recovers the original curve and the original map.

## The polynomial equations have no unwanted degenerate points

Conversely suppose the square identity and the stated open condition
hold. First $B$ and $C$ are square-free. For example, if a nonzero
root of $B$ has multiplicity $m\ge2$, it has multiplicity $m-1$
in $A$. But the numerator $C^5+s x^2B^4$ is nonzero there, a
contradiction. The same argument at a multiple root of $C$ works
because the coefficient five is nonzero. Neither polynomial meets
$A$, and $A(0)=2B(0)C(0)\ne0$.

At a root of $A$ of multiplicity $m$, let the multiplicity in $F$
be $r\ge0$. The zero order of $q$ is $2m+r$, so its derivative
has order $2m+r-1$. The derivative formula gives order $m$.
Consequently $m+r=1$: $A$ is square-free and coprime to $F$.
At a root of $F$ outside $A$, the same derivative formula shows
that $F$ is square-free. The constant identity gives
\[
F(0)=C(0)^3/(4B(0)^2)\ne0.
\]
These arguments also work in characteristic greater than twenty.

The six branch points of $y^2=xF$ are therefore distinct. Reading
the three fibers proves precisely the two displayed passports:
the double cover doubles the four simple first-fiber points and
the two middle-fiber points at $0,\infty$, and is unramified at
every other point of those fibers. No cancellation in $q$ occurs.

## Exact number of normalizations

The [focused count](../../scripts/orbifolds/triangle245_model_count.py)
reads the previously verified full degree-forty permutation list.
It finds exactly21,15,4 classes with the required hyperelliptic
fixed-point counts and full deck orders $2,4,8$, respectively.
The [executed count and class numbers](../../../litt3-computation-data/uniform_triangle_continuation_20260921/triangle245_normalized_model_count.json)
are retained externally.

For a fixed rational quotient there are two choices for the ordered
pair placed at $0,\infty$, and four scalings giving $C(0)=1$.
Two choices give the same normalized map exactly when related by
an automorphism of the rational cover. Every such automorphism
lifts to the hyperelliptic double cover, since it preserves its
six branch points; its lift is unique up to the hyperelliptic
involution. The rational deck order is therefore half the full
deck order. Its action on coordinate choices is free. The number
of distinct coefficient tuples is thus $8,4,2$, respectively.
Their total is
\[
21\cdot8+15\cdot4+4\cdot2=236.
\]
This is a geometric-point count; it does not use the finite-field
solver's reported dimension or assert reducedness of its input ideal.

## Differentiated equations

Write $N=C^5+s x^2B^4$ and $P=A^2F$. The derivative identity gives
\[
CN'-5C'N=sxB^3A.
\]
Consequently $P=N$ implies the differentiated equation in the
statement. Conversely that equation gives
\[
C(P-N)'-5C'(P-N)=0.
\]
In characteristic zero, $(P-N)/C^5$ is constant. Its value at zero
vanishes by $4B(0)^2F(0)=C(0)^3$, so $P=N$. In characteristic
greater than twenty the same conclusion holds: after cancellation,
a nonconstant $p$th power cannot have
numerator and denominator of degrees at most twenty.

The leading terms cancel when $F_4=1/4$. At the next four steps,
the coefficient of the new unknown $F_k$ is $8-2k$ for
$k=3,2,1,0$. The [equation generator](../../scripts/orbifolds/triangle245_square_factor_models.py)
uses exactly these divisions and verifies the resulting degree bound.
