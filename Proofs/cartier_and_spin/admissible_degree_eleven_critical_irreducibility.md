## Every proper critical factor is on the finite content boundary

Suppose $D$ has a monic proper factor of degree
$e\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ over $K$. Count roots with their
multiplicities, without a squarefreeness assumption. The infinity
profiles have ten source roots of
pole at most one and one root of exact pole
$a_*=13-d$. If any critical root $c$, in an algebraic closure
with valuation normalized over the base, had larger pole than
$a_*$, then
\[
F'(c)/F(c)=\sum_i\frac1{c-W_i}
\]
would have leading term $11/c=1/c\ne0$ in characteristic
five. This contradicts $D(c)=0$, since $F'=\phi D$.
Thus every critical root has pole at most $13-d$.
Let $s\in K$ be the sum of the proper factor's short roots;
valuation triangle gives the same bound on $s$. In the finite
frame let $s_f=s+eZ/y$ be their sum.

The finite root bound below gives $vs_f$ affine. Therefore
$vs=(\text{affine})-e(Z/y)v$ has
infinity pole at most thirteen. The two high gap constraints
seventeen and fourteen force $v$ into the two-row kernel.
For $d=0,3$ these rows are invertible on its allowed constants
and linear polynomials, contradiction. For $d=6$ their kernel
is the line generated by $q=([13],[18],[24])$. For $d=9$
the cubic kernel is spanned by $q,q_3$, so the normalized
content is $q_3+\lambda q$.

Whenever $v$, as a polynomial in $x$, is squarefree and
coprime to $P$, all its finite zeros on $X$ have order one.
The missing fourth critical coefficient then forces $s_f$
to have no finite poles, as proved below. But its infinity
pole is exactly seventeen, because $s$ has pole at most
thirteen while $eZ/y$ has exact pole seventeen and $e\ne0$
in characteristic five. This is an
impossible affine-function gap. The exact small certificates
below establish squarefreeness and coprimality for $q$ and
$q_3$. Thus $d=6$ is excluded, and $d=9$ is possible only
on the displayed discriminant or branch-resultant boundary.
The discriminant parameter polynomial has degree at most
four by the cubic discriminant formula; the resultant has
degree at most ten, since it is a product of ten linear
parameter expressions over the roots of $P$. At
$\lambda=0$ both are nonzero by the $q_3$ certificates.
Their union contains at most fourteen geometric parameter
values at this intermediate step. The next argument excludes
the entire boundary as well.

## Exact fixed cubic and simple finite zeros

The fixed field is $\mathbf F_{25}$ with $\beta^2=\beta+3$
and code $[a+5b]=a+b\beta$. On $1,x,x^2,x^3$ the three
gap rows, in degree-nine, degree-eight, degree-seven order, are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
[16]&[5]&[14]&[10]\\
[18]&[16]&[2]&[16]\\
[24]&[0]&[13]&[18]
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their monic cubic kernel is
$q_3=([1],[22],[9],[1])$. In ascending coefficient codes the
literal identities are
\[
q_3([1]+[2]x)+q_3'([0]+[11]x+[1]x^2)=1,
\]
and
\[
q_3(15,21,16,16,0,8,2,16,23,13)
+P(1,14,17)=1.
\]
The parenthesized tuples denote polynomials in ascending powers.
Thus $q_3$ is squarefree and none of its roots is a cubic
branch point of $X:y^3=P(x)$. Every finite zero of this fixed
cubic on $X$ consequently has order exactly one.

For the quadratic $q$ the additional literal identities are
\[
q[12]+q'([24]+[24]x)=1,
\]
and
\[
q(1,23,14,24,1,21,24,19,4,4)+P(11,24)=1.
\]
Its roots are $[12]$ and $[16]$, where $P$ takes values
$[6]$ and $[1]$. These exact values give an independent
short check on its simple, unramified finite zeros.

## Finite factor-trace poles and a quadratic clearing polynomial

The coefficient of $T^4$ in $D$ is identically zero: $D$
is the derivative of a degree-six polynomial, and the derivative
of its degree-five term vanishes in characteristic five. Translation
preserves this missing coefficient, because $(T+a)^5=T^5+a^5$.
Thus the affine finite-frame critical polynomial has degrees
five and at most three below its leading term.

At a finite point put $\ell=\operatorname{ord}_P v$.
If any finite-frame critical root has pole $r$, in a valuation
normalized over the base, its leading term has valuation
$\ell-5r$, while every lower term has valuation at least
$-3r$. Thus $2r\le\ell$ is necessary, including fractional
root valuations. The rational sum $s_f$ has integral valuation,
so its pole is at most $\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$. This proves
$vs_f$ affine and $s_f$ affine when all finite zeros
of $v$ are simple. Affinity would give its impossible exact
infinity pole seventeen, since $s$ has pole at most thirteen.
This justifies the earlier generic boundary reduction.

It remains to treat $d=9$. The polynomial $v$ has degree
three. At an unramified $x$-root of multiplicity $t=1,2,3$,
a possible pole of $s_f$ is cleared by the corresponding
linear $x$-factor with exponent respectively $0,1,1$.
At a branch root the valuation of $v$ is $3t$, so the
necessary exponents are respectively $1,1,2$, using
$r\le\lfloor3t/2\rfloor$ and a linear $x$-factor of
valuation three. Since the total polynomial multiplicity is
three, these exponents have sum at most two, except when
$v$ has three distinct simple branch roots.

In every case except that last one choose the corresponding
nonzero polynomial $J(x)$ of degree at most two. Then
$Js_f$ is affine. Since $s$ has pole at most four on this
profile, $Js=(\text{affine})-e(Z/y)J$ has pole at most ten.
Descending gap orders seventeen, fourteen and eleven force
all three fixed rows on $J$ to vanish, since $e\ne0$.
Their restriction to
quadratics is invertible, by the retained $K_3$ determinant
$[23]$ in degree-seven, degree-eight, degree-nine row order.
This contradicts $J\ne0$. The exponent-two case of a triple
branch root is explicitly included here.

The sole remaining possibility has three distinct simple
branch roots. Since $P$ is squarefree, it means $v\mid P$.
But $v$, after scalar normalization, lies in the pencil
$q_3+\lambda q$. The exact following certificate excludes
every such divisor.

## No cubic in the fixed pencil divides the branch polynomial

Monic polynomial division in $x$ gives the three coefficients
of $P\bmod(q_3+\lambda q)$ as polynomials in $\lambda$.
The first two, in ascending code tuples, are
\[
r_0=(17,19,6,9,14,3,13,16,14),
\quad r_1=(22,7,14,22,11,23,24,13,22).
\]
They have the exact identity
\[
r_0(14,21,2,11,15,24,19,14)
+r_1(21,2,10,12,2,16,14,18)=1.
\]
No geometric value of $\lambda$ makes even these two
coefficients vanish. Therefore no member of the pencil divides
$P$. This closes the final branch-root boundary, and proves
that $D$ has no proper factor over $K$ in any profile.

## Denominator-degree consequences

Irreducibility makes the common factor of $D,U$ either one
or all of $D$. The latter gives $D\mid U$, hence a polynomial
annihilator of degree at most one, excluded in degree eleven.
Therefore they are coprime and the reduced denominator has
degree five. No squarefreeness of $D$ enters. The entire
argument allows inseparable irreducible critical polynomials;
the proper-factor sum uses only $e=1,2,3,4\ne0$.
