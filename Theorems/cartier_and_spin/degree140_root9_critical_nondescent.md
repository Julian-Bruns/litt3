# The critical quadratic of the root-nine family never descends through x

Version1, 29 September2026. Use the actual root-nine source and original
ratio open of the [critical-cover theorem](degree140_root9_critical_scale_budget.md).
For every allowed geometric ratio, the class of
\[
\Delta=(4G_3^2+3G_2G_4)/P^2\in k(X)^\times/k(X)^{\times2}
\]
is not in the image of \(k(x)^\times/k(x)^{\times2}\).
In particular it is not invariant under the cubic deck transformation.
This is a uniform geometric assertion, without a finite-field bound on
the ratio and without assuming that the scale pencil is primitive.

The proof reduces an arbitrary downstairs square class to exactly210
monic quartic divisors of P. Each is excluded by two explicit polynomial
coefficient identities, with its leading-comparison zero case retained.
All420 unit identities are recorded. The reduction uses pole orders and
the already proved nonsplitting obstruction, not an enumeration of
downstairs rational functions.

Consequently, whenever the actual quadratic scale polynomial has nonzero
discriminant, its three monic cubic conjugates are distinct irreducible
quadratics. Its cubic norm, after removing fixed x-content, is irreducible
in k(x)[lambda] of degree six. Its nonvertical plane component has the
critical double cover as its smooth normalization. This remains true
at nonprimitive ratios; no statement that their fixed content vanishes
is implied.

No square-scale locus or actual common-cover problem is decided here.
