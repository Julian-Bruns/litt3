# A minimum-degree full positive trace has reduced section defect

Version1,2 October2026. Independent focused root review PASS:
[audit](../../Research/audits/MINIMAL_POSITIVE_TRACE_REDUCED_DEFECT_AUDIT_2026_10_02.md).
Retain BOTH actual finite etale maps
$X\xleftarrow hS\xrightarrow gY$ from the SAME smooth connected
projective source, with the fixed genus-nine $X$, a selected genus-two
$Y$, and degrees $n,8n$. On the corresponding Frobenius targets, let
$I\subset U\subset B_X$ be the original three-section image and its
saturation, and let $P_X\subset U$ be the canonical positive plane.
Let $J,K,H\subset B_Y$ be their COMPLETE first actual trace images.

If the positive-plane trace $K$ has rank four and degree two, then
\[
\boxed{H/J\text{ is reduced torsion at every support point}.}
\]
Precisely, every local elementary divisor of $J\subset H$ has
exponent at most one. Several independent simple defects at one point
and defects at several points are not excluded.

The proof uses a nonempty regular graph of generically transverse
conjugate positive planes. Its determinant collision budget, taken
in the ACTUAL degree-two trace lattice, is incompatible with any
repeated section defect. The argument retains duplicate sheet labels
and all infinity/branch losses. It assumes no simultaneous Galois
closure and remains valid when five divides a covering degree.

Together with
[the rank-three branch exclusion](actual_first_trace_positive_rank_three_exclusion.md),
this constrains the degree-one original-section trace: its positive
plane trace has rank four, and when that trace has minimum degree
two its defect relative to the saturated-section trace is reduced.
The surviving simple-defect cases, larger positive-trace degrees,
and the original unmarked common-cover problem remain open.
