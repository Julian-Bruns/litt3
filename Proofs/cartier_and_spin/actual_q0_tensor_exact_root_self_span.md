The original tensor and exactness inputs are recorded in [the projective-q0 experiment](../../Research/experiments/oct02_global_extraction/PROJECTIVE_Q0_TENSOR_AND_EXACT_ROOT.md); the fixed polynomials and exact primitive are established in [Cartier–Petri excess one](cartier_petri_excess_one.md).

The two Kummer classes P,q are independent: a root of P detects the first class and a root of q detects the second. Therefore X′→P1 has group C3². The map X′→X ramifies with index3 at the six ordinary q-zero points, and nowhere else. In particular the pole order of q on X at infinity is SIX and divisible by3. Hurwitz gives 2g(X′)−2=3·16+6·2=60, so g(X′)=31.

At a finite root of P the inertia subgroup in C3² is generated by (ζ,1); at a root of q it is generated by (1,ζ). At infinity the valuations of P,q are −10,−2, so its inertia is the antidiagonal subgroup generated by (ζ²,ζ). The diagonal subgroup generated by (ζ,ζ) meets all three inertia subgroups trivially. These are all branch points. Its action is consequently free, including at infinity.

The diagonal fixed field is k(x,r), r=y/w, with r³=P/q. This cubic x-map ramifies at the ten roots of P, the two roots of q, and infinity. Hurwitz gives 2g(Z)−2=−6+13·2=20, hence g(Z)=11. Alternatively z=qr gives the model z³=q²P used in the earlier experiment. The invariant differential w⁸dx/y² descends to η=q²dx/r². Since r⁵ has zero derivative and Q′=qP,
\[
d(Q/r⁵)=qP\,dx/r⁵=q²dx/r²=η.
\]
At U,V the orders of q,dx,r are3,2,−1, so η has order10. At a root of P the orders of dx,r are2,1, so η is a unit. At infinity their orders are −4,−8, while ord(q)=−6, so η again is a unit. There are no other zeros or poles. This proves divη=10(U+V), and X′→Z is the free diagonal quotient.

Now take the actual S and h1,h2. Write qi=hi*q and θi=hi*θ. Both maps have the same degree by étale Hurwitz. The zeros of qi are simple; hence qi is not a cube in k(S). Let S′ be the smooth projective normalization of k(S)(w1), w1³=q1. This connected degree-three cover is the actual base change of X′→X along h1. Since h1 is étale, the map S′→X′ is étale. Choose δ∈k× with δ³=c. The form ηS=w1⁸θ1 satisfies ηS³=c q2⁸θ2³. Put a2=ηS/(δθ2), so a2³=q2⁸, and set
\[
w2=a2²/q2⁵.
\]
Then w2³=q2 and w2⁸=a2. Thus k(S′) also contains the actual second X′-field, and ηS=δw2⁸θ2. Because q2 is not a cube in k(S), this is the same degree-three extension obtained by base-changing X′→X along h2. Its map to X′ is therefore also finite étale. Smooth normalization does not alter that étale base-change assertion.

Composing both maps with the finite étale diagonal quotient gives the two actual étale Z-maps. Their pulled differentials differ by δ, and equality of their effective differential divisors gives equality of the pulled reduced divisor U+V. Each map comes with an actual lift to the SAME étale torsor X′→Z. Equivalently, each pullback of that torsor to S′ is trivialized by the constructed lift; it is not a connected degree-three cover of S′. These additional markings come from the constructed X′-maps, rather than being inferred from η alone.

The cubic S′→S ramifies at every simple zero of q1, hence at6deg(h1) points. If S→Y was étale, ramification transitivity shows that S′→Y is ramified there. Likewise the composite S′→X is ramified, although its factor S′→X′ and the original map S→X are étale. This is precisely the source-retention limitation stated in the theorem.
