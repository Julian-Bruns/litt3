# Finite generalized-Cartier eigenform pools and two-leg compatibility

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: root.
Status: direct proof; not yet independently audited. The finiteness theorem
is unconditional for every fixed curve. Its application to Litt3 covers
only the nonzero-Cartier branch of a positive coreless invariant.
It does not assert that such an invariant exists.

## 1. A parameterized finiteness theorem

Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, C/k a smooth
projective connected curve of genus at least two, and d a positive divisor
of p−1. Put

\[
 r=p-\frac{p-1}{d},\qquad n=d-1,\qquad
 V=H^0(C,\omega_C^d),\qquad N=\dim_k V .
\]

Thus 1≤r<p and rd=pn+1. Write

\[
 C_n:H^0(C,\omega_C^{pn+1})\longrightarrow H^0(C,\omega_C^{n+1})
\]

for the inverse-Frobenius-semilinear twisted absolute Cartier operator.
In a rational differential frame θ it satisfies

\[
 C_n(a\theta^{pn+1})=\theta^n C(a\theta).
\]

This is the same convention as the
[Frobenius/Cartier note](../../Theorems/Thm_cartier_generator.md);
the identical construction works with 5 replaced by p.

**Theorem.** The normalized eigen-equation

\[
                              C_{d-1}(s^r)=s                     \tag{1}
\]

defines a finite k-scheme of length exactly p^N. In particular the set
of projective classes

\[
 \mathcal E_d(C)=
 \{[s]\in\mathbf P(V)(k): C_{d-1}(s^r)=c s
                          \text{ for some }c\ne0\}
                                                                  \tag{2}
\]

is finite. Every class in (2) has exactly p−r=(p−1)/d nonzero
normalizations satisfying (1). If ℓ₀ is the length supported at the
zero solution, then

\[
                  |\mathcal E_d(C)|\le
                     \frac{p^N-\ell_0}{p-r}
                     \le\frac{p^N-1}{p-r}.                        \tag{3}
\]

Lengths, geometric point counts, and the number of projective classes
must not be identified: the finite scheme can be nonreduced.
The theorem permits \(\mathcal E_d(C)\) to be empty.

### Proof

Choose a basis e₁,…,e_N of V and write s=Σx_i e_i.
Expansion of s^r followed by the additive semilinear operator shows
that

\[
 P_i(x_1,\ldots,x_N)
   :=\left(\text{i-th coordinate of }C_{d-1}(s^r)\right)^p
\]

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree r. Each degree-r monomial in
the expansion of s^r contributes its coefficient times the p-th power
of the corresponding Cartier coordinate. Taking p-th powers removes
the inverse Frobenius on the coordinates x_i.

Equation (1) on k-points is therefore equivalent to

\[
                           x_i^p-P_i(x)=0,\quad 1\le i\le N.       \tag{4}
\]

These equations give the finite-scheme assertion its precise meaning.
It is intrinsic: a change of basis transports both s and its Cartier
image, and the ideals generated by their p-th-power coordinate
differences transform by an invertible Frobenius-twisted matrix.

For any degree-compatible monomial order, the leading monomial of
the i-th equation in (4) is x_i^p, since r<p. These leading monomials
are pairwise relatively prime. The product criterion for Gröbner
bases (or its immediate S-polynomial reduction) proves that the
displayed equations are a Gröbner basis. Its standard monomials are
exactly

\[
                    x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_N^{a_N},\qquad 0\le a_i<p.
\]

They form a basis of the coordinate algebra, proving finiteness
and length p^N.

Now suppose C_{d-1}(s^r)=c s with c≠0. For λ≠0, semilinearity gives

\[
 C_{d-1}((\lambda s)^r)=\lambda^{r/p}c\,s .
\]

The section λs is normalized exactly when

\[
                           \lambda^{p-r}=c^p.                    \tag{5}
\]

The positive integer p−r is prime to p. Since k is algebraically
closed, equation (5) has exactly p−r distinct solutions. Conversely
every nonzero solution of (1) gives a class in (2). Each geometric
point contributes length at least one to the finite scheme.
Subtracting its local part at zero proves (3). ∎

For p=5 the cases are d=1,2,4 with r=1,3,4. Their dimensions are,
respectively, g, 3g−3, and 7g−7. These sizes are finite even though
the degrees of hypothetical common covers are unrestricted.

If C and its data are given over a finite field, all equations (4)
are effectively available over a finite extension. A zero-dimensional
polynomial solver can, in principle, recover the complete geometric
pool. Checking only points over the original field does not do so.
The union of the finitely many normalized solutions is defined over
one finite extension, independent of any subsequent cover.

## 2. Application preserving both maps on the same source

Let f:Z→X and g:Z→Y be finite étale maps of smooth projective curves.
Suppose s_X,s_Y are nonzero regular d-differentials with

\[
                         f^*s_X=g^*s_Y=s_Z.                      \tag{6}
\]

Assume C_{d-1}(s_Z^r)=c s_Z with c≠0. Étale Cartier compatibility
and injectivity of pullback imply

\[
          C_{d-1}(s_X^r)=c s_X,\qquad
          C_{d-1}(s_Y^r)=c s_Y.
\]

One common scalar λ from (5) normalizes all three sections and
preserves (6). Hence the endpoint pair lies in the finite product
\(\mathcal E_d(X)\times\mathcal E_d(Y)\).

In particular, for a coreless span with canonical-ring intersection
k[s], the earlier Cartier theorem shows that its nonzero-Cartier
branch has d∈{1,2,4}. All possible endpoint differentials in this
branch lie in a fixed finite union of products, independently of
the degree of the span.

The normalized canonical d-th root covers attached to this finite
list also form a finite list of endpoint curves, up to their
components and isomorphisms over the endpoints. The
[root normal form](CORELESS_INVARIANT_KUMMER_EXACT_LOG_NORMAL_FORM.md)
supplies an actual finite étale span between appropriate components.
It does not claim that this new span is coreless, or that there are
only finitely many possible common sources.

## 3. An étale-invariant divisor fingerprint

For a nonzero regular d-differential s_C and e≥1, let

\[
 n_e(C,s_C)=|\{P\in C(k):\operatorname{ord}_P(s_C)=e\}|,\qquad
 \nu_e(C,s_C)=\frac{n_e(C,s_C)}{2g(C)-2}\in\mathbf Q.
\]

If (6) holds, then for EVERY e≥1

\[
                        \nu_e(X,s_X)=\nu_e(Y,s_Y).                \tag{7}
\]

Indeed an étale pullback preserves each zero order and has exactly
deg(f) geometric points above every point. Thus

\[
 n_e(Z,s_Z)=\deg(f)n_e(X,s_X)=\deg(g)n_e(Y,s_Y).
\]

Riemann–Hurwitz gives the same equalities for 2g−2, proving (7).
This argument uses both differential pullbacks on the SAME Z;
arbitrary ramified maps do not satisfy it.

Consequently disjoint finite fingerprint lists rule out the
nonzero-Cartier branch in any degree of common cover. Matching
fingerprints are only a necessary condition, not an existence test.

In the primitive coreless case, the established uniqueness of the
clump forces \(\operatorname{div}(s_C)=eT_C\) on both endpoints,
with the SAME multiplicity e. Thus one first discards every pool
element with unequal zero orders. The remaining numbers satisfy

\[
 e\mid d(2g(X)-2),\quad e\mid d(2g(Y)-2),\quad e+d\not\equiv0
 \pmod5
\]

in characteristic five. For a uniform divisor its only nonzero
fingerprint is ν_e=d/e. Therefore fingerprint comparison in this
case amounts exactly to comparison of the possible e-values.
It does not secretly supply a finer restriction once e agrees.

## 4. What remains outside this reduction

There are three logically separate cases:

1. The coreless canonical-ring intersection is k. Existence of a
   positive invariant is an unresolved issue; this theorem does not
   supply one.
2. The intersection is k[s], but its generalized Cartier image is
   zero. Its primitive degree is prime to five but not bounded by
   this theorem; d need not divide four.
3. The Cartier image is nonzero. The theorem gives a finite list of
   endpoint pairs and necessary divisor tests, but has not proved
   that all these pairs are incompatible for our proposed curves.

The separate quadratic
[exact certificate](SUPERSPECIAL_GENUS_TWO_QUADRATIC_CARTIER_EIGENFORMS_CERTIFICATE.md)
has total length125, local length75 at zero, and 25 projective
eigenlines with two normalizations each. It illustrates why the
scheme and geometric counts must be separated.

We do not claim that the existence or historical finiteness of
these objects is new. The direct finite-equation proof and its
same-source compatibility formulation are the content needed here.
