# Proof: the wild (20,23;7) row is already Hermitian

## 1. Its completed wild extension has one scalar and no further moduli

Write K=k((t)) at the wild coarse point. An ACTUAL scheme atlas of an
effective orbifold gives a finite Galois completed extension L/K.
This is the intrinsic-extension assertion of
[completed-local orbifold rigidity](completed_local_orbifold_rigidity.md),
not an inference from a numerical ramification index.

The inertia I has order20. Its wild subgroup P has order5 and is cyclic,
and I/P is cyclic of order4. If j is its sole lower break, Hilbert's
different formula reads

    delta=(20-1)+4j.

Thus delta=23 forces j=1. The lower ramification filtration for the
subextension L/L^P is obtained by intersecting with P, so its cyclic-five
Artin--Schreier break is also1.

The tame fixed field is K1=L^P=k((v)), with t=v^4: extract a fourth
root of the initial unit using Hensel's lemma after choosing its
residue root in the algebraically closed constant field. Reduced
Artin--Schreier normal form for conductor1 gives

    L=K1(y),             y^5-y=a/v,             a in k^*.

For clarity, this normal form is over the FIXED K1: successively remove
negative exponents divisible by5 by adding h^5-h; remove the integral
part by surjectivity of h->h^5-h on k[[v]] (the residue field is
algebraically closed, and the derivative is -1). Conductor1 leaves
only a/v. No change of the base uniformizer is hidden in this step.
Since y has pole order1 in L, z=1/y is a uniformizer and the original
coarse parameter is

    t = a^4 z^20/(1-z^4)^4.                              (1)

Conversely this gives the Galois extension of order20: y->y+c for
c in F5 and y->lambda*y for lambda in F5^* preserve t. The resulting
group is C5 semidirect C4 with faithful tame action.

Consequently every such completed extension is the same after scaling
t by one nonzero constant. Put the wild and tame coarse points at0
and infinity. That scaling is a GLOBAL automorphism of P1 fixing both
points. The tame completed extension of index7 is unique. The
completed-local rigidity theorem now proves that there is AT MOST ONE
orbifold with the stated data. This uses the full completed extensions,
not merely j and the different.

## 2. An actual A7 action supplies the model

There is a subgroup G=A7 of PSU_3(5). A primary reference is
[Montanucci--Zini, Theorem2.1(vi)](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.03398v1),
which supplies it for odd powers of5. We compute its branch data here;
neither its quotient signature nor a genus-two source census is assumed.

In A7, a Sylow-five subgroup P is generated by a5-cycle. All such
subgroups are conjugate, their normalizers have order20, and there
are126 of them. Indeed, N_S7(P)=N_S5(P) times S2 has order40;
intersection with A7 has index2. Each automorphism of P occurs in
this intersection, since the permutation of the two remaining letters
can correct the sign. The centralizer in A7 is exactly P.

Every nonidentity order-five automorphism of H fixes exactly one
point, and its lower break is either1 or6. These facts can be checked
directly. On H:y^5+y=x^6 an explicit Sylow-five group consists of

    x -> x+a,        y -> y+a^5*x+b,
    a,b in F25,      b^5+b=a^6.

It has125 elements and hence is a Sylow subgroup. All its nonidentity
elements act freely on the affine part and fix infinity. For the local
parameter z=x/y, using ord_infinity(x)=-5 and ord_infinity(y)=-6,

    g(z)-z = (a*y-a^5*x^2-b*x)/(y*(y+a^5*x+b)).

Its valuation is2 if a!=0, and7 if a=0, b!=0. Sylow conjugacy gives
the assertions for every order-five element of PSU_3(5). These local
indices also agree with Montanucci--Zini, Theorem2.3(8)--(9).

Let Q be the unique point fixed by P. Its stabilizer G_Q equals N_G(P).
The normalizer fixes Q by uniqueness. Conversely the wild subgroup of
the inertia G_Q is normal and has order5, so every element of G_Q
normalizes P. Thus |G_Q|=20. Distinct Sylow-five subgroups have distinct
fixed points, for the same uniqueness of the wild subgroup in a
point stabilizer. There is exactly ONE wild orbit, of length126.

Let j in {1,6} be the break there, B=H/G, and let m_i be its other,
tame inertia orders. Riemann--Hurwitz gives

    18/2520 = 2g(B)-2 + (19+4j)/20 + sum_i(1-1/m_i).       (2)

If j=6, the right side is at least -2+43/20=3/20,
strictly greater than1/140. Hence j=1. Substitution in(2) gives

    sum_i(1-1/m_i)=6/7-2g(B).

Nonnegativity forces g(B)=0. Each nonzero tame summand is at least1/2,
so there is exactly one such point and its order is7. This proves that
[H/A7] has exactly the completed-local type of Section1: wild order20,
break1, different23, and a single tame point of order7.

## 3. Actual atlas extension and the backup consequence

Sections1--2 and completed-local rigidity identify S with [H/A7]
after a global coarse coordinate scaling. Subgroup inclusion supplies
representable finite etale maps

    [H/A7] -> [H/PSU_3(5)] -> [H/PGU_3(5)]

of degrees126000/2520=50 and3. Compose the GIVEN curve atlas with
these actual maps. No descent of a Hermitian structure along an
arbitrary etale cover is invoked, and no simultaneous Galois closure
of the original two endpoint maps is needed.

For a genus-two curve, the first composite has degree280*50=14000;
the second has degree42000. The
[audited backup theorem](../genus_two/backup_hermitian_atlas_exclusion.md)
excludes every such atlas on C_alpha, including all cubic determinant
characters. Its untwisted PSU part alone already suffices here.

Therefore the degree280 row cannot occur in an actual cored span for
the backup pair. The original unmarked common-cover problem and the
other small cored profiles are not settled by this result.
