# Proof: degree-three actual q0-tensor self-spans are diagonal

Version1,3 October2026. [Independent whole-scope root review PASS](../../Research/audits/Q0_DEGREE_THREE_RECOGNITION_AUDIT_2026_10_03.md). Every group below acts on an actual curve or field; no simultaneous Galois closure is presumed.

Assume d0=3. The [actual joint-field cubic alternative](actual_q0_tensor_elliptic_joint_field_cubic_alternative.md) excludes root index ONE at this degree. Hence the actual root C0′ has a cubic y-deck A, fixing the elliptic joint curve D and scaling BOTH original yi by ζ. It commutes with the root w-deck C. It descends to a nontrivial cubic automorphism A of C0. Write B=C0/A. The actual root field C0′=C0(w1), and D=C0′/A. Thus D→B is the genuine cyclic cubic root w1³=q0(x1).

Each xi induces B→P¹ of degree THREE. Its local ramification indices are only ONE and THREE. Indeed the actual tower C0→B→P¹ has numerator indices ONE or THREE, because C0→Xi is étale and Xi→P¹ has these indices; C0→B is tame of degree THREE. In the Galois closure of this one actual degree-three map, its monodromy lies in S3, and all inertia groups are trivial or generated by a three-cycle. The sign quotient is therefore an unramified cover of P¹ and is trivial. Consequently the degree-three map B→P¹ is cyclic Galois. This is an individual Galois-closure argument, not a simultaneous closure.

Since A acts nontrivially on each Xi-field, B and Xi generate C0. Both B→P¹_xi and Xi→P¹_xi are cyclic cubic maps, and their composite has degree NINE. Therefore C0→Xi is a genuine Galois étale C3 cover. Let Hi be its actual deck group. Each Hi commutes with A. Its image in Aut(B) is the deck group of B→P¹_xi.

The two groups on B are distinct. Otherwise their invariant rational fields coincide, so x2=M(x1) for a Möbius transformation M. The branch sets of C0→P¹ computed through the two original étale X-maps must coincide. Thus M preserves the eleven points consisting of the P-roots and infinity. The common reduced tensor-zero divisor likewise forces it to preserve the two q0-roots: equality of their inverse images under a finite surjective map implies equality of the base divisors. Any such M lifts to Z:r³=P/q0. Indeed its Kummer branch weights are ONE at all eleven P/∞ points and TWO at the q0 pair, so the transformed radicand differs from the original by a cube, up to a constant. The independently recorded [marked-Z automorphism calculation](actual_q0_tensor_low_degree_recognition.md), Section3, gives Aut(Z,{U,V})=C3. Hence M acts trivially on x. Therefore x1=x2, y2/y1∈μ3, and the two original X-fields coincide. This contradicts joint degree THREE. Notice that equality of rational fields alone would not have sufficed; both actual étale branch data and the q0 marking have been used.

The common reduced q0-zero divisor on C0 has degree EIGHTEEN. A acts freely along it, since neither original yi is zero or infinite there. Its image on B is therefore a set M of SIX distinct points, stable under both Hi-images. On D, this is the common reduced inverse image of E's q0-marked pair. Both projections D→E have degree THREE and are unramified along that divisor. Their image in E×E has at least TWO delta invariants at the four marked pairs. Hodge index and adjunction give arithmetic genus at most TEN, so
\[
g(D)\le8.
\]
The root D→B branches at all six points of M. Away from them it can branch only over infinity of x1. Since B→P¹_x1 is cyclic of degree THREE, that infinity fiber is either one point of multiplicity THREE or three simple points. Thus its branch count is SIX or NINE and
\[
g(D)=3g(B)+4\quad\text{or}\quad3g(B)+7.
\]
In particular g(B)≤1. If g(B)=1, only the first case is possible and g(D)=7.

It remains to exclude the two distinct quotient groups, separately for rational and elliptic B.

Suppose first B is rational. Let H be generated by the two distinct cubic groups on B. Since it preserves the six-point set M, it acts faithfully on that set and embeds in A6. If |H| is odd, it divides45. Any nontrivial Sylow-five subgroup is normal by Sylow's theorem. Its unique fixed point on P¹ is then H-stable; conjugate that point to infinity and its generator to z↦z+1. The affine multiplier of any normalizing element lies in F5×. Because H has odd order, all multipliers are ONE, and its translation subgroup has order FIVE. This contradicts generation by cubic elements. The remaining odd possibilities have order THREE or NINE. A subgroup of PGL2 of order NINE is abelian and tame, hence cyclic: a nontrivial semisimple element's two fixed points are preserved individually by its odd-order centralizer. Such a cyclic group cannot be generated by two distinct cubic subgroups.

Hence |H| is even. Its Sylow-two subgroup P cannot be cyclic. If it were, N_H(P)/C_H(P) would have odd order, since P⊂C_H(P) is Sylow, while it embeds in the two-group Aut(P). Thus N_H(P)=C_H(P). Burnside's normal-complement theorem gives a normal odd-order subgroup with two-group quotient. Every cubic generator lies in that subgroup, a contradiction. The exact theorem used is the usual normal-complement criterion N_H(P)=C_H(P); see [Kurzweil–Stellmacher, Theorem7.2.1](https://theory.uchicago.edu/~sethi/Teaching/P342-W2017/Kurzweil-Stellmacher_Theory%20of%20finite%20groups.pdf).

Every H-orbit on P¹ is now even. An odd orbit would have a point stabilizer containing a Sylow-two subgroup. A finite two-subgroup fixing a point of P¹ in characteristic FIVE is cyclic: conjugate it into the affine group, whose multiplier map is injective on a two-group. This contradicts the preceding noncyclicity. But the branch set of C0→B is H-invariant because both lifted Hi commute with A. Hurwitz for this tame cubic map, with g(C0)=25,g(B)=0, gives branch cardinality TWENTY-SEVEN. Its odd cardinality contradicts the orbit assertion.

Suppose instead B is elliptic. An origin identifies Aut(B) with translations semidirect origin-preserving automorphisms. Each cubic quotient group has nontrivial linear part μ3, since it has rational quotient. Thus H lies in translations semidirect μ3. Its translation kernel N acts freely on M, so |N| divides SIX. It is μ3-stable. It cannot have an order-two part: such a unique nonzero two-torsion point would be fixed by μ3, whereas the kernel of ζ−1 has order THREE. Distinct cubic subgroups therefore force |N|=3. The μ3 action on this cyclic three-group is trivial. Hence H≅C3².

Each Hi lifts canonically to C0′ by fixing wi. This lift has order THREE and commutes with A and C: check on C0 and the generator wi of the root field. It consequently induces an actual order-three action on D, fixing Ei pointwise and inducing its specified action on B. The group generated on D by these two lifts and the central w-deck C has order TWENTY-SEVEN. It is a central extension of C3² by C3, generated by order-three elements, so it has exponent THREE: commutators are central of exponent THREE, and (uv)³=u³v³[v,u]³=1.

This tame order-twenty-seven group cannot act on the genus-SEVEN curve D. Its point stabilizers are cyclic and hence have order ONE or THREE. Every branched orbit contributes18 to Hurwitz, while the unramified genus term is54 times an integer. The left side 2g(D)−2=12 is not divisible by18. This contradiction completes the distinct-group case.

Both genus cases are impossible. Together with the equal-group argument and the exclusion of cubic index ONE, this proves that d0 cannot equal THREE. The conclusion is a bounded-degree recognition statement, not an unrestricted tensor theorem or a solution to the common-cover problem.
