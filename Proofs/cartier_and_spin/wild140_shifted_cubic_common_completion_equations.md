# Proof: shifted cubic equations and the four-dimensional packet

Version1, 3 October2026. Independent review pending. [Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/wild140_shifted_cubic_common_completion_equations.md).

Since P is Weierstrass, every function in L(20P) has a unique form P₁₀+yQ₇. A w⁷ term in Q would give a pole19 for its odd part. Its differential would have pole20:19 is prime to five. The required dG=cF³σ has exact pole19 at P, and the even part has derivative poles with different parity. Thus that term vanishes and degQ≤6. Equivalently the odd differential operator ΦQ′+Φ′Q/2 has a degree-eleven term2Q₇, whereas the required odd coefficient A³+3AΦB² has degree at most ten.

At any F zero the condition gcd(A,B)=1 makes B a unit. Such zeros correspond bijectively to the seven roots of N, with y=−A/B, including a possible Weierstrass zero. A multiple root of N there would be a multiple F zero: its conjugate factor A−yB is a unit away from a Weierstrass point, while at a Weierstrass zero Φ has a simple zero and A=0,B≠0, directly giving N′≠0. Thus N is squarefree. The regular expression yF′ evaluates as
\[
D=yA'+\frac{\Phi'}2B+\Phi B'
=-\frac{N'}{2B}\quad\text{at every }N=0.
\]
This remains regular at a Weierstrass point; it does not require the individually singular derivative dy/dw there. In particular D is nonzero everywhere on this fiber.

The [actual weak-invariant specialization](../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/weak_local_completed_extension_invariant.md) says precisely that D⁵/G² has one common nonzero value. If G²=λD⁵, then G=R/B at N=0 gives
\[
R^2B^3=\lambda(-1/2)^5(N')^5.
\]
Since N is squarefree this pointwise equality is equivalent to divisibility by N. Put μ=λ(−1/2)⁵. Conversely a congruence with μ≠0 makes R nonzero at every root, and supplies the required common value. The normalized weak extensions are therefore equal over the SAME base by the accepted local classification. There is no replacement by numerical inertia alone.

Every difference of primitives lies in the kernel of d:k(Y)→Ω¹. In characteristic five this kernel is k(Y)⁵. If such a difference has pole at most20P, its fifth root has pole at most4P and is a quadratic polynomial in w. Thus the kernel inside L(20P) is exactly span{1,w⁵,w¹⁰}. The nonzero derivative of G₀ makes its extension by that kernel four-dimensional. This proves the packet description and the three corrections to R.

For any element H of the packet with nonzero derivative, at every wild point ord(dH)=3. If H vanishes there its order is exactly four: the first nonconstant local term forced by the derivative is the nonzero fourth-order term, whereas the constant has been set to zero. Therefore H cannot vanish at six wild points, since24 exceeds its allowed pole degree20. An element of the derivative kernel is the fifth power of a quadratic and cannot vanish at three of the distinct w-coordinates. Consequently evaluation on any six points is injective, and evaluation on all seven is injective. If the primitive evaluation vector lay in the correction packet, subtracting that correction would give a nonzero primitive vanishing at all seven points, which is impossible by the same degree count.

Choose a square root dᵢ of Dᵢ⁵ at each of the seven points. A projective G with a common completion value has evaluations proportional to (εᵢdᵢ), εᵢ∈{±1}. There are2⁶=64 such sign vectors modulo simultaneous negation. Injective packet evaluation gives at most one projective G for each vector. This counts possible G only for a fixed F; it imposes no asserted isolation on the family of F.

Finally, in the packet basis (1,w⁵,w¹⁰,G₀), each dual evaluation point has coordinates (1,tᵢ,tᵢ²,gᵢ), tᵢ=wᵢ⁵. They lie on X₀X₂−X₁²=0, a quadric cone in P³. The four-dimensional rank just proved prevents all seven points from lying in a plane, in particular in the correction conic plane. The nonzero primitive offset is thus an ACTUAL geometric obstruction to importing the centered five-point argument.
