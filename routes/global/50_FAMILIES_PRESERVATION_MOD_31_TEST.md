# The mod-31 shadow of families preservation

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved.**  **Audit: PASS** -- `/root/families_notes_audit`,
2026-09-04.  The audit found no breaking objection and recommends treating
this as a finite diagnostic, not as a route to full families preservation.
[Audit record](audits/50_FAMILIES_PRESERVATION_MOD_31_AUDIT.md).

Let

\[
 S=\mathbf P^1_k(31,31,31),\qquad k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5,
\]

and let

\[
 u,v:\mathcal T\rightrightarrows S
\]

be a connected representable finite etale self-correspondence.  This note
extracts the cheapest finite-quotient consequence of the
``families-preserving'' hypothesis in Minamide--Sawada--Tsujimura,
Theorem 3.11(iii).

The consequence is already very strong.  For a minimal correspondence in
the profile

\[
 d=31+m,\qquad g(C)=15-m,\qquad 1\leq m\leq13,
\]

normalized families preservation forces the correspondence to be visible.
Thus any minimal non-visible correspondence of degree \(32\leq d\leq44\)
must fail families preservation already after passage to

\[
 \pi_1(S)^{\mathrm{ab}}/31\simeq \mathbf F_{31}^2.
\]

This does not prove that no such correspondence exists.  Rather, it shows
that applying the new literature theorem would require proving a condition
which, in this range, is already nearly the desired rigidity statement.

## 1. A scalar lemma in the first abelian quotient

Fix compatible geometric base points and put

\[
 G=\pi_1(S),\qquad H=\pi_1(\mathcal T).
\]

The two legs give injections \(i,j:H\hookrightarrow G\).  Let

\[
 V=G^{\mathrm{ab}}/31G^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]

Prime-to-\(5\) specialization, or the root-stack Kummer sequence, gives

\[
 V\simeq \mathbf F_{31}^2.                                    \tag{50.1}
\]

The three canonical inertia vectors \(t_0,t_1,t_\infty\in V\) satisfy

\[
 t_0+t_1+t_\infty=0,                                         \tag{50.2}
\]

and any two of them form a basis.  In particular, the three lines
\(\mathbf F_{31}t_i\) are distinct.

Let \(\sigma\in S_3=\operatorname{Aut}(S)\), and let
\(\bar\sigma\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\) be its action on (50.1).  Define

\[
 A=q\circ i:H\longrightarrow V,
 \qquad
 B=\bar\sigma^{-1}q\circ j:H\longrightarrow V,               \tag{50.3}
\]

where \(q:G\to V\) is the quotient map.

### Lemma 50.1 (projective pointwise rigidity)

Suppose that, for every closed procyclic subgroup \(I\subseteq i(H)\),
the subgroup

\[
 \sigma_*^{-1}\bigl(j(i^{-1}(I))\bigr)
\]

is conjugate to \(I\) in \(G\).  If \(A(H)\ne0\), then there is a single
\(\lambda\in\mathbf F_{31}^{\times}\) such that

\[
                         B=\lambda A.                         \tag{50.4}
\]

Equivalently, on cohomology,

\[
 v^*=\lambda\,u^*\sigma^*
 :H^1(S,\mathbf F_{31})\longrightarrow
  H^1(\mathcal T,\mathbf F_{31}).                             \tag{50.5}
\]

If \(31\nmid [G:i(H)]\), then \(A\) is surjective.  If
\([G:i(H)]=31), its image has dimension one or two; it has dimension one
exactly when \(i(H)\triangleleft G\), equivalently when the degree-31
monodromy is cyclic.

#### Proof

Apply \(q\) to the asserted conjugacy for the procyclic group generated by
an arbitrary \(h\in H\).  Conjugacy disappears in the abelian quotient,
and hence

\[
       \mathbf F_{31}A(h)=\mathbf F_{31}B(h)                  \tag{50.6}
\]

as subgroups of \(V\), including equality of their zero subgroups.  Thus
\(\ker A=\ker B\), and \(B\) factors through an injective linear map

\[
 \overline B:A(H)\longrightarrow V
\]

which carries each nonzero vector into its own line.  If \(A(H)\) has
dimension two, apply this to two independent vectors and their sum; the
three resulting scalar factors are equal.  If it has dimension one, the
same conclusion is immediate.  This proves (50.4), and dualizing gives
(50.5).

The index

\[
 [V:A(H)]=[G:q^{-1}(A(H))]
\]

divides \([G:i(H)]\).  It is a power of \(31\), proving surjectivity when
the latter index is prime to \(31\).  If that index is \(31\), the image
cannot be zero.  A one-dimensional image gives

\[
 i(H)=q^{-1}(A(H)),
\]

so \(i(H)\) is normal.  Conversely a normal subgroup of index \(31\) is
the kernel of a nonzero character \(G\to\mathbf F_{31}\), and its image
in \(V\) is the corresponding one-dimensional kernel.  This also proves
the monodromy assertion.  \(\square\)

## 2. What (50.4) says in a generalized profile

Assume now the generalized profile of file 16:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 x^{-1}(a)&=31P_a+E_a,\\
 r^{-1}(a)&=31Q_a+F_a,
 \qquad a\in\{0,1,\infty\},                                 \tag{50.7}
\end{aligned}
\]

where each \(E_a,F_a\) is reduced of degree \(m\geq1\), and

\[
 U=E_0+E_1+E_\infty=F_0+F_1+F_\infty                       \tag{50.8}
\]

is the stacky divisor of \(\mathcal T\).

At a point \(P\in E_a\), the map of root stacks carries the canonical
generator of source inertia to the canonical generator over \(a\).  One
way to see the absence of a hidden power is to write the target stack
divisor as \(\mathcal D_a\).  On \(\mathcal T\),

\[
 u^*\mathcal D_a=P_a+\sum_{P\in E_a}\mathcal P,              \tag{50.9}
\]

where \(31\mathcal P=P\) on the coarse curve; the coefficient of every
stack divisor is one.  The same statement holds for \(v\).

### Proposition 50.2 (partition and Kummer rigidity)

Under the normalized families-preserving hypothesis of Lemma 50.1, there
is a permutation \(\sigma\in S_3\) such that

\[
                 F_{\sigma(a)}=E_a
                 \quad(a=0,1,\infty),                        \tag{50.10}
\]

and the scalar in (50.4) is \(\lambda=1\).

After replacing \(r\) by the corresponding fractional-linear transform,
so that the permutation in (50.10) becomes the identity, there are
functions \(A_0,A_1\in k(C)^\times\) satisfying

\[
       \frac r x=A_0^{31},\qquad
       \frac{r-1}{x-1}=A_1^{31},                              \tag{50.11}
\]

and

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{div}(A_0)&=Q_0+P_\infty-P_0-Q_\infty,\\
 \operatorname{div}(A_1)&=Q_1+P_\infty-P_1-Q_\infty.         \tag{50.12}
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular,

\[
                 \deg A_0\leq2,\qquad \deg A_1\leq2.        \tag{50.13}
\]

#### Proof

Here \(d=31+m\) is prime to \(31\), so Lemma 50.1 makes \(A\) surjective.
For \(P\in U\), compare (50.4) on a canonical generator of its inertia.
Equations (50.2) and (50.9) give

\[
                    t_{b}=\lambda t_{\sigma(a)}              \tag{50.14}
\]

when \(P\in E_a\cap F_b\).  The three inertia lines are distinct, so
\(b=\sigma(a)\); then (50.14) also gives \(\lambda=1\).  This proves
(50.10).

After normalization, (50.5) says that the Kummer classes pulled back by
the two legs are equal.  The two standard classes are represented
generically by \(x,x-1\) for the first leg and by \(r,r-1\) for the
second.  Restriction of a finite etale torsor on a normal connected stack
to its generic point is faithful.  Kummer theory over \(k(C)\) therefore
gives (50.11).  Taking divisors and using (50.7) and (50.10) gives
(50.12), from which (50.13) is immediate.  \(\square\)

The two parts of Proposition 50.2 are logically distinct.  Equality of
the residual partitions is forced by local inertia.  After that equality,
the divisors of the two ratios in (50.11) are divisible by \(31\), but
(50.11) additionally says that the resulting two classes in
\(J(C)[31]\) vanish.  The common-boundary statement (50.8) alone supplies
neither assertion.

## 3. In coarse genus at least two this already gives visibility

### Theorem 50.3 (families preservation excludes the main low-degree range)

Let \(u,v\) be a minimal-degree non-visible correspondence, so that file
16 gives

\[
                         k(C)=k(x,r).                          \tag{50.15}
\]

If it has the generalized profile with

\[
                  1\leq m\leq13
                  \quad\text{and}\quad
                  32\leq d=31+m\leq44,                       \tag{50.16}
\]

then its induced open-subgroup isomorphism is not families preserving in
\(G\), even after any \(S_3\)-normalization.

#### Proof

Suppose otherwise and normalize as in Proposition 50.2.  If \(A_0\) is
constant, then \(r=cx\) for a constant \(c\ne0\).  Both maps have branch
locus exactly \(\{0,1,\infty\}\), and multiplication by \(c\) fixes
\(0,\infty\); hence \(c=1\) and \(r=x\).  The same argument applied to
\(r-1=c(x-1)\) handles the case in which \(A_1\) is constant.  Either case
is visible.

Otherwise (50.13), together with \(g(C)=15-m\geq2\), shows that \(A_0\)
and \(A_1\) are both degree-two functions.  A curve of genus at least two
has at most one degree-two map to \(\mathbf P^1\), up to a target
automorphism.  Thus

\[
                       k(A_0)=k(A_1)=k(h)                     \tag{50.17}
\]

is the unique hyperelliptic subfield of \(k(C)\).  Subtracting the two
identities in (50.11) yields

\[
       x\bigl(A_0^{31}-A_1^{31}\bigr)=1-A_1^{31}.             \tag{50.18}
\]

If the coefficient of \(x\) vanished identically, (50.18) would make
\(A_1\) constant.  Otherwise (50.18) puts \(x\), and then
\(r=xA_0^{31}\), in \(k(h)\).  This contradicts (50.15), because
\([k(C):k(h)]=2\).  \(\square\)

## 4. The two endpoints and the strategic boundary

For \(m=14\), the coarse curve has genus one.  Equations (50.11)--(50.13)
still hold, but two degree-two functions on an elliptic curve need not
generate the same quadratic subfield.  The proof of Theorem 50.3 therefore
stops exactly at the degree-\(45\) elliptic profile.

For the degree-\(31\), genus-\(15\), scheme-source profile, there is no
source inertia with which to force \(\lambda=1\).  Lemma 50.1 still applies.
After normalization it says

\[
 v^*=\lambda u^*\sigma^*.                                   \tag{50.19}
\]

If the leg monodromy is noncyclic, \(u^*\) is injective; if it is cyclic,
its kernel has dimension one.  In divisor language, (50.19) gives scalar
relations, after the same permutation, between the two pairs of
31-torsion classes

\[
 [P_0-P_\infty],\ [P_1-P_\infty]
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 [Q_0-Q_\infty],\ [Q_1-Q_\infty]                            \tag{50.20}
\]

in \(J(C)[31]\).  No argument here turns (50.20) into equality of the
coarse maps.

The practical conclusion is that one should not try to weaken the general
Minamide--Sawada--Tsujimura theorem as the next step.  Its proof genuinely
uses all procyclic conjugacy classes, and the authors themselves leave a
related weakening open.  In our application, even its first abelian
shadow would force (50.10) and (50.11), neither of which follows from the
known profile bookkeeping.  A narrower and more geometric target is:

1. force the two three-part partitions of \(U\) to agree after one
   permutation; and
2. kill the two resulting discrepancy classes in \(J(C)[31]\).

For \(1\leq m\leq13\), those two statements imply visibility directly by
Theorem 50.3, without invoking the full families-preserving theorem or
constructing an almost-surface quotient.
