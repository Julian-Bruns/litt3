## 1. Ordinary-atlas local different bound

### Non-Galois local uniformity

At a stacky point with completed chart `[Spec k[[u]]/I]`, base change of
the representable finite etale atlas is a finite etale cover of the
strictly henselian formal disc.  It is therefore a disjoint union of
copies of that disc.  On each copy, the coarse map is the invariant-ring
map

\[
                    k[[u]]^I\longrightarrow k[[u]].
\]

Thus every point in the coarse fiber has the same ramification index
`e=|I|` and different exponent `d`.  This argument does not require the
global atlas to be Galois.  A finite extension induced by a faithful
finite automorphism group is separable even when `p` divides `|I|`, so
the asserted separability of the coarse map is also correct.

### Rational differential and Cartier calculation

The valuation formula

\[
 \operatorname{ord}_P(q^*\eta)
   =e_i\operatorname{ord}_{q(P)}(\eta)+d_i
\]

is the standard different formula.  It shows exactly that
`H0(P1,omega(D))`, with `D=sum floor(d_i/e_i)s_i`, pulls back into
holomorphic differentials.  Pullback is injective because the map is
separable, and Cartier commutes with this pullback.

The displayed partial-fraction basis has the stated dimension

\[
 (w-1)+\sum_i(h_i-1)=\deg D-1.
\]

The logarithmic part has dimension `w-1` and Cartier is bijective on it.
For a higher pole `u^{-j}du`, Cartier either kills it or replaces its pole
order by

\[
                         1+(j-1)/p,
\]

when `j` is congruent to one modulo `p`; for `j>=2` this remains a
higher pole and repeated Cartier application eventually kills it.  Thus
the logarithmic and higher-pole summands are separately Cartier-stable,
with the claimed bijective and nilpotent dimensions.

For a smooth projective curve, the dimensions of the stable and
nilpotent parts of Cartier on `H0(omega)` are respectively `f_C` and
`g(C)-f_C`.  The one-step kernel count is also correct: among
`j=2,...,h_i`, the form `u^{-j}du` is a nonzero exact differential
precisely when `p` does not divide `j-1`, giving

\[
 (h_i-1)-\left\lfloor\frac{h_i-1}{p}\right\rfloor.
\]

This verifies both boxed inequalities.

### Consequences

For an ordinary curve the nilpotent dimension is zero, so every
`floor(d_i/e_i)<=1`, equivalently `d_i<2e_i`.  The shorter exact-form
proof is valid as well: a coordinate with a sole simple pole gives a
nonzero exact holomorphic pullback if `d_i>=2e_i`.

For pure `p`-group inertia,

\[
 d=2(|P|-1)+\sum_{j\ge2}(|G_j|-1)<2|P|.
\]

The tail is therefore less than two.  In odd characteristic every
nonzero summand is at least two, so the tail vanishes and `G_2=1`.
The one-point exclusion and the fixed-pair bound `w<=f_X+1=7` then
follow exactly as written.

## 2. Weak two-point power-of-two theorem

For weak wild inertia `P semidirect C_m`, with `Q=|P|`, the different is
`Qm+Q-2`.  File 13's graded tame-character lemma gives `m|(Q-1)` because
the sole positive ramification quotient has dimension `log_p Q` at
break one.  With `x=n/(Qm)` and `y=n/N`, Riemann--Hurwitz is exactly

\[
                         A=x(Q-2)-y.
\]

Writing `d=gcd(x,y)`, `x=da`, `y=db`, and `D=A/d` gives

\[
 b=a(Q-2)-D,\qquad Nb=Qma,
\]

with all three coprimality claims in the proof.  Comparing `p`-adic
valuations gives `b=Qc`, and elementary coprimality then gives

\[
 m=ct,\qquad N=at,\qquad t\mid(Q-1)/c.
\]

Conversely, the listed divisor and coprimality conditions reconstruct
these integers and ensure both inertia orders divide `n`; hence the list
construction is exhaustive as necessary numerical data.

The bound on `Q` is correct.  From

\[
 k(Q-2)=2c+D,qquad k=a-c\ge1,qquad c\le Q-1,
\]

the assumption `Q>D+4` forces `k<=2`.  If `k=1`, parity makes the
power-of-two integer `D` equal to one, and `c|(Q-1)=2c+2` gives
`Q in {5,7}`.  If `k=2`, then
`c=Q-2-D/2` and `c|(D/2+1)`, forcing `Q<=D+3`, a contradiction.
Thus

\[
                         Q\le\max(A+4,7).
\]

### Independent characteristic-five enumeration

For `A=16`, the bound leaves only `Q=5`.  Exhaustively iterating

\[
 D\in\{1,2,4,8,16\},\quad c\mid4,\quad t\mid4/c
\]

and applying the stated integrality and coprimality filters produces
exactly

\[
\begin{array}{r|r|r}
n&e&N\\\hline
35&5&7\\
60&5&3\\
70&10&14\\
120&10&6\\
140&20&28\\
160&5&2\\
240&20&12\\
320&10&4\\
640&20&8\\
2240&20&7
\end{array}
\]

with no duplicates or omitted divisor choices.  Exact rational
substitution verifies `n(3/e-1/N)=16`, `e|n`, `N|n`, and `m|4` in every
row.  In particular, the endpoint `n=2240` is genuine necessary
arithmetic data, not a floating-point artifact.

## Scope and non-breaking comments

Both files prove necessary conditions only.  Neither proves realization
of a listed signature, existence of a finite common orbifold for an
arbitrary common cover, or anything about coreless correspondences.
The ordinary-atlas theorem deliberately does not make mixed wild inertia
weakly ramified.  These limitations are stated correctly in the files.
