# An explicitly soluble second-order equation for higher differentials

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: /root.
Independent local/naturality check: /root/schwarzian_pluriform_local_regularity,
GPT-6 Astra medium, 2026-09-05; no breaking issue found. This was a scoped
check, not an audit of every assertion or cited theorem below.
Seventy-two exact F5 power-series checks verified the solution formulas,
the differential equation, and the Wronskian through order 44.

Everything here is intrinsic to characteristic five. No lifting or
ordinary-indigenous hypothesis is used.

## 1. Equation and explicit answer

Let C/k be a smooth curve, k algebraically closed of characteristic five,
K=k(C), and t a separating rational parameter. Write ∂=∂/∂t.
Let d>0 be prime to five and let

    s=a(dt)^d,       a∈K*,       ℓ=a'/(d a).

Define

    Q_s = ℓ'−ℓ²/2
        = a''/(d a) − (2d+1)(a'/a)²/(2d²).

The associated equation is

    y'' + (Q_s/2)y = 0.                                  (1)

Choose the unique j∈{1,2,3,4} such that 2dj≡−1 modulo five, and put

    v=a^j,       b=v^(−2)=a^(−2j).

Then v is always a nonzero rational solution of (1). This is precisely
a Frobenius phenomenon: writing 2dj+1=5m gives

    a v^(2d)=(a^m)^5.

Thus v has the logarithmic derivative of the formal fractional power
a^(-1/(2d)), without adjoining that root. Fifth powers have derivative
zero; they explain both explicit solvability and the large constant
field in the answer below. This is not a lifting construction.

Let r∈{1,2,3,4} satisfy rd≡1 modulo five and put n=(rd−1)/5.
The following conditions are equivalent:

    C_n(s^r)=0;                                         (2)
    C(b dt)=0;
    b''''=0;
    (1) has two independent rational solutions over K.

Independence here is over the differential constant field K^5, not k.
Under these conditions an explicit fundamental system is

    y₁=v,     y₂=v u,
    u=tb − (t²/2)b' + (t³/6)b'' − (t⁴/24)b''' .          (3)

The denominators 2,6,24 are invertible in characteristic five. There
is no unevaluated integral in this formula. One has u'=b and

    y₁ y₂'−y₁' y₂=1.

Every rational solution is

    y=c₁y₁+c₂y₂,        c₁,c₂∈K^5.                     (4)

For reference, the residues determining the formulas are:

| d modulo 5 | r | j |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 3 | 1 |
| 3 | 2 | 4 |
| 4 | 4 | 3 |

The construction applies to arbitrarily large d, not only these four
small weights.

## 2. Proof of the solution formula

The logarithmic derivative of v is

    v'/v = j a'/a = −ℓ/2.

Consequently

    v''/v = −ℓ'/2+ℓ²/4 = −Q_s/2,

which proves that v solves (1). Substituting y=vz into the equation
and using this identity gives

    z''+2(v'/v)z'=0,
    equivalently (v²z')'=0.                              (5)

The constant field of ∂ on K is K^5. Thus a second independent
solution exists precisely when b=v^(−2) has a rational primitive.
The nonzero constant in v²z' can be divided out.

Because t is separating, K has basis 1,t,t²,t³,t⁴ over K^5, and
∂⁵=0. Writing b=Σ_(i=0)^4 B_i t^i with B_i∈K^5, one obtains

    b''''=24 B₄,
    C(b dt)=B₄^(1/5)dt.

Thus b has a primitive exactly when either displayed expression
vanishes. Direct differentiation of (3) telescopes to

    u'=b − (t⁴/24)b''''.

This proves the asserted primitive formula. Its Wronskian follows
from v²u'=v²b=1.

The frame formula for generalized Cartier is

    C_n(a^r(dt)^(5n+1))=(dt)^n C(a^r dt).

Moreover −2j≡r modulo five, so there is an integer m with
−2j=r+5m. Hence b=a^(5m)a^r and

    C(b dt)=a^m C(a^r dt).

This proves the equivalence with (2).

Finally, express the jet of any other solution in the two columns
(y₁,y₁') and (y₂,y₂'). Their determinant is one. Differentiating
its two coefficient functions using (1) shows that both have
derivative zero. They therefore belong to K^5, proving (4).

If (2) fails, the rational solution space has dimension exactly one
over K^5, generated by v. Nor does a second independent solution
appear in a finite separable function-field extension: rational
Cartier commutes with such extension, and the nonzero form C(b dt)
stays nonzero. This qualification concerns separable extensions
equipped with the extended derivation; no assertion about arbitrary
differential-field extensions is needed.

## 3. Exact curvature: what the equation remembers

Use variables z=y/v and w=v²z'. The horizontal system becomes

    z'=b w,        w'=0.

Thus its connection matrix for ∇_∂ is ∂−N, with

    N = [0 b; 0 0].

Since ∂⁵=0, its p-curvature in this gauge is

    ψ(∂) = [0 −b''''; 0 0].                              (6)

For example this follows by applying the fifth connection iterate
to the two constant basis vectors; p-curvature is K-linear.
Its nilpotence is automatic. It is zero exactly in the Cartier-zero
branch. Equivalently, if C(b dt)=c dt, the upper-right entry is c⁵,
because 24=−1 in characteristic five.

Thus (1) repackages the precise Cartier obstruction, rather than
creating an extra nonvanishing condition. Its useful additional
features are its second-order form, explicit solutions, and controlled
projective singularities.

## 4. Naturality: retaining the two actual maps

Under a separating change t=t(z), the coefficient of s becomes

    a_z=a(t(z))(t')^d.

Direct differentiation gives

    ℓ_z=t'ℓ_t+t''/t',
    Q_z=(t')²Q_t+{t,z},                                  (7)

where

    {t,z}=t'''/t'−(3/2)(t''/t')²

is the Schwarzian. Hence Q_s is a meromorphic projective connection,
not a quadratic differential by itself. Differences of two such
connections are quadratic differentials. Local solutions transform
as half-densities y_z=(t')^(−1/2)y_t.

Let f:Z→X and g:Z→Y be the actual finite étale maps and assume

    f*s_X=g*s_Y.

Applying (7) to each pullback proves that the resulting projective
connections on Z are equal, with their prescribed pullback
identifications. This uses the same actual Z and both maps. It is
not a statement about independent equations on unrelated covers.

The construction does not require the span to be coreless. In the
coreless setting, the earlier [Cartier constraint](../../Theorems/Thm_cartier_generator.md)
supplies (2) whenever the positive invariant generator has weight
not dividing four, and also in the zero-scalar cases of weights 1,2,4.
It still does not prove that a positive invariant exists.

## 5. Exact local regularity criterion

Let t now be a local parameter at P, write

    a=t^e A(t),    A(0)≠0,    E=e/d∈F₅,
    h=A'/(d A).

The integer e may be positive, zero, or negative. Then

    ℓ=E/t+h,
    Q_s=−E(E+2)/(2t²) − E h(0)/t + regular.              (8)

Without a Cartier hypothesis this is regular if and only if either
E=0, or E=−2 and A'(0)=0.

Assume (2). The Laurent expansion of a^r has no nonzero term in
an exponent congruent to four modulo five. Its leading exponent
er is congruent to E.

- E=4 is impossible, since the leading term would violate (2).
- When E=3=−2, the next exponent is four modulo five. Its
  coefficient r A(0)^(r−1) A'(0) must vanish, so A'(0)=0.
- For E=1 or 2, the double-pole coefficient in (8) is exactly 1.

Consequently

    Q_s is regular at P
      ⇔ ord_P(s)≡0 or −2d modulo five.                  (9)

The other two allowed residue classes give genuine double poles,
with the same leading coefficient. The simple-pole coefficient
need not agree between them.

For a common generator with divisor eS and e uniform, this tests
regularity simultaneously at the entire clump. It covers two
of the four allowed residue classes in every weight d prime to five.

## 6. Intrinsic global interpretation and finite endpoint reduction

If C is projective and Q_s is regular everywhere, choose a theta
characteristic L, so L²≅ω_C. Equation (1) glues to the operator

    L^(−1) → L³,      y ↦ y''+(Q_s/2)y.

Its companion connection is on J¹(L^(−1)), with filtration

    0 → L → J¹(L^(−1)) → L^(−1) → 0.

The second fundamental map from L to L^(−1)⊗ω_C is an isomorphism,
and the determinant connection is trivial. This is an SL2-oper.
Its projectivization is independent of the theta characteristic.
Under (2), the explicit rational horizontal basis proves zero
p-curvature generically; regularity extends that identity globally.
It is therefore a regular dormant PGL2-oper.

This uses dormant in its literal zero-p-curvature sense, not any
ordinariness condition. The characteristic-five lifting discussion
elsewhere is not used.

For a fixed projective curve of genus g≥2 with 5 not dividing g−1,
there are only finitely many dormant PGL2-opers. See
[Joshi–Pauly, Theorem 6.1.3 and Corollary 6.1.6](https://math.univ-cotedazur.fr/~pauly/Hit-Moc.pdf):
nilpotent opers form a closed subscheme of an affine space, and
their valuative properness follows by semistable reduction and
preservation of the maximal oper Harder–Narasimhan polygon.
Dormant opers are included in that finite locus. Their Section 3.2
specifies the determinant and projective conventions and imposes
p>rank and p not dividing g−1. Both conditions hold for rank two
and our genera 9 and 25. The finiteness consequence here is stated
only in this sufficient range; the elementary ODE and regularity
theorems above have no genus-congruence restriction.

Thus, for endpoints in that range, the regular part of the
Cartier-zero higher-weight branch
maps into a finite endpoint list of dormant projective connections,
even though d itself is unbounded. If these two finite lists have
no compatible pullbacks on the actual source, this branch is
excluded. We have not proved that incompatibility for the proposed
endpoints. Existence of dormant opers is not an obstruction.

## 7. What explicit solvability does not prove

The constants in (4) range over K^5, which is much larger than k.
For local primitives, equality of Schwarzian connections therefore
allows fractional-linear transformations with fifth-power rational
coefficients, not just constant Möbius transformations.
This is the [already established Schwarzian kernel](39_SCHWARZIAN_TRIANGLE_REDUCTION_AND_LIMIT.md),
not a new ambient-extension theorem.

Consequently rational solvability of (1), or equality of the two
projective connections, does not by this argument give a common
function-field core, simultaneous Galoisness, or a common-cover
contradiction. The construction also forgets part of the original
pluriform data. Any subsequent use must retain these limitations.
