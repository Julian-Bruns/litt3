## The invariant canonical different section

A nonzero constant would make s descend toΓ, contradicting its zero order one at a tame index-two point. Therefore τ_1=0.

At a ramified image z, the fiber has two length-two factors and two reduced factors. Modulo z the s values are0,0,0,0,b,c, with b,c nonzero. Sinceτ_1=0, c=−b. Thusτ_3(z)=b³+c³=0. The pulled third trace has order at least two along qP, so its normalized ratio has poles at mostP. It is again constant, and a nonzero constant would make s³ descend with impossible odd zero order three. Henceτ_3=0. This works for EVERY P.

## Use the elementary fifth coefficient directly

Let e_j be the elementary coefficient section in N^j of the degree-six characteristic polynomial of s. These sections are G-invariant by functoriality of finite-flat characteristic polynomials. Newton's identities with one and three invertible give e_1=e_3=0. We do NOT use the fifth Newton identity, whose scalar five vanishes in this characteristic.

Complete the target local ring at a ramified image z and trivialize N. Each index-two source factor is generated by s, because s has a simple zero there. Its monic degree-two characteristic polynomial has form
\[
Z^2+A_iZ+B_i\quad(i=1,2),
\qquad \operatorname{ord}_z(A_i)\ge1,
\qquad\operatorname{ord}_z(B_i)=1.
\]
Indeed local conjugation has tame sign−1 on the source uniformizer; trace of a uniformizer is divisible by z, and its norm has simple target zero. The two unramified factors give a monic quadratic U(Z) with integral coefficients and unit constant term. In the product of the two ramified quadratics, both its constant coefficient B1B2 and its linear coefficient A1B2+A2B1 have order at least TWO. Multiplication by U preserves that lower bound for the full coefficient of Z. Therefore e_5 vanishes to order at least TWO at every ramified image point.

The ratio φ*e_5/s⁵ now has pole order at mostONE along qP: numerator order at least four, denominator order five. It has no other poles, is G-invariant, and descends to H0(Y,O(P))=k. If that constant were nonzero, s⁵ would descend toΓ with odd zero order five at an index-two point. Hence e_5=0. The full degree-six characteristic polynomial is EVEN.

## Primitivity and the actual minus involution

Choose a rational trivialization e of N and put a=s/φ*e. Its minimal degree divides six. Degree one is impossible as above. In degree two, its degree-six trace is three times its quadratic trace; three is invertible andτ_1=0, so a² would lie in k(Γ). This would make s² descend, impossible in a mixed fiber: the section would vanish also at its unramified companion sheets, where s is a unit.

In degree three the characteristic polynomial is the square of its monic irreducible cubic. The square can be even only if that cubic has zero quadratic and constant coefficients: the degree-five coefficient is twice the quadratic coefficient, and then the degree-three coefficient is twice the constant coefficient. Such a cubic is divisible by Z and cannot be the minimal polynomial of nonzero a. Thus its minimal degree is SIX.

The even minimal polynomial defines an ACTUAL order-two deck involution ι ofT overΓ, with a↦−a. It COMMUTES with G. To verify this without choosing a G-invariant rational trivialization, note that g(a)=f_g a for a rational f_g onΓ, since s is G-invariant and e lies onΓ. The involution fixes every f_g and changes the sign of a. Thereforeιg=gι on a and on k(Γ), which together generate k(T).

## The one-ramified-sheet section is primitive

Now every ramified target fiber has profile(2,1,1,1,1). Choose a rational trivialization e ofN which is a unit at one such target point, and put a=s/φ*e. The intermediate curve with field k(Γ)(a) has exactly ONE point with a zero lying over this target point, because the full T-fiber has just one zero of s. The zero has order one onT. In the intermediate tower its ramification index and intermediate zero order must therefore both be one. If the upper intermediate degree exceeded one, its fiber over that intermediate zero point would have total degree larger than one, forcing another zero onT. Consequently that upper degree is one: a is PRIMITIVE of degree six.

The first trace is still zero by the earlier H0(O(P)) argument. Let e_j be the characteristic coefficient sections. The normalized ratios f_3=φ*e_3/s³ and f_5=φ*e_5/s⁵ both descend to H0(Y,O(3P)). At P any nonzero pole order of either ratio is ODD, since the numerator vanishing order pulls back with factor two and the denominator exponent is odd.

IfP is Weierstrass, neither one nor three is a nongap. Both ratios are then constant; a nonzero constant would make an odd power of s descend, impossible at index two. Hence e_3=e_5=0, producing the forbidden even primitive polynomial. ThusP is NOT Weierstrass.
