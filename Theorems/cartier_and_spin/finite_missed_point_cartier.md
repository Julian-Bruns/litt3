# A single missed finite point forces a fifth-power norm

Version 1, 24 September 2026. For the fixed genus-nine X and R_X,
let \(P\in R_X-\{O\}\). Then
\[
\alpha\in H^0(X,\omega_X(R_X)),\quad C\alpha=\alpha,
\quad\operatorname{ord}_P\alpha\ge2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\alpha=0.
\]
This holds for every one of the twelve geometric finite points and for
arbitrary algebraically closed coefficients. The full stable intersection
of the double-zero space is a Cartier-killed line; there is no nonzero
Cartier-fixed vector in it.

Consequently the norm and integral occupancy assertions of
[the seven-point theorem](seven_point_norm_cartier.md) hold for any actual
primitive of that divisor type whose projected E misses even ONE finite
point of R_X, with no restriction on degree or monodromy. In particular
they apply to all support sizes at most eleven, and to twelve-point support
when the missing point is finite. Under the actual order-five hypothesis
the same trivial-norm and two-torsion relations follow.

For projected support size r in this range, the primitive quotient degree
m necessarily satisfies \(r\le m\le r\lfloor m/5\rfloor\).
This scalar lemma alone does not treat support missing only O or using
all points. For ACTUAL primitives, the later
[unrestricted norm theorem](uniform_admissible_norm.md) removes every
missed-support hypothesis by using their infinity and finite regular jets.
The norm-zero case itself remains an existence problem.
