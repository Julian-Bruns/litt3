# Coprime-product mixed Jacobian vanishing forces descent

Date: 2026-09-05.
Author: `/root/x_elliptic_quotient_maps`.
Status: proved and self-checked.

This note gives a two-leg descent criterion which is valid in every
characteristic.  The only exceptional geometry in the proof is the
hyperelliptic pencil, and coprimality removes that exception.

## The theorem

**Theorem 1.**  Let `k` be an algebraically closed field.  Let

\[
                         H=H_1\times H_2
\]

act freely on a connected smooth projective curve `W`, and assume

\[
                         \gcd(|H_1|,|H_2|)=1.             \tag{1.1}
\]

Let `T` be a smooth projective curve of genus at least two, and let
`r:W -> T` be a nonconstant morphism.  Suppose that, for every
`h_i in H_i`, the homomorphism of Jacobians

\[
 r_*\,(h_{1*}-1)(h_{2*}-1):J(W)\longrightarrow J(T)       \tag{1.2}
\]

is zero.  Then one of the two entire factors fixes `r`: either

\[
                  r\circ h=r\quad\text{for every }h\in H_1,
\]

or the analogous statement holds for `H_2`.  Consequently `r` factors
through one of the quotient maps

\[
                         q_i:W\longrightarrow W/H_i.       \tag{1.3}
\]

If `r` is finite etale, then the descended map `W/H_i -> T` is finite
etale as well.

Equivalently, (1.2) says that `r_*` kills the abelian subvariety of
`J(W)` generated by all mixed images
`(h_{1*}-1)(h_{2*}-1)J(W)`.

## Proof

Choose a point `t_0 in T(k)` and write

\[
             \iota:T\longrightarrow J(T),\qquad
             P\longmapsto[ P-t_0],
             \qquad a=\iota\circ r.
\]

Fix `h_i in H_i`.  Since the two factors commute, the morphism

\[
 \delta_{h_1,h_2}
   =a\circ h_1h_2-a\circ h_1-a\circ h_2+a:W\longrightarrow J(T)
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]

induces on `J(W)` exactly the homomorphism in (1.2).  The universal
property of the Jacobian therefore says that (2.1) is constant; denote
its value by `c(h_1,h_2)`.

Put `n_i=ord(h_i)`.  Summing (2.1), evaluated at
`w,h_1w,...,h_1^{n_1-1}w`, makes both first differences telescope and
gives

\[
                         n_1c(h_1,h_2)=0.                  \tag{2.2}
\]

The same argument in the `h_2` direction gives `n_2c(h_1,h_2)=0`.
The two orders divide the coprime integers `|H_1|` and `|H_2|`; hence
they are coprime.  Bezout's identity in the abelian group `J(T)(k)`
now gives

\[
                         c(h_1,h_2)=0.                     \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus, at every `w in W`, there is an equality in `Pic^2(T)`

\[
 [r(h_1h_2w)]+[r(w)]
       \sim [r(h_1w)]+[r(h_2w)].                            \tag{2.4}
\]

We use the elementary classification of linearly equivalent effective
divisors of degree two on a curve of genus at least two.  If `T` is not
hyperelliptic, two such divisors are linearly equivalent only when they
are equal.  If `T` is hyperelliptic, with involution `iota_T`, then two
distinct such divisors must both belong to the unique hyperelliptic
pencil and hence have the form `P+iota_T(P)`.

Suppose first that `T` is nonhyperelliptic.  Equality of the two
unordered pairs in (2.4) says, at each point of `W`, that either

\[
                         r\circ h_2=r                       \tag{2.5}
\]

or

\[
                         r\circ h_1=r.                      \tag{2.6}
\]

The equalizer loci in (2.5) and (2.6) are closed and cover `W`.
Because `W` is irreducible, one of them is all of `W`.  Thus, for this
fixed pair `(h_1,h_2)`, at least one of the two elements fixes `r`.

Now suppose that `T` is hyperelliptic.  The same argument, including
the case in which the two divisors in (2.4) coincide, shows that `W` is
the union of the following three closed loci:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A&:\quad r\circ h_2=r,\\
 B&:\quad r\circ h_1=r,\\
 C&:\quad r\circ h_1h_2=\iota_T\circ r
       \quad\text{and}\quad
       r\circ h_2=\iota_T\circ r\circ h_1.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{2.7}
\]

Again irreducibility implies that one of `A,B,C` is all of `W`.  If
neither `h_1` nor `h_2` fixes `r`, the third alternative must hold.
Composing its second equality with `h_1`, using commutativity, and
comparing with its first equality gives

\[
                         r\circ h_1^2=r.                    \tag{2.8}
\]

Interchanging the two factors gives likewise

\[
                         r\circ h_2^2=r.                    \tag{2.9}
\]

For either kind of target, define

\[
                         K_i=\{h\in H_i:r\circ h=r\}.
\]

Assume for contradiction that both `K_i` are proper, and choose
`h_i notin K_i`.  At least one of `H_1,H_2` has odd order by (1.1);
rename the factors so that it is `H_1`.  In the nonhyperelliptic case
the preceding conclusion already contradicts the choice of the pair.
In the hyperelliptic case, (2.8) says `h_1^2 in K_1`.  But squaring is
an automorphism of the cyclic group generated by the odd-order element
`h_1`, so `h_1` is a power of `h_1^2`.  Hence `h_1 in K_1`, again a
contradiction.  Therefore `K_1=H_1` or `K_2=H_2`.

If `K_i=H_i`, ordinary quotient descent gives a unique morphism
`bar r:W/H_i -> T` such that `r=bar r q_i`.  This part did not use
etaleness.  Finally suppose that `r` is finite etale.  The free action
makes `q_i` finite etale.  The extension of function fields attached
to `bar r` is an intermediate extension of the separable extension
attached to `r`, and ramification indices satisfy

\[
                  1=e(r,w)=e(q_i,w)e(\bar r,q_i(w)).
\]

Thus `bar r` is finite, separable, and unramified.  Between smooth
projective curves it is therefore finite etale.  This proves the
theorem.  \(\square\)

## Scope

The proof needs etaleness only to retain an etale map after descent;
the factorization statement itself holds for every nonconstant `r`.
It also does not require semisimplicity of a group algebra or any
restriction on the characteristic.  In applications, the substantive
input is precisely the mixed-Jacobian vanishing (1.2).

This criterion is complementary to
[the earlier coprime cyclic-atlas descent theorem](38_COPRIME_DESCENT_THROUGH_THE_CYCLIC_ATLAS.md):
the present proof uses a mixed second difference and the geometry of
degree-two divisor classes on the target, rather than a norm identity
on a cyclic atlas.
