# Equal-characteristic signed orbits and the wild defect

**Status: proved below; independent audit pending.**

Files 81, 83, and 84 assumed that the odd orbit prime was different
from the characteristic. This note removes that restriction when the
orbit prime *is* the characteristic. The full interpolation field and
the sign norm require only characteristic different from two. Wild
ramification changes the individual conductor terms, but, after the two
permutation covers are compared, it gives exactly the same common-defect
identity with each branch point weighted by its lower jump.

In particular, coprime residual degree still makes the sign field carry
all ramification, the spectral degree bounds are unchanged, and the
extra-sign Prym bound is unchanged. A Deuring--Shafarevich calculation
also records the additional \(p\)-rank constraint available only in equal
characteristic.

## 1. Setup and the interpolation square

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed of odd characteristic \(p\). Assume

\[
 g(X)=s+1,\qquad g(Y)=ps+1,\qquad
 k(Y)=k(t,z),\qquad z^2=f(t),\qquad s\geq1,
\]

and an equal-degree diamond of finite etale maps

\[
 V\xrightarrow{a}Y,\qquad V\xrightarrow{q}C\xrightarrow{c}X,
 \qquad \deg q=p,\qquad \deg a=\deg c=M.
\tag{86.1}
\]

Suppose \(q\) is a cyclic \(C_p\)-torsor with generator \(\beta\), and
\(a\beta\ne a\). Put

\[
 F=k(C),\quad K=k(V),\quad
 t_i=\beta^i(t),\quad z_i=\beta^i(z),\quad
 P(T)=\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(T-t_i).
\]

Let \(B\subset F\) be the field generated by the coefficients of \(P\).
Let \(Q(T)\) be the unique polynomial of degree less than \(p\) satisfying
\(Q(t_i)=z_i\), and define

\[
 D=B(\text{coefficients of }Q),\qquad E=B(t),\qquad
 E'=B(t,z),\qquad E_*=D(t)=D(t,z).
\]

Write

\[
 e=[F:B],\quad j=[D:B],\quad m=[F:D],\quad
 d=[E:k(t)],\quad N=\deg(E_*\to Y).
\tag{86.2}
\]

Finally, set

\[
 L=B(t_i,z_i:0\leq i<p),\qquad
 H=\operatorname{Gal}(L/B)\leq C_2\wr S_p,
\]

let \(H_0\) stabilize the selected signs \(\{(t_i,z_i)\}_i\), and put

\[
 w=\prod_i z_i,\qquad A=B(w),\qquad
 \epsilon:H\longrightarrow C_2,\qquad
 \nu=[A:B]\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{86.3}
\]

Thus \(D=L^{H_0}\), \(A=L^{\ker\epsilon}\subseteq D\), and
\(w^2=\operatorname{Norm}_{E/B}(f(t))\).

### Theorem 86.1 (the full orbit square in characteristic \(p\))

All fields in the following display are separable over their indicated
bases, and

\[
 \boxed{
 e=mj,\quad [E_*:D]=p,\quad [K:E_*]=m,\quad FE_*=K,\quad
 N=\frac M m=\frac{jd}{2},\quad 1\leq j\leq2^p.}
\tag{86.4}
\]

The curve \(V\) is the normalization of the integral fiber square

\[
                         C\times_D E_* .               \tag{86.5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 j=1\Longleftrightarrow z\in E,\qquad
 j>1\Longrightarrow 2\mid j,\qquad
 \frac j\nu\equiv1\pmod p,\qquad v_p(|H|)=1.
\tag{86.6}
\]

If \(J(Y)\) is absolutely simple, then

\[
 ps+1\leq g(D)\leq Ns+1,\qquad N\geq p,                \tag{86.7}
\]

and, on putting

\[
                         \eta=Ns+1-g(D),               \tag{86.8}
\]

one has

\[
 \deg\operatorname{Diff}(C/D)=2m\eta,\qquad
 \deg\operatorname{Diff}(E_*/D)=2p\eta.                \tag{86.9}
\]

If \(J(X)\) is also absolutely simple, then

\[
                 m\geq\operatorname{gon}(X)
                    \quad\hbox{or}\quad N\geq p+2.     \tag{86.10}
\]

#### Proof

If two \(t_i\)'s were equal, the prime-order group generated by
\(\beta\) would fix \(t\). Its odd order would then force it to fix one
of the two square roots \(z\), contrary to \(a\beta\ne a\). Thus \(P\)
is separable and irreducible of degree \(p\) over \(F\), even though
\(p=\operatorname{char}k\). Interpolation is therefore valid. Its
uniqueness makes the coefficients of \(Q\) \(\beta\)-invariant, and
\(Q(t)=z\) gives

\[
 [E_*:D]=[FE_*:F]=p,\qquad FE_*=K.
\]

This proves the field identities and the integral square. The scheme of
square roots of \(f(T)\) in the finite etale \(B\)-algebra \(B[T]/(P)\)
is finite etale of degree \(2^p\), because \(p\ne2\). The interpolation
point has residue field \(D\), proving \(j\leq2^p\), separability, and
the first two assertions of (86.6), exactly as in Theorem 81.1.

The coordinate cycle \(\beta\) has just two fixed vertices on
\(\{0,1\}^p\): zero and the all-one vertex. Its orbit on the selected
sign choices has one fixed vertex when \(\epsilon=0\), and two when
\(\epsilon\ne0\); in the second case the even and odd halves have equal
size. Hence \(j/\nu\equiv1\pmod p\). In particular \(p\nmid j\).
The faithful action of \(H_0\) on the selected \(p\) signed roots embeds
\(H_0\) in \(S_p\), and it contains \(\beta\). Thus
\(v_p(|H_0|)=1\), and \(v_p(|H|)=1\).

The proofs of (86.7)--(86.10) use no prime-to-characteristic assertion.
For completeness, the orbit norm

\[
 (E_*/D)_*(E_*/Y)^*:J(Y)\longrightarrow J(D)
\]

is nonzero: otherwise the moving degree-\(p\) divisors give a degree-\(p\)
pencil on \(Y\). Such a pencil is separable (a purely inseparable
degree-\(p\) pencil would be the \(p\)-th power of a degree-one pencil),
and Castelnuovo--Severi with the separable hyperelliptic degree-two map
would give \(g(Y)\leq p-1\), impossible. Simplicity gives the lower
genus bound, while Riemann--Hurwitz for the separable map \(E_*\to D\)
gives the upper one and (86.9). The orthogonal-images argument of
Theorem 81.2 then gives (86.10). \(\square\)

## 2. Wild inertia and coprime descent

### Theorem 86.2 (classification of the local groups)

Every inertia group \(I\) of \(L/B\) acts freely on the \(2p\) signed
roots. Its possibilities are

\[
 1,\qquad C_2,\qquad C_p,\qquad C_{2p},\qquad D_{2p},  \tag{86.11}
\]

where \(D_{2p}\) denotes the dihedral group of order \(2p\).

In the last three wild cases the wild subgroup is \(P_I=C_p\). There is
a unique lower jump \(b_I\geq1\), and

\[
 I_0=I,\qquad I_1=\cdots=I_{b_I}=P_I,\qquad
 I_{b_I+1}=1.                                          \tag{86.12}
\]

The inertia of \(L/A\) is either trivial or \(C_p\), and every
ramification index of \(C/A\) is one or \(p\). If \(p\nmid m\), then

\[
                        L/A,\qquad C/A,\qquad D/A       \tag{86.13}
\]

are finite etale.

#### Proof

The stabilizer in \(H\) of a signed root is the Galois group of
\(L/E'\). Since \(K/E'\) is intermediate in the etale cover \(V/Y\),
the map \(L/E'\) is etale. Inertia therefore has no fixed signed root,
so all of its orbits there have cardinality \(|I|\). Hence
\(|I|\mid2p\). The standard structure of inertia in characteristic
\(p\) now gives (86.11): its wild subgroup is a normal \(p\)-group and
its tame quotient is cyclic. The filtration (86.12) follows because
the wild subgroup has prime order.

Every fixed-point-free signed involution has odd total sign. Indeed its
coordinate involution has an odd number of fixed coordinates; those
coordinates must be sign-flipped, while transposed pairs contribute an
even number of flips. Therefore an even-order inertia group maps
surjectively under \(\epsilon\), whereas its \(C_p\) subgroup lies in
\(\ker\epsilon\). This proves the assertion about \(L/A\), and
etaleness of \(K/L\) and \(K/F\) transfers the same ramification indices
to \(C/A\).

Suppose \(p\nmid m\). If \(\beta\) fixed a point of \(L\), it would act
without fixed points on the etale fiber of \(V\to L\) above it. Every
orbit in that fiber would have length \(p\), forcing
\(p\mid[K:L]\mid m\), a contradiction. By (86.6), every subgroup of
order \(p\) in \(H\) is Sylow and hence conjugate to
\(\langle\beta\rangle\). No inertia group can therefore contain its
wild subgroup. Its remaining possible involution is killed on passage
to \(A=L^{\ker\epsilon}\). Thus \(L/A\) is etale. The other two maps
in (86.13) are intermediate in the etale extension \(K/A\). \(\square\)

## 3. The wild common-defect formula

Let \(\mathcal R_p\) and \(\mathcal R_{2p}\) be the branch values of
\(L/B\) whose inertia has order \(p\) and \(2p\), respectively. In the
second set both the cyclic and dihedral possibilities are allowed. Put

\[
 \mathfrak a=\sum_{x\in\mathcal R_p}(b_x+1),\qquad
 \mathfrak h=\sum_{x\in\mathcal R_{2p}}(b_x+1).        \tag{86.14}
\]

Also write \(u\) for the number of order-two branch values and
\(h_0=|\mathcal R_{2p}|\).

### Lemma 86.3 (the two local conductor budgets)

For a branch value with inertia \(I\), the local different contribution
to the degree-\(2p\) signed-root scheme and to the degree-\(j\)
selected-sign cover \(D/B\), respectively, is as follows:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 I & \text{signed roots} & \text{selected signs}\\ \hline
 C_2 & p & j/2\\[2mm]
 C_p\text{ of jump }b
   &2(p-1)(b+1)
   &\dfrac{(p-1)(j-\nu)(b+1)}p\\[3mm]
 |I|=2p\text{ of jump }b
   &2p-1+b(p-1)
   &\dfrac{(2p-1)j}{2p}-\dfrac{p-1}p
      +\dfrac{b(p-1)(j-2)}{2p}.
\end{array}                                             \tag{86.15}
\]

The last row can occur only when \(\nu=2\).

#### Proof

For a permutation set \(\Omega\), the conductor--discriminant formula
gives the local different contribution

\[
 \delta_I(\Omega)=\sum_{q\geq0}
   \frac{|I_q|}{|I|}\bigl(|\Omega|-|I_q\backslash\Omega|\bigr).
\tag{86.16}
\]

Inertia is free on the signed roots. Formula (86.12) immediately gives
the middle column.

An order-\(p\) subgroup is conjugate to \(\langle\beta\rangle\). It
fixes exactly \(\nu\) selected signs and has
\((j-\nu)/p\) other orbits. This gives the second row in the last
column. An inertia involution exchanges parity and fixes no selected
sign, giving the first row.

For inertia of order \(2p\), its \(C_p\) subgroup fixes zero and the
all-one sign, while every other orbit has length \(p\). Every element
above the nontrivial element of \(I/C_p\) exchanges parity. It therefore
interchanges the two fixed signs and pairs every other \(C_p\)-orbit with
one of the opposite parity. Thus

\[
 |I\backslash(H\cdot0)|=1+\frac{j-2}{2p},\qquad
 |C_p\backslash(H\cdot0)|=2+\frac{j-2}{p}.
\]

Substitution in (86.16), with weight one at \(q=0\) and weight \(1/2\)
at \(1\leq q\leq b\), proves the last entry. This argument treats
\(C_{2p}\) and \(D_{2p}\) identically. \(\square\)

### Theorem 86.4 (Swan-weighted common defect)

With \(\eta\) as in (86.8), one has

\[
 \boxed{
 \eta=\frac{p-1}{2p}(\nu\mathfrak a+\mathfrak h),\qquad
 g(A)=\frac{\nu ds}{2}+1-\eta,\qquad
 g(D)=\frac{jds}{2}+1-\eta.}                           \tag{86.17}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 g(D)-g(A)=\frac{(j-\nu)ds}{2},\qquad
 \eta\in\frac{p-1}{2}\mathbf Z_{\geq0}.}               \tag{86.18}
\]

The remaining different degrees are

\[
 \deg\operatorname{Diff}(D/A)
       =2\left(\frac j\nu-1\right)\eta,\qquad
 \deg\operatorname{Diff}(C/A)
       =\frac{2mj}{\nu}\eta.                            \tag{86.19}
\]

In particular,

\[
                         \eta>0\Longrightarrow p\mid m. \tag{86.20}
\]

#### Proof

The signed-root scheme is either the connected cover \(E'/B\) or the
disjoint union of the two degree-\(p\) sign components. In both cases
the sum of \(2g-2\) over its components is \(2dps\), because every
component is etale over \(Y\). Riemann--Hurwitz and the middle column of
(86.15) give

\[
 2dps=2p(2g(B)-2)+pu+2(p-1)\mathfrak a
                 +(p-1)\mathfrak h+ph_0.              \tag{86.21}
\]

Riemann--Hurwitz for \(D/B\), using the last column and
\(\sum_{\mathcal R_{2p}}b_x=\mathfrak h-h_0\), gives

\[
\begin{split}
 2g(D)-2={}&j(2g(B)-2)+\frac j2(u+h_0)\\
 &+\frac{(p-1)(j-\nu)}p\mathfrak a
  +\frac{(p-1)(j-2)}{2p}\mathfrak h.                  \tag{86.22}
\end{split}
\]

Multiply the signed-root equation by \(j/(2p)\) and subtract the
selected-sign equation. A \(C_2\) branch cancels. A \(C_p\) branch of
jump \(b\) leaves

\[
                    \frac{(p-1)\nu(b+1)}p,
\]

and an order-\(2p\) branch leaves

\[
                    \frac{(p-1)(b+1)}p.
\]

The left side is \(jds-(2g(D)-2)=2\eta\), proving the first formula of
(86.17).

If \(\nu=1\), there is no even inertia, \(A=B\), and the signed-root
equation gives \(g(A)=ds/2+1-\eta\). If \(\nu=2\), the quadratic cover
\(A/B\) is tame and branches exactly over the order-two and order-\(2p\)
values. Combining its Riemann--Hurwitz formula with the signed-root
equation gives \(g(A)=ds+1-\eta\). This proves (86.17) and hence the
genus difference.

The integer \(\eta\) is nonnegative by Riemann--Hurwitz for
\(E_*\to D\) (this is also the upper bound in (86.7)). Since
\(\gcd(p,(p-1)/2)=1\), the first formula forces
\(p\mid\nu\mathfrak a+\mathfrak h\), proving the quantization in
(86.18). Applying Riemann--Hurwitz to \(D/A\) and \(C/A\) now gives
(86.19). If \(p\nmid m\), Theorem 86.2 makes both maps etale, so
(86.19) forces \(\eta=0\). This is (86.20). \(\square\)

## 4. The unchanged spectral and Prym bounds

### Theorem 86.5

If the sign norm is a square in \(B\), equivalently \(\nu=1\), then
\(E'/E\) is etale and

\[
 g(E)=\frac{dps}{2}+1,\qquad
 g(B)\geq\frac{ds}{2}-\frac{(p-1)d}{p}+1.              \tag{86.23}
\]

If the coefficient system is nonpencil, then

\[
                              d\geq s+2.               \tag{86.24}
\]

If also \(p\nmid m\), then

\[
                 g(B)=\frac{ds}{2}+1,\qquad d\geq s+3. \tag{86.25}
\]

If \(J(Y)\) is absolutely simple and \(j>\nu\), then independently of
whether the sign norm is square,

\[
 \boxed{
                    \frac{(j-\nu)ds}{2}\geq ps+1.}     \tag{86.26}
\]

#### Proof

When \(\nu=1\), the parity lemma excludes all even inertia. Hence the
degree-at-most-two map \(E'\to E\) is etale. Its genus and the incidence
curve in \(B\times\mathbf P^1\) give (86.23), exactly as in Theorem
83.2. A birational plane projection and the plane genus bound give
(86.24). If \(p\nmid m\), Theorem 86.2 gives \(\eta=0\), yielding the
genus equality and the stronger plane bound (86.25).

For (86.26), the nonzero orbit norm \(J(Y)\to J(D)\) is killed by
pushforward to \(J(A)\). If \(\nu=1\), this follows by factoring through
the hyperelliptic norm \(J(Y)\to J(E)\). If \(\nu=2\) and \(j>2\), the
fields \(A\) and \(E'\) are distinct over \(B\); after their quadratic
base change the same hyperelliptic norm vanishes. These arguments use
only \(p\ne2\). Simplicity makes the image have dimension \(ps+1\),
while (86.18) gives the dimension of the Prym of \(D/A\). \(\square\)

## 5. The equal-characteristic \(p\)-rank constraint

Write \(\gamma(T)\) for the \(p\)-rank of a smooth curve \(T\).

### Proposition 86.6 (Deuring--Shafarevich refinement)

The original etale \(C_p\)-torsor \(V\to C\) satisfies

\[
 \gamma(V)-1=p(\gamma(C)-1),\qquad
 g(V)-\gamma(V)=p\bigl(g(C)-\gamma(C)\bigr).            \tag{86.27}
\]

In particular \(V\) is ordinary if and only if \(C\) is ordinary.

More generally, fix a Sylow subgroup \(P_0=C_p\leq H\), put

\[
 n_0=[N_H(P_0):P_0],\quad
 a_0=|\mathcal R_p|,\quad h_0=|\mathcal R_{2p}|,
\]

and let \(\bar L=L/P_0\). The number \(R_0\) of fixed points of \(P_0\)
on \(L\) is

\[
                         R_0=n_0\left(a_0+\frac{h_0}{2}\right). \tag{86.28}
\]

One has

\[
 \gamma(L)-1=p(\gamma(\bar L)-1)+(p-1)R_0             \tag{86.29}
\]

and

\[
\begin{split}
 g(L)-\gamma(L)={}&p\bigl(g(\bar L)-\gamma(\bar L)\bigr)\\
 &+\frac{p-1}{2}n_0\left(
   \sum_{x\in\mathcal R_p}(b_x-1)
   +\frac12\sum_{x\in\mathcal R_{2p}}(b_x-1)
                         \right).
\end{split}                                             \tag{86.30}
\]

Thus \(L\) is ordinary if and only if \(\bar L\) is ordinary and every
wild lower jump appearing in (86.30) equals one. In the coprime case
\(p\nmid m\), the action of \(P_0\) is free and

\[
                g(L)-\gamma(L)
                   =p\bigl(g(\bar L)-\gamma(\bar L)\bigr). \tag{86.31}
\]

#### Proof

Formula (86.27) is Deuring--Shafarevich for a free \(C_p\)-action,
together with etale Riemann--Hurwitz.

For (86.28), count pairs consisting of a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup and a
point it fixes. Above a branch value with inertia \(C_p\), there are
\(|H|/p\) such pairs; above one with inertia of order \(2p\), there are
\(|H|/(2p)\). Divide by the number
\(|H|/|N_H(P_0)|\) of Sylow subgroups. Deuring--Shafarevich then gives
(86.29).

Riemann--Hurwitz for \(L\to\bar L\) gives

\[
 g(L)-1=p(g(\bar L)-1)
       +\frac{p-1}{2}\sum_{y\in L^{P_0}}(b_y+1).
\]

The same incidence count, now weighted by \(b_y-1\), and subtraction of
(86.29), gives (86.30). Every term on its right is nonnegative. This
proves the ordinarity assertion. The final statement follows from the
fixed-point argument in Theorem 86.2. \(\square\)

## 6. Scope

The equal-characteristic extension does not create a new automatic
contradiction. Its main structural gain is that wild ramification is
confined to one explicit \(C_p\)-layer and measured by the integer
\(\eta\); its Swan conductors cannot vary independently in the full-orbit
and sign-carrier curves. The implications \(p\nmid m\Rightarrow\eta=0\),
\(d\geq s+2\), \(d\geq s+3\) in the coprime square-norm case, and the
Prym cost (86.26) are therefore valid without excluding the important
case in which the orbit prime equals the characteristic.
