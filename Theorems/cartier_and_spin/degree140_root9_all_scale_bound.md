# Uniform root-nine scale bounds, including content ratios

Version1,29 September2026. At every allowed geometric ratio of the
actual root-nine family, the full normalized polynomial-square scale
scheme has at most12 geometric points and length at most21. The
former primitivity hypothesis is no longer necessary for these bounds.

At a ratio with positive fixed content the sharper bounds are
\[
\#\{\text{geometric square scales}\}\le4,
\qquad\operatorname{length}(\text{square scale scheme})\le8.
\]
Nilpotents are retained: the proof only asserts that the square of
a quartic local coefficient lies in the square ideal, not that the
quartic itself must lie in that ideal.

Let k0/K be the coefficient field of an actual source ratio h,w,
with K=F_(5^8) and d=[k0:K]. At a positive-content ratio that admits
an actual étale degree-ten cover, its scale belongs to k0. If the
discriminant class of that cover is nontrivial in J(X)[2], then
\[
171\mid d.
\]
For the field of the cubic-invariant ratio coordinates alone, the
corresponding necessary divisibility is57. Without assuming actual
étaleness, an even-divisor square scale at a content ratio already
forces57|d unless its curve-square class is trivial.

These are uniform bounds and arithmetic restrictions, not an
emptiness assertion. Arbitrary coefficient fields and the curve-square
alternative remain, as does the unmarked common-cover problem.
