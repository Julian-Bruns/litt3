# An odd equivariant rational map excludes a fourth-order line obstruction

Version1,3 October2026. [Root whole-scope review PASS](../../Research/audits/RANK_THREE_SCALAR_TARGET_AND_TAME_IDENTITY_AUDIT_2026_10_03.md).

Let A be a finite group acting on a smooth projective connected curve D over an algebraically closed field. Let M be an A-invariant line class with scalar linearization obstruction α∈H²(A,k×). Let f:D→P¹ be an ACTUAL finite A-equivariant map of degree a, with any action of A on P¹; that action may have a kernel. Then
\[
\boxed{2a\alpha=0.}
\]
In particular, if a is ODD, the TWO-primary component of α cannot have order at leastFOUR. The assertion allows inseparable maps: it concerns the actual finite flat norm, not a chosen separable completion.

Consequently a curve carrying an invariant line whose obstruction pulls back to orderFOUR orEIGHT on its original acting group has no equivariant odd-degree rational pencil. This conclusion uses the ORIGINAL group and obstruction; replacing it by an effective target quotient does not remove the pullback obstruction.

This does not assert that every curve admits such a pencil, classify its finite group, or produce a second étale endpoint map.
