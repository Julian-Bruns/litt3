## 2. The exact infinity critical input

h_\infty³-U^{10}=c(q/\rho_\infty)\delta h_\infty.
\tag{2}

It has δh∞≠0 because h∞³=U¹⁰ is impossible in k(z).

The exact actual residue, expressed in regular local frames, gives

Differentiating(2) and using δ(U¹⁰)=0 yields

\frac{\delta q-\lambda\rho_\infty h_\infty²}{q}

At an ordinary point where Uρ∞ is a unit, (3) makes the left side regular. Thus a positive critical order of δh∞ is divisible by five: otherwise its logarithmic derivative in(4) has a nonzero simple pole. This is an actual residue condition, beyond the rational cubic equation.

## 5. Multiplicity and tangency leave only two cases

## 6. Both auxiliary cases contradict genus

If div(G)=5Z and ZF=1, the normalization is P¹, because its degree-one map to the smooth rational base is an isomorphism. By(11), u has degree3 there. The identity h³=P(u−1) gives the genus-nine cubic field of X as a subfield, of degree3 over k(u); equality of these degrees identifies it with the rational field of Z. This is impossible. In particular there is no inseparable map escape in this case.

Suppose instead div(G)=Z reduced with ZF=5. Tangency gives u=f⁵, with deg f=3. The function t is separating of degree5, and f is separating because its degree3 is prime to the characteristic. On the integral image Z the conic coordinates generate its field, and the conic is quadratic in v, so
\[
[k(\widetilde Z):k(t,u)]\le2.
\]
This index divides [k(Ẑ):k(t)]=5, hence equals one. Since f belongs to the same field, k(Ẑ)=k(t,u)=k(t,f). The generating separating maps of degrees5 and3 give the Castelnuovo–Severi bound
\[
g(\widetilde Z)\le(5-1)(3-1)=8.
\tag{13}
\]

The auxiliary cubic identity handles inseparability precisely. Let P^{1/5} be obtained by taking the fifth root of every coefficient, possible since k is perfect. Then
\[
h³=P(f⁵-1)=\bigl(P^{1/5}(f-1)\bigr)^5.
\]
If w=P^{1/5}(f−1), the Bézout identity h=(w²/h)⁵ makes h=h₀⁵ in this function field. Frobenius injectivity gives
\[
h₀³=P^{1/5}(f-1).
\tag{14}
\]
This squarefree degree-ten cubic is the inverse Frobenius twist of X and has genus9. Its trigonal coordinate f has degree3 on Ẑ, equal to its degree on that cubic, so its field inclusion has degree one. Hence g(Ẑ)=9, contradicting(13). This proves that the ENTIRE ρ cannot divide a.
