# Quadratic multiplication and incidence for a rank-two new-line orbit

Version1,23September2026. In the actual full-radical backup span,
suppose the canonical second lines lambda_i have saturated rank two.
Use the notation of positive_cartier_plane_orbits: their trace is
S of degree zero, M is their number of distinct embedded lines,
8|M, and gcd R_i=q^*C, with c=deg C equal to0 or1.

The second fundamental divisor W_S is reduced. For c=1 it consists
of two distinct points disjoint from C. For c=0 it has six distinct
points, of which five or six are used by the R_i; five used implies
M in{8,16,24,32,40}. If k_y counts incident distinct orbit lines
over a point of W_S, then
\[
\sum k_y=(13-8c)M/8,\qquad
\sum k_y(k_y-1)\le M(M-1)/2.
\]
The common fiber C must be removed in this count.

The actual squared-line trace
J2=image(q_*(lambda_0^2)->Sym^2 S) has rank three and colength at
most one. Thus at least three orbit directions occur in every
fiber except possibly one.

The augmented Frobenius algebra canonically gives
\[
m_S:\operatorname{Sym}^2(F_Y^*S)\longrightarrow
\mathscr A_Y=\ker(F_Y^*B_Y\to\omega_Y),\qquad
\operatorname{div}\det m_S=12C+3W_S.
\]
This map is generically invertible. For the actual evaluation-kernel
lines K_i of the positive planes,
\[
(F_T^*\lambda_i)^2\longrightarrow K_i
\quad\text{has zero divisor }3R_i.
\]
Consequently their rank-three saturated span follows from the
quadratic construction; it is not an independent contradiction to
rank two for the lambda orbit.

If S is isotropic, pairing the old radical with S gives a divisor
h_i^*Delta_X plus an effective divisor of degree5d. If S is
nonisotropic and c=1, C^(1) belongs to its pairing divisor D_S.
The degree9d HN line's intersection with q^*S-perp loses an effective
divisor supported on q^*D_S of degree between9d and16d.

All four pairing/base-divisor alternatives remain unexcluded.
These are actual-orbit necessary conditions, not realizations.
