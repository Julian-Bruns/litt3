## 1. Actual double cover and uniform geometric data

Let k be algebraically closed of ODD characteristic p. Let a:U→Y be
an actual connected finite etale double cover with Y ordinary of genus2.
Then g(U)=3. On scalar Frobenius twists put

    J=J(U^(1)), A=im(a^(1)*), P=(ker Nm_a^(1))^0,
    π:J→Q=J/A, ψ=π|P.

P is elliptic, the principal Jacobian polarization restricts to degree2
on P, and ψ has kernel P[2]. Indeed ker(a^*) is the order-two group
generated by the defining line ε. Pullback of the restricted polarization
on A is twice the genus-two principal one, so its polarization-map
degree is2^4/2²=4. Complementarity inside principally polarized J
gives the same degree on P, hence line degree2. Identifying Q=P^∨ by
the Jacobian polarization and P^∨=P by its elliptic principal class
identifies ψ with[2]. These are actual maps; the quotient isomorphism
is over the field of definition, not merely an isogeny-class assertion.

Write Θ_U for the Raynaud determinant divisor, NOT the classical
principal theta divisor. Since J/P is isogenous to ordinary J(Y), the
[ordinary-complement criterion](RESTRICTED_RAYNAUD_THETA_SUFFICIENT_CONDITIONS_AND_STABILITY_BOUNDARY.md),
Section2, gives P not contained in Θ_U. Thus

    D=Θ_U|P is effective,   deg D=2(p−1).                   (1)

Its compulsory contribution is:

- Ordinary P: the p−1 NONZERO geometric points of ker(V_P) lie in D.
  For such a line α, F_U^*α=O and α≠O, so the Frobenius exact
  sequence injects k into H⁰(B_U⊗α).
- Supersingular P: D has multiplicity exactly p−1 at0. The
  prime-to-p polarization decomposition makes the connected component
  of ker(V_J) equal to ker(V_P), of length p. On k[t]/(t^p), Tong's
  Dirac property restricts its equation to a nonzero socle t^(p−1).

These use Raynaud's class and symmetry and
[Tong, §1.2.3 and Theorem1.2.7.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.2046).
The zero fiber A is GOOD: the C2 character decomposition makes its
bad locus on J(Y^(1)) the union of Θ_Y and its ε-translate, both proper.

The bad set B={z∈Q:π^(-1)(z)⊂Θ_U} is closed: its complement is
π(J∖Θ_U), open because π is smooth. Since0∉B and Q is elliptic,
B is finite. Every bad fiber is an irreducible divisor component,
with multiplicity≥1, restricting to a reduced degree-four ψ-fiber
on P. Distinct bad fibers have disjoint restrictions, and all avoid0.

## 2. A characteristic-dependent bound

If r=|B|, the r restrictions first give4r≤2(p−1). If P is ordinary,
each restriction contains at most ONE point of ker(V_P), since its
differences lie in P[2] whereas differences of those points are
p-torsion. At least p−1−r compulsory points remain outside. Hence

    4r+(p−1−r)≤2(p−1),   r≤floor((p−1)/3).                (2)

The first inequality ensures r≤p−1, so that subtraction is legitimate.
For supersingular P, all restrictions miss the compulsory origin
multiplicity. Thus

    4r+(p−1)≤2(p−1),   r≤floor((p−1)/4).                  (3)

The inequalities count ACTUAL divisor components and mandatory
scheme-theoretic multiplicity, not finite-field sampling.

In characteristic3 both bounds are ZERO: every A-coset is good for
every such double of an ordinary genus-two base. By Section6 this
gives proper axis theta for all its abelian towers, including
characteristic-divisible degrees. This broader consequence is author-only.

## 3. Checked characteristic-five special cases

For p=5 the degree is8 and the compulsory contribution has degree4.
Two bad fibers would consume8; in the ordinary case their restrictions
hit at most two of the four mandatory nonzero five-points, and in
the supersingular case both miss the order-four origin zero. Either
leaves compulsory degree unaccounted for. Hence there is AT MOST ONE
bad fiber, without any torsion assumption on its class.
Symmetry forces a unique bad class z to satisfy z=−z; since0 is good,
z has exact order2. On P it is represented by an exact-order-four
point. These are only possible exceptions, not asserted actual bad fibers.

This also retains the earlier odd-character theorem for EVERY ordinary
genus-two Y, not just the open of Section5. An odd-order prime-to-five
class γ∈J cannot have π(γ) equal to a nonzero two-torsion bad class.
Thus every such translate is good. For any connected abelian etale
b:W→U of odd degree prime to5, its character decomposition has only
these γ, and Section6 proves proper axis theta. No anti-invariance
under the double involution, bound on character orders, or Galoisness
of W/Y is assumed. Five-group refinements and actual intermediates
are allowed as in Section6.

If a is defined over F_q and `P[2](F_q)=0`, there is NO exceptional fiber:
its unique class would be Frobenius-fixed in Q[2], and the actual
F_q-isomorphism P≅Q contradicts that hypothesis. In particular this
test requires the true two-torsion representation, not just numerical
isogeny data.

## 4. One exact good pair over F5

Put f=t(t−1), c=t³+t+1 and g=(t−2)c=t⁴+3t³+t²+4t+3. Set

    Y:y²=fg,   U:(u²=f, v²=g),   E:v²=g,
    a:U→Y, (t,u,v)↦(t,uv),   b:U→E, (t,u,v)↦(t,v).

The cubic c takes values1,3,1,1,4 on F5 and is irreducible;
f,g are squarefree with disjoint supports. Their square classes are
independent, so U is geometrically connected. The two inertia types
change just u or just v; infinity is unramified because both degrees
are even. The diagonal sign change avoids inertia, proving a etale.

The map b ramifies at the four points of E above t=0,1. Its Jacobian
pullback is injective: its kernel is killed by2 and a nontrivial kernel
line would give an etale double of E dominated by U. Equality of
degrees would identify it with the ramified b, a contradiction.
The diagonal involution induces−1 on J(E), so Nm_a b^* has image
in finite ker(a^*) and is zero by connectedness. Thus b^* identifies
J(E) with P over F5. The ramified auxiliary map identifies the Prym;
it is NOT substituted for an etale leg of a common cover.

With coefficient convention `H_ij=[t^(5i−j)](fg)²`,

    fg=t^6+2t^5+3t^4+3t^3+4t²+2t,
    H_Y=[[3,1],[3,4]], det=4,   [t^4]g²=1.

Hence Y and E are ordinary. Frobenius cycles three of E's four branch
points and fixes the fourth; it cycles the three pair partitions,
which represent nonzero E[2]. Thus `P[2](F5)=0`; direct counting also
gives #E(F5)=5. The original
[exact Sage certificate](EXPLICIT_ORDINARY_GENUS_TWO_DOUBLE_WITH_FROBENIUS_CYCLIC_PRYM_TWO_TORSION_CERTIFICATE.sage)
checks polynomials, Hasse–Witt entries, branch partitions and point count.
Its author computation and independent hand-check by
/root/gluing_cohomology_rigidity2026-09-05 are retained evidence.

This exhibits ONE good double, not fifteen good doubles of this Y.

## 5. A nonempty open with all fifteen doubles good in characteristic5

Over k=bar(F5), let M be the ordinary genus-two moduli stack and
R→M the degree15 finite ETale stack of nonzero two-torsion classes,
with scalar ambiguity rigidified. Double covers exist etale-locally;
auxiliary level structures can supply a scheme presentation.
No etaleness of a coarse-moduli map is asserted.

R is irreducible of dimension3: order the six distinct hyperelliptic
branch points, distinguish two, and quotient the irreducible configuration
space by PGL2 and the appropriate finite permutations. Restrict to
the nonempty ordinary open. Over k there is no remaining quadratic-twist
ambiguity in this geometric assertion.

Let F→R parametrize exact-order-four points α of the elliptic Prym;
it is finite etale. On its relative J(Y^(1)), the condition

    H⁰(U^(1),B_U⊗α⊗a^(1)*L)=0

is open by semicontinuity. Projection from the smooth Jacobian family
is open, so existence of a good L is open in F. The complementary
bad locus is closed and has closed image B_R in R. Section3 shows
that it detects EVERY bad coset, not just chosen torsion tests.
Section4 gives R∖B_R≠∅. Thus dim B_R≤2, and its image under finite
R→M is closed of dimension≤2. The complement V⊂M is a nonempty open.

For every Y∈V(k), ALL fifteen doubles have EVERY degree-zero translate
good. This is a finite-type open with actual k-points, not an infinite
intersection or a “very general” point outside bar(F5). It does not
identify the particular Y of Section4 as a point of V.

## 6. All abelian degrees and preservation of the second map

For a prime-to-p connected abelian Galois etale b:W→U, with finite
character subgroup Λ⊂J(U^(1)), etale base change gives

    H⁰(W^(1),B_W⊗(ab)^(1)*L)
       =⊕_(α∈Λ) H⁰(U^(1),B_U⊗α⊗a^(1)*L).                 (4)

If each translate is good, intersect the FINITELY many good opens.
This proves proper axis theta; the open may depend on b. A common
L for infinitely many covers is NOT asserted.

For abelian b of arbitrary degree, quotient its group by its Sylow
p-subgroup. The intermediate cover is prime-to-p abelian, and the
remaining Galois p-refinement preserves the vanishing locus by the
[general p-refinement theorem, Section6](SOCLE_CRITERION_FOR_RESTRICTED_RAYNAUD_THETA.md).
The same holds after further actual Galois p-refinements.

Pullback of sections then gives the result for every ACTUAL intermediate
Z/Y. For any second actual etale g:Z→X, its mixed theta locus is
proper by setting the X-line parameter to O. The second map need
not descend to U or to a quotient used for the proof.

In characteristic5 this covers, on V, a Y-leg Galois closure whose
group has a normal five-subgroup and abelian quotient subgroup of
index≤2, with no inversion requirement. The odd-index-two case of
Section3 holds for every ordinary genus-two base. In characteristic3
every ordinary genus-two base permits all these double-abelian towers.

None of this controls arbitrary finite groups or iterated nonabelian
depth, supplies the cofinal correspondence-tower step, or excludes
an actual common cover. The current fixed pair is unchanged; Litt3
remains UNSOLVED. The characteristic-five genus-nine packet exclusion
may already cover some of these groups, but the present conclusion is
theta properness for arbitrary actual second targets.
