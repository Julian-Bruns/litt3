# Transfer the source-root residue method across a marked cubic branch

30 September2026.
[Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/root9_actual_divided_traces.md).
The accepted root-nine source and its boundary exclusions are reused;
their verification programs are not replayed. The new ingredient is
the factor v in the divided critical trace. Besides clearing a local
correction, it is the natural factor from the scale coefficient of
the actual source polynomial.

## Actual necessity away from the marked point

Where v is a unit, divide F_ell by v and apply the
[source-root residue lemma](actual_split_critical_residue_integrality.md)
with cubic S/v, constant term t^3/v and multiplier t*x^i*y^j. Its
second derivative is -2*eta/v. The resulting critical residue is,
up to a fixed nonzero constant,
\[
t v x^i y^j\frac{(\delta_0\Lambda)^2}
 {\eta(\Lambda-\ell)}\,\omega_0,\qquad
\omega_0=dx/(3y^2).
\]
At every point of an actual finite etale source the source roots
split in the completed local ring. The accepted
[common-critical exclusion](degree140_root9_common_critical_exclusion.md)
ensures that the reduction of S' is not the zero polynomial at any
affine base point. Every relevant multiple cluster therefore has
size two or three: phi is a unit and the nonzero reduced S' has
degree at most two. In particular no cluster of size divisible by
five is being passed to the source-root lemma.
The lemma makes its summed critical residue zero. It covers arbitrary
critical-discriminant multiplicities without assuming that the divided
function is individually regular on each normalized critical branch.

Primitive endpoint charts have poles of Lambda, not finite nonzero
values. Actual positive-content cases were separately excluded by
the accepted content theorem. No actual scale is discarded here.

## The marked point

At Q9=([9],0), use y as uniformizer, so v has order three and
delta0 is a unit multiple of d/dy. The regular source coefficients
satisfy a in (y), b(0)!=0 and c(0)!=0. More precisely, the newly
exported jets give
\[
[y]a=\langle9004\rangle w(H-\langle356769\rangle),\qquad
b(0)=\langle113149\rangle w(H-\langle42135\rangle).
\]
The whole b(0)=0 line was already excluded by the marked-content
parity argument. Hence eta is a unit at both normalized critical
points above Q9. One critical W is integral, and the other has pole
order m>=1. On the latter, Lambda has order at least 3m-3 and is
therefore finite. If Lambda is finite at either point, delta0(Lambda)
is regular. The factor v makes the traced function zero there,
independently of the critical value. This proves actual necessity
at the marked fibre as well. It also covers the a-leading boundary;
the generic pole-one assumption is not used to discard that boundary.

## Global polynomiality and degree

At an ordinary critical collision, the quadratic trace dual is
generated by eta^-1. In an integral critical chart, Lambda and
delta0(Lambda) belong to the quadratic order. At a reciprocal critical
chart the same statement follows by dividing by the monic reciprocal
quadratic; its derivative still divides the partial derivative of
Lambda. Thus positive principal-part coefficients of the trace are
residues of an integral quadratic trace, and vanish. This is the
same paired-residue argument as in the
[constant-family divided trace](degree140_inverse_discriminant_quadratic_traces.md).
At Q9 finite values the traced function is regular, as just shown.
There are no other possible finite-scale poles. Therefore U is a
polynomial, not just a rational function agreeing with a polynomial
at tested values.

Both points over infinity have Lambda pole four, eta pole sixteen
and delta0(Lambda) pole twenty-one. The multiplier t*v*x^i*y^j has
pole p=12+3i+10j. Their contributions have degree at most
floor((5+p)/4). At the marked integral critical branch Lambda has
pole at most three; in the primitive pole-three case the multiplied
function has pole at most one, so this contributes at most a constant.
The other marked branch has finite Lambda. The ordinary phi and
fixed t-endpoint pole calculations give degree at most two, using
the quadratic trace when eta vanishes. This proves the seven bounds.

## The exact endpoint correction remains small

Put Lambda0=v*Lambda and a0=delta0(v)/v. Before proper-in-W polar
subtraction one has
\[
v\frac{(\delta_0\Lambda)^2}{\Lambda}
=\frac{(\delta_0\Lambda_0)^2}{\Lambda_0}
 -2a_0\delta_0\Lambda_0+a_0^2\Lambda_0.
\]
Both Lambda0 and delta0(Lambda0) are proper rational functions in W
with poles only at phi. The proper-part operation therefore commutes
with this identity. At a fixed t-endpoint v is a unit. The last two
terms have no correction: the difference between their two-critical
sum and the pole-branch term is regular on the other branch. The
first term has exactly the old endpoint correction. Consequently,
for multiplier t*v*x^i*y^j the constant correction is
\[
2\operatorname{Tr}_{K[x]/(t)}
 \left(x^i t' U_0\frac{G_2^{(2-j)}}{P}\right),\qquad
U_0=(Q-L_0^5)/t^3,\quad L_0=(18,20,20,15).
\]
All corrections in positive scale degree vanish.

At Q9 this assertion can be proved on the dense chart m=1. The
pole-critical branch has Lambda0 of order three, so
-2a0*delta0(Lambda0)+a0^2*Lambda0 is integral there. The other branch
has unit phi and regular Lambda0. The proper polar expression for
the first term is regular on both branches in the regular W chart.
Subtracting the actual scale-pole branch therefore leaves no residue.
For coefficient one the only new proper term is a scalar multiple of
v*a0*(delta0(phi)/phi); it is regular at Q9, as is the subtracted
v*(delta0(Lambda)/Lambda)^2. Later coefficients have no proper-phi
part and no residue. The identity extends from this dense chart
because the trace coefficients have a regular algebraic presentation
and the final formula inverts no a-leading coefficient.

Changing to the short primitive coordinate adds only a base-field
scalar times eta^-1 to the polynomial-part correction. Its quadratic
trace is zero. This permits the short infinity circuit without
asserting that its coefficient frame is integral at every cubic branch.
