# The remaining discriminant case and its complete boundary reductions

Version1, 28 September2026. Use the fixed root-nine cubic ratio atlas
and its complete square ideal. Write
\[
g=bu^2+2cu+3e,\quad \Delta_2=c^2-4be,\quad C=c^2-3be,
\quad J=\Delta_2u^2+3ceu+2e^2.
\]
The [ramification exclusion](degree140_root9_ramification_exclusion.md)
makes g a unit on the whole square scheme. On f(u)=0 one has
\[
\operatorname{Disc}_U(f)=g^2J/u^6.
\]
Thus a possible square over the cubic discriminant must lie on J=0:
the selected root is simple and the other two roots coincide.

This remaining curve is birational to the SAME genus-six curve
xi^2=C(q), but the source residual is different. On its square locus
all of b,e,Delta2,C are units. Specifically:

- e=0 is impossible already from J=0 and the exact identity gcd(e,c)=1.
- C=0 forces the selected root to be triple and is already excluded.
- The entire Delta2=0 divisor consists of14 geometric ratios; all
  are excluded for every scale by exact C71,C72 Bezout identities.
- The entire b=0 divisor consists of ten geometric ratios, both
  companions over each of five q-values; all are similarly excluded.

Consequently the remaining discriminant question has the exact model
\[
\xi^2=C(q),\qquad u=\frac{e(q)(c(q)+3\xi)}{\Delta_2(q)},
\]
with the original units and b e Delta2 C inverted. It retains both
ratio sheets and arbitrary geometric scales. This model and its
boundary calculation alone do not decide the curve. The subsequent
[full discriminant exclusion](degree140_root9_full_discriminant_exclusion.md)
now excludes it by a separate certificate for its actual residual.
The old ramification exclusion cannot be transported to it merely
because the two normalized curves are isomorphic.
