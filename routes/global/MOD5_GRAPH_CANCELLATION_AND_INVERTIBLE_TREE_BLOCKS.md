# Mod-5 graph cancellation and invertible tree blocks

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: new conditional proof and abstract graph
counterexample; not independently audited. No geometric counterexample
is claimed.

Let Gamma be an actual connected joint-minimal coreless bi-etale span,
and let G be a connected component of its generic bipartite graph. Put
P=Gamma* Gamma, with actual minimal-image cycle coefficients in
k=Fbar_5. Fix an X-vertex x.

## 1. Exact cancellation criterion

For X-vertices u,v, the coefficient kernel of P^n is the number of
length-2n walks from u to v, reduced modulo 5. Indeed, the geometric
generic fiber of the iterated etale path source is exactly this set of
walks, and the degree of its map to its joint-minimal endpoint image is
the cycle multiplicity. Distinct minimal images have disjoint generic
endpoint fibers. Consequently taking the row at x is injective on the
space of cycles generated by powers of P: its basis images correspond
to disjoint nonempty orbits of possible second endpoints. The graph
component contains those endpoints because each image comes from paths.

Thus P is algebraic if and only if the supports of these rows, for all
n>=0, lie in one finite ball about x. One direction follows from a
polynomial recurrence: every row then lies in the span of finitely many
initial rows. Conversely, a finite ball has finitely many vertices, so
its row space is finite dimensional, and row injectivity gives a
polynomial relation in the actual cycle algebra.

Equivalently, the precise obstruction is that reduction modulo 5 can
make the entire walk orbit of the root finitely supported, uniformly
in time. In particular, it suffices for transcendence that for
unbounded n there is an X-vertex at distance 2n for which the number
of geodesics from x is not divisible by 5. Requiring this for every n
gives the usual successive-leading-support proof directly. The
unbounded-n condition is sufficient, but is not asserted necessary:
longer walks may survive where shortest-walk counts vanish.

## 2. A condition strictly weaker than being a tree

Suppose G has a color-preserving map to an infinite locally finite
bipartite tree T. Assume every fiber has the same finite size h, every
edge maps to an edge, and the h-by-h zero-one adjacency matrix across
each tree edge is invertible over F_5. There are no other edges.
The choice of orderings within fibers is immaterial.

Choose a tree ray starting at the image of x. A walk of length 2n
whose final image is the vertex at position 2n on this ray must project
to that unique tree geodesic. Its endpoint multiplicity row is therefore
e_x M_1 M_2 ... M_(2n), where each matrix uses the appropriate direction
(and hence possibly a transpose). This row is nonzero by invertibility.
Some endpoint thus has a nonzero length-2n walk count. Its distance in
G is exactly 2n, because projection gives the lower bound. Section 1
proves transcendence for any actual span with such a graph.

This hypothesis allows cycles. For example take a d-regular tree,
d>=3, give each fiber three labels, and connect distinct labels across
each tree edge. Its block matrix is J_3-I_3, with determinant 2, a unit
modulo 5. The subgraph over each tree edge is a six-cycle. The graph is
connected: within a fiber, any two labels have a common neighbor in an
adjacent fiber. It is therefore a concrete non-tree example satisfying
the criterion. This is a combinatorial illustration, not an assertion
of actual curve realization.

## 3. No twins and infinite stabilizers do not suffice

Let T be the infinite d-regular bipartite tree, d>=3. Above each X-vertex
place the 31 points of the projective plane PG(2,5); above each Y-vertex
place its 31 lines. Across a tree edge, connect a point and a line
exactly when they are nonincident. Let M be the resulting 31-by-31
matrix. A point lies on six lines and hence is nonincident with 25.
Two distinct points together lie on 11 lines and hence have 20 common
nonincident lines. Therefore, over the integers,

    M M^t = 5 I_31 + 20 J_31.

In particular MM^t=0 over F_5. If B is the X-to-Y adjacency operator
of the new graph and B_T the analogous tree operator, then

    B = B_T tensor M,
    B B^t = (B_T B_T^t) tensor (M M^t) = 0  over F_5.

Equivalently every pair of X-vertices has a number of common neighbors
divisible by 5. Thus the abstract analogue of P is already zero.

The graph nevertheless has all of the following properties.

- It is simple, bipartite, infinite, and locally finite of degree 25d.
- It is connected. Any two vertices within one point fiber have common
  neighbors over an adjacent tree vertex, and the dual assertion holds
  within line fibers. Connectivity then follows along T.
- It has no distinct vertices with equal neighborhoods. Within one
  fiber, point and line incidence sets are distinct. Across different
  fibers of the same color, equality would imply equal neighbor sets
  of distinct vertices of the tree, which is impossible for d>=3.
- It is edge transitive under the color-preserving tree automorphisms
  and the simultaneous projective linear action on all fibers. The
  latter action is transitive on nonincident point-line pairs: choose
  a basis adapted to a point complementary to the plane representing
  the line.
- Under this faithful action, every finite vertex-set pointwise
  stabilizer is infinite. Tree automorphisms supported on branches
  outside a finite subtree fixing the projected finite set supply an
  infinite subgroup, acting identically on all finite labels.

These checks use elementary projective geometry: the plane has
(5^3-1)/(5-1)=31 points and lines; each line has six points; two points
lie on a unique line. No classification input is used.

Thus simplicity, no twins, edge transitivity, and the infinite
finite-set-stabilizer restriction from the generic-tree note do not
force mod-5 transcendence. If this particular graph were realizable as
the generic graph of an actual joint-minimal coreless bi-etale span,
the walk/multiplicity identification would give P=0 in its actual
cycle algebra. Such a realization is neither constructed nor claimed;
the graph may violate additional field-theoretic or geometric
constraints. The unconditional actual-span question remains open here.

## 4. Consequence for canonical realizations

For spans satisfying Section 2, or the noncancelling geodesic criterion
of Section 1, every nonzero polynomial q(P) remains a nonzero actual
k-cycle. The finite-support interpolation theorem in
PLURICANONICAL_REALIZATIONS_JOINTLY_DETECT_ACTUAL_BIETALE_CYCLES.md
therefore detects it in every sufficiently high pluricanonical weight.
Its bounded-degree detection corollary then implies unbounded minimal
polynomial degrees of T_m(P) as m tends to infinity. This witnesses
unboundedness of the generated closure, without asserting that powers
of P span every individual minimal image in that closure.
