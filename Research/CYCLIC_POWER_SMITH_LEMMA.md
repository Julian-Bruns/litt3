# A universal two-part Smith form for cyclic-five norm comparisons

2026-09-10. Proof independently audited PASS by
/root/audit_cyclic_power_smith (medium effort); see
[audit record](audits/CYCLIC_POWER_SMITH_AUDIT_2026_09_10.md).
This is an INTEGRAL
ALGEBRA theorem, not a higher inverse-Cartier or cover-descent theorem.

## Statement

Let k be perfect of characteristic5, a>=1, q=5^a, O=W_(a+1)(k),
and

    R=O[e]/((1+e)^q-1), N=sum_(i=0)^(q-1)(1+e)^i.

Let A in R reduce to e² modulo5. Multiplication by A on the free
rank-q O-module R has cokernel

    R/A R ≅ O ⊕ (O/(5^a))*e                              (1)

as an O-module. In these coordinates the class of N is (5^a,0).
Equivalently its Smith factors are q-2 units, 5^a and zero.
Also

    image(ann_R(A)→R/5R)=k*e^(q-1).                       (2)

For any scalar eta in O, solutions to

    A*y=N*eta                                            (3)

exist iff eta mod5=0. If that condition holds, their reductions
modulo5 are EXACTLY the line k*e^(q-1).

Thus if the first comparison is normalized as

    y mod5=c*e^(q-3)+d*e^(q-2)+b*e^(q-1), eta mod5=c,

equation(3) forces c=d=0 and places no restriction on b. The coefficients
of y here are the already coefficient-Frobenius-transported ones when
applying this algebra to a semilinear comparison. No extra Frobenius
has been inserted by the algebra.

## 1. A rank-two quotient without importing preparation theory

Choose a polynomial representative A=e²+5B(e) and put
S=O[[e]]/(A). The natural map O⊕O e→S is an isomorphism of O-modules.

For existence, split any series f=r0+e²g0, with deg r0<2. Modulo A,
replace the high part by -5B*g0. Repeat this division of its high
part. Each repetition gains a factor5, so after a+1 repetitions the
remaining high part vanishes. This gives a representative of degree<2.

For uniqueness, if r=A*g has degree<2, reduce modulo5. Since A=e²
there, both r and g reduce to zero. Divide their coefficients by5
and repeat over W_a, then W_(a-1), down to k. Both were zero.
In particular e² belongs to5S and

    e^j belongs to 5^floor(j/2)*S.                        (4)

The original ring R is also O[[e]]/((1+e)^q-1): its e is nilpotent
since e^q belongs to5R and5^(a+1)=0. Thus completing does not alter
the finite polynomial quotient, and R/A R=S/(F_q), F_q=(1+e)^q-1.

## 2. The group relation and the norm collapse to two terms

For 1<=j<=q,

    v5(binomial(q,j))=a-v5(j).

For j>=2 one has floor(j/2)>=v5(j)+1. Therefore (4) gives

    F_q=sum_(j=1)^q binomial(q,j)e^j =5^a e in S.         (5)

For j>=3 one similarly has floor((j-1)/2)>=v5(j)+1. Hence

    N=sum_(j=1)^q binomial(q,j)e^(j-1)
      =5^a*(1+(q-1)e/2) in S.                            (6)

All discarded terms have valuation at least a+1 and are actually zero,
not just zero in a further unspecified obstruction quotient.

The ideal generated by5^a e in S is its O-span: multiplying by e
gives5^a e²=0 by(4). Since S has basis1,e, quotienting by(5) leaves
O⊕(O/(5^a))*e. Equation(6) has class5^a. This proves(1).

## 3. Kernel reduction and the norm equation

The Smith factors follow from(1), since R is free of rank q over the
principal Artin ring O. Its kernel is isomorphic as O-module to
O⊕5O. Its image modulo5 inside R/5R consequently has dimension one:
in Smith bases only the free kernel generator is primitive, and
invertible O-basis changes preserve that fact.

This one-dimensional image is an R/5R-submodule. Since e is nilpotent,
it acts by zero on that line. The annihilator of e in k[e]/e^q is
exactly k e^(q-1). This proves(2).

In(1), N*eta is zero iff5^a*eta=0 in O, equivalently eta in5O.
Thus(3) has a solution iff that condition holds. When eta=5*eta1,
there is a particular solution reducing to zero: base-change the
established cokernel and norm-class description to O/(5^a), keeping
q=5^a unchanged. This shows N in image(A mod5^a). Choose a preimage z at
that precision and lift it; then y=5*eta1*z solves(3) modulo5^(a+1).
All other solutions differ from this one by ann(A), whose reduction
is exactly(2). This proves the full assertion about reductions.

## 4. Exact diagnostic and the geometric boundary

verify_cyclic_power_integral_carry.py independently eliminates the
first a-1 residual digits for perturbed A=e²+5B1+...+5^a Ba. It tests
all125 leading triples for q=5 and25, plus40 random perturbations for
q=125 and3 for q=625. Unknown higher coefficients, free kernel digits
and lower-reference digits are varied. The last divided augmentation
coordinates are always (-c,-2c-d), and the first a-1 stages have zero
cokernel. Total293 cases,0.42s. These finite tests are evidence for
the proof above, not its exhaustiveness argument.

The algebra explains why a cover of order5^a can require a extra
comparison digits. It also avoids computing all unknown integral
coefficients of its linearized operator.

For an ACTUAL higher Hodge problem, one must still derive(3), modulo
the relevant geometric corrections, from the full filtered/graded
construction, previous tuple and flat periodicity twist. Cochain
products are not automatically controlled by a group-algebra
augmentation ideal. In particular initial nonlinear terms for C25
are not removed by this Smith calculation. No new geometric D25
or arbitrary cyclic-tower descent theorem is asserted here.

There is a second distinction: an actual two-digit comparison may have
mixed coefficient-linear and Frobenius-semilinear corrections. An
additive deck-equivariant map is not automatically multiplication by
an element A after one fixed coefficient Frobenius. The Smith theorem
as stated must not be applied to that larger class without a separate
argument. That separate argument is now proved and independently
audited in [cyclic_power_additive_norm](../Theorems/Thm_cyclic_power_additive_norm.md).
It covers arbitrary additive deck-equivariant corrections and infinite
free coefficient rank. The scalar proof here remains a useful independent
special-case proof. Identifying the true geometric comparison, including
nonlinear repair terms, is still required.
