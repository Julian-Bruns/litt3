# Proof: finite atlases of a fixed quotient

[Statement](../../Theorems/atlases/fixed_quotient_atlas_avoidance.md).
Author /root,2026-09-09. No independent audit claimed.

The atlas D→[D/G] is a finite etale G-torsor of degree |G|, even at
fixed points of the action and when p divides |G|. Hence
deg omega_S=(2d-2)/|G|. For a representable finite etale map Y→S,
canonical pullback gives deg(Y/S)=(h-1)|G|/(d-1)=n. This proves the
integrality condition. It does not substitute ramified coarse maps
for etale stack maps.

The geometric fundamental-group sequence is

    1→pi_1(D)→pi_1(S)→G→1.

The group pi_1(D) has at most2d topological generators: lift the smooth
proper curve to characteristic zero and use the full surjective
[specialization map](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C0P).
Only this ONE curve is lifted; no simultaneous lift of a span is used.
This is the same input as in the audited
[bounded-atlas counting proof](bounded_atlas_partner_finiteness.md).

A finite group G has at most floor(log_2|G|) generators. Indeed, start
with the identity subgroup and adjoin an element outside the current
subgroup until it is all of G; every step at least doubles its order.
Lifts of these generators, together with generators of pi_1(D), generate
pi_1(S). No splitting of its extension is assumed. Thus pi_1(S) is
generated by at most a elements.

Connected degree-n covers correspond to transitive permutation actions
on n letters, up to conjugation. There are at most (n!)^a homomorphisms
to S_n, since their values on a fixed generating set determine them.
Counting every such homomorphism overcounts the connected covers and
therefore their possible underlying genus-h curves. Restricting to
scheme covers only decreases the count. This argument does not require
the pullback Y×_S D to be connected.

Since S has an F_q model, coefficient q-Frobenius permutes this finite
set of geometric curve classes. For a finite list of targets, their
union has size at most the sum N of the bounds. Every Frobenius orbit
in that union has size at most N.

Now let t have prime degree r over F_q. Its curve's moduli orbit has
length dividing r, hence length1 or r. Length1 would put all r distinct
conjugates of t in the same isomorphism-class fiber of the family. If
r>c this is impossible. Therefore the orbit has length r>N, and the
curve cannot belong to the finite atlas set. This proves the family
assertion without computing any covering, subgroup, or oper.

## The Hermitian specialization and the existing parameter bound

The Hermitian sextic is smooth and has genus10. Its projective unitary
group acts over F25 and has order

    5^3(5^3+1)(5^2-1)=378000.

These are the usual Hermitian-curve data; see
[Montanucci–Zini, Sections1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.03398), also
used in hermitian_monodromy_genus_sieve. In particular
2^18<=378000<2^19. The preceding count gives n=378000/9=42000
and a=20+18=38. Its canonical-degree calculation remains valid with
the wild unitary stabilizers.

For the genus-two family, the
[affine branch-family theorem](../genus_two/prime_field_branch_family.md) gives
full moduli orbit length for prime degree r>5. Thus r>N_H already
excludes all the Hermitian quotient atlases. Such a parameter lies
outside F5, so the curve is smooth and ordinary by its Hasse–Witt
calculation.

For any subgroup G'≤G the map [H/G']→[H/G] is representable finite
etale, as is seen after pullback to the atlas H, where it is the finite
constant coset cover. Thus an atlas to any of the smaller quotients
would contradict the exclusion for G. There is no assumption that
the frame torsor is connected or its determinant character trivial.

Finally the selected no-cored parameter uses

    B=336000, D_0=(B-1)!, G_0=1+8D_0, L_0=B^2,
    K=D_0*(D_0!)^18*3^(4G_0^2 L_0)*(42000!)^(2G_0+L_0).

All factors are positive integers, and 2G_0+L_0>38. Hence
K>(42000!)^38=N_H. The already imposed r>K therefore proves
the stronger endpoint atlas avoidance with no additional selection.

This is not a shortcut proving the common-connection-to-atlas step.
Both A18 emptiness on X and this avoidance on Y only exclude branches
where an ACTUAL Hermitian quotient atlas has first been obtained.
In particular, an atlas on an arbitrary further etale cover of Y need
not descend to Y. The theorem does not exclude such refinements, or
permit interchanging endpoints in an asymmetric atlas-construction
theorem. A18 remains a different assertion about the fixed X.
