# Odd-prime deck groups: the Hecke obstruction and a counterexample

**Status: proved below; independent audit pending.**

The degree-two bi-etale theorem does not extend to arbitrary odd prime
degree.  There are complex genus-six curves \(D\) with several distinct
free deck groups \(C_5\), all having isomorphic genus-two quotients with
absolutely simple Jacobian.  These are supplied by a very general member
of the Wiman--Edge pencil.

What does extend is a useful structural test.  Simplicity forces the
quotient by the group generated by the two deck groups to have genus zero,
and it forces the quotient by the normalizer of either deck group to have
genus zero.  The rational group algebra then gives a finite
Chevalley--Weil test: only one rational representation packet may contribute
to the quotient Jacobian, and its fixed-space rank over its Schur division
algebra must be one.

The note also applies the normalizer test uniformly to two first
\(C_3\)-generated groups, \(A_5\) and \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(7)\).
Both are impossible when the common quotient Jacobian is simple.

## 1. Genus-zero and normalizer obstructions

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed.  Let \(D\) be a smooth projective curve,
let \(\ell\) be a prime, and let

\[
                    H_1,H_2\leq\operatorname{Aut}(D)
\]

be distinct free subgroups of order \(\ell\).  Suppose

\[
                         X_i=D/H_i
\]

have genus \(g\geq2\), and put \(G=\langle H_1,H_2\rangle\).

### Theorem 85.1 (two necessary genus-zero quotients)

If \(J(X_1)\) is absolutely simple, then

\[
                             g(D/G)=0.                 \tag{85.1}
\]

More generally, if \(H=H_1\) and \(N=N_G(H)\), then

\[
                    N>H\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                    g(D/N)=0.                          \tag{85.2}
\]

The same conclusions hold with \(X_2\) in place of \(X_1\).

Moreover, if the action of \(G\) on \(D\) is tame, then, after identifying
\(X_1\) and \(X_2\) if they are isomorphic, their two pullback copies in
\(J(D)\) have finite intersection:

\[
     H^0(D,\Omega_D)^{H_1}\cap H^0(D,\Omega_D)^{H_2}
       =H^0(D,\Omega_D)^G=0.                           \tag{85.3}
\]

Thus transversality of the two copies by itself is not a contradiction.

#### Proof

Put \(Q=D/G\).  The map \(X_1\to Q\) has degree
\([G:H_1]>1\).  If \(g(Q)>0\), pullback

\[
                         J(Q)\longrightarrow J(X_1)
\]

has finite kernel, since norm after pullback is multiplication by the
degree.  Its image is a nonzero abelian subvariety.  Simplicity forces the
image to be all of \(J(X_1)\), and hence \(g(Q)=g(X_1)\).  Riemann--Hurwitz,
with the effective different in arbitrary characteristic, then gives

\[
 2g(X_1)-2\geq [G:H_1]\bigl(2g(Q)-2\bigr),
\]

which is impossible.  This proves (85.1).

For (85.2), use the same argument for the nontrivial quotient map

\[
                         D/H\longrightarrow D/N.
\]

Finally, pullback identifies differentials on a tame quotient with
invariant differentials.  Intersecting the two invariant spaces gives
invariance under the subgroup they generate.  For the tame quotient
\(D\to D/G\), these are precisely the pullbacks of differentials on
\(D/G\).  Equation (85.1) makes the last space zero, proving (85.3).
\(\square\)

The normalizer obstruction is often stronger than a full character
calculation: a single positive-genus curve \(D/N_G(H)\) already disproves
simplicity.

## 2. The rational Hecke criterion

Work in this section over \(\mathbf C\).  Let \(D\to\mathbf P^1\) be a
\(G\)-Galois cover, let \(H\leq G\), and put \(X=D/H\).  Write

\[
                    e_H={1\over |H|}\sum_{h\in H}h
                    \in\mathbf Q[G].
\]

Up to isogeny,

\[
                         e_HJ(D)\sim J(X).              \tag{85.4}
\]

For a rational irreducible \(G\)-module \(W\), let \(e_W\) be its rational
central primitive idempotent and put

\[
                 \Delta_W=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf QG}(W),
\]

a division algebra.  Regard \(W^H\) as a right \(\Delta_W\)-module.

### Theorem 85.2 (rational-packet simplicity criterion)

If \(J(X)\) is absolutely simple, then:

1. among the rational irreducible packets \(W\) occurring in
   \(H^1(D,\mathbf Q)\), exactly one can satisfy \(W^H\ne0\);
2. for that packet,
   \[
                      \dim_{\Delta_W}W^H=1.             \tag{85.5}
   \]

Equivalently, the image of the rational Hecke algebra

\[
                 e_H\mathbf Q[G]e_H
                    \longrightarrow\operatorname{End}^0(J(X))
\tag{85.6}
\]

may contain no nontrivial idempotent.

If the branch inertia groups of \(D\to\mathbf P^1\) are
\(I_1,\ldots,I_b\), equivariant Euler characteristic gives

\[
 [H^1(D,\mathbf Q)]
   =2[\mathbf1]+(b-2)[\mathbf QG]
       -\sum_{i=1}^b[\mathbf Q(G/I_i)].                 \tag{85.7}
\]

Consequently, for a nontrivial complex irreducible representation
\(\rho\), its multiplicity in \(H^1(D,\mathbf C)\) is

\[
 \mu_\rho=(b-2)\dim\rho-\sum_{i=1}^b\dim\rho^{I_i}.
\tag{85.8}
\]

Thus (85.5) is a finite calculation from the rational character table and
the inertia classes.

#### Proof

The central idempotents \(e_W\) commute with \(e_H\).  Hence every nonzero

\[
                         e_We_HJ(D)
\]

is an abelian subvariety of \(e_HJ(D)\).  It is nonzero precisely when
\(W\) occurs in \(H^1(D,\mathbf Q)\) and \(W^H\ne0\).  Two contributing
rational packets therefore give two complementary nonzero abelian
subvarieties, contradicting simplicity.

The simple component of \(\mathbf Q[G]\) belonging to \(W\) is

\[
                    \operatorname{End}_{\Delta_W}(W).
\]

Its \(e_H\)-corner is

\[
                    \operatorname{End}_{\Delta_W}(W^H).
\]

If the dimension in (85.5) were at least two, this matrix algebra would
contain a nonzero idempotent different from its identity.  Its image in
\(\operatorname{End}^0(e_HJ(D))\) would split off a proper abelian
subvariety.  This proves the criterion.

Formula (85.7) is the equivariant Euler-characteristic formula for the
punctured base, with one induced permutation module restored at each
branch point.  Taking the inner product with a nontrivial character and
using Frobenius reciprocity gives (85.8). \(\square\)

The same argument works with an auxiliary \(\mathbf Q_q\)-adic
representation in tame positive characteristic.  Formula (85.7) is used
below only in characteristic zero.

## 3. Two uniform \(C_3\) exclusions

The next proposition illustrates that the normalizer quotient can settle
whole infinite sets of signatures without inspecting them one at a time.
Continue to work over \(\mathbf C\).

### Proposition 85.3

Suppose \(D/G\simeq\mathbf P^1\), a subgroup \(H=C_3\) acts freely on
\(D\), and \(X=D/H\) has genus at least two.

1. If \(G=A_5\), then \(J(X)\) is not simple.
2. If \(G=\operatorname{PSL}_2(7)\), then \(J(X)\) is not simple.

#### Proof for \(A_5\)

All subgroups of order three in \(A_5\) are conjugate.  Freeness of \(H\)
therefore excludes inertia of order three.  Let \(a,b\) be the numbers of
branch points with inertia of orders two and five.  Riemann--Hurwitz gives

\[
                         g(X)=-19+5a+8b.                \tag{85.9}
\]

The normalizer \(N_G(H)\) is \(S_3\).  Its quotient by \(H\) is an
involution on \(X\).  Over each order-two branch point this involution has
two fixed points, and over an order-five point it has none.  Indeed, a
fixed involution of \(A_5\) has centralizer of order four, so it fixes two
points in the corresponding \(G/C_2\)-fiber; the three reflections in
\(S_3\) form one \(H\)-orbit.

The involution therefore has \(2a\) fixed points.  Riemann--Hurwitz for
\(X\to D/N_G(H)\) yields

\[
 g(D/N_G(H))
   ={g(X)+1-a\over2}
   =-9+2a+4b.                                          \tag{85.10}
\]

This integer is odd.  Since it is the genus of a curve, it is nonnegative
and hence at least one.  Theorem 85.1 now contradicts simplicity.

For the smallest signature \((2,5,5)\), one has

\[
        g(D)=4,\qquad g(X)=2,\qquad g(D/N_G(H))=1.
\]

Formula (85.8) says that the canonical representation is the irreducible
four-dimensional representation of \(A_5\); its \(C_3\)-fixed space has
dimension two, agreeing with the splitting criterion (85.5).

#### Proof for \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(7)\)

The possible inertia orders are two, three, four, and seven, and freeness
again excludes order three.  Let \(a,b,c\) count the remaining orders
two, four, and seven.  Then

\[
                         g(X)=-55+14a+21b+24c.          \tag{85.11}
\]

Here also \(N_G(H)\simeq S_3\).  An involution has centralizer of order
eight.  The induced involution on \(X\) has four fixed points per
order-two branch point and two per order-four branch point; the latter
count uses the unique involution in a cyclic group of order four.  It has
none over order-seven branch points.  Hence

\[
 g(D/N_G(H))
   ={g(X)+1-2a-b\over2}
   =-27+6a+10b+12c.                                    \tag{85.12}
\]

This is again a nonnegative odd integer, so it is positive.  Theorem 85.1
applies.

For the smallest signature \((2,4,7)\), the genera are

\[
        g(D)=10,\qquad g(X)=4,\qquad g(D/N_G(H))=1.
\]

The canonical representation is
\(\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf7\), or its Galois-conjugate version, and the
\(C_3\)-fixed dimensions are one and three.  The two rational packets
display the resulting elliptic and three-dimensional factors. \(\square\)

## 4. A counterexample in degree five

### Theorem 85.4 (odd-prime deck normality is false)

Over \(\mathbf C\), there exist smooth curves \(D,X\), with
\(g(D)=6\), \(g(X)=2\), such that:

1. \(J(X)\) is absolutely simple;
2. \(D\) has distinct free subgroups \(H_1,H_2\simeq C_5\);
3. \(D/H_1\simeq D/H_2\simeq X\);
4. \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) generate \(A_5\), and neither is normal.

#### Proof

Consider the Wiman--Edge pencil of genus-six curves with faithful
\(A_5\)-action.  Every smooth member has quotient orbifold

\[
                         (0;2,2,2,3).
\tag{85.13}
\]

Existence can also be seen directly from Riemann's existence theorem.  In
\(A_5\), the tuple

\[
 (1\,3)(2\,5),\quad
 (1\,2)(3\,4),\quad
 (1\,4)(2\,5),\quad
 (3\,4\,5)
\tag{85.14}
\]

has product one, has orders \(2,2,2,3\), and generates \(A_5\).  Moving
the four branch points gives the one-dimensional Hurwitz family.

Let \(H\) be a Sylow-five subgroup.  Since the only point stabilizers in
(85.13) have orders two and three, \(H\) acts freely.  Riemann--Hurwitz
gives

\[
             g(D)=6,\qquad g(D/H)=1+{6-1\over5}=2.     \tag{85.15}
\]

Distinct Sylow-five subgroups are conjugate, so their quotient curves are
isomorphic.  Any two distinct ones generate \(A_5\): a subgroup containing
two of them has at least six Sylow-five subgroups and hence at least
\(1+6(5-1)=25\) elements.  Its order divides \(60\); order \(30\) would
give an index-two normal subgroup of the simple group \(A_5\).  Thus its
order is \(60\).

It remains to choose a member for which the genus-two quotient has simple
Jacobian.  This follows from the arithmetic monodromy theorem for the
Wiman--Edge pencil.  We give the deduction because it is what rules out a
hidden product of elliptic curves.

Let

\[
                           K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5).
\]

The rational \(A_5\)-module \(E_{\mathbf Q}\) occurring in the first
homology of a smooth Wiman--Edge curve has dimension six over
\(\mathbf Q\), dimension three over \(K\), and

\[
       \operatorname{End}_{\mathbf QA_5}(E_{\mathbf Q})=K.
\]

The canonical representation is
\[
        H^0(D,\Omega_D)\simeq\mathbf3\oplus\mathbf3',
\tag{85.16}
\]
the two Galois-conjugate icosahedral representations.  Equivalently,
\[
       H^1(D,\mathbf Q)
          \simeq E_{\mathbf Q}\otimes_K\mathbb V,
\tag{85.17}
\]
where \(\mathbb V\) is a rank-two \(K\)-local system.  A subgroup
\(H=C_5\) has
\[
                         \dim_K E_{\mathbf Q}^H=1,
\]
so
\[
          H^1(D/H,\mathbf Q)=H^1(D,\mathbf Q)^H
             \simeq\mathbb V.                          \tag{85.18}
\]

Farb and Looijenga prove that the monodromy on \(\mathbb V\) is a
finite-index subgroup of
\(\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathcal O)\), for an order
\(\mathcal O\subset K\).  In particular its rational Zariski closure is
\[
                   \operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbf Q}
                         \operatorname{SL}_2.
\]
See [Arithmeticity of the monodromy of the Wiman--Edge
pencil](https://doi.org/10.5802/aif.3423), Theorem 1.1 and Sections
2 and 3.3.  The universal \(A_5\)-family and the orbifold type (85.13)
are established in [Geometry of the Wiman
pencil](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.09256).

The standard four-dimensional rational representation of this restriction
of scalars is irreducible.  Indeed, over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}\) it is the
sum of the two standard representations of the two
\(\operatorname{SL}_2\)-factors.  They are exchanged by
\(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf Q)\), so neither summand descends to a
proper rational subspace.

If the geometric generic Jacobian of \(D/H\) had a proper abelian
subvariety, then after a finite base change it would produce a proper
sub-local system of (85.18), invariant under a finite-index subgroup of
monodromy.  The preceding irreducibility forbids this.  Equivalently, the
centralizer of geometric monodromy is \(K\); the Hecke action supplies the
reverse inclusion, so
\[
                  \operatorname{End}^0 J(D/H)=K
\]
for a very general member.  A field contains no nontrivial idempotent,
and hence this abelian surface is absolutely simple.

Choose such a member and two distinct Sylow-five subgroups.  This proves
all four assertions. \(\square\)

For completeness, \(N_{A_5}(C_5)=D_{10}\).  The involution of
\(N/C_5\) has six fixed points on the genus-two quotient and is its
hyperelliptic involution, so
\[
                            g(D/N)=0.
\]
Thus this family attains, rather than violates, the normalizer condition
(85.2).

## 5. Consequence for the present strategy

Theorem 85.4 is a characteristic-zero counterexample to any unrestricted
odd-prime analogue of the bidegree-two theorem.  It does not by itself
construct such a counterexample over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_5\), and no
such claim is made here.  For the characteristic-five problem, the robust
surviving tools are Theorems 85.1 and 85.2: compute the normalizer quotient
first, then apply the rational-packet test only when the normalizer has
genus zero.
