# Branch rigidity controls all abelian prime-power covers

**Status: proved; independently audited PASS, 2026-09-04.**

[Audit record](audits/92_BRANCH_RIGID_ABELIAN_PRIME_POWER_COVERS_AUDIT.md).

This is the arbitrary-rank, arbitrary-exponent extension of the cyclic
prime-cover result in file 87. Its external input is exactly the
Glauberman--Thompson normal-complement theorem. In particular, we do not
assume that a nonabelian Sylow subgroup is abelian, or that a
self-normalizing Sylow subgroup alone has a normal complement.

All curves are smooth, projective, connected over an algebraically closed
field \(k\), and \(g(X)\ge2\). Fix an odd prime \(p\). The prime \(p\)
is allowed to equal \(\operatorname{char}k\).

Assume

\[
 J(X)\text{ is simple},\qquad \operatorname{Aut}(X)=C_p,
 \qquad X/C_p\simeq\mathbf P^1,                                    \tag{92.1}
\]

and that the reduced branch set \(\mathcal B\) of \(X\to X/C_p\)
has trivial stabilizer in \(\operatorname{PGL}_2(k)\).

## 1. The exact external theorem

For a finite \(p\)-group \(P\), let \(J_o(P)\) be the subgroup
generated by its abelian subgroups of largest **order**. For odd \(p\),
the Glauberman--Thompson theorem states that a finite group \(A\), with
Sylow subgroup \(P\), has a normal \(p\)-complement if

\[
                          N_A(Z(J_o(P)))
\]

has a normal \(p\)-complement.

The primary source is G. Glauberman, *A characteristic subgroup of a
\(p\)-stable group*, Canadian J. Math. 20 (1968), 1101--1135,
[Theorem D, p. 1105](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1968-107-2).
The maximal-order definition is on p. 1104; its proof is in section 8,
p. 1128. Unlike the adjacent fusion and characteristic-subgroup
theorems, Theorem D requires neither \(p\)-stability nor exclusion of
\(Qd(p)\). These definitions, the statement, and that proof have been
checked in the original paper.

## 2. The full automorphism-group theorem

### Theorem 92.1

Under (92.1) and branch rigidity, let \(D\to X\) be a connected finite
etale Galois cover with finite abelian \(p\)-group deck group \(H\).
Then

\[
 H\triangleleft\operatorname{Aut}(D),\qquad
 \operatorname{Aut}(D)/H\hookrightarrow C_p.                       \tag{92.2}
\]

Thus \(\operatorname{Aut}(D)\) is a \(p\)-group of order \(|H|\)
or \(p|H|\), and has no involutions.

#### Proof

The trivial-cover case is immediate from \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)=C_p\).
Assume \(H\ne1\), put \(A=\operatorname{Aut}(D)\), and choose a
Sylow \(p\)-subgroup \(P\) containing \(H\).

**Step 1: the Sylow subgroup is at most one layer larger.** If \(P=H\),
the normalizer injection gives

\[
                         N_A(P)/P\hookrightarrow C_p.
\]

Since this quotient has order prime to \(p\), it is trivial:
\(N_A(P)=P\).

Suppose \(P>H\), and put \(Q=N_P(H)\). The strict-normalizer property
gives \(Q>H\), while

\[
                         Q/H\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X)=C_p
\]

gives \([Q:H]=p\). Therefore \(D/Q=X/C_p\simeq\mathbf P^1\), with
reduced branch set exactly \(\mathcal B\), since \(D\to X\) is etale.
If \(Q<P\), the nontrivial group \(N_P(Q)/Q\) acts faithfully on
\(D/Q\) and preserves \(\mathcal B\), contrary to branch rigidity.
Hence \(P=Q\). The same argument for \(N_A(P)/P\) gives

\[
              H\triangleleft P,\quad [P:H]=p,
              \quad N_A(P)=P,\quad N_A(H)=P.                       \tag{92.3}
\]

The last equality follows because \(P\subseteq N_A(H)\) and
\(N_A(H)/H\hookrightarrow C_p\).

**Step 2: the relevant characteristic normalizer is a \(p\)-group.**
If \(P\) is abelian, \(Z(J_o(P))=P\), so this follows from
\(N_A(P)=P\).

Assume \(P\) is nonabelian. Its abelian subgroup \(H\) has index
\(p\), so the largest possible order of an abelian subgroup is
\(|H|\). If \(H\) is the unique abelian subgroup of this order, then

\[
                      J_o(P)=H=Z(J_o(P)),
\]

and (92.3) gives \(N_A(Z(J_o(P)))=P\).

Otherwise choose another abelian subgroup \(H'\) of order \(|H|\).
Both have index \(p\), are normal in \(P\), and generate \(P\).
Their intersection centralizes both, hence is contained in \(Z(P)\).
Conversely \(Z(P)\subseteq H\cap H'\), since adjoining a central
element outside either abelian maximal subgroup would make \(P\)
abelian. Thus

\[
           Z:=Z(P)=H\cap H',\qquad [P:Z]=p^2,
           \qquad J_o(P)=P.                                       \tag{92.4}
\]

As \(Z\subseteq H\), it acts freely on \(D\), and

\[
                            D/Z\longrightarrow X
\]

is a connected finite etale cyclic cover of degree \([H:Z]=p\).
Theorem 87.5 and Corollary 87.6 apply to this cover and show that
\(\operatorname{Aut}(D/Z)\) is a \(p\)-group. The normalizer injection

\[
                      N_A(Z)/Z\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(D/Z)
\]

therefore makes \(N_A(Z)\) a \(p\)-group. In view of (92.4), this is
exactly \(N_A(Z(J_o(P)))\).

**Step 3: normal complement and geometric normality.** In every case,
\(N_A(Z(J_o(P)))\) is a \(p\)-group, so has trivial normal
\(p\)-complement. The theorem in section 1 supplies

\[
                    A=R\rtimes P,\qquad R\triangleleft A,
                    \qquad p\nmid|R|.                             \tag{92.5}
\]

In all cases \(H\triangleleft P\). Let \(G\) be the normal closure
of \(H\) in \(A\), and put \(R_0=G\cap R\). Projection to
\(A/R=P\) sends the normal closure of \(H\) to \(H\), precisely
because \(H\triangleleft P\). Hence

\[
                              G/R_0\simeq H.                       \tag{92.6}
\]

If \(G>H\), the nontrivial separable quotient \(D/H=X\to D/G\)
and simplicity of \(J(X)\) imply \(D/G\simeq\mathbf P^1\).
Equation (92.6) says that \(H\) is a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup of \(G\).
Every such Sylow subgroup is conjugate to the free deck group \(H\),
so no point stabilizer for \(G\) has order divisible by \(p\).
Its image in \(G/R_0=H\) is therefore trivial. The map
\(D/R_0\to D/G\) would be a nontrivial connected finite etale
cover of \(\mathbf P^1\), impossible.

It follows that \(G=H\), so \(H\triangleleft A\). The normalizer
injection now gives \(A/H\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X)=C_p\).
This proves (92.2), and since \(p\) is odd, also the involution
assertion. \(\square\)

## 3. Removing the second abelian-deck assumption

The next argument is separate from the normal-complement theorem. It
compresses a putative second Galois presentation to a cyclic one.

### Lemma 92.2 (compression through an abelian normal subgroup)

Let a finite group \(A\) act on a curve \(D\). Suppose
\(H\triangleleft A\) is abelian, \([A:H]=p\), and \(B\le A\)
is not contained in \(H\). Put \(N=H\cap B\). Then

\[
 A=HB,\qquad N\triangleleft A,\qquad B/N=C_p.                     \tag{92.7}
\]

If \(H\) and \(B\) act freely, so do the induced groups \(H/N\)
and \(B/N\) on \(D/N\).

#### Proof

The image of \(B\) in \(A/H=C_p\) is nontrivial, hence surjective.
This proves \(A=HB\) and \(B/N=C_p\). The group \(B\) normalizes
\(N\) because \(H\triangleleft A\), and \(H\) normalizes \(N\)
because \(H\) is abelian. Thus their product \(A\) normalizes \(N\).
The freeness assertions follow because an intermediate quotient of a
finite etale Galois cover is again finite etale. \(\square\)

### Theorem 92.3 (all abelian prime-power Galois common covers excluded)

Assume (92.1), branch rigidity, and \(\operatorname{char}k\ne2\).
Let \(Y\) be any hyperelliptic curve of genus at least two. There is
no connected common finite etale cover

\[
                          D\longrightarrow X,
                    \qquad D\longrightarrow Y                    \tag{92.8}
\]

such that both maps are Galois and the deck group over \(X\) is an
abelian \(p\)-group. No condition on the second deck group is assumed.

#### Proof

Let \(H\) and \(B\) be the two deck groups. Theorem 92.1 makes
\(H\triangleleft A=\operatorname{Aut}(D)\), with \([A:H]\le p\),
and makes \(A\) a group of odd order.

If \(B\subseteq H\), then \(B\) is abelian. The hyperelliptic
involution of \(Y\) lifts through the abelian cover \(D\to Y\) to
a nontrivial involution fixing a chosen point over a Weierstrass point,
as in Theorem 87.8. This contradicts the odd order of \(A\).

If \(B\not\subseteq H\), then \([A:H]=p\). Apply Lemma 92.2 and
put \(D_0=D/(H\cap B)\). The two quotient maps are finite etale
Galois with groups

\[
 D_0\longrightarrow X:\ H/(H\cap B),
 \qquad D_0\longrightarrow Y:\ B/(H\cap B)=C_p.                   \tag{92.9}
\]

The first is still an abelian \(p\)-group cover, so Theorem 92.1
makes \(\operatorname{Aut}(D_0)\) a \(p\)-group of odd order.
The second is now abelian, so the hyperelliptic involution lifts to
\(D_0\), giving the same contradiction. \(\square\)

### Corollary 92.4 (the current genus-nine curve)

For the genus-nine \(X\) of file 76, and for every hyperelliptic \(Y\)
of genus at least two over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_5\), no common
cover is Galois over both curves with abelian three-power deck group
over \(X\).

#### Proof

File 87 verifies \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)=C_3\), simplicity, and the
required branch-set rigidity. Apply Theorem 92.3 with \(p=3\).
\(\square\)

## Scope and next boundary

This removes restrictions on the exponent and the number of generators
of the first deck group, as well as any abelianness assumption on the
second deck group. The conclusion is valid for arbitrarily large
three-power degrees for the fixed genus-nine and genus-25 pair.

Both maps are still required to be Galois, and the first deck group must
be abelian. Neither condition is known for an arbitrary common cover.
The theorem therefore does not settle Litt Problem 3 or the surviving
generic coefficient case.
