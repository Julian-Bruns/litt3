## 1. The quadratic block field is unique and σ-invariant

Let L/F be the SINGLE normal closure of E/F, M its group, and P0 its sheet stabilizer. Quadratic intermediate fields of E/F correspond to nonzero characters χ:M→C2 trivial on P0. We show that only the block character qualifies.

In the diagonal case M=C2×S10 and P0={1}×S9. Since S9 contains odd permutations, χ vanishes on the S10 factor and factors through the two-block quotient.

In the equal-sign case the index-two block kernel M0 contains A10×A10 with quotient C2, its common sign. A10 is perfect. The sheet stabilizer contains a pair of odd permutations with first coordinate in S9 and second coordinate in S10, so χ is also trivial on that common-sign quotient. Hence χ vanishes on M0.

In the full-product case M0=S10×S10 and the sheet stabilizer contains S9×S10. It therefore kills both sign factors, so again χ vanishes on M0. These arguments do not assume that the equal-sign or full-product block extension splits.

Thus the unique quadratic subfield is R. Since σ preserves F setwise, it transports quadratic subfields and consequently preserves R. Its restriction σR is nontrivial because it sends t to−t.

## 2. An actual genus-at-most-five hyperelliptic Q inside C0

Put Q=R^σR. The R/F deck involution δ commutes with σR: conjugation by σR preserves the unique nonidentity automorphism of the quadratic extension R/F. Therefore R/k(z) is a V4 extension, with intermediate fields F and Q; in particular [Q:k(z)]=2.

The R/F map is unramified over t=0,∞, because t has simple zeros and poles after pullback to B′. It has two points above each. A σR-fixed point must lie above t=0 or∞, and at either value σR fixes both points or exchanges them. Its fixed-point count rσ is consequently0,2 or4. Tame Hurwitz gives

## 3. Two elementary norm obstructions on X

We first record two consequences of the fixed inputs.

**(a) No finite effective F of degree5 has 2F∼10O.** Indeed L=O_X(F−5O) is two-torsion and has a nonzero section after twisting by5O. **(b) No finite effective E2 of degree2 has 5E2∼10O.** A function with divisor5E2−10O has exact pole order10. Write it, up to scalar, as y+g(x), deg(g)≤3, using L(10O). The y coefficient must be nonzero, since a polynomial in x of degree≤3 has pole order≤9. A finite branch point x=a of X has local orders ord(y)=1 and ord(x−a)=3. for the monic degree-two polynomial H whose roots are the x-coordinates of E2 with multiplicity. This is the forbidden mixed norm with H=J. Repeated points are included in that established exclusion.

## 4. Norms exclude every uniform fiber of C0→Q

Let h1:C0→X be the first actual étale map and π:C0→Q. Consider the homomorphism
\[
\Psi=\operatorname{Nm}_{h_1}\circ\pi^*:J(Q)\longrightarrow J(X).
\]
It is zero by §2. Therefore the norm of π*A has the same line-bundle class for every point A∈Q.

Choose a point A over z=0. If Q/z ramifies there, π*A=D1 and its norm is10O. If Q/z is unramified, π*A=2D_A, where D_A consists of five points of the original h1-infinity fiber; its norm is again10O. Consequently for EVERY B∈Q,
\[
\operatorname{Nm}_{h_1}\mathcal O_{C_0}(\pi^*B)\simeq\mathcal O_X(10O).
\]

Suppose π has a uniform fiber π*B=eD_B, with e=2,5 or10 and D_B reduced of degree10/e, and suppose B does not lie over z=0. The pushforward E_B=(h1)_*D_B is effective, finite, of degree10/e, and
\[
eE_B\sim10O.
\]
For even e, (e/2)E_B has degree5 and violates §3(a). For e=5 it violates §3(b). Thus no such uniform fiber exists away from z=0.

Applying the same argument with h2 and the point z=∞ shows that no such uniform fiber exists away from z=∞ either. Since0 and∞ are distinct, there is NO uniform e=2,5 or10 fiber anywhere on Q.

In particular Q/z cannot be unramified at either0 or∞: §2 would then give forbidden uniform e=2 fibers. Therefore Q/z ramifies at both, σR has no fixed points and R→Q is étale. We now have

## 5. Exact local dichotomy for the whole canonical base change

The ordinary image of P0 in Γ/G implies that φ:T→Γ has at most ONE folded point above every Γ-point. To see this with actual orbits, q*P0 is a free G-orbit of N reduced points because q is étale G-Galois. The Γ-fiber above this ordinary quotient value is another free G-orbit of N points. Their equivariant map is a bijection. Outside this Γ-orbit there are no folds.

Fix r∈R and a point a∈Γ above it, and write K=k((r)), A_a for its completed field. For each b∈B′ above r, the completed algebra E_b⊗K A_a is étale over E_b, because it is part of the WHOLE normalization T→B′, which is étale. Residues are k, so every local factor equals E_b. Consequently every K-embedding of A_a lies in E_b, and A_a embeds in EVERY E_b. The relative degrees of all these factors over A_a are one or two by the retained folds of φ.

If a fold occurs in E_b⊗K A_a, then [E_b:A_a]=2 and the tensor has [A_a:K] copies of this quadratic extension, hence that many folded points above a. The preceding one-fold bound forces [A_a:K]=1. The degree-ten φ-fiber then consists of one folded point and eight simple points, so B′/R has profile(2,1^8). One of its simple completions equals K; since EVERY other Γ-completion embeds there, all Γ-completions over r are K. This is alternative(A).

If there is no fold at all above r, choose embeddings of the completions in one separable closure of K. Every E_b and every A_a are then the SAME embedded field: all conjugate embeddings of A_a lie in E_b, and their relative degrees are one. Moreover its tensor with itself is split over this common field; thus this common finite separable K-extension is Galois. All fibers are uniform of one common index e. Since the B′/R degree is ten, e divides10. If ramified, e=2,5 or10. This is alternative(B).

Because R→Q is now étale, a ramified uniform B′/R fiber in alternative(B) descends to the SAME uniform C0/Q fiber at its Q-image. It is excluded by §4. Hence Γ→R has no ramification at all: alternative(A) is already unramified on Γ, and alternative(B) has e=1. Riemann–Hurwitz now gives
\[

so g(R)=9 and g(Q)=5.

This dichotomy handles every wild conductor: the e=5 and10 fibers were excluded through their actual uniform effective divisors, with no assumption about lower breaks or semiregularity in the other residual sector.
