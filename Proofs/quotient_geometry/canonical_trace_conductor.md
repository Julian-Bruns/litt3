# Proof: residues and the conductor lattice

This proves Version3 of the [statement](../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/canonical_trace_conductor.md).
The starting point is the exact evaluation annihilator in
[canonical trace detection](../../Theorems/quotient_geometry/canonical_trace_detection.md).
Here it is extended across every special fiber, using the actual
equation of the joint image. Both given maps remain finite étale.

## 1. The joint equation and its conormal derivative

Joint minimality makes Z the normalization of its integral image
\(\Gamma\subset X\times Y\). The surface is smooth, so \(\Gamma\)
is Cartier. Restrict its divisor line to each \(\{x\}\times Y\).
The class differences are the actual cross norm
\(g_*f^*([x-x_0])\), which is zero. The seesaw principle therefore
gives \(\mathcal O(\Gamma)=M\boxtimes Q\). Restriction to the
two fibers gives \(\deg M=m\), \(\deg Q=n\).

Let F be the section of this line defining \(\Gamma\). Its
derivative in the Y-direction, restricted to \(\Gamma\) and
pulled to Z, is an intrinsic section
\[
s_Y\in H^0(Z,f^*M\otimes g^*(Q\omega_Y)).
\tag{7}
\]
Terms differentiating a local frame multiply F and vanish on
\(\Gamma\), so this definition does not depend on that frame.
We claim that \(\operatorname{div}(s_Y)=\Delta\).

For an explicit proof, complete at \((x_0,y_0)\) on the image.
Since f is étale, its normalization branches have a common
parameter x and are distinct graphs \(y=\phi_i(x)\).
Locally F is a unit times \(\prod_i(y-\phi_i(x))\). On branch i,
\[
F_y=\text{unit}\prod_{j\ne i}(\phi_i-\phi_j).
\tag{8}
\]
The conductor into this branch is generated by the same product.
Indeed, a tuple supported only on branch i belongs to the plane
curve ring exactly when its value on that branch is divisible
by this product: a polynomial vanishing on all other branches
is divisible by \(\prod_{j\ne i}(y-\phi_j)\), by successive
monic division. This also follows from finite duality for a
Gorenstein curve; the local argument establishes the precise
multiplicities needed here. Thus (8) proves the claim.

Since g and f are étale, \(g^*\omega_Y=\omega_Z=f^*\omega_X\).
For \(T=\omega_Y^{1-r}Q\) and \(J=M\omega_X^r\), (7) is a
section of \(f^*J\otimes g^*T\). Consequently
\[
\mathcal O_Z(\Delta)\simeq f^*J\otimes g^*T.
\tag{9}
\]
This is a divisor identity on the given normalization, not only
an equality of degrees. The conductor interpretation agrees
with the earlier [joint-image calculation](../../routes/global/100_JOINT_IMAGE_CONDUCTOR_CRITERION_FOR_BIETALE_COVERS.md);
the proof above supplies the needed input directly.

## 2. Residues identify the annihilator, with its integral lattice

At the generic point of X, the f-fiber gives n distinct points
on Y. For \(q\in H^0(Y,\omega_Y^r)\) and
\(\sigma\in H^0(Y,T)\), the expression \(q\sigma/F\), after
trivializing M at that generic point, is a rational one-form
on Y with possible simple poles exactly at those n points.
Its residues sum to zero. In local coordinates its residue at
the ith point is \(q(y_i)\sigma(y_i)/F_y(y_i)\).

Put \(h_i=dy/dx\) on the corresponding branch. In the actual
canonical evaluation, q has coefficient \(q(y_i)h_i^r\)
relative to \((dx)^r\). Therefore the tuple
\[
\left(\frac{\sigma(y_i)}{F_y(y_i)h_i^r}\right)_i
\tag{10}
\]
is in its trace annihilator. Intrinsically (10) is division
of \(g^*\sigma\) by \(s_Y\); it is a rational section of
\(f^*J^{-1}\). This confirms the power and sign of J in (1).

These vectors form the full generic annihilator. The map from
H is injective: a section of T vanishing at all n generic points
would have at least n zeros, whereas
\(\deg T=n-(r-1)(2g(Y)-2)<n\). Its dimension is \(h^0(T)\),
which equals the evaluation defect by the divisor sequence and
Serre duality. Hence there are no additional annihilator vectors.

It remains to determine which generic combinations are regular.
This step cannot be replaced by taking dimensions on the generic
fiber. Write \(p_X:\Gamma\to X\). The evaluation map
\[
H\otimes\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow
(p_X)_*(\operatorname{pr}_Y^*T|_\Gamma)
\tag{11}
\]
is fiberwise injective. Indeed the fiber scheme of \(\Gamma\)
is the divisor of F(x,-), of degree n, and \(\deg T<n\).
Both sides of (11) are vector bundles: \(p_X\) is finite flat
because \(\Gamma\) is a Cartier curve without vertical components.
Thus its cokernel is locally free.

A numerator with meromorphic base coefficients whose pullback
vanishes along \(\Delta\) lies in the conductor and therefore
descends to a regular section on \(\Gamma\). Since (11) is a
subbundle inclusion, its base coefficients were regular already.
This argument uses the entire fiber scheme. Evaluation on the
normalization's reduced points alone can lose rank at collisions.

Division by s_Y is regular exactly when the pulled-back numerator
vanishes along \(\Delta\), by (7)--(9). The preceding paragraph
shows that allowing meromorphic coefficient combinations introduces
no further regular vectors. Therefore the saturated annihilator
inside \(\mathcal A\) is precisely
\[
J\otimes\ker\left(H\otimes\mathcal O_X
 \longrightarrow f_*(g^*T|_\Delta)\right).
\tag{12}
\]
All identifications commute with the actual trace pairing on the
finite étale algebra. In particular they do not divide by n.

## 3. The one-dimensional residue space

Now suppose H has dimension one and choose its generator \(\sigma\),
with divisor E. At x, a rational multiple \(a(x)g^*\sigma/s_Y\)
is regular on every normalization branch exactly when
\[
\operatorname{ord}_x(a)\ge
\max_{f(z)=x}(c_z-e_z).
\tag{13}
\]
The extremal value is attained, so after imposing it at least one
component of this vector is a unit. This proves saturation and
\(\mathcal K_r=J(-D)\), with D in (2). Its pullback map into
\(\mathcal O_Z\) has zero divisor
\(R=f^*D+g^*E-\Delta\); its coefficients are nonnegative and
at least one is zero in each fiber. Also D is effective. If its
coefficient at x were negative, group the f-fiber points by their
common image y on Y. A group of size a has conductor exponent
at least a-1 on each branch by (8). Negativity would force E to
have multiplicity at least a at that y. Summing over the groups
would give \(\deg E\ge n\), contradicting \(\deg T<n\).

Taking degrees in (9) gives
\(\deg\Delta=n\deg J+m\deg E\). Thus
\[
\deg R=n(\deg D-\deg J)=-n\deg\mathcal K_r.
\tag{14}
\]
This proves the nonpositivity of the kernel degree directly.
It is also consistent with the fact that every subbundle of
\(f_*\mathcal O_Z\) has nonpositive slope after passage to its
finite étale trivializing cover.

## 4. Exact differentials impose the small residual degree

Apply the preceding discussion to the first Frobenius twists.
Étale base change gives
\(B_Z=f^{(1)*}B_X\). Since the kernel of the coefficient-O map
is \(\mathcal K_r\), the kernel with coefficient bundle B_X
is \(B_X\otimes\mathcal K_r\). Thus the nonzero annihilated
section comes from
\[
0\ne t\in H^0(X^{(1)},B_X\otimes\mathcal K_r).
\tag{15}
\]
The canonical injection \(B_X\hookrightarrow F_{X*}\omega_X\)
and Frobenius adjunction send it to a nonzero section
\(\alpha\in H^0(X,\omega_X\otimes F_X^*\mathcal K_r)\).
Hence \(2G-2+p\deg\mathcal K_r\ge0\). Together with (14)
this proves (4).

The SAME degree bound holds for $B_{a,X}$ at every finite height.
Indeed, if $\deg K<-(2G-2)/p$, the one-step adjunction above gives
$H^0(B_{1,X}\otimes K)=0$. Every successive pullback of K has
degree $p^i\deg K$, so the same one-step vanishing holds at each
stage. The one-step Frobenius maps on $H^1$ of these negative
lines are therefore injective. Their composite is injective too.
Since both K and its $p^a$-pullback have no sections, the a-step
Frobenius sequence identifies
\[
H^0(B_{a,X}\otimes K)
=\ker\bigl(H^1(K)\longrightarrow H^1(F^{a*}K)\bigr)=0.
\]
Thus any nonzero section of $B_{a,X}\otimes K$, also after a
degree-zero twist, requires
$\deg K\ge-\lfloor(2G-2)/p\rfloor$. All scalar twists in these
successive relative Frobenius maps are retained.

If the coefficients belong to a stable degree-zero summand
\(\mathcal E\subset\mathcal A\) of rank greater than one,
the nonzero generic coefficient line is the generic fiber of
\(\mathcal K_r\). The map \(\mathcal K_r\to\mathcal A/\mathcal E\)
vanishes generically, hence everywhere. Thus \(\mathcal K_r\)
is a saturated line subbundle of \(\mathcal E\), and stability gives
\(\deg\mathcal K_r<0\).

Let \(E_X=\operatorname{div}(\alpha)\) and put
\(\deg\mathcal K_r=-j\). The inclusion
\(f^{(1)*}\mathcal K_r\to\mathcal O_{Z^{(1)}}\) is the section
with divisor R just constructed. Its Frobenius pullback multiplies
the pulled-back \(\alpha\) to give the ordinary differential
represented by \(\eta\). Taking divisors proves (5), with
\(\deg E_X=2G-2-pj\).

For G=9 and p=5, strict negativity gives j=1,2 or3.
For genus-two Y at \(r=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\), the preceding
trace theorem says that h0(T) can be one only for T trivial
when n is even, or T=O_Y(y) when n is odd. Substitution into
(2) gives the stated two conductor conditions. No primitivity
or corelessness assertion is deduced from those conditions.

## 5. Generic twists remove the last residual line

For any degree-zero line bundle N on \(X^{(1)}\), Raynaud's
full-Jacobian theta theorem gives
\(H^0(B_X\otimes N\otimes L)=0\) for generic L. This is also
the case A=J(X), quotient zero, of the proved
[restricted corank bound](../../Theorems/jacobians/theta_divisors/restricted_raynaud_complement_rank.md).
If K is any line bundle of degree at most zero, choose an effective
divisor A of degree \(-\deg K\). The inclusion
\(K\hookrightarrow K(A)\), where K(A) has degree zero, proves
the same generic vanishing for \(B_X\otimes K\otimes L\).

If h0(T)=0 the trace map is already a sheaf injection. If h0(T)=1,
its entire coefficient-B_X kernel is \(B_X\otimes\mathcal K_r\),
and (14) gives the needed nonpositive degree. The preceding
generic vanishing proves (6). At finite height e, étale base
change still gives \(B_{e,Z}=f^{(e)*}B_{e,X}\), and the cited
full-Jacobian result holds for \(B_{e,X}\). The identical
inclusion argument proves the all-finite-height statement.

This improves the canonical detecting degree for generic twists.
It does not eliminate the generic inherited Raynaud sections:
their nonzero traces have a positive canonical twist on X.
No argument here identifies them with the cohomology obstruction
whose vanishing is the current unmarked bottleneck.

## 6. Varying the multiplier and the optimal generic degree

For a degree-zero N on Y put \(\mathcal L_N=\omega_Y^rN^{-1}\).
The evaluation map now has target
\(\omega_X^r\otimes f_*g^*N^{-1}\). Its trace dual is (7)
of the statement: the trace pairing between the pushforwards of
\(g^*N\) and \(g^*N^{-1}\) is perfect because f is étale.
Since r is at least two, \(H^1(\mathcal L_N)=0\). The divisor
sequence on the geometric generic fiber therefore identifies
its evaluation defect with
\[
h^1(\mathcal L_NQ^{-1})
 =h^0(\omega_Y\mathcal L_N^{-1}Q)
 =h^0(\omega_Y^{1-r}QN).
\tag{16}
\]
This proves the exact kernel formula. A general line bundle of
degree d on a genus-h curve has \(h^0=\max\{0,d+1-h\}\):
for d below h the effective-divisor locus is proper by its
dimension, and for d at least h use the same statement on the
Serre-dual line and Riemann--Roch. Here
\(d=n-(r-1)(2h-2)\), giving (8).

The multiplier space has dimension \((2r-1)(h-1)\). If this is
less than n, no evaluation at n distinct points can be surjective.
If it is at least n, (16) vanishes for generic N. This proves
the exact optimal cutoff. For genus two its residual degree is
zero when n is even, and one when n is odd. The corresponding
effective loci are a single line class and the Abel image of Y,
respectively. The generic-X kernel is zero precisely off those
loci; a map between vector bundles with zero generic kernel is
a sheaf injection. Tensoring by any coefficient bundle preserves
it, proving the assertion for every first parameter.

Finally tensor the divisor sequence of \(\Gamma\) in \(X\times Y\)
by \(\operatorname{pr}_Y^*\mathcal L_N\) and push to X.
For generic N at the stated degrees, (16) gives
\(H^1(\mathcal L_NQ^{-1})=0\); also
\(H^1(\mathcal L_N)=0\). The resulting exact sequence is (9).
Riemann--Roch gives the first dimension
\(a=(2r-1)(h-1)-n\). The finite normalization has exact sequence
\(0\to\mathcal O_\Gamma\to\nu_*\mathcal O_Z\to\mathcal T\to0\),
whose last sheaf has length \(p_a(\Gamma)-g(Z)\). Tensor by the
invertible line \(\mathcal L_N|_\Gamma\) and push to X to obtain
(10), using \(g^*\omega_Y=f^*\omega_X\). The maps and the
torsion quotient are the actual normalization data. They have
not been replaced by abstract bundles with the same ranks.

## 7. The apparent Abel-curve exception is finite for Raynaud sections

Fix any finite height a and work on the a-fold Frobenius twists.
The argument of Sections2--3
also describes the line kernel when the source coefficient is
\(g^*N\) and the multiplier is twisted by \(N^{-1}\). In that
case the residue numerator belongs to
\(H^0(Y,\omega_Y^{1-r}QN)\), and division by the same s_Y gives
a rational section of \(f^*J^{-1}\otimes g^*N\). If the numerator
has one-dimensional section space and zero divisor E, the identical
pole calculation gives
\[
\mathcal K_r(N)=J(-D_E),\qquad
(D_E)_x=\max_{f(z)=x}(c_z-\operatorname{mult}_z g^*E).
\tag{17}
\]
The inclusion \(f^*\mathcal K_r(N)\to g^*N\) has effective zero
divisor \(f^*D_E+g^*E-\Delta\). Since N has degree zero,
\(\deg\mathcal K_r(N)\le0\) still follows. A nonzero section
in its coefficient-B_(a,X) kernel, even after any degree-zero first
twist L, requires
\[
\deg\mathcal K_r(N)\ge-b,
\qquad b=\lfloor2s/p\rfloor,
\tag{18}
\]
by the uniform negative-line vanishing in Section4. This bound is
uniform in L AND a; it is a degree bound, not a generic-vanishing
assertion. Relative coefficient Frobenius twists preserve the conductor
multiplicities and every numerical degree in the formulas below.

Adjunction for the Cartier curve \(\Gamma\), or the divisor
identity (9), gives
\[
\deg\Delta=2mn+2(g(Z)-1)=2sn(n+1),
\qquad m=ns.
\tag{19}
\]
The sum of the maxima over the fibers is at least the average,
so \(\deg D_0\ge\deg\Delta/n=2s(n+1)\). Thus the integer
\(\kappa\) in (11) is nonnegative.

For even n the only parameter with a nonzero generic-X trace
kernel has E=0. At \(r=n/2+1\),
\(\deg J=m+2sr=2s(n+1)\). Formula (17) makes its kernel degree
\(-\kappa\). Equation (18) proves the even-degree assertion.

For odd n, every possible bad parameter is uniquely of the form
\(N_y=\omega_Y^{r-1}Q^{-1}(y)\), and E=y. At a fixed x,
subtracting the indicator of g(z)=y from the conductor coefficients
reduces their maximum by one exactly when all its maximizers have
that Y-image. Otherwise the maximum is unchanged. Thus
\[
D_y=D_0-\sum_{x\in S_y}x,
\qquad \deg\mathcal K_r(N_y)=-s-\kappa+|S_y|,
\tag{20}
\]
since now \(\deg J=s(2n+1)\).

Each x lies in at most one S_y. Further, membership implies a
positive maximal conductor: if its maximum were zero and all n
branches had the same Y-image, they would meet there and have
conductor at least n-1, a contradiction. Hence these sets lie
in the finite support of D_0, and only finitely many are nonempty.
In particular
\(\sum_y|S_y|\le|\operatorname{Supp}D_0|\le\deg D_0\).
For odd p, \(s-b>0\). Combining (18) and (20) gives (13), and
dividing the last finite-support bound by \(s+\kappa-b\) gives
the first inequality of (14). The ratio
\((2s(n+1)+\kappa)/(s+\kappa-b)\) decreases with nonnegative
\(\kappa\), giving the second inequality. This proves finiteness
uniformly for every first parameter L.

The result uses the individual étale projections at two precise
points: their conormal divisor is the conductor, and the correction
from E=y has multiplicity one on every point above y. Dropping either
étale condition would change (17) and the degree calculation.
