# Finite-flat Lagrangian interpolation

Author proof2026-09-05, compressed2026-09-07; primary-source inputs
checked, no independent audit claimed. This concerns EVERY maximal
isotropic finite subgroup scheme, not just a Verschiebung kernel.

## 1. Statement and polarized quotient

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic p>0, (A,L) a ppav of
dimension g≥1, and K⊂A[p] a subgroup scheme of length p^g isotropic
for the commutator pairing of L^p. It may be nonreduced or disconnected.
For EVERY T≡L^m, 1≤m<p, and every a∈A(k), restriction is injective:

    H⁰(A,T) → H⁰(a+K,T|_(a+K)).                            (1)

The target uses the FULL scheme, not just geometric-point evaluations.

For v:A→Y=A/K, polarization descent gives a principal ample M with

    v̂ λ_M v=pλ_L,   v^*M≡L^p.

Indeed deg(λ_M)=p^(2g)/(p^g)²=1. This descends the polarization
homomorphism, without assuming a linearization of the chosen L^p.
Put N_A=(λ_L^(-1))^*L on Â and N_Y=(λ_M^(-1))^*M on Ŷ.
Inverting the polarization identity in rational homomorphisms gives

    v λ_L^(-1) v̂=pλ_M^(-1),   v̂^*N_A≡N_Y^p.              (2)

These are valid for inseparable isogenies as well.

## 2. A positive Fourier splitting

First take T=L^m and a=0. The bundle E=v_*T is IT_0:
H^i(Y,E⊗α)=H^i(A,T⊗v^*α)=0 for i>0 and all α∈Pic⁰(Y).
For normalized Poincare Fourier functors put

    Q=Φ_Y(E)^∨=v̂^*Q_m,   Q_m=Φ_A(T)^∨,
    W=H⁰(A,T)^∨.

The ample-line transform formula φ_T^*Q_m=W⊗T, with φ_T=[m]λ_L,
gives the ACTUAL splitting

    [m]_(Ŷ)^*Q ≅ W⊗v̂^*N_A^m.

Constant rigidification lines are absorbed into W. For G=Q⊗N_Y^(-1),

    [m]^*G ≅ W⊗R,
    R=v̂^*N_A^m⊗[m]^*N_Y^(-1) ≡ N_Y^(m(p−m)).              (3)

R is ample. Hence [m]^*(G⊗β) has no higher cohomology for any
β∈Pic⁰(Ŷ). The invertible degree m^(2g) makes the normalized trace
split O→[m]_*O. Projection formula therefore realizes G⊗β as a
direct summand of [m]_*[m]^*(G⊗β), proving G IT_0.
Both G and N_Y are M-regular, so their tensor product Q is globally
generated by Pareschi–Popa's M-regularity theorem. No trace splitting
along the characteristic-divisible isogeny v or v̂ has been used.

## 3. Check the direction of Fourier evaluation

For an IT_0 vector bundle E_0 on an abelian g-fold Z, write
F_0=Φ_Z(E_0), Q_0=F_0^∨. The composition pairing describing evaluation
of E_0 at0 is

    Hom(O,E_0)⊗Hom(E_0,k(0)) → Hom(O,k(0)).

Fourier sends O to k(0)[−g] up to a constant line and k(0) to O,
so this becomes Ext^g(k(0),F_0)⊗Hom(F_0,O)→Ext^g(k(0),O).
For the one-dimensional D=Ext^g(k(0),O), the local Koszul resolution
identifies it with

    ((F_0)_0⊗D)⊗H⁰(Q_0) → D,   (u⊗d,s)↦s(0)(u)d.

Thus evaluation of E_0 has the rank of the TRANSPOSE of evaluation
of Q_0. Global generation of Q_0 makes the former injective.
Apply this to E. Finite base change identifies its target fiber with
H⁰(K,T|_K), including all nilpotents, proving(1) in the initial case.

For general T=L^m⊗α, choose β with β^m=α in Pic⁰(A). Replace L by
L⊗β, preserving its polarization and the isotropic condition, to obtain
the same conclusion. Apply it to t_a^*T to obtain every coset.
Numerical and algebraic equivalence of line bundles agree on A.

## 4. Intrinsic Dirac line and the retained scope

Fibers of an isogeny are complete intersections in smooth varieties,
so K=∐K_x has local Gorenstein rings; the identity socle is
one-dimensional. For any invertible sheaf T|_K define

    Δ_0(T|_K)={u:u|_Kx=0 for x≠0, m_0 u|_K0=0}.

This one-dimensional subspace is intrinsic: units preserve the socle.
By(1), its inverse image in H⁰(A,T) has dimension≤1, and every nonzero
section in that inverse image has a NONZERO Dirac restriction.
Equivalently let I_Δ⊂O_K be the identity socle ideal, zero on other
components. The subscheme Z_Δ=V(I_Δ) has length p^g−1; these sections
are exactly those vanishing scheme-theoretically on Z_Δ.

For any ppav P, the kernel of V:P^(p)→P satisfies the hypotheses:
VF=[p], F^*L^(p)=L^p and injectivity of numerical pullback give
V^*L≡(L^(p))^p; polarization descent makes ker(V) isotropic, of
length p^g and contained in P^(p)[p]. Thus Tong's nonzero Dirac section
in the Raynaud determinant line is unique within that fixed line.
The [audited ppav theorem](CANONICAL_DIRAC_DIVISOR_UNIQUENESS_IN_THE_POLARIZATION_CLASS.md)
now proves the stronger existence and uniqueness across its entire
numerical class; its proof is not repeated here.

For ordinary kernels Z_Δ removes the reduced identity. For nonordinary
kernels it removes only the socle; a nonzero Dirac restriction can have
zero value at the reduced origin. Merely vanishing on nonidentity
components is insufficient, and is vacuous for a connected kernel.
The general interpolation theorem does NOT supply Dirac existence
for arbitrary K. Positivity and invertible trace require1≤m<p;
nothing at m=p, or about arbitrary common curve covers, is asserted.
