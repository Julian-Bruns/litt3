# Pushing the Tango line to the fixed curve

## Status and purpose

**Status: proved.** [Composite audit by two independent helpers](audits/43_TANGO_PUSHDOWN_AUDIT.md).

In the equal-degree seven-diamond of file 38, the
exact differential \(dx\) gives a line of degree \(5M\) in the Cartier
bundle of \(V\). This note determines exactly what ordinary descent, its
seven translates, its norm, and the first Frobenius--Harder--Narasimhan
quotient force.

Descent through \(V\to C\) does not put the degree-\(5M\) norm line inside
\(B_C^1\): it puts it inside \(\operatorname{Sym}^7B_C^1\), and stability
actually forbids any nonzero map from the norm line to \(B_C^1\).

Pushing the original line all the way through \(V\to X\) is stronger. It
produces a strongly semistable bundle of slope \(5/7\) mapping to the fixed
rank-four bundle \(B_X^1\). The residual zero divisor \(3B_\infty\)
disappears after all sheets are combined, so the induced map to
\(\omega_X\) is surjective. This leaves only

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \operatorname{rank}S&\deg S\\ \hline
 2&2\\
 3&3\text{ or }4\\
 4&3,4,5,6,7\text{ or }8.
\end{array}                                                \tag{43.1}
\]

This is a finite, base-level subbundle problem on the explicit
genus-three curve. It is not yet a contradiction: an explicit
rank-three degree-four subbundle of \(B_X^1\) has the required surjective
first Frobenius projection. At the generic point, finite-etale adjunction
is a nondegenerate trace-coordinate map, and a cyclic-seven Kummer model
shows that rank four is generic. Thus any continuation must use the full
global three-fiber divisor profile, not merely the ambient Cartier bundle,
its pairing, or the generic pushforward algebra.

Throughout, \(k=\overline{\mathbf F}_5\), and we use the diamond

\[
 V\xrightarrow[7]{\pi}C\xrightarrow[M]{c}X,\qquad
 a:V\xrightarrow[M]{}Y,
\]

where

\[
 X:\ v^2=x^7-x+1,\qquad Y:\ y^{31}=x(x-1).
\]

Frobenius twists are displayed when they matter and otherwise suppressed;
this does not affect any degree.

## 1. The exact line and its first Frobenius projection

Let \(B_0,B_1,B_\infty\) be the reduced degree-\(M\) divisors pulled back
from the three totally ramified points of \(Y\to\mathbf P^1\). File 38
gives

\[
 \operatorname{div}(dx)
 =30B_0+30B_1-32B_\infty
 =5D_x+3B_\infty,                                        \tag{43.2}
\]

where

\[
 D_x=6B_0+6B_1-7B_\infty,\qquad \deg D_x=5M.             \tag{43.3}
\]

Let

\[
                         L_x\subset B_V^1                \tag{43.4}
\]

be the saturated \(k(V)^5\)-line generated by \(dx\). Locally, \(u\)
belongs to its lattice exactly when
\(\operatorname{div}(u)+D_x\geq0\), because \(u^5dx\) must be regular.
Consequently

\[
 L_x\simeq\mathcal O_{V^{(1)}}(D_x),\qquad \deg L_x=5M.  \tag{43.5}
\]

Let

\[
 \rho_V:F_V^*B_V^1\longrightarrow\omega_V               \tag{43.6}
\]

be the first quotient of the canonical Frobenius filtration. Under the
diagonal-ideal description of file 22, its restriction to \(F_V^*L_x\)
is represented by \(dx\). Thus

\[
 F_V^*L_x\simeq\omega_V(-3B_\infty),                     \tag{43.7}
\]

and the restricted map has zero divisor exactly \(3B_\infty\).

## 2. The seven translates on \(C\)

Let \(\beta\) generate the deck group of \(\pi\), and put
\(L_j=\beta^{j*}L_x\). Finite-etale duality gives a canonical map

\[
 \epsilon_C:\pi^{(1)}_*L_x\longrightarrow B_C^1.         \tag{43.8}
\]

After pullback to \(V^{(1)}\), this is the sum of the seven inclusions

\[
                 \bigoplus_{j=0}^6L_j\longrightarrow B_V^1. \tag{43.9}
\]

Let \(S_C\) be its torsion-free image, with rank \(r\) and degree \(d\).

### Proposition 43.10 (orbit-span bounds)

The bundle \(\pi^{(1)}_*L_x\) has rank seven, degree \(5M\), and is
strongly semistable of slope \(5M/7\). Consequently

\[
                         d\geq\frac{5Mr}{7}.             \tag{43.10}
\]

If \(r<4\), stability of \(B_C^1\) gives \(d<2Mr\); if \(r=4\), then
\(d\leq8M\).

Moreover, \(r=1\) holds exactly when the embedded line \(L_x\) is
\(C_7\)-invariant. In that case

\[
                         7\mid M                         \tag{43.11}
\]

and \(B_\infty\) is \(C_7\)-invariant. No converse to the last statement
is asserted.

#### Proof

Galois base change gives

\[
 \pi^{(1)*}\pi^{(1)}_*L_x\simeq\bigoplus_{j=0}^6L_j.     \tag{43.12}
\]

All summands have degree \(5M\). The direct sum is semistable, and the
same is true after every Frobenius pullback. Semistability descends under
a finite faithfully flat map, proving strong semistability. Riemann--Roch
and etale Riemann--Hurwitz show that finite-etale pushforward preserves
degree, so \(\deg\pi^{(1)}_*L_x=5M\).

The image \(S_C\) is a torsion-free quotient, giving (43.10). Its
saturation is a rank-\(r\) subbundle of the stable bundle \(B_C^1\), whose
slope is \(g(C)-1=2M\). This proves the upper bounds.

If \(r=1\), the seven saturated lines in (43.9) have the same generic line,
and hence are equal. Thus \(L_x\) is invariant. The converse is immediate.
An invariant line descends through the \(C_7\)-torsor, so its degree \(5M\)
is divisible by seven. Finally, (43.7) and its \(\beta\)-translate have
the same image when \(L_x\) is invariant. Comparing their zero divisors
gives \(\beta^*B_\infty=B_\infty\). \(\square\)

There is no exact-differential or representation-theoretic reason for
\(r=1\). To see the full-rank escape, let \(K/L\) be the function-field
extension of any connected etale \(C_7\)-torsor. Choose
\(\zeta\in k\) of order seven, an eigenfunction \(w\in K\) with
\(\beta(w)=\zeta w\), and a separating \(u\in L\). Put

\[
                         q=\sum_{i=1}^4w^{5i}u^i.        \tag{43.13}
\]

Then

\[
 d(\beta^jq)=\sum_{i=1}^4i\,\zeta^{5ij}w^{5i}u^{i-1}du.
                                                               \tag{43.14}
\]

For \(j=0,1,2,3\), the coefficient determinant is a nonzero Vandermonde
determinant in the four distinct elements
\(\zeta^5,\zeta^{10},\zeta^{15},\zeta^{20}\). Hence four members of this
single \(C_7\)-orbit of exact lines span all four exact-differential
directions. Truncating the same construction gives generic orbit spans of
ranks two and three. This example need not have the triangle divisor
profile; it proves that exactness and the free \(C_7\)-action do not force
a smaller span.

The equality \(\operatorname{ord}_7(5)=6\) does not alter this conclusion.
That order controls an \(\mathbf F_5\)-linear \(C_7\)-representation which
is itself preserved by Frobenius. Here the action in (43.12) is semilinear
over \(k(V)^5/k(C)^5\), and the selected line \(L_x\) is not supplied with
a Frobenius-stability datum. Moreover all data may be defined only after a
finite constant extension; over \(\mathbf F_{5^6}\), Frobenius fixes every
\(C_7\)-character. Thus no rank-one-or-rank-six dichotomy applies to the
rank-\(\leq4\) orbit span.

There is also an exact projection formula. Let

\[
G=\bigcap_{j=0}^6\beta^{j*}B_\infty=\pi^*G_C,\qquad
a_0=\deg G_C\leq M/7,                                   \tag{43.15}
\]

where the intersection is the coefficientwise minimum. The sum of the
seven image ideals from (43.7) is \(\omega_V(-3G)\), so descent gives

\[
 \operatorname{im}\bigl(F_C^*S_C\to\omega_C\bigr)
                         =\omega_C(-3G_C).               \tag{43.16}
\]

If \(K_C\) is the kernel, then

\[
                   \deg K_C=5d-(4M-3a_0).               \tag{43.17}
\]

Using \(K_C\subset I_C^2\) and the HN quotients
\(\omega_C^2,\omega_C^3,\omega_C^4\) gives only

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
r&\text{HN upper bound for }d\\ \hline
1&(4M-3a_0)/5\\
2&(20M-3a_0)/5\\
3&(32M-3a_0)/5\\
4&(40M-3a_0)/5.
\end{array}                                               \tag{43.18}
\]

These are compatible with (43.10). The semistable bundle
\(F_C^*\pi_*L_x\), of slope \(25M/7\), maps onto the line in (43.16);
the resulting inequality is just \(a_0\leq M/7\). Thus the first
Frobenius projection supplies no hidden divisibility or contradiction on
\(C\).

## 3. The norm line

Put

\[
                 N_C=\operatorname{Nm}_{V^{(1)}/C^{(1)}}(L_x).
                                                               \tag{43.19}
\]

It has degree \(5M\). Multiplying the seven conjugate inclusions and
symmetrizing gives a nonzero map

\[
                         N_C\longrightarrow
                         \operatorname{Sym}^7B_C^1.       \tag{43.20}
\]

It does not give a map to \(B_C^1\). Indeed,

\[
                         \operatorname{Hom}(N_C,B_C^1)=0, \tag{43.21}
\]

because \(B_C^1\otimes N_C^{-1}\) is stable of slope
\(2M-5M=-3M<0\).

The norm retains the residual divisor exactly. Put
\(D_\infty=\pi_*B_\infty\), of degree \(M\). Taking the symmetric product
of (43.7) gives

\[
          F_C^*N_C\simeq\omega_C^{\otimes7}(-3D_\infty). \tag{43.22}
\]

After pullback to \(V\), its zero divisor is
\(3\sum_j\beta^{j*}B_\infty=3\pi^*D_\infty\); both sides have degree
\(25M\).

For the cyclic torsor,
\(\pi_*\mathcal O_V=\bigoplus_{i=0}^6\eta^{-i}\) for a seven-torsion
line bundle \(\eta\). Hence
\(\det\pi_*\mathcal O_V=\eta^{-21}\simeq\mathcal O_C\), and

\[
                         \det(\pi_*L_x)\simeq N_C.        \tag{43.23}
\]

Thus the degree-\(5M\) line is the determinant of the rank-seven source
of (43.8), not a line subbundle of its rank-four target. Equations
(43.20)--(43.21) make the failure of the norm shortcut precise.

## 4. Pushdown to \(X\)

Let

\[
 h=c\pi:V\longrightarrow X,\qquad\deg h=7M,\qquad
 E_X=h^{(1)}_*L_x.                                       \tag{43.24}
\]

Finite-etale duality turns (43.4) and
\(B_V^1\simeq h^{(1)*}B_X^1\) into a nonzero map

\[
                         \epsilon_X:E_X\longrightarrow B_X^1. \tag{43.25}
\]

### Theorem 43.26 (fixed-bundle reduction)

The bundle \(E_X\) is strongly semistable, with

\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_X=7M,\qquad
 \deg E_X=5M,\qquad \mu(E_X)=\frac57.                    \tag{43.26}
\]

Let \(S\) be the torsion-free image of (43.25), with rank \(r\) and degree
\(d\). Then

\[
 F_X^*E_X\longrightarrow F_X^*S\longrightarrow
 F_X^*B_X^1\xrightarrow{\rho_X}\omega_X                  \tag{43.27}
\]

is surjective, and \((r,d)\) is one of the pairs in (43.1).

#### Proof

Riemann--Roch gives the degree:

\[
 \chi(L_x)=5M+1-(14M+1)=-9M,
\]

whereas a rank-\(7M\) bundle on the genus-three curve \(X^{(1)}\) has
Euler characteristic \(\deg E_X-14M\).

Choose a connected finite-etale Galois cover \(W\to X^{(1)}\) dominating
\(V^{(1)}\). Flat base change decomposes the pullback of \(E_X\) into the
direct sum of the \(7M\) conjugate pullbacks of \(L_x\). They are line
bundles of equal degree. The same holds after every Frobenius pullback, so
\(E_X\) is strongly semistable.

For surjectivity, use a Galois cover \(W\to X\) dominating \(V\to X\).
The \(7M\) conjugate summands in (43.27) have images

\[
                         \omega_W(-3B_{\infty,s}),       \tag{43.28}
\]

where \(B_{\infty,s}\) runs through all sheet-pullbacks of \(B_\infty\).
A point common to all \(B_{\infty,s}\) would say that an entire reduced
fiber of \(V\to X\), of degree \(7M\), lies in the degree-\(M\) divisor
\(B_\infty\). This is impossible. The sum of the ideals in (43.28) is
therefore \(\mathcal O_W\), and faithfully flat descent proves
surjectivity.

Since \(S\) is a quotient of the semistable bundle \(E_X\),

\[
                         \frac dr\geq\frac57.             \tag{43.29}
\]

The saturation of \(S\) in the stable slope-two bundle \(B_X^1\) has
degree at most \(2r-1\) when \(r<4\), and at most \(8\) when \(r=4\).
Initially this gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
r&\text{possible }d\\ \hline
1&1\\
2&2,3\\
3&3,4,5\\
4&3,4,5,6,7,8.
\end{array}                                               \tag{43.30}
\]

Put \(H=F_X^*S\). Surjectivity gives

\[
 0\longrightarrow K_2\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow\omega_X
 \longrightarrow0,\qquad \deg K_2=5d-4.                 \tag{43.31}
\]

For \(r=1\), this would be a surjection between line bundles of degrees
\(5d\) and \(4\), hence an isomorphism, which is impossible.

Suppose \(r=2\). The Cartier connection on \(H\) gives the second
fundamental form

\[
                         \theta_2:K_2\longrightarrow\omega_X^2. \tag{43.32}
\]

If it vanished, \(K_2\) would be horizontal and Cartier descent would
make its degree divisible by five. But
\(\deg K_2=5d-4\equiv1\pmod5\). Thus \(\theta_2\ne0\), so
\(5d-4\leq8\). Equation (43.30) now forces \(d=2\).

Suppose \(r=3\). The analogous map \(K_2\to\omega_X^2\) is again nonzero.
Write its image as

\[
 Q_2=\omega_X^2(-D_2),\qquad e=\deg D_2\geq0,
\]

and let \(K_3\) be its kernel. Then

\[
                         \deg K_3=5d-12+e.               \tag{43.33}
\]

There is an induced second fundamental form

\[
                         \theta_3:K_3\longrightarrow Q_2\otimes\omega_X.
                                                               \tag{43.34}
\]

If it vanished, \(K_3\) would descend to a line subbundle of \(S\), hence
of \(B_X^1\). Every line subbundle of \(B_X^1\) has degree at most

\[
 n(X)=\left\lfloor\frac{2g(X)-2}{5}\right\rfloor=0.       \tag{43.35}
\]

But (43.30) and (43.33) give \(\deg K_3\geq3\), so
\(\theta_3\ne0\). Comparing degrees yields

\[
                         5d+2e\leq24.                    \tag{43.36}
\]

Thus \(d=3\) or \(4\); more precisely, \(e\leq4\) when \(d=3\), and
\(e\leq2\) when \(d=4\). This proves (43.1). \(\square\)

### Corollary 43.37 (full-rank Frobenius positions)

If \(r=4\), the filtration induced on \(H=F_X^*S\) by the Cartier
filtration has line quotients

\[
 \omega_X,\quad\omega_X^2(-D_2),\quad
 \omega_X^3(-D_3),\quad\omega_X^4(-D_4).                 \tag{43.37}
\]

Writing \(e_i=\deg D_i\), one has

\[
 0\leq e_2\leq e_3\leq e_4,\qquad
 e_2+e_3+e_4=5(8-d),                                     \tag{43.38}
\]

and strong semistability of the source adds

\[
                         e_2\leq4,\qquad e_2+e_3\leq13.   \tag{43.39}
\]

#### Proof

The first quotient is all of \(\omega_X\) by Theorem 43.26. Since \(S\)
has full rank, its generic fiber is that of \(B_X^1\), so the three
successive oper maps are nonzero. Each map from the \(j\)-th quotient to
the preceding quotient tensored with \(\omega_X\) gives
\(e_j\geq e_{j-1}\), with \(e_1=0\). Finally,

\[
 4+8+12+16-(e_2+e_3+e_4)=\deg F_X^*S=5d.
\]

This proves (43.38). The bottom two- and three-step quotients of
\(F_X^*S\) are also quotients of the semistable bundle \(F_X^*E_X\), of
slope \(25/7\). Their degrees and ranks are respectively

\[
 (12-e_2,2),\qquad(24-e_2-e_3,3).
\]

Their slopes are at least \(25/7\). The resulting integral inequalities
are exactly (43.39). \(\square\)

The full-rank alternative is genuinely present at the level of the
Cartier bundle with connection.

### Proposition 43.40 (local models for every full-rank degree)

For every \(d\in\{3,4,5,6,7,8\}\), there is a full-rank subsheaf

\[
                         S_d\subset B_X^1,\qquad \deg S_d=d,
\]

such that \(F_X^*S_d\to\omega_X\) is surjective and its induced defects in
(43.37) are

\[
                         (e_2,e_3,e_4)=(0,0,5(8-d)).
                                                               \tag{43.40}
\]

#### Proof

Choose a point with completed parameter \(s\) on \(X^{(1)}\), and let
\(t\) be the corresponding parameter on \(X\), so \(F_X^*s=t^5\).
The Cartier bundle has the standard horizontal local frame

\[
                         e_i=[t^i],\qquad1\leq i\leq4,
\]

and the first oper quotient sends

\[
                         e_i\longmapsto i\,t^{i-1}dt.     \tag{43.41}
\]

Put \(\delta=8-d\). Make an elementary modification at this point by
using the lattice

\[
 S_d=\mathcal Oe_1+\mathcal Oe_2+\mathcal Oe_3+
                         \mathcal O\,s^\delta e_4        \tag{43.42}
\]

there, and \(S_d=B_X^1\) away from the point. Its colength is \(\delta\),
so its degree is \(8-\delta=d\). After Frobenius pullback, the last
generator is \(t^{5\delta}e_4\), while \(e_1\) is unchanged. Equation
(43.41) shows that the first projection remains surjective.

The change from the horizontal frame \(e_1,\ldots,e_4\) to the
diagonal-ideal frame \(\alpha,\alpha^2,\alpha^3,\alpha^4\) is triangular
with unit diagonal. Intersecting (43.42) with that filtration therefore
leaves the second and third graded lattices unchanged and multiplies the
fourth by \(t^{5\delta}\). This gives (43.40). \(\square\)

This proposition does not construct the required pushforward
\(h_*L_x\). It shows precisely that rank, degree, the symplectic ambient
bundle, horizontality, surjectivity onto \(\omega_X\), and all the defect
relations (43.38) cannot exclude the rank-four branch.

## 5. Self-duality and the remaining finite problem

The Cartier pairing gives

\[
                    (B_X^1)^\vee\otimes\omega_{X^{(1)}}
                    \simeq B_X^1.                        \tag{43.43}
\]

If a rank-three subsheaf \(S\) has saturation \(\overline S\) of degree
\(d'\), its symplectic annihilator is a line subbundle of degree \(d'-4\).
Equation (43.35) therefore gives \(d'\leq4\), independently confirming
the rank-three bound.

Self-duality cannot eliminate degree four. A degree-zero Tango line
\(A\subset B_X^1\), whose existence follows from \(n(X)=0\), has a
rank-three orthogonal \(A^\perp\) of degree four.

There is nevertheless a concrete extra equation in every rank-three case.
Let \(A=\overline S^\perp\), and choose a separating
\(u\in k(X)\) whose exact-differential line is \(A\). Since the original
inclusion \(L_x\to h^*B_X^1\) factors through \(h^*\overline S\), the
Cartier pairing gives

\[
                         \operatorname{Car}(u\,dx)=0.     \tag{43.44}
\]

Using \(u\) as a \(5\)-basis coordinate, write uniquely in \(k(V)\)

\[
 x=b_0^5+b_1^5u+b_2^5u^2+b_3^5u^3+b_4^5u^4.            \tag{43.45}
\]

The standard Cartier formula gives

\[
 \operatorname{Car}(u\,dx)=4b_4\,du.
\]

Thus rank three forces the Frobenius-cubic normal form

\[
                         b_4=0.                           \tag{43.46}
\]

Consequently, stability, the norm, ordinary equivariant descent, and the
first Frobenius HN projection do not yet contradict the seven-diamond.
They reduce it to (43.1). A continuation must use the special origin of
\(E_X=h_*L_x\), rather than only its rank, degree, determinant, and first
oper position. In rank three, it must rule out the additional Cartier
pairing equation (43.44), equivalently the missing fourth coefficient in
(43.46). Proposition 43.40 shows that rank four remains the principal
formal escape.

## 6. Exact base-curve escape and the generic trace formula

The preceding rank-three warning can be made completely explicit on the
chosen curve \(X\). The following local criterion is also useful beyond
this example.

### Proposition 43.47 (the orthogonal Tango line criterion)

Let \(C\) be a smooth curve in characteristic five, let
\(A\subset B_C^1\) be the saturated exact line defined by a separating
function \(u\), and write

\[
 D=\left\lfloor\frac{\operatorname{div}(du)}5\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \operatorname{div}(du)=5D+R,
 \qquad A\simeq\mathcal O_{C^{(1)}}(D).                 \tag{43.47}
\]

Every coefficient of \(R\) belongs to \(\{0,1,2,3\}\). Put
\(S=A^\perp\) for the Cartier alternating pairing. Then

\[
 \deg S=2g(C)-2+\deg A,                                 \tag{43.48}
\]

and the first canonical projection

\[
                         F_C^*S\longrightarrow\omega_C \tag{43.49}
\]

is surjective if and only if no coefficient of \(R\) is three.

#### Proof

At a point \(P\), subtract a fifth power from \(u\) and choose a local
parameter \(t\). If

\[
                 \operatorname{ord}_P(du)=5m+r,
\]

then the first non-fifth-power term of \(u\) has exponent
\(5m+s\), where \(s=r+1\). Such an exponent is not divisible by five,
so \(s\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) and \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\).

Use the diagonal-ideal model

\[
 F_C^*B_C^1=I=(\alpha)/(\alpha^5),
 \qquad \alpha=t\otimes1-1\otimes t.
\]

The normalized local generator \(t^{-5m}du\) of \(F_C^*A\) has nonzero
fiber in \(I^s/I^{s+1}\). The pulled-back Cartier pairing pairs
\(I^i/I^{i+1}\) perfectly with \(I^{5-i}/I^{6-i}\); in particular,

\[
                            (I^2_P)^\perp=I^4_P.
\]

The kernel of \(F_C^*B_C^1\to\omega_C\) is \(I^2\). Since both
\(F_C^*S_P\) and \(I^2_P\) have dimension three, (43.49) fails at \(P\)
exactly when \(F_C^*S_P=I^2_P\). Taking orthogonals, this is equivalent
to \(F_C^*A_P=I^4_P\), hence to \(s=4\), or \(r=3\).

Finally the perfect pairing identifies
\(B_C^1/S\simeq A^\vee\otimes\omega_{C^{(1)}}\). Since
\(\deg B_C^1=4(g(C)-1)\), this gives (43.48). \(\square\)

On

\[
                     X:\quad v^2=t^7-t+1,
\]

let \(P_\pm=(0,\pm1)\), let \(Q=(-1,-1)\), and let \(\infty\) be the
unique point at infinity. The separating function

\[
                  u=\frac{(t+3)v+3t+2}{t^2}             \tag{43.50}
\]

satisfies

\[
          \operatorname{div}(du)
             =-4\infty-3P_-+P_++10Q.                   \tag{43.51}
\]

For a direct check, differentiation gives

\[
 du=\frac{A+(2t+1)v}{t^3v}\,dt,
 \qquad A=2t^7+3t^2+t+4,
\]

and

\[
 A^2-(2t+1)^2(t^7-t+1)=4t^4(t+1)^{10}.
\]

The two sheets over \(t=0,-1\), together with the order at infinity,
then give (43.51). Hence

\[
 D=-\infty-P_-+2Q,
 \qquad R=\infty+2P_-+P_+.                              \tag{43.52}
\]

The exact line \(A=\mathcal O(D)\subset B_X^1\) has degree zero, and
\(A^\perp\) is therefore a genuine rank-three degree-four subbundle.
No coefficient of \(R\) is three, so Proposition 43.47 proves that
\(F_X^*A^\perp\to\omega_X\) is everywhere surjective. Thus even the
exact fixed bundle and the first Frobenius projection do not eliminate
the rank-three degree-four case in (43.1).

There is an equally precise generic explanation for the rank-four escape.

### Proposition 43.53 (finite-etale trace coordinates)

Let \(\pi:V\to C\) be finite etale, with function fields \(K/L\), and
let \(u\in L\) be separating. Write, at the generic point,

\[
 dq=\sum_{i=1}^4c_i\,d(u^i),\qquad c_i\in K^5.          \tag{43.53}
\]

After rationally trivializing the exact line \(L_q=K^5dq\), its adjoint
map \(\pi_*L_q\to B_C^1\) is

\[
 \lambda\longmapsto
 \sum_{i=1}^4\operatorname{Tr}_{K^5/L^5}(\lambda c_i)\,d(u^i).
                                                               \tag{43.54}
\]

Its generic rank is therefore

\[
             \dim_{L^5}\langle c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4\rangle. \tag{43.55}
\]

#### Proof

Finite-etale adjunction is the trace counit after the generic base change
\(K^5/L^5\). This gives (43.54). The trace pairing of the finite separable
extension \(K^5/L^5\) is nondegenerate, so the rank of the four trace
functionals in (43.54) is the dimension of the span of their representing
elements \(c_i\). \(\square\)

For example, take a connected etale cyclic extension
\(K=L(w)\), with

\[
 w^7=a,\qquad\beta(w)=\zeta_7w,
\]

and put

\[
                         q=\sum_{i=1}^4w^{5i}u^i.        \tag{43.56}
\]

Then four translates of \(dq\) have coefficient determinant equal, up to
the nonzero constants \(1,2,3,4\), to

\[
 \left(\prod_{i=1}^4w^{5i}\right)
 \prod_{1\leq i<j\leq4}
       (\zeta_7^{5j}-\zeta_7^{5i}),                     \tag{43.57}
\]

which is nonzero. Thus one cyclic orbit of an exact line can have generic
span four. The Cartier pairing does not obstruct it: in the exact basis
\(d(u^i)\),

\[
 \left\langle d(u^i),d(u^j)\right\rangle
  =\begin{cases}
      j\,du,&i+j=5,\\
      0,&i+j\ne5,
    \end{cases}                                         \tag{43.58}
\]

whose Pfaffian is \(4\cdot3=2\ne0\) in characteristic five.

Finally, determinant bookkeeping supplies no missing congruence. If the
full-rank image is \(S\subset B_X^1\), put
\(\det S=\omega_X^2(-T)\), and let \(R_E\) be the kernel of
\(E_X\to S\). With

\[
 \delta_h=\det(h_*\mathcal O_V),\qquad\delta_h^2\simeq\mathcal O_X,
\]

finite-etale norm and (43.7) give

\[
 F_X^*\det R_E\simeq
 \omega_X^{7M-10}(-3h_*B_\infty+5T)\otimes\delta_h.     \tag{43.59}
\]

This determines only the otherwise unconstrained kernel determinant. The
Pfaffian of the restricted alternating form is a section of
\(\mathcal O_X(T)\) and has divisor \(T\), so it gives no parity
condition on \(T\).

The Kummer example (43.56) does not have the special identities

\[
 \operatorname{div}(q)=31(B_0-B_\infty),\qquad
 \operatorname{div}(q-1)=31(B_1-B_\infty),              \tag{43.60}
\]

and is not a map etale over \(Y\). It is therefore a no-go theorem only
for arguments based on generic trace algebra, cyclic character counts,
the symplectic form, or the Pfaffian. The global divisor identities
(43.60), simultaneously with the special pushed origin of \(E_X\), are
the remaining possible source of a contradiction.

## 7. The four-power frame and its exact limitation

The actual triangle function supplies a useful global check on the last
sentence. On \(Y\), let \(L_i\subset B_Y^1\) be the saturated exact line
generated by \(d(x^i)\), for \(1\leq i\leq4\). Directly from the three
fibers,

\[
 \operatorname{div}(d(x^i))
  =(31i-1)P_0+30P_1-(31i+1)P_\infty,
\]

so

\[
 L_i\simeq\mathcal O_{Y^{(1)}}
  \bigl(6iP_0+6P_1-(6i+1)P_\infty\bigr),
 \qquad \deg L_i=5.                                    \tag{43.61}
\]

Its residual divisor in the first Frobenius projection is

\[
                       (i-1)P_0+(4-i)P_\infty.          \tag{43.62}
\]

The four exact differentials are a basis at the generic point. Their
residual orders are pairwise distinct at \(P_0\) and at \(P_\infty\), so
the determinant is a unit there, and it is a unit wherever \(x\) is an
etale parameter. Since

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^4
  \bigl(6iP_0+6P_1-(6i+1)P_\infty\bigr)
 =60P_0+24P_1-64P_\infty
\]

whereas

\[
                    \det B_Y^1\simeq\omega_Y^2
   =\mathcal O_Y(60P_0+60P_1-64P_\infty),
\]

the resulting generically invertible map has exact determinant divisor

\[
       \det\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^4L_i\longrightarrow B_Y^1\right)
                              =36P_1.                    \tag{43.63}
\]

Pull this frame to \(V\), and push it through \(h:V\to X\). It gives a
surjection

\[
 h^{(1)}_*\left(a^{(1)*}\bigoplus_{i=1}^4L_i\right)
                              \longtwoheadrightarrow B_X^1. \tag{43.64}
\]

Indeed, after pullback to a Galois closure, a sheetwise determinant
vanishes precisely on \(36B_{1,s}\). A common zero for all sheets would
put an entire reduced \(h\)-fiber of degree \(7M\) inside the degree-
\(M\) divisor \(B_1\), which is impossible. The source of (43.64) has
rank \(28M\), degree \(20M\), and is strongly semistable of slope
\(5/7\), because on a Galois cover it splits into equal-degree lines.

Thus the genuine three-fiber profile is fully compatible with a global
surjection from a strongly semistable slope-\(5/7\) bundle onto the fixed
Cartier bundle. This does not show that the single summand \(h_*L_1\) has
rank-four image. It shows that source semistability, all four exact-power
lines, and the aggregate determinant cannot by themselves supply the
missing contradiction.

For completeness, the only elementary collision information is also
limited. Write \(L_{j,i}=\beta^{j*}a^*L_i\). Equality of two embedded
lines forces equality of their residual divisors

\[
 (i-1)B_{0,j}+(4-i)B_{\infty,j}.
\]

The coefficient-one/coefficient-two patterns show that the fourteen lines
with \(i=2,3\) are pairwise distinct. A same-power collision would preserve
both \(B_0\) and \(B_\infty\), hence make the corresponding \(x\)-functions
equal; a collision between powers two and three would make an order-seven
element interchange \(B_0\) and \(B_\infty\), which is impossible. A
collision involving power one or four can, however, force only invariance
of one branch divisor (and hence \(7\mid M\)) or can carry \(B_0\) to
\(B_\infty\). There is no unconditional collision, and no bound on a
rank-four stable bundle forbids fourteen such nonmaximal exact lines.
Indeed,

\[
 n(V)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2g(V)-2}{5}\right\rfloor
       =5M+\left\lfloor\frac{3M}{5}\right\rfloor,        \tag{43.65}
\]

so their degree \(5M\) is strictly below the absolute Tango bound for
\(M\geq2\). Maximal-line uniqueness and dormant-oper uniqueness theorems
therefore do not apply.
