## 1. Audited actual image, intersection class and conductor

The map (p,a):V→C×Y is birational onto its reduced image D.
Its generic degree divides seven; degree seven would force a to
factor through p, contradicting aβ≠a. Hence V is its normalization,
and BOTH normalization projections are the original etale maps.

Etale-locally on C the seven branches are graphs y=f_i(t) with
each f_i etale. Every branch is smooth; singularities consist only of
intersections among distinct branches. With F_C=C×{y_0},
F_Y={c_0}×Y and γ_h the correspondence component,

\[
 [D]=7F_C+MF_Y+\gamma_h,\qquad
 \gamma_h\cdot F_C=\gamma_h\cdot F_Y=0,\qquad
 \gamma_h^2=-\operatorname{Tr}(s\mid H^1(Y)).
\]

Fiber degrees and the induced map h give the class. The last equality
is the standard negative cohomological Rosati intersection pairing.
Using the norm-trace identity proved in Section 3,

\[
 D^2=14M-\operatorname{Tr}s
      =\sum_{j=1}^6I_j-28M=2(I_1+I_2+I_3)-28M.          \tag{1}
\]

More strongly, the exact conductor DIVISOR on V and the normalization
defect are

\[
 \mathfrak C_D=\sum_{j=1}^6E_j,\qquad
 \delta(D)=\tfrac12\sum_jI_j=I_1+I_2+I_3,\qquad
 p_a(D)=14M+1+\delta(D).                                \tag{2}
\]

On branch i the conductor exponent is ∑_(k≠i)ord(f_i−f_k);
the delta invariant of the union is the corresponding sum over i<k.
E_j records branch i against branch i+j, so the ordered sum gives
the conductor and counts each unordered collision twice. This retains
local multiplicities, not just the number of coincidence points.

Independently, D·K_(C×Y)=28M+28M=56M, so adjunction with (1) gives
(2). Thus the conductor introduces no extra inequality beyond those
already contained in the I_j. Locally y=t and y=t+t^q have etale
projections and contact order q for every integer q≥1 in characteristic
five. No parity or congruence of an individual contact is forced.

For a primitive genus-fifteen self-correspondence Γ with actual etale

gives

\[
 \Gamma^2=2n^2-\operatorname{Tr}(u^\dagger u),\qquad
 \delta(\Gamma)=n^2+14n-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(u^\dagger u).

If u=0, these are 2n² and n²+14n, while Γ·Δ_Y=2n.
The large required defect is numerically possible, not a contradiction.

## 2. Audited low-degree hyperelliptic effectivity — Theorem 45.4

Independently let Y be ANY hyperelliptic curve of genus g≥2, with
π:Y→P¹ and H=π^*O(1). Let Γ⊂Y×Y be a REDUCED IRREDUCIBLE
effective Cartier divisor, finite dominant of degree n over each
factor, with induced homomorphism u_Γ=0. If n≤g, then n=2r is even,
and SCHEME-THEORETICALLY

\[
             \Gamma=(\pi\times\pi)^*\Gamma_0,
 \qquad\Gamma_0\subset\mathbf P^1\times\mathbf P^1
                  \text{ of bidegree }(r,r).            \tag{4}
\]

If its normalization Z has BOTH projections etale, the two separate
hyperelliptic involutions lift to FREE involutions on Z. Quotienting
by either gives an actual etale degree-r cover Z_i→Y of the other
factor. The remaining map q_i:Z_i→P¹ has degree2r, branch support
exactly the2g+2 hyperelliptic branch values, and fiber type2^r at each.

Proof. The zero action gives O(Γ)=A⊠B with deg A=deg B=n.
Each is basepoint-free: otherwise a vertical or horizontal fiber would
be a component of integral Γ. A basepoint-free line L of degree d≤g
on Y is H^(d/2). To see this, choose a basepoint-free pencil and write
its rational coordinate φ=ψ^(5^e) with ψ separable. If ψ did not factor
through π, the degree-two extension would force (π,ψ) to generate k(Y);
Castelnuovo--Severi would give g≤deg ψ−1≤d−1. Hence φ factors through
π, and its hyperplane pullback is H^(d/2). This explicitly retains the
inseparable-pencil check.

Therefore A=B=H^r. Since r≤g/2<g+1, the decomposition
π_*O_Y=O⊕O(−g−1) gives H^0(Y,H^r)=H^0(P¹,O(r)).
The defining section of Γ therefore descends to bidegree(r,r), proving
(4) as a scheme identity.

Each involution preserves Γ and lifts to its normalization. For
1×ι the lift is a nontrivial deck transformation of the FIRST etale
projection, hence free. Its quotient Z_2→Y has degree r and genus
r(g−1)+1. The second hyperelliptic coordinate gives q_2 of degree2r;
Z is the normalized pullback of the hyperelliptic double along q_2.
Since Z→Z_2 is etale, all indices of q_2 over its2g+2 branch values
must be even. Each such fiber contributes at least r to the different.
But Riemann--Hurwitz gives total different2r(g+1), exactly that lower
bound summed over the2g+2 values. Equality forces every index to be
two, each different exponent one, and no other ramification, including
no hidden wild contribution. Reverse factors for the other quotient.

For the genus-fifteen seven-diamond with M=9, EVERY cross homomorphism
a_*β^(j*)a^*, j≠0, is therefore nonzero. This includes nonbirational
cross images: if their generic degree is e_j, it divides M, and zero
of [e_j]u_Γj implies u_Γj=0 by torsion-free Hom. The reduced image
has odd degree M/e_j∈{1,3,9}, contradicting (4).
Thus the abstract model in Section 4 with six zero cross maps is NOT
realizable by actual effective curve correspondences.

The bound n≤g is essential to this argument. Starting in degree g+1,
hyperelliptic curves admit basepoint-free pencils not factoring through
π. No arbitrary-degree visibility or common-cover exclusion follows.
