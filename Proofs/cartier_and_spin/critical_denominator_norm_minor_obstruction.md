# Proof of the norm-minor denominator obstruction

Version1, 2 October2026.
[Statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/critical_denominator_norm_minor_obstruction.md).

Every nonzero maximal minor is affine regular on $C_0$. Since x is
finite, its norm is in $k[x]$. At least one minor is nonzero by the
generic-rank hypothesis, so their monic gcd $D_V$ exists and is nonzero.
A constant change of basis multiplies each maximal minor by the same
nonzero determinant; the monic norm gcd is unchanged.

Fix $a\in\mathbf A^1$. Write $e_Q=\operatorname{ord}_Q(x-a)$ and
$h_Q=\max\{0,-\operatorname{ord}_Q(c)\}$ for the points above a.
The required exponent of $x-a$ in the minimum clearing polynomial is
$\ell=\max_{Q\mid a}\lceil h_Q/e_Q\rceil$.
This follows directly from regularity of $Jc$ at each such point; it
also proves that the ideal of clearing polynomials is generated by
the product of these powers over the finitely many pole fibers.

If $\ell>0$, choose Q attaining this maximum. Then
$h_Q\ge e_Q(\ell-1)+1\ge\ell$.
At Q, the coefficient valuation of $T-c$ is $-h_Q$. Multiplicativity
of Gauss content in the DVR and the affine regularity of $(T-c)v$
therefore force EVERY coefficient of v to vanish to order at least
$h_Q$. Complete the nonzero constant vector v to a constant basis of V.
Every maximal minor now has a column with that vanishing order, while
all other columns are affine regular. Thus every nonzero maximal minor
M satisfies $\operatorname{ord}_Q M\ge h_Q$.

The norm valuation at a is the sum of the local valuations over its
fiber. Residue degrees are one because k is algebraically closed.
In particular it is NOT divided by $e_Q$ at a ramified point. All
other local orders of M are nonnegative, so
$\operatorname{ord}_a\operatorname{Nm}(M)\ge h_Q\ge\ell$.
The same inequality holds for every nonzero maximal minor, hence for
their gcd. This proves the required exponent for each factor of
$\delta_c$ and establishes divisibility.

The parameter corollary is polynomial division followed by the stated
Bezout identity: if $\delta_\lambda$ divided $D_V$, all remainder
coefficients would vanish at that parameter, contradicting a linear
combination equal to one. The test is geometric and includes repeated
denominator roots. No finite-field point search is used.
