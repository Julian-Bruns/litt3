# Minimal bi-etale correspondences and the relative Gauss map

Date: 2026-09-05. Author: Codex, canonical-trace comparison agent.
Status: elementary proof; not independently audited. No novelty claim.

This note concerns the **translated tangent map to an abelian variety**,
not the projective Gauss map sending a projective curve to its tangent
lines. It proves no restricted-Raynaud-theta statement.

## Statement

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic different from 2. Let
X, Y, Z be smooth proper connected curves, with g(X), g(Y) at least 2,
and let f: Z -> X and g: Z -> Y be finite etale. Suppose the actual
correspondence is minimal:

\[
                       k(Z)=k(X)k(Y)\quad\hbox{inside }k(Z).
\]

After choosing Abel-Jacobi base points, consider

\[
 a=(\operatorname{AJ}_X f,\operatorname{AJ}_Y g):Z\longrightarrow J_X\times J_Y.
\]

Its translated tangent map

\[
 \gamma_a:Z\longrightarrow
 \mathbf P\bigl(H^0(X,\omega_X)\oplus H^0(Y,\omega_Y)\bigr)^\vee
\]

is defined by the pullback differential system

\[
 W=f^*H^0(X,\omega_X)+g^*H^0(Y,\omega_Y)\subset H^0(Z,\omega_Z).
\]

The displayed target can contain a proper linear subspace if these
pullback spaces have linear relations; no independence is assumed.

1. The map gamma_a is everywhere defined, nonconstant, and separable.
2. If at least one of X and Y is nonhyperelliptic, gamma_a is birational
   onto its image.
3. If both are hyperelliptic, gamma_a has degree at most 2. The degree-2
   case occurs precisely when there is an involution sigma of Z inducing
   the hyperelliptic involution on both actual targets:

   \[
        f\sigma=\iota_Xf,\qquad g\sigma=\iota_Yg.
   \]

No assumption on Hom(J_X,J_Y) is required. In particular, the result
applies to any proposed minimal correspondence having the nonhyperelliptic
trigonal genus-nine curve as one target, without modifying either leg.

## Proof

The canonical bundle of a curve of genus at least 2 is globally generated.
Etaleness identifies f^*omega_X and g^*omega_Y with omega_Z. Thus either
block generates omega_Z pointwise. The tangent map is everywhere defined,
and projection to the X block is the composite of f with the canonical
map of X. That canonical map is separable in characteristic different
from 2: an embedding in the nonhyperelliptic case, and a separable double
cover followed by a rational normal embedding in the hyperelliptic case.
Therefore gamma_a is nonconstant and separable. This part needs only one
etale leg and does not use minimality.

Let K_gamma be the function field of its image, viewed as a subfield of
k(Z). It is generated by ratios of the nonzero sections in W.

Suppose first that X is nonhyperelliptic. Ratios within its canonical
block recover k(X), so k(X) is contained in K_gamma. If Y is also
nonhyperelliptic, the other block recovers k(Y), and minimality gives
K_gamma=k(Z).

Otherwise write

\[
 k(Y)=k(t,y),\qquad y^2=F(t),\qquad
 H^0(Y,\omega_Y)=\left\langle t^j\frac{dt}{y}:0\le j<g(Y)\right\rangle.
\]

The ratios in the Y block recover t. Put K_0=k(X)(t), so
K_0 is contained in K_gamma and minimality gives k(Z)=K_0(y).
Choose any nonzero regular differential omega on X. Since K_0/k(X)
is finite separable, the ratio

\[
                         b=\frac{dt}{f^*\omega}
\]

belongs to K_0. It is nonzero: t is a separating function on Y and
k(Z)/k(Y) is separable. The cross-block ratio

\[
             \frac{g^*(dt/y)}{f^*\omega}=\frac{b}{y}
\]

lies in K_gamma and recovers y. Hence K_gamma=k(Z). The case with Y
nonhyperelliptic is symmetric.

Finally suppose both targets are hyperelliptic, and write their function
fields as k(t,u), u^2=F(t), and k(s,v), v^2=G(s). Set K_0=k(t,s).
Minimality gives k(Z)=K_0(u,v). The function t is separating on Z, hence
also on K_0; consequently c=ds/dt belongs to K_0 and is nonzero. The
canonical block ratios and their cross ratio show exactly that

\[
 K_\gamma=K_0(u/v),\qquad
 \frac{ds/v}{dt/u}=c\frac{u}{v}.
\]

Since u^2 belongs to K_0, the remaining extension has degree at most 2.
If its degree is 2, its unique nontrivial automorphism fixes t,s,u/v
and changes the signs of both u and v. The corresponding automorphism
of the smooth proper curve Z induces the two hyperelliptic involutions.
Conversely, such an automorphism acts by -1 on every section in W, fixes
the projective tangent map, and is nontrivial. It therefore forces degree
2. No claim that this involution is free is made. QED.

## Exact boundary for the theta question

The two-leg conclusion above is stronger than mere immersion or
separability of the relative tangent map. Neither assertion by itself
proves

\[
 \operatorname{im}(f^{(1)*}+g^{(1)*})\not\subset\Theta_{B_Z}.
\]

In particular, any one-leg finite etale cover of a hyperbolic curve has
a separable relative tangent map to the old Jacobian, whether or not its
restricted Raynaud theta locus is proper.

The literature check found no theorem converting the birationality proved
here into one-step Frobenius-generic injectivity. This is a report on a
bounded search, not a claim that no such theorem exists.

One near-match that does **not** supply the missing step is G. Pareschi,
*Gaussian maps and generic vanishing I: subvarieties of abelian varieties*,
[Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.7442). It proves ordinary generic
vanishing for an embedded normal Cohen-Macaulay subvariety in arbitrary
characteristic. For a curve its direct-image assertion concerns degree
zero, not the kernel of Frobenius on degree-one cohomology. Moreover,
normalization birational onto a possibly singular image is not its
embedding hypothesis. The paper's Gaussian/Wahl maps must not be confused
with the translated tangent map defined above.
