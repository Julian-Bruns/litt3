# Minimal hyperbolic bi-étale correspondences remain minimal under specialization

**Status:** author proof, 2026-09-05.
**Author/checker:** canonical_trace_algebra.
**Scope:** an already existing smooth proper bi-étale diagram over a complete
DVR. This is not a theorem asserting that arbitrary special diagrams lift.
The hyperbolic hypothesis is essential; §5 gives a genus-one counterexample
to the unrestricted wording.

This adds specialization and distinctness statements not contained in
[Minimal common covers and rational incidence descent](MINIMAL_COMMON_COVER_AND_INCIDENCE_DESCENT.md).
Here *minimal* means minimal for the **specified two maps**: the source
function field is generated by their two target subfields. It does not
mean least degree among all possible correspondences between the targets.

## 1. Statement

Let \(R\) be a complete DVR, with fraction field \(K\), residue field \(k\),
and maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\). Let \(X,Y,W\) be smooth proper
\(R\)-curves with geometrically connected fibers, equipped with finite étale
maps
\[
                        f:W\to X,\qquad g:W\to Y.
\]
Assume the target fibers have genus at least two. Degrees may be divisible
by the residue characteristic, and neither map need be Galois.

### Theorem 1: common factors lift

Suppose on the special fiber both maps factor as
\[
 W_s\xrightarrow{q_s}Z_s\xrightarrow{u_s}X_s,\qquad
 W_s\xrightarrow{q_s}Z_s\xrightarrow{v_s}Y_s,
 \tag{1}
\]
where all three maps are finite étale and \(Z_s\) is smooth proper
connected. Then (1) lifts to a factorization of the given \(R\)-diagram
\[
 W\xrightarrow{q}Z\xrightarrow{u}X,\qquad
 W\xrightarrow{q}Z\xrightarrow{v}Y
 \tag{2}
\]
by finite étale maps. It is unique up to the unique isomorphism preserving
the given special-fiber marking and factorization. In particular
\(\deg q=\deg q_s\).

### Corollary 2: minimality specializes

If the generic bi-étale diagram is minimal, the special diagram is minimal.
The same assertion holds geometrically, after the relevant extension of
constants. Consequently the special source is still the normalization of
the reduced image in \(X_s\times Y_s\).

The proof below actually needs genus at least two only for the target
used for the second-leg rigidity argument. In the present bi-étale
hyperbolic setting both targets automatically satisfy this condition.

## 2. The special minimal factor is indeed étale

Let \(L=k(W_s)\), and let
\[
                    M=f_s^*k(X_s)\,g_s^*k(Y_s)\subset L.
\]
Normalize \(X_s\) in \(M\), obtaining \(Z_s\); the other field inclusion
gives its finite map to \(Y_s\). A subextension of a finite étale curve
cover is again étale. For example, take a finite étale Galois closure
and apply the subgroup correspondence. Thus all three maps in (1) are
finite étale, including \(q_s\). They are separable even in degree
divisible by the characteristic. This is the same elementary
normalization mechanism used in Proposition D.1 of the earlier note.

If the special diagram is not minimal, \([L:M]>1\). Theorem 1 then
produces a generic common factor of that same degree, contradicting
generic minimality. This proves Corollary 2 once Theorem 1 is established.

## 3. Actual algebraization and coherent second-leg descent

### Step 1: lift the first-leg factor as an actual finite étale cover

For a proper scheme over a henselian local ring, restriction induces
an equivalence
\[
                 \operatorname{FÉt}(X)\simeq
                 \operatorname{FÉt}(X_s).
 \tag{3}
\]
This includes full faithfulness on morphisms, not only existence of
objects. See [Stacks Project, Lemma 58.9.1, tag 0BQC](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BQC).
In the complete Noetherian case its proof first lifts through all
nilpotent thickenings and then algebraizes the finite algebra by
Grothendieck existence. Thus (3) supplies precisely the formal
existence and algebraization needed here.

Apply (3) to \(Z_s\to X_s\), giving \(u:Z\to X\). Full faithfulness
lifts \(q_s:W_s\to Z_s\), over \(X_s\), to \(q:W\to Z\), over \(X\).
A morphism between finite étale \(X\)-schemes is finite étale; this
is evident after an étale trivialization. The map \(q\) is surjective
of degree \(\deg q_s\), since its rank is locally constant and \(Z\)
is connected. The curve \(Z\) is smooth proper over \(R\).

### Step 2: rigidity of the two maps on the actual equivalence relation

Set
\[
                      E=W\times_Z W.
\]
This is smooth proper over \(R\), possibly disconnected, and both
projections \(p_i:E\to W\) are finite étale. The two actual maps
\[
                         a=g p_1,\qquad b=g p_2
\]
to \(Y\) agree on \(E_s\), by (1).

We prove that they agree modulo every power of \(\mathfrak m\).
Suppose equality holds modulo \(\mathfrak m^n\). The difference
between their two lifts modulo \(\mathfrak m^{n+1}\) is a derivation,
hence an element of
\[
 H^0(E_s,a_s^*T_{Y_s})\otimes_k
                         (\mathfrak m^n/\mathfrak m^{n+1}).
 \tag{4}
\]
On every connected component of \(E_s\), the map \(a_s\) is finite
étale onto \(Y_s\). The line bundle \(a_s^*T_{Y_s}\) has negative
degree, so its \(H^0\) is zero. The difference in (4) therefore
vanishes. Induction gives equality on all infinitesimal thickenings.
No trace denominator and no restriction on the degrees enter this
argument.

For completeness, formal equality implies actual equality here.
Since \(Y/R\) is separated, the equalizer of \(a,b\) is closed in
\(E\), with coherent ideal \(I\). Equality modulo all
\(\mathfrak m^n\) puts \(I\) in \(\bigcap_n\mathfrak m^n\mathcal O_E\)
at every point of \(E_s\). Krull intersection gives \(I=0\) at those
points. Its support is closed and proper over \(R\); a nonempty
proper closed subset cannot have image contained only in the generic
point of \(\operatorname{Spec}R\). Thus \(I=0\) everywhere and \(a=b\).

### Step 3: descend the second map

The finite étale surjection \(q\) is an fpqc cover. The equality
\(g p_1=g p_2\) is exactly the descent condition for a morphism to
the scheme \(Y\). It gives a unique \(v:Z\to Y\) with \(g=vq\).

The map \(v\) is étale because this may be checked after the
surjective étale source cover \(q\). It is proper, since \(Z/R\)
is proper and \(Y/R\) separated, and quasi-finite, since \(g\) is
finite and \(q\) surjective. Therefore it is finite étale. This
proves Theorem 1.

### Relation to file 116 and coherence at every order

One may equivalently apply
[file 116](116_ETALE_REFINEMENT_PRESERVES_SIMULTANEOUS_DEFORMATIONS.md)
at \(R/\mathfrak m^n\): lift \(Z_s/X_s\) and \(Z_s/Y_s\)
separately. Both induced marked \(W_s\)-deformations are the supplied
one. Lemma 116.3 identifies the two \(Z\)-deformations uniquely.
Uniqueness makes these identifications compatible under every
transition \(R/\mathfrak m^{n+1}\to R/\mathfrak m^n\).
The finite-étale equivalence (3) algebraizes the first leg and its
maps; the rigidity/descent argument above algebraizes the compatible
second leg. Thus there is no unspecified choice of incoherent
isomorphisms in the passage from Artinian levels to \(R\).

The direct proof also avoids needing a chosen presentation of \(R\)
as a coefficient-ring algebra. It uses only the square-zero
difference calculation (4).

## 4. Distinct reduced correspondences cannot collapse

### Proposition 3

Fix the target models \(X,Y\) above. Specialization is injective on
isomorphism classes of actual smooth proper bi-étale diagrams over \(R\).

Indeed, suppose \(W,W'\) have a special-fiber isomorphism
\(\psi_s:W_s\to W'_s\) commuting with both target maps.
Full faithfulness in (3) lifts it uniquely to an isomorphism
\(\psi:W\to W'\) over \(X\). The two maps \(g\) and \(g'\psi\)
to \(Y\) agree specially. Apply the argument of Step 2 with source
\(W\): \(H^0(W_s,g_s^*T_{Y_s})=0\), so they agree over \(R\).

Consequently pairwise distinct generic reduced correspondences, each
minimal and each possessing such a smooth proper bi-étale model
with these target models, have pairwise distinct reduced special
images. If two special reduced images agreed, minimality from
Corollary 2 would identify their normalizations as special diagrams;
Proposition 3 would then identify the generic diagrams.

Pairwise comparisons may be made after a common finite extension
of the DVR when individual models require different finite extensions.
This assertion does not supply good reduction or simultaneous lifts
for a proposed Hecke family: those hypotheses must still be proved.
It also does not assert that a normalization of an arbitrary relative
product image is smooth.

## 5. The unrestricted genus-one assertion is false

Here is an actual mixed-characteristic counterexample in residue
characteristic five, not just a deformation-space analogy.

Let \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5\), and over \(R_0=W(k)\) take
\[
                         E:\ y^2=x^3-x.
\]
Its discriminant is \(64\), a unit. Choose \(i\in R_0\) with \(i^2=-1\) and
\(i\bmod5=2\). The automorphism
\(\iota(x,y)=(-x,iy)\) acts on the tangent line by \(i\).
The dual degree-five isogenies
\[
                         [2]+\iota,\qquad [2]-\iota
\]
compose to \([5]\). Their being dual follows from
\(\iota^\vee=\iota^{-1}=-\iota\); their equal degrees multiply to
\(\deg[5]=25\), so each degree is five. The first has invertible tangent map \(2+i\)
and hence étale kernel of order five. Its dual kernel is
multiplicative. Since \(R_0\) is strictly henselian, and the sum of
the two isogenies is \([4]\), which is invertible on \(E[5]\), this gives
\[
                         E[5]\simeq\mu_5\times(\mathbb Z/5)_{R_0}.
 \tag{5}
\]
More explicitly, the étale kernel is constant and its Cartier dual
is \(\mu_5\); the two kernels split \(E[5]\).

Pass to the complete DVR \(R=R_0[\zeta_5]\), where \(\zeta_5\) is
a primitive fifth root of unity. Inside (5), take the two closed
étale subgroup schemes
\[
 K_0=\{(1,j):j\in\mathbb Z/5\},\qquad
 K_1=\{(\zeta_5^j,j):j\in\mathbb Z/5\}.
\]
Both are isomorphic to the constant group of order five. The quotient
elliptic schemes \(X=E/K_0\), \(Y=E/K_1\) give finite étale maps
\[
                            E\longrightarrow X,\qquad E\longrightarrow Y.
\]
On the generic fiber \(K_0\cap K_1=1\). The translations by all of
\(E[5](K)\) form a Galois group \((\mathbb Z/5)^2\) on its function
field, so the compositum of the two fixed fields is the whole field.
The generic correspondence is minimal.

On the special fiber \(\zeta_5=1\), so \(K_{0,s}=K_{1,s}\).
Both maps factor through the same étale quotient of degree five.
The special correspondence is not minimal.

The obstruction to the hyperbolic proof is visible exactly in (4):
an elliptic target has a trivial tangent bundle and nonzero global
tangent sections. Thus the unrestricted smooth-proper assertion is
false, while the hyperbolic specialization theorem needed for the
intended correspondence application is valid.
