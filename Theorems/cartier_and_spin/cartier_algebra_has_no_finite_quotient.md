# The actual Cartier algebra generates no nontrivial finite coefficient

Version3, 1 October2026. Let $k$ be algebraically closed of
characteristic $p\ge5$, and retain an actual no-clump finite étale span
$X\leftarrow S\to Y$ of smooth proper connected hyperbolic curves.
Suppose $p\nmid g(C)-1$ at least at one endpoint. Let $\mathcal C_1$
be the full neutral tensor subcategory of common vector bundles
generated by the actual common algebra $F_*\mathcal O$, including
duals and all subquotients, with its specified source comparisons.
The monodromy group is the actual Tannaka image at a source point,
not a group of deck transformations of the source.

The [full first-Cartier monodromy theorem](first_cartier_full_monodromy.md)
identifies this group as either
\[
\mathcal G_p=\operatorname{Aut}_{k\text{-alg}}(k[t]/t^p)
\]
or, in the common dormant-projective-oper branch,
\[
\mathcal N_p=F_{\mathrm{PGL}_2}^{-1}(B^{(1)}),
\]
where $B$ is the projective Borel fixing zero. In both cases every
homomorphism from the actual monodromy group to any finite group
scheme over $k$ is trivial. Consequently every object of
$\mathcal C_1$ with finite COMMON monodromy is a direct sum of the
unit object. In particular tensor powers, duals and subquotients of
the common Cartier algebra cannot produce a nontrivial common
essentially finite coefficient.

In the no-common-projective-connection branch this holds at every
height and for the tensor category generated by the entire actual
Frobenius algebra tower. Each finite-rank object in that category
belongs to a category generated at one finite height. No analogous
all-height identification in the common-oper branch is assumed.

Assume additionally $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_p$. Every COMMON strongly
semistable degree-zero object in these generated categories is trivial.
More generally every common strongly semistable object in such a
category is a direct sum of copies of one common character line.
This is a restriction inside the generated category; it does not
classify arbitrary common strongly semistable bundles.
In the full-coordinate branch the character lines are integral
powers of the canonical line, with the scalar Frobenius twists
retained. This identification comes from the actual coordinate-jet
transitions, not merely from the determinant of the Cartier bundle.

## Independence of arbitrary common finite coefficients

Let $V$ be any independently supplied common finite-monodromy
coefficient, and let $Q$ be its actual finite monodromy image. In the
full common tensor category jointly generated by $V$ and the Cartier
algebra, the joint image in $H\times Q$ is the entire product, where
$H$ is the actual Cartier monodromy above. The finite group scheme
$Q$ need not be reduced, commutative, or of order prime to $p$.
Consequently, for Cartier-generated objects $E,F$,
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm common}(V\otimes E,F)
\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_Q(V,k)\otimes
\operatorname{Hom}_H(E,F).
\]
Thus finite coefficients contribute only their trivial quotient to
these mixed morphisms. A nontrivial simple finite coefficient has
no such mixed morphisms; a nonsimple coefficient is not presumed
to have zero trivial quotient.

On the compatible finite $Q$-torsor refinement corresponding to
restriction to the kernel of this common finite representation,
the actual Cartier image remains all of $H$. In particular this
restriction preserves the full-coordinate or common-oper branch.
This assertion requires that exact common restriction. It does not
apply automatically to an arbitrary one-leg cover, alternating path,
or selected source component after discarding other comparison edges.

## Algebraic input and scope

The algebraic input is more general. For any $q=p^r\ge2$, the full
coordinate group
\[
\mathcal G_q=\operatorname{Spec}
k[a_0,a_1,a_1^{-1},a_2,\ldots,a_{q-1}]/(a_0^q)
\]
has no nontrivial finite group-scheme quotient. Its reduced subgroup
$J_q$ of coordinate jets fixing zero is connected, and its normal
closure in $\mathcal G_q$ is the entire group. The same normal-closure
statement holds for the reduced Borel in $\mathcal N_p$.

This distinguishes the finite Frobenius KERNEL subgroup from a finite
quotient of common monodromy. A finite kernel torsor lies over the
positive coordinate-jet torsor and is not thereby a common finite
torsor on the endpoint curves. The result makes no assertion that
every common finite coefficient lies in this generated category;
other objects of the full common category may exist. It supplies no
admissible line, canonical-power section, or common-cover exclusion.
