# A sixfold degree-five norm cannot use all three centered points

Version1, 3 October2026. [Fresh independent four-check review PASS](../../Research/audits/OCT03_CENTERED_SIXFOLD_FULL_SUPPORT_NORM_HAND_AUDIT_2026_10_03.md) for the repaired statement. The original missing cube term and its author correction are recorded. No computation is used.

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic five, let p(U) be monic of degree ten, with coefficients p_i and p9,p7 nonzero. If a polynomial G of degree at most four and a nonzero c satisfy
\[
p(U)+G(U)^3=cU^{12},
\]
then necessarily
\[
p_6+2p_9p_7+4p_8p_7/p_9=0.
\]
For the fixed BACKUP centered p(U)=P(U−1), this expression equals d0+2≠0. Hence the displayed polynomial identity is impossible.

On the fixed X:y³=p(U), let T0,T1,T2 be the three ordinary points with U0, and O its unique infinity. There is NO effective divisor E of degree five, supported on these three points and containing each of them, with
\[
6E\sim30O.
\]
Thus any such sixfold centered norm, if it exists with partial support, must omit at least one centered point. The missing-point alternatives are not excluded.

This is a fixed-curve arithmetic obstruction, not an existence decision for a common cover. Its coefficient criterion also applies to other characteristic-five monic degree-ten endpoint polynomials.
