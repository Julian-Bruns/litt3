# A corrected source energy has only a simple cubic-endpoint pole

Version2,30 September2026. Let R=k[[r]], with k algebraically closed
of characteristic five. Suppose
\[
F=(W^5+q)H(W)+\tau
\]
has H in R[W] with unit leading coefficient, all other coefficients
in R, and distinct roots in the fraction field which in fact all
belong to R. There is no degree restriction on H. Let gamma be its
W^3 coefficient, or equivalently for the pole bound the W^3 coefficient
of H mod(W^5+q). Assume
\[
q=q_3r^3+O(r^4),\qquad \tau=\tau_3r^3+O(r^4),
\qquad q_3\tau_3\ne0.
\]
Write phi=W^5+q. The following quadratic differential has at most a
simple pole at r=0:
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal Q}_F=
\sum_{F(w)=0}\frac{dw\,dw}{\phi(w)}
-\frac{dq\,d\gamma}{\tau}
-\frac{\gamma\,dq\,d\tau}{\tau^2}.
\]
In particular r times this differential is regular. The assertion
does not require a primitive critical polynomial or any restriction
on its discriminant, its vertical content, or the mutual collisions
of the five small roots. A constant source translation allows q(0)
to be removed first whenever q-q(0) has exactly order three.

For the degree-ten subfamily
H=L(W^5+q)+gamma W^3+bW^2+cW+e, the same corrected expression transforms
by a^{-3} under W'=aW+d, with the matching transformed q,S,tau.
That covariance assertion has this narrower scope. The local pole
bound holds in every degree, including a nonzero quartic coefficient
in H. It is not a global vanishing or a whole-family exclusion.
