# Divide by the critical discriminant coordinate, then take the trace

Use the accepted local source charts and pole profiles from the

## The local quadratic order and its dual trace

At a collision of the critical roots choose R=k[[r]]. In an integral
critical-coordinate chart its normalization has quadratic function
algebra generated by eta with eta^2=d in R. Write m=ord_r(d)>0.
This algebra may split; the trace and sum of residues below include
both branches when it does. A unit change of eta has no effect on
the regularity arguments.

Suppose Lambda is finite at the collision. There are a,kappa in R with
\[
\Lambda=a+\kappa d\eta,\qquad
\delta_0\Lambda=A+B\eta,
\quad A=\delta_0a,\quad
B=d\delta_0\kappa+\tfrac32\kappa\delta_0d.
\]
Here is why the extra factor d occurs. The derivative of Lambda with
respect to the integral critical coordinate vanishes modulo its
quadratic equation. Divide its regular local expression by that monic
quadratic. If its remainder is a+b*eta, differentiating the division
identity modulo eta^2-d gives b+2eta*H=0. Writing H=h0+h1*eta gives
h0=0 and b=-2d*h1. This applies to convergent formal expressions by
monic division. It also applies in the reciprocal W chart: the partial
derivative there is still divisible by the critical quadratic.
At a finite nonzero critical value the unit-phi cubic term shows that
kappa is a unit. Equivalently the first nonconstant odd term of the
critical value at a double critical point is cubic, with unit
coefficient. The accepted unit-phi chart includes all such points.

For every J=j0+j1*eta in the quadratic order one has
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{k(C)/k(X)}(J/\eta)=2j_1\in R.
\]
Thus eta^-1 generates the trace-dual order even when it is not integral
on its normalization. This elementary identity is the cancellation
used below; individual summands are not discarded.

## The divided trace has no finite poles

Away from eta=0, regularity follows from the actual derivative trace
charts. At a collision over a finite value lambda0, the coefficient
of (ell-lambda0)^(-j), for j>=1, in U_f is a sum of residues of
\[
f\frac{(\delta_0\Lambda)^2}{\eta}
             (\Lambda-\lambda_0)^{j-1}\omega_0.
\]
The numerator preceding eta^-1 belongs to R[eta]. Its quadratic trace
is in R by the displayed identity. Since omega0 is a regular unit on
affine X, the sum of those residues is zero. This covers both odd and
even discriminant multiplicities. The reciprocal critical-coordinate
chart gives the same argument at a collision over scale zero.
Hence U_f is regular on the affine ell line and is a polynomial.

We use compatibility of residues with the field trace, also in wild
characteristic. This is the ordinary residue-trace identity for smooth
curves; see John Tate,
[Residues of differentials on curves](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/asens.1162/).
In particular, the constant coefficient at a regular value lambda0 is
the sum of residues of g*dLambda/(Lambda-lambda0), for g the traced
function. Principal parts use the corresponding positive powers of
Lambda-lambda0 as above.

## Actual splitting forces the constant coefficient to vanish

The [source-root residue lemma](actual_split_critical_residue_integrality.md)
now gives a shorter uniform proof of this paragraph's conclusion,
without the following discriminant-order cases. The explicit cases
are retained because they also give the useful collision bound m>=2.

If eta is a unit at a point of an actual nonzero fibre, then
delta0Lambda vanishes by the actual splitting theorem. The traced
function is regular and zero there, and its local trace has zero
constant coefficient.

At a collision the actual polynomial has exactly three roots with the
same residue. To see this, its W derivative is phi*S'(W), with phi a
unit and S' having a double zero, so its cubic Taylor coefficient is
a unit. Over R these three roots are in R, because the source is
etale. Translate their common residue to zero. Their monic cubic
factor has constant, linear and quadratic coefficients in r^3 R,
r^2 R and r R, respectively. The remaining factor is a unit. Its
derivative therefore has constant and linear coefficients in r^2 R
and r R. Dividing by the unit phi preserves these inequalities.
The critical quadratic consequently has discriminant in r^2 R:
\[
m\ge2.
\]
This also gives a short direct proof of the simple-collision exclusion.

Replace a by a-lambda0 and put v=ord_r(a)>0, allowing v=infinity.
The quadratic trace of the differential whose residue computes the
local constant term is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{k(C)/k(X)}
 \left(f\frac{(\delta_0\Lambda)^2}
                 {\eta(\Lambda-\lambda_0)}\right)\omega_0
=2f\,
 \frac{2ABa-\kappa d A^2-\kappa d^2B^2}
      {a^2-\kappa^2d^3}\,\omega_0.
\]
Since omega0 is a unit, delta0 preserves the usual derivative
valuation bound. Thus ord(A)>=v-1 and ord(B)>=m-1.
If 2v!=3m, the denominator has order min(2v,3m), while the numerator
has order at least min(2v+m-2,4m-2). Their quotient has order at
least m-2>=0. Its residue is zero.

If 2v=3m, then m is even and the quadratic algebra splits over R.
On either branch eta has order m/2 and Lambda-lambda0 has order
at least 3m/2. Consequently delta0Lambda/eta has order at least
m-1>=1 on each branch. It is regular and zero there, so each local
trace has zero constant coefficient. This also covers arbitrary
additional cancellation in either critical value, including wild
ramification. Separability excludes an identically constant branch.

Summing proves U_f(lambda0)=0. The proof does not assume that
delta0Lambda/eta is regular in the unequal-order case.

## Pole degree at infinity of the scale line

At the two points over O, eta has pole order16, while delta0Lambda
has pole orders21 and24. Hence the divided function has pole orders
5 and8. Multiplication by f adds at most p. The pole orders of Lambda
there are4 and7, yielding the two floor bounds in the statement.

Every affine pole contributes scale degree at most two. For a type-A
endpoint eta is a unit and the earlier bound is unchanged. At type B
the critical discriminant is simple (the type-B chi coefficient is
a unit), so eta has order1. Lambda has pole5, and the pole of the
divided function is at most8; its local trace has degree at most one.

For an ordinary phi zero with eta a unit, the earlier local bound is
again sufficient. If eta also vanishes, use the quadratic trace
instead of an invalid bound on its individual branches. Set
J=phi*S+t^3. Here t and J are units. Coefficient differentiation gives
delta0Lambda=G/phi^3 with G in the local quadratic order. For n>=3,
\[
\frac{(\delta_0\Lambda)^2}{\eta\Lambda^{n+1}}
=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}G^2\phi^{2n-4}}{\eta J^{n+1}}.
\]
The numerator and the reciprocal of J are integral in the quadratic
order. Its trace divided by eta is regular, so the residue computing
the coefficient of ell^n vanishes. All affine pole types are retained.
This proves the uniform degree bound.
