# Sparse-map exclusions and a uniform lower-component restriction

Version2, 25 September2026. Let R_xi be the actual nineteen-coordinate
extension0->L->R_xi->K->0, L=O_X(-O). Let rho:F_abs^{r*}R_xi->K
be a nonzero map, and let f be its restriction from F^r L followed by
K->O(6O). Then for every r>=2,
\[
f=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\dim\operatorname{Hom}(F^{r*}R_\xi,L)\ge2.
\]
This follows from the already proved positive line-bundle presentation
of F^2 K, without a new matrix certificate, and needs no stability.

At r=2, use the fixed35-coordinate lower map (f,alpha) in
L_X(31O) direct-sum L_X(20O). If Hom(F^2 R_xi,L)=0, every nonzero map
to K has at least five nonzero lower-map coordinates. This holds over
the algebraic closure for all source parameters. It follows from exact
homogeneous ideal identities on all52360 four-coordinate subspaces.

More strongly in another direction, if f is a nonzero scalar multiple
of ANY one of its23 monomial basis elements, then xi belongs to the
pure-v P5, even when all twelve alpha coordinates are arbitrary. Thus
no requested stable quotient-window point has monomial f. This local
extension treats entire geometric families, not only rational points.
Together these statements require at least two monomials in f and at
least five coordinates in (f,alpha) for a possible window witness.

The earlier source-containment theorem for all595 two-coordinate
pencils remains valid, including directions with f=0; its independent
certificate is retained. None of these results excludes dense maps in
the full P18 family or settles a stable second return.
