# Fixed denominators determine the high-genus character completion

Version1,26September2026. Retain the actual V4 setup and
[coupled pole identities](klein_four_coupled_pole_derivatives.md).
Fix E, the triple-pole partition, and the three nonzero polynomials T_i.
Put u=29-e, D=sum d_i=27+2j-g and H=15+D-u.
Let V be the vector space of differences between solutions of the
linear conditions on f_i, with these fixed T_i: the prescribed C_i
zeros, unused-node values and coupled single/double-pole conditions.

If g>57-2u-2j2, the generic k(t)-span of V has rank at most one.
If in addition
\[
2c_i+u>15+d_i\qquad(i=1,2,3),
\]
then either V=0 or every nonzero member has three nonzero coordinates,
and
\[
\dim_k V\le H-\lceil2j/3\rceil+1.
\]
More precisely V consists of bounded polynomial multiples of one vector.
If j>H, its projective direction after dividing coordinate i by T_i
is defined over M=F_(25^7). This is a field statement about the
homogeneous direction, not about the hypothetical curve or its T_i.

Under these hypotheses, if
\[
H\le\lceil2j/3\rceil+3,
\]
the forced first jets at both parameter endpoints determine at most one
completion (f_1,f_2,f_3) for the fixed T_i. The zero-leading endpoint
cases use their actual forced double vanishings and are included.

All109 surviving character allocations in the eight degree87 numerical
profiles meet these conditions. Hence the actual degree87 character
completion, once its denominators and endpoint labels are fixed, is
unique. The same conclusion applies to580 of the729 retained degree86
allocations. These counts concern necessary numerical profiles only.
No denominator choice or actual curve is constructed or excluded here.
The original unmarked common-cover problems remain unresolved.
