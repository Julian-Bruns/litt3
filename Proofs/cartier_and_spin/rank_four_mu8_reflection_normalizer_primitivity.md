# Proof: the four diagonal reflections would make the normalizer monomial

Version1,3 October2026. Pending independent review; see the [statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/rank_four_mu8_reflection_normalizer_primitivity.md).

Suppose K imprimitive. Since it is irreducible, an imprimitivity system in dimensionFOUR consists either of TWO blocks of dimensionTWO or FOUR blocks of dimensionONE. A rank-ONE involutive reflection cannot interchange TWO-dimensional blocks: such an interchange has a minus-ONE eigenspace of dimension at leastTWO. Thus in the first possibility every generating reflection preserves each block, making K reducible. Only a monomial system of FOUR lines remains.

In that basis, every generating reflection has one of two forms: a diagonal matrix with exactly ONE entry−ONE, or a phased transposition exchanging two coordinates by entries a,a⁻¹ and fixing the other two. The graph of transpositions appearing among the generators is connected, or K would preserve the sum of the coordinates in a connected component. Its permutation image is consequently S4.

The product of the FOUR nonzero monomial entries is a homomorphism on the monomial group. It equalsONE on every phased-transposition reflection. If no diagonal reflection occurred, it would beONE on K; a scalar λI in K would satisfy λ⁴=ONE. This contradicts the given primitive eighth root in its center. Therefore a diagonal reflection occurs, and conjugation by the transitive permutation image yields all FOUR diagonal reflections.

Normalize phases along a spanning tree of the transposition graph by scaling the FOUR basis vectors. The tree generators become plain transpositions and generate the plain S4 permutation matrices. Any remaining phased transposition, multiplied by its plain transposition, gives a paired diagonal matrix with entries a,a⁻¹. Let μ_m be the finite cyclic subgroup generated by all these phases and−ONE; it has even order m prime toFIVE. Conjugating paired diagonals by plain permutations yields ALL determinant-ONE diagonals with entries in μ_m. Together with the FOUR diagonal reflections, the entire diagonal kernel is
\[
D=\{\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_1,\ldots,\zeta_4):\zeta_i\in\mu_m,
\ \zeta_1\zeta_2\zeta_3\zeta_4\in\{1,-1\}\}.
\]
Thus K is exactly this D times the plain S4. A scalar in it has λ∈μ_m and λ⁴∈{ONE,−ONE}, so its scalar center has order gcd(m,EIGHT). The given center μ8 forces EIGHT|m.

Its involutive rank-ONE reflections are exactly the FOUR diagonal reflections and the6m phased transpositions. The diagonal reflections constitute one conjugacy class of sizeFOUR. The phased transpositions constitute one class of size6m: permutations move their coordinate pair, and conjugation by a paired diagonal using a THIRD coordinate changes the phase arbitrarily through μ_m. Their class size is at least48.

Every normalizer of K preserves rank-ONE reflections and their conjugacy class sizes. It must therefore preserve the unique FOUR-element class of diagonal reflections. Their minus-ONE eigenspaces are exactly the FOUR coordinate lines, so R is projectively monomial. Its permutation quotient lies in S4, of prime-to-FIVE order, and must be trivial. R is then projectively diagonal; a finite diagonal subgroup in characteristicFIVE has order prime toFIVE, and the hypothesis again makes it trivial.

A trivial projective normalizer cannot contain the nontrivial projective image of K, as required explicitly in the statement. This contradiction proves primitivity.
