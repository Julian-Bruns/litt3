# Norm coefficients recover exactly the incidence field

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: author proof; not independently audited.

Let k be algebraically closed in arbitrary characteristic. Let Gamma be
an integral joint image in X x Y with normalization C and finite etale
maps f:C->X, g:C->Y of degrees a,b. Both curves have genus at least two.
Put V=H^0(X,omega_X^3). Compare the divisor-family map

    h:Y -> Sym^b X,       y |-> Gamma_y,

with the projectivized degree-b norm polynomial map

    n:Y -> P(Sym^b(V^*)), y |-> [N_{Gamma,3}(-)(y)].

Here projective space parametrizes nonzero polynomials up to scalar.
The coefficients of the norm have no common zero: on any fiber the
polynomial is a product of nonzero evaluation linear forms. Gamma->Y
is finite flat, since its local rings are torsion-free over the smooth
base curve. At a collision its fiber divisor counts branches with their
intersection multiplicities, so projectivization still gives the product
of evaluation forms with the same multiplicities. Thus n is the Chow
product map applied to h. The differential trivializations affect only
the scalar discarded by projectivization.

## 1. Equality of fields, including inseparability

**Proposition.** The subfields of k(Y) generated by the projective norm
coefficient ratios and by the incidence map are equal. Consequently the
maps to their smooth normalized images agree. No strict degree range or
Hom hypothesis is needed.

**Proof.** Write L=k(Y), E for the incidence image field, and K for the
norm coefficient field. Since the norm projectivization is the product
of the embedded points of the divisor, K is contained in E. We give an
explicit inverse on the generic reduced divisor to prove E is contained
in K, without deducing separability from pointwise injectivity.

Choose a basis s_0,...,s_r of V such that s_0 does not vanish on any of
the b geometric generic fiber points x_i and t_i=s_1(x_i)/s_0(x_i) are
pairwise distinct. Such choices exist over the infinite field k: very
ampleness separates the points, and the finitely many excluded linear
conditions are proper. The generic points are distinct because C->Gamma
is birational and g is etale. Put u_{ji}=s_j(x_i)/s_0(x_i) for j>=2.

Divide the generic norm by N(s_0). This divides all polynomial
coefficients by a nonzero coefficient, so the resulting polynomial has
coefficients in K. In it make the substitution

    s = z s_0 - s_1 + sum_{j>=2} w_j s_j.

It becomes

    F(z,w) = product_i (z-t_i+sum_{j>=2} w_j u_{ji}).

Thus the following polynomials belong to K[z]:

    P(z)=F(z,0)=product_i(z-t_i),
    Q_j(z)=[w_j]F(z,w)=sum_i u_{ji} product_{l!=i}(z-t_l).

The bracket means the coefficient linear in w_j and independent of the
other w variables. It requires no division by an integer. The
discriminant of P is nonzero, so in the etale K-algebra R=K[z]/(P) the
element P'(z) is invertible. The assignments

    s_1/s_0 -> z,       s_j/s_0 -> Q_j(z)/P'(z)

define a closed immersion Spec R into the affine chart of the
tricanonical projective space: the first coordinate already generates R.
After extending to an algebraic closure of L, these coordinates are
exactly those of the points x_i, since Q_j(t_i)=u_{ji}P'(t_i).
All equations of X therefore vanish by faithful scalar extension.
This gives the generic degree-b reduced divisor itself over K.
Its symmetric-product point is K-rational and base changes to h at
the generic point of Y. Hence every incidence coordinate ratio belongs
to K, proving E=K. This excludes any purely inseparable defect. QED.

Combined with Theorem 106.1 in
[file 106](106_CANONICAL_CONNECTING_FIELD_EQUALS_INCIDENCE_FIELD.md),
the canonical connecting map in its strict range and this norm map
have the same normalized quotient. The norm gives a uniform input
weight and explicit nonlinear reconstruction, but no finer incidence
field. Factoring geometric polynomials alone would not prove this field
assertion; the inverse above uses the reduced generic fiber essentially.
For example, replacing a norm by its p-th power in characteristic p
replaces its coefficient field K by K^p. Recovery of integer multiplicity
from factorization does not justify ignoring that phenomenon.

## 2. Exact normline identities and their limit

Assume O(Gamma)=A box B, as follows from Hom(J(X),J(Y))=0. Then
deg A=b and deg B=a. For every line bundle H on X,

    Nm_g(f^*H) ~= B^(deg H).                         (1)

Indeed, write H=O_X(D) with D=sum_x m_x[x]. Norm of the pulled-back
Cartier divisor gives O_Y(g_*f^*D). For each x, the divisor g_*f^*[x]
is the intersection fiber Gamma_x on Y, including multiplicities at
collisions, and O_Y(Gamma_x)~=B. One may check the multiplicities in
the completed local rings: etaleness over X writes every branch as a
graph over X, and restricting the product of branch equations to x
counts each branch once. Multiplication over D proves (1). These are
line-bundle isomorphism classes; no preferred scalar is asserted.

Taking norms of the canonical differential isomorphism
f^*omega_X ~= g^*omega_Y gives, using (1) and its symmetric version,

    omega_Y^b ~= B^(2g(X)-2),
    omega_X^a ~= A^(2g(Y)-2).                        (2)

These identities hold on the normalization without requiring the
canonical unit to descend to the singular image. They are already
consequences of the actual etale maps, differential compatibility,
and split divisor class. They are not a new independent condition
on the full canonical-gluing setup of files 101 and 106.

For fixed candidate A and B, (2) is stronger than the numerical
Riemann--Hurwitz identity and can reject those candidates. If one
retains only a power M^n|Gamma~=O_Gamma, taking norms gives only the
n-th powers of (2); a possible n-torsion discrepancy cannot be erased
from that weakened premise. The full setup already supplies the
unpowered differential isomorphism on C.

If A and B remain free, (2) alone has no Picard-theoretic obstruction
once a(2g(X)-2)=b(2g(Y)-2). For example choose any B_0 of degree a;
omega_Y^b B_0^{-(2g(X)-2)} has degree zero and has a (2g(X)-2)-th root
in Pic^0(Y), since multiplication by any positive integer on an
abelian variety over algebraically closed k is surjective on k-points,
also in characteristic dividing that integer. Adjusting B_0 by this
root gives B; the argument for A is identical. For a fixed degree the
root choices form a finite torsor, possibly with nonreduced group
scheme structure. This proves solvability of the line-bundle equations
only, not existence of a section defining an integral Gamma or of
etale projections. Any additional obstruction must use that geometry.
