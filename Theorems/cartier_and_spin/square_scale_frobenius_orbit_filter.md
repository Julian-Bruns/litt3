# A Frobenius-orbit filter for even-divisor square scales

Version1,29 September2026. For the fixed X/F25, let K=F_(5^8).
Fix an actual source ratio in either the primitive constant degree140
family or the primitive root-nine degree140 family. Let k0/K be the
smallest finite field containing its coefficients; write d=[k0:K].
Use the actual coordinates h,w, not just their cubic-invariant ratios.

Let lambda be a geometric nonzero scale whose residual is a square.
Suppose also that the corresponding regular function e_lambda on X
has even divisor. Then either e_lambda is a square in bar(F5)(X), or
\[
19\mid d.
\]
Thus if the ratio field has degree prime to19, the stronger even-divisor
condition forces the curve-square alternative; a nontrivial unramified
quadratic twist is impossible. This applies in particular to any actual
etale candidate, whose discriminant has even divisor and whose square
class is represented by e_lambda.

More precisely, if the divisor-half class is nontrivial, its Frobenius
orbit length over k0 is
\[
t=171/\gcd(171,d).
\]
This t divides the scale orbit length, which is at most12. If19|d and
3 does not divide d, the scale orbit therefore has exactly nine points.
There can be at most one such nine-point orbit on that ratio fibre.

The divisibility19|d is also necessary for the field of the cubic-
invariant ratio coordinates: adjoining the chosen cube root w changes
the field degree by at most three. It cannot supply a factor19.

This is an arithmetic restriction on candidate coefficient fields,
not an exclusion over the algebraic closure. The curve-square
alternative and ratio fields of degree divisible by19 remain open.
