## 1. Lemma 71.1: signed pullback rigidity

For nonconstant maps f,g:R→X, with g(X)≥2,

\[
 f^*=g^*\Longrightarrow f=g,\qquad
 f^*=-g^*\Longrightarrow X\text{ hyperelliptic and }g=\iota_X f.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Dualize and apply Abel–Jacobi to P−P_0. In the plus case the two
Abel–Jacobi images differ by a fixed translation preserving the curve;
the induced automorphism acts trivially on its Jacobian. Faithfulness
of the curve automorphism action on its principally polarized Jacobian
makes the translation trivial. In the minus case,

\[
 f(P)+g(P)\sim f(P_0)+g(P_0).
\]

This degree-two family is nonconstant since f and g are nonconstant.
It is a moving g¹_2, so X is hyperelliptic; uniqueness of that pencil
gives the second assertion. No etaleness is needed for this lemma.

## 2. The scalar window and both capacity inequalities

Define SW_M to mean that every integral v∈End(J(X)) with
v^†=v and M±v Rosati-semipositive is an INTEGER scalar.
This is a finite lattice condition, not an assertion End(J(X))=Z:
the eigenvalue interval[−M,M] is bounded for the positive Rosati trace
form on the self-adjoint subspace.

For the coefficient involution put u=c_*δ^*c^*. Then u is integral,
self-adjoint, and M±u are semipositive, since, for
φ_±=(1±δ^*)c^*,

\[
                  \phi_\pm^\dagger\phi_\pm=2(M\pm u). \tag{2}
\]

Suppose we are in the quadratic-root case and u=λ is scalar.
For A=im h, q_*h=0 gives δ^*|_A=−1, and dim A=rs+1.
The two exact capacity inequalities (Lemma 71.2) are

\[
 \lambda\ne-M\Longrightarrow g(B)\ge s+1,\qquad
 \lambda\ne M\Longrightarrow g(B)\le(M-r-1)s-1.        \tag{3}
\]

Indeed a nonzero scalar norm in (2) gives φ_± image dimension s+1;
simplicity of J(X) is NOT required. The plus image lies in q^*J(B).
The minus image lies in Prym(C/B) and is orthogonal to A, because
c^*J(X)⊥A and δ^*A=−A. Thus that Prym contains orthogonal subvarieties
of dimensions s+1 and rs+1. Since g(C)=Ms+1, (3) follows.

Theorem 71.3: if M=r+2, X is nonhyperelliptic and J(X) satisfies
SW_(r+2), then the coefficient degree cannot be two. SW forces u=λ.
If λ differs from both endpoints, (3) gives s+1≤g(B)≤s−1.
If λ=M, (2) gives cδ=c by (1), forcing the odd M to be even.
If λ=−M, (1) makes X hyperelliptic. All cases contradict a hypothesis.

## 3. Birational coefficient bound and the exact endpoint scope

If the coefficient map is birational, its integral image has degree 2M
and normalization C. Generic projection to a plane and the plane
arithmetic-genus formula give (Lemma 71.4)

\[
 Ms+1\le(2M-1)(2M-2)/2 .
\]

Thus s>2M−3 excludes it. This plane bound works in every allowed
characteristic, including when the original high-dimensional model is

The corrected [signed-orbit short-degree sieve](81_FULL_ORBIT_INTERPOLATION_AND_CUBIC_SIGN_MONODROMY.md#corollary824)

Section 2 excludes e=2, and s>2(r+2)−3 excludes e=1.
This retains Corollary 71.5 using the audited short interval, NOT the
discarded unrestricted prime-M field-intersection inference.

Larger M remain open here. Existence of a suitable scalar window is an
additional arithmetic task, not a missing condition declared true.

[−2√q,2√q]. If it is nonscalar and M≥2√q, then SW_M FAILS.

## 5. The explicit audited quartic and its failed scalar window

A genuine example for the full-span theorem is the plane quartic X_0/F_5

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0={}&3x^4+2x^2y^2+xy^3+3x^3z+4x^2yz+3y^3z\\
     &+x^2z^2+2xyz^2+2y^2z^2+xz^3+2yz^3+3z^4 .
 \end{aligned}                                        \tag{5}
\]

The unchanged mathematical
[degree-19 Sage certificate](72_DEGREE19_REDESIGN_CERTIFICATE.sage)
checks geometric smoothness, the three exact point counts 7,31,145,
and the Honda counts (4). A smooth plane quartic is geometrically
integral by Bezout, has genus 3 and is nonhyperelliptic. The counts give

\[
 P(T)=T^6+T^5+3T^4+9T^3+15T^2+25T+125.                \tag{6}
\]

The certificate checks irreducibility and the EXACT ratio resultant

\[
 \operatorname{Res}_u(P(u),P(vu))
              =5^9(v-1)^6A(v)B(v)^2,
\]

where A,B are the explicitly stored degree 6 and 12 primitive irreducible
integer polynomials, each of leading coefficient 125. They are not
cyclotomic: neither is an associate of a monic integer polynomial.
Thus distinct Frobenius roots have no root-of-unity ratio, so every
positive power of Frobenius still has minimal polynomial of degree 6.
The Jacobian is simple over every finite constant extension, hence
absolutely simple (Proposition 72.5). The certificate retains all
coefficients and checks; they are not duplicated in this prose.

Nevertheless X_0 does NOT satisfy SW_19. Its integral self-adjoint
F+V_5 has eigenvalues in[−2√5,2√5]⊂[−19,19] and is nonscalar:
if F+5F^(-1)=λ, F would satisfy T²−λT+5, contradicting (6).
Thus the full-span theorem applies to this actual quartic, but scalar
compression cannot silently remove its lower-span quadratic cases.
This old genus-three/genus 35 example is not a replacement for the
active pair, and none of these endpoint results solves the common-cover
