## Theorem

Let \(k\) be algebraically closed and let
\[
\Gamma=(X\xleftarrow f C\xrightarrow g Y)
\]
be a connected joint-minimal finite bi-etale correspondence between
smooth projective curves of genus at least two. Put
\[
P=\Gamma^*\Gamma\in\mathcal E(X,X).
\]
For the generated subcategory below, regard the two endpoint roles as
formally labelled objects, even when the underlying curves are isomorphic
or equal. Thus composable words alternate \(\Gamma,\Gamma^*\); no assertion
about nonalternating self-composition after identifying the two labels is
made.

The following conditions are equivalent:

1. \(\Gamma\) has a core: the two embedded endpoint fields intersect in
   a function field of transcendence degree one.
2. The positive subcategory generated by \(\Gamma,\Gamma^*\), allowing
   selection of components and joint minimalization, has finitely many
   joint-minimal basis images in its four spaces of morphisms.
3. \(P\) is algebraic over \(\mathbb C\) in the actual cycle algebra:
   a nonzero polynomial \(q\) satisfies \(q(P)=0\).
4. Some determinant
   \[
   H_n=\det\bigl[\tau_X(P^{i+j})\bigr]_{0\le i,j\le n}
   \tag{1}
   \]
   is zero.

If \(\Gamma\) is coreless, every \(H_n\) is a strictly positive integer.
Thus the algebra generated by this one self-adjoint correspondence is
isomorphic to \(\mathbb C[t]\) in the coreless case.

No assertion is made about algebraicity after passing to numerical
equivalence, rational equivalence, Jacobians, or cohomology.

## 1. A core bounds every generated image

Let \(B\) be the smooth projective curve of the intersection field.
There are finite maps \(X\to B\), \(Y\to B\), commuting on \(C\).
They need not be etale; only the two original legs and their compositions
are asserted etale.

Every alternating word in \(\Gamma,\Gamma^*\) has equal images in \(B\)
at its two endpoints. Every resulting joint-minimal image is therefore
an irreducible component of one of
\[
X\times_B X,\quad X\times_B Y,\quad
Y\times_B X,\quad Y\times_B Y.
\]
These schemes are finite over either curve and have finitely many
irreducible one-dimensional components. Selecting components, composing
again, or taking etale refinements cannot leave this finite list of
possible images. This proves (1) implies (2).

Condition (2) puts all powers of \(P\) in a finite-dimensional vector
space, proving (3).

## 2. An infinite generic graph separates every successive power

We use the generic two-coloured graph of the actual span. Its vertices
are compatible embeddings of the endpoint function fields in a fixed
algebraically closed field \(\Omega\) of transcendence degree one over
\(k\); its edges are embeddings of \(k(C)\). Fix a connected component
and an \(X\)-vertex \(x_0\). The graph is locally finite, with valencies
given by the two leg degrees.

Krishnamoorthy, Proposition 5.10, says that this graph is infinite
precisely when the hyperbolic-source correspondence has no core.
[Primary source, Sections 5--6](https://msp.org/ant/2018/12-5/ant-v12-n5-p05-p.pdf).
The earlier [generic-path proof](CORELESS_GENERIC_GRAPH_FORCES_UNBOUNDED_PRIMITIVE_CORRESPONDENCES.md)
verifies that paths give actual bi-etale images and that minimalization
retains them.

Assume the graph is infinite. For each \(n\ge0\), it has an \(X\)-vertex
\(x_n\) at distance exactly \(2n\) from \(x_0\). Indeed a connected locally
finite infinite graph has unbounded distance from a fixed root, and a
shortest path to a sufficiently distant vertex supplies every intermediate
distance. Bipartiteness gives colour \(X\) at even distances.

The pair of generic \(\Omega\)-points \((x_0,x_n)\) lies on the support of
the cycle \(P^n\), because a length-\(2n\) path is exactly an alternating
fiber-product construction for that power. Its multiplicity in the
component concerned is positive.

For \(j<n\), the same pair lies on no component of \(P^j\). Otherwise
membership in such a component, over this algebraically closed generic
field, would lift through its finite path source and supply an alternating
walk of length \(2j\) from \(x_0\) to \(x_n\), contradicting their distance.
Equivalently, the generic pair determines a joint-minimal image occurring
in \(P^n\) but in none of \(1,P,\ldots,P^{n-1}\).

For precision, this is not counting paths or embeddings as distinct
images. Embeddings with the same joint-minimal image lie in an orbit of
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\Omega/k(X))\). This group fixes \(x_0\) and preserves
the connected generic graph, hence preserves distance from \(x_0\).
An image first encountered at distance \(2n\) cannot also be encountered
at a smaller distance.

It follows successively that
\[
1,P,P^2,\ldots
\]
are linearly independent in the actual cycle algebra. Thus a coreless
correspondence violates (3). Together with Section 1, this proves the
equivalence of (1)--(3).

## 3. The finite positive-moment test

The element \(P\) is self-adjoint. For any vector \(c=(c_0,\ldots,c_n)\),
the faithful positive trace gives
\[
\sum_{i,j}\overline{c_i}c_j\,\tau_X(P^{i+j})
=\tau_X\left(
\left(\sum_i c_iP^i\right)^*
\left(\sum_j c_jP^j\right)\right).
\]
It is positive for nonzero \(c\) exactly when the displayed powers are
linearly independent. Thus the matrix in (1) is their Gram matrix:
its determinant is positive in the independent case and zero otherwise.
This proves equivalence with (4).

Each moment is an integer: it is the coefficient of the diagonal in an
effective integral correspondence cycle. The determinants are therefore
integers as well.

For scale, if \(a=\deg(C/X)\), \(b=\deg(C/Y)\), then
\[
\tau_X(1)=1,\qquad \tau_X(P)=a,\qquad
0\le\tau_X(P^n)\le (ab)^n.
\]
The last bound follows because the total left degree of \(P^n\) is
\((ab)^n\), while the diagonal coefficient is one contribution to that
degree. This supplies a finite exact test whenever the relevant fiber
products and their diagonal components can actually be calculated.
We claim no uniformly terminating computational procedure in the
coreless case.

## 4. Why ordinary linear algebra is not yet the obstruction

Any finite-dimensional realization of \(P\) satisfies its own
Cayley--Hamilton relation. Section 2 shows that in the coreless case
such a relation cannot be faithful to the actual correspondence cycle.
For Jacobian-orthogonal endpoints the discrepancy is extreme: the
Jacobian realization of \(\Gamma\), and hence of \(P\), is zero, although
the actual powers above remain linearly independent.

Consequently the missing theorem is not merely a polynomial relation on
cohomology. One would need a relation in the actual positive correspondence
category, or another bound excluding the required increasing family of
geometric supports. A faithful finite-dimensional realization for the
seed-generated algebra would suffice, but is not constructed here.

The result organizes the infinite obstruction created by one hypothetical
common cover. It does not prove the needed finite-dimensionality for our
curves, exclude every cored partner, or solve Litt3.
