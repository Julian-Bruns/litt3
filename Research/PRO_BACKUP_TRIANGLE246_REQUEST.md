# One remaining degree-24 map on a specified genus-two curve

Please decide the single existence question below. This is a bounded
subsidiary lemma for Litt's common-cover problem, not that whole problem.
The established inputs are supplied to avoid repeating completed work.

## Target

Work over k=algebraic closure of F5. Fix alpha with alpha^3+alpha+1=0,
and the smooth projective genus-two curve

    C: v^2=F(u)=u(u-1)(u-2)(u-3)(u-alpha).

Does there exist a finite separable degree-24 map f:C->P1 with precisely
three branch values, normalized to 0,1,infinity, and COMPLETE fibers

    f^*(0)=2D0,       f^*(1)=4D1,       f^*(infinity)=6Dinf,

where D0,D1,Dinf are reduced, pairwise disjoint divisors of degrees12,6,4,
and f is unramified everywhere else?

All ramification is tame. The map is NOT assumed Galois, and the
geometric monodromy group may have order divisible by5. Decide existence
over k, not merely among maps defined over F125.

## Established facts about this C — no new verification requested

* Aut_k(C) is just its hyperelliptic involution. Its moduli point has
  Frobenius orbit of length3 over F5.
* J(C) is ordinary and geometrically simple. Its geometric endomorphism
  algebra is the quartic CM field generated by a root of

      z^4-8z^3+182z^2-1000z+15625.

  Its Rosati-fixed real subfield is Q(sqrt21). In particular C has no
  nonconstant map to an elliptic curve.
* Every connected cyclic etale cover of C of degree1,2,3 or6 has ordinary
  Jacobian. Do not extend this assertion to arbitrary covers.
* The five regular dormant projective connections are reduced. Their
  twisted tangent kernels vanish for EVERY twist in Pic(C)[3] and
  Pic(C)[4]. These stronger endpoint tests are already proved.

The last fact does NOT immediately exclude the desired map. In the
convention z''=r z, put E(r)=r''-3r^2 and
N(r)=-(E')^2-3E(E''+3rE). The target-line connection

    r0(t)=3/[t^2(t-1)]

has E(r0)=1/[t^2(t-1)^3] and N(r0)=0. The proposed f pulls it back
to a REGULAR ACTIVE nilpotent connection, not a dormant one. The
previous radical-quadratic/dormant argument stops exactly here.

## A supplied finite scalar reduction

Put eta=du/v and delta=v*d/du. Every possible f supplies the normalized
quartic s=A(u)eta^4 with divisor2Dinf, and satisfies

              (delta f)^4 = 3 A(u) f^2(f-1)^3.             (1)

There are exactly85 possible s on this C. The following formulas specify
the ENTIRE pool, including divisors containing infinity; use them as
inputs rather than recomputing the nilpotent scheme.

Ten have A=P^2, where P is quadratic, squarefree and coprime to F, and

    [u^(5i+4)] F(u)^2 P(u)^3 = P_i^5,    i=0,1,2.

Here P=sum P_i u^i; P and -P give the same A. All coefficient powers
are genuine fifth powers. These give exactly ten distinct A.

The other75 are indexed by the fifteen pairs of the six Weierstrass
points {0,1,2,3,alpha,infinity}. For each pair let R be the monic product
of its finite branch factors, so deg R=1 or2, and set

    S=F/R,  D=R S^2,  J=R'S+2RS',
    K=D^[6],       c=[u^4](S R^2),

where D^[6] is the sixth HASSE derivative in u. The five quartics for
this pair are

    A=cS;     A=K(h)R(u)(u-h)^2 for the four roots h of J.

For this C, all these J are squarefree, K(h)!=0, and c!=0.
Roots h may lie in extension fields. For each listed s define
D_s=(1/2)div(s). Any desired f lies in the COMPLETE23-dimensional space
H0(C,O(6D_s)), has pole order exactly6 at all four points of D_s, and
satisfies (1). Conversely, (1) and the pole divisor EXACTLY6D_s imply
the requested complete profile: at a zero of f, 4 ord(df)=2 ord(f)
forces index2; at a zero of f-1 it forces index4. A wild index is
incompatible with either equality, and df is a unit elsewhere. Thus
this scalar test is exact, not merely a norm or necessary subsystem.

One potentially useful geometric interpretation is available. For a
connected component B of the normalized fourth-root curve z^4=A(u)
over C, equation(1) gives an actual map to the fixed genus-one curve

    E: w^4=3t^2(t-1)^3,
    t=f,  w=delta(f)/z,      pullback(dt/w)=z eta.

In fact E is isomorphic to y^2=x^3+3x and is ordinary. If A=P^2 then
g(B)=5 and deg(B->E)=12; otherwise g(B)=9 and deg(B->E)=24.
B->C is RAMIFIED. Thus neither simplicity of J(C) nor the supplied
ordinary-etale-cover tests alone excludes this map. No elliptic factor
of J(B) has been excluded by the supplied facts.
