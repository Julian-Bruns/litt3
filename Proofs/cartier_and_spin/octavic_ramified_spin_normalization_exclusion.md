## The zero fourth coefficient cannot supply four order-two critical points

Write F=bg³+Bg²+g(1−f+Af²)+cf⁴. It is an irreducible plane quartic: as a cubic in g over k(f), it is the minimal equation since degf=3 and k(f,g)=k(Y). In particular all its plane points together acquire at most ONE extra normalization branch.

Assume B=0. The finite order-two cone has four DISTINCT points ofY, all critical for β=b+cf⁴/g³. At each of these, f and g are units: their zeros and poles lie only above the other cones or the distinguished ordinary value. At a smooth plane point, differentiating F and β gives the necessary critical condition2fF_f+gF_g=0. At a singular point both partials vanish, so the same condition holds. Modulo F the identity is
\[
2fF_f+gF_g=g(-Bg-2+2Af^2).
\]
Thus B=0 forces f²=1/A at every one of the four critical points. Their common β value a differs from b, and β−b=cf⁴/g³ now forces g³=d=c/(A²(a−b))≠0 at all four.

Substitute these two relations into F. It becomes
\[

There are only TWO possible f values, opposite nonzero square roots of1/A. If neither is two, each determines at most one g, so there are at most two plane coordinate pairs. If one f value is two, it can occur only when the right side is zero; the opposite value−2 then gives g=0 and is inadmissible.
