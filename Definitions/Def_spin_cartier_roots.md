# Spin Cartier roots

Work over an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic p. Put
ell=p+2. A spin line on a smooth projective curve C is a line bundle L
with a specified isomorphism L^2 -> omega_C. Write L_1 for its Frobenius
twist on C^(1); then F_C^*L_1=L^p.

The spin Cartier space is

    K(C,L)=ker[H0(C,omega_C L^p)
                    --Cartier--> H0(C^(1),omega_(C^(1)) L_1)].

The spin identification identifies the domain with H0(C,L^(p+2)).
Cartier here is the relative twisted Cartier map; after absolute-twist
identifications it is p^(-1)-semilinear. Its kernel is a k-vector space.

For h in H0(C,L^ell) with nonempty reduced zero divisor, the spin root
cover is Spec_C of the algebra O_C + L^(-1) + ... + L^(-(ell-1)), with
multiplication determined by h. In a frame l of L it has equation
w^ell=h_l. Put eta_l equal to the image of l^2 in omega_C. Its canonical
one-form is alpha=w^2 pi^*eta_l. The expression is frame independent.
These are finite tame RAMIFIED covers of C, not finite etale ones.

A spin-preserving etale span includes an isomorphism f^*L_X=g^*L_Y
compatible with their spin isomorphisms via omega_Z. Equality of pulled
back sections is equality under this specified isomorphism. Do not infer
such an identification from abstract equality of the degrees of L_X,L_Y.

If L is globally generated with r=h0(C,L)>=2, its complete spin-series
map is C->P^(r-1) defined by ALL global sections. Its spin-series field
is the subfield of k(C) generated by their ratios. Write S for the smooth
projective model of that field, phi:C->S for the finite map, and M for
the pullback to S of O(1) on the image curve. Then phi^*M=L and
H0(S,M)=H0(C,L); the complete series of M is birational onto its image.
Separability of phi is not automatic and must be proved when used.
