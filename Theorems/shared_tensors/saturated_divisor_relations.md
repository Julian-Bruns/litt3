# Saturated divisor relations in an actual étale span

Version3,24 September2026. Let k be algebraically closed and let
X<-f-Z-g->Y be finite étale maps of smooth projective connected
curves of genus at least two. In E=k(Z) put F=f*k(X), G=g*k(Y),
and assume F intersect G=k. Joint minimality is not required.

Write
\[
Q=E^*/(F^*G^*),\qquad
\mathcal D=\frac{\operatorname{Div}(Z)}
{f^*\operatorname{Div}(X)+g^*\operatorname{Div}(Y)},\qquad
R=\ker\bigl(Q\xrightarrow{\operatorname{div}}\mathcal D\bigr).
\]
All divisors are integral with finite support. Let P be the group
of isomorphism classes of invariant line-bundle triples
(L_X,L_Y,phi:f*L_X~g*L_Y), and
let T be its degree-zero subgroup, equivalently
\[
T=\ker\bigl(J(X)\times J(Y)\xrightarrow{f^*-g^*}J(Z)\bigr)(k).
\]
Write \(\mathscr T=\ker(f^*-g^*:J(X)\times J(Y)\to J(Z))\)
for the finite scheme-theoretic kernel, so \(T=\mathscr T(k)\).
It need not be reduced. The last section
below also determines the possible infinitesimal types of this kernel.

1. The group D is torsion-free. Thus R is saturated in Q:
   R intersect Q^r=R^r for every positive integer r.
2. P is finitely generated of rank one. If e is the positive generator
   of its Y-degree image, then e divides h_Y=2g(Y)-2 and
   \(0\to T\to P\to e\mathbf Z\to0\).
3. Let A be the group of endpoint divisor pairs with f*A=g*B.
   There is an exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow0.
\]
   In the absence of a clump A=0, and R has rank one and torsion T.
   If a clump S exists, it is unique, A is generated by its reduced
   endpoint images, and R is finite. For s=|g(S)|,
\[
   |R|=(s/e)|T|.
\]
4. The entire torsion subgroup of Q is finite: it is T in the no-clump
   case and R in the clump case.

For arbitrary nonzero rational endpoint differentials let
\(\xi=[f^*\theta_X/g^*\theta_Y]\in R\).
In the no-clump case the normalized Y-degree homomorphism on R
sends xi to h_Y/e. Consequently xi can belong to Q^r only if
r divides h_Y/e. This is a necessary divisibility condition,
not a proof that a root exists for every such r.

In characteristic p>0 the p-power condition is exact. Without a clump,
\[
\xi\in Q^{p^a}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p^a\mid h_Y/e
\qquad(a\ge1).
\]
With a clump xi has p-power roots of every order. Thus the finite
height in the no-clump case is exactly v_p(h_Y/e). This concerns
roots in the actual multiplicative quotient, not Witt lifts of curves.

Consequently, for any \(a\ge1\) with \(p^a\nmid h_X\) or
\(p^a\nmid h_Y\), the following are equivalent:

1. The common canonical ring has a positive-weight element, or
   equivalently there is a clump.
2. \(\xi\in Q^{p^a}\).
3. \(f^*\theta_X/g^*\theta_Y=c^{p^a}v/u\) for actual functions
   \(c\in k(Z)^*\), \(u\in k(X)^*\), \(v\in k(Y)^*\).

One uniform choice is
\(a=1+\min\{v_p(h_X),v_p(h_Y)\}\), independent of the
covering degrees, including in characteristic two. The factorization
is an existence test, not an asserted construction for the selected
pairs.

For \(a=1\), without any degree condition, membership has the
differential description
\[
\xi\in Q^p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
d\log(f^*\theta_X/g^*\theta_Y)=\beta-\alpha
\]
for rational endpoint one-forms \(\alpha,\beta\). If one such
decomposition exists, all form a torsor under the space \(V\) of
shared rational one-forms, which are regular and have dimension at
most one. At least one decomposition is logarithmic:
\(\alpha=d\log u\), \(\beta=d\log v\). The logarithmic choices form
a torsor under \(V^{C=1}\), of size one or \(p\). If the degree-one
part \(A_1\) of the common canonical ring vanishes, then
\(V=0\), \(Q[p]=0\), and the decomposition is unique. Both selected
characteristic-five/genus-two pairs have \(a=1\) and \(A_1=0\).

## The characteristic-primary part

In characteristic p>0 there is a canonical F_p-linear isomorphism
\[
Q[p]\ \simeq\
\{\alpha\in f^*\Omega^1_{F/k}\cap g^*\Omega^1_{G/k}:C\alpha=\alpha\}.
\]
Here the rational differential spaces mean their actual images in
Omega^1_(E/k), with no extension of scalars to E. The right side
consists of regular forms and has F_p-dimension at most one.
Hence the p-primary torsion of Q is cyclic. It is nontrivial exactly
when a shared regular one-form has nonzero Cartier image.

In particular, if there is no shared regular one-form, Q has no
p-primary torsion. If also p does not divide h_Y, then
\[
R/R^p=
\begin{cases}
0,&\text{with a clump},\\
\mathbf F_p\cdot\overline{\xi},&\text{without a clump}.
\end{cases}
\]
The natural map R/R^p->Q/Q^p is injective. These assertions apply to
both selected characteristic-five/genus-two pairs. For them e=1 or2,
and a clump would have image size s prime to5. No clump is constructed
and neither common-cover question is decided.

## The full Picard kernel and its Frobenius directions

Assume characteristic p>0. Let V be the space of shared regular
one-forms, with its inherited Cartier operator, and put
\[
K=\ker\bigl(H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)\oplus H^1(Y,\mathcal O_Y)
\xrightarrow{f^*-g^*}H^1(Z,\mathcal O_Z)\bigr)
=\operatorname{Lie}\mathscr T.
\]
Keep the scalar twists in the Frobenius maps; equivalently use the
semilinear operators on these k-spaces. Then
\[
\dim\operatorname{Lie}(\mathscr T^D)=\dim V\le1,
\qquad \dim\ker(F|K)=\dim\ker(C|V).
\]
Here D denotes Cartier duality. In particular:

- If V=0, the WHOLE finite group scheme \(\mathscr T\) is of
  multiplicative type, and conversely. Its p-primary part can be
  nonreduced even though T has no p-torsion. Frobenius on K is bijective.
- If V is a line with nonzero Cartier, the p-primary group scheme
  \(\mathscr T_p\) is a product of a multiplicative group scheme
  and one nontrivial cyclic constant p-group. Frobenius on K is again
  bijective.
- If V is a line killed by Cartier, \(\mathscr T_p\) is connected
  and has a nonzero local-local part. The nilpotent Frobenius part of
  K is exactly one Jordan block. If its length is ell, then
\[
\dim\ker(F^a|K)=\min\{a,\ell\}\qquad(a\ge1).
\]
  This block injects into the nilpotent Frobenius part of each endpoint.
  Thus ell is at most the smaller of their nilpotence indices.

Equivalently, for
\(B_{a,C}=\operatorname{coker}(\mathcal O_{C^{(a)}}
\to F^a_{C/k*}\mathcal O_C)\), the kernel of the actual pullback
map on \(H^0(B_{a,X})\oplus H^0(B_{a,Y})\) has dimension zero
in the first two cases and \(\min\{a,\ell\}\) in the third.
This is an assertion at every finite height, not an assertion of a lift.

If either endpoint is ordinary, the third case is impossible. More
generally its exclusion only requires Cartier to be injective on V.
In that situation
\(\dim K\le f_p(X)+f_p(Y)\); for both selected pairs it is at most8.
The no-clump case always lies in the first row. These group-scheme
restrictions do not eliminate that case or force a canonical root.

The saturation and finite-rank idea comes from the returned canonical
power calculation. The statement removes its special-curve,
ordinariness and finite-field assumptions and identifies the
characteristic-primary torsion explicitly.
