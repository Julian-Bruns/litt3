# The displayed basin-127 quotient

## Status

The structure of the displayed ideal \(J\) and its base-residual cut are
**proved-text** below. Given the recorded local-model specialization
\(\Delta=2\gamma^2\) when \(\alpha=\beta=0\), its disjointness from that
discriminant divisor is also proved. The derivation of this discriminant
formula from the original basin parameterization is not retained here.

The essential H127 assertion is still **open**: this checkout does not show

## Definition of the displayed ideal

Let

\[
R_0=\mathbb F_5[r_0,r_1,r_2,r_3,r_4,r_5],
\]

and let \(J\subset R_0\) be generated by

\[
\begin{aligned}
&r_2-2,\quad r_5-2,\\
&r_0+2r_1-2r_3+2r_4^2+r_4+1,\\
&r_1^2-r_3-2r_4-1,\\
&r_1r_3+2r_1-2r_3-r_4^2+2r_4,\\
&r_1r_4-r_1+2r_4^2-2,\\
&r_3^2+r_3-2,\\
&r_3r_4-r_3-r_4+1,\\
&r_4^3-2r_4^2-r_4+2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{159.1}
\]

Write \(J_k=J\,k[r_0,\ldots,r_5]\) for scalar extension to
\(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_5\).

## Proposition

The algebra \(R_0/J\) is isomorphic to \(\mathbb F_5^5\).  In particular,
\(J\) and \(J_k\) are radical of length \(5\).  Their five geometric points
are

\[
\begin{gathered}
(0,0,2,1,4,2),\quad
(0,1,2,3,1,2),\quad
(3,4,2,1,2,2),\\
(4,2,2,1,1,2),\quad
(4,4,2,3,1,2).
\end{gathered}
\tag{159.2}
\]

### Proof

The last three generators in (159.1) factor as

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_3^2+r_3-2&=(r_3-1)(r_3-3),\\
r_3r_4-r_3-r_4+1&=(r_3-1)(r_4-1),\\
r_4^3-2r_4^2-r_4+2&=(r_4-1)(r_4-2)(r_4-4).
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(r_4=2\), the equations force \(r_3=1\), \(r_1=4\), and
\(r_0=3\).  If \(r_4=4\), they force \(r_3=1\), \(r_1=0\), and
\(r_0=0\).  If \(r_4=1\), then either

\[
(r_3,r_1,r_0)=(1,2,4),\ (3,1,0),\ \text{or }(3,4,4).
\]

Together with \(r_2=r_5=2\), these are exactly the five points in
(159.2), and direct substitution shows that all generators vanish at them.

It remains to exclude nilpotent thickening. With lexicographic order
\(r_1>r_3>r_4\), the six displayed relations have leading monomials

\[
r_1^2,\ r_1r_3,\ r_1r_4,\ r_3^2,\ r_3r_4,\ r_4^3
\]

and therefore reduce every monomial to the span of

\[
1,\ r_1,\ r_3,\ r_4,\ r_4^2.
\]

After eliminating \(r_0,r_2,r_5\), this proves
\(\dim_{\mathbb F_5}(R_0/J)\le5\).  Evaluation at the five distinct
\(\mathbb F_5\)-points induces a surjection

\[
R_0/J\longrightarrow\mathbb F_5^5
\]

by the Chinese remainder theorem, so its dimension is at least \(5\).
The map is therefore an isomorphism.  This proves the proposition.

## Base-residual cut

Let

\[
B_{127}=r_3+r_4^2.
\]

Its values at the five points in the order (159.2) are

\[
2,\ 4,\ 0,\ 2,\ 4.
\]

Consequently

\[
J+(B_{127})
=(r_0-3,r_1-4,r_2-2,r_3-1,r_4-2,r_5-2).
\tag{159.3}
\]

Thus the displayed quotient plus the base residual consists only of the
six-parameter point

\[
r_{129}=(3,4,2,1,2,2).
\]

Older notes call this point \(P129\).  The terminal-strata files use that
same name for a 19-coordinate low-data point.  The affine basin
parameterization identifying \(r_{129}\) with that 19-coordinate point is
not retained in this checkout.  Both the identification and the later claim
that its image dies at \(e50\) therefore remain certificate-transcript; they
are not proved by (159.3).

## Discriminant open

On the displayed family, the stated auxiliary quantities are

\[
\alpha=\beta=r_5-r_2,\qquad
\gamma=-r_0-2r_1-r_2+2r_3-2r_4-2r_5+1.
\]

Assume the recorded local-model identity
\(\Delta=2\gamma^2\) on \(\alpha=\beta=0\). Modulo \(J\),

\[
\gamma=2r_4^2-r_4+1,\qquad
\Delta=2\gamma^2=-r_4^2-2r_4+1.
\]

For \(r_4=1,2,4\), the values of \(\Delta\) are \(3,3,2\),
respectively.  Hence \(\Delta\) is a unit in \(R_0/J\simeq\mathbb F_5^5\),
and \(V(J_k)\subset D(\Delta)\). This calculation proves the open condition
for the displayed formula; it does not reconstruct the missing map from the
original basin coordinates or independently derive that formula.

## Exact remaining H127 claim

Let \(I_{127}\) be the original six-parameter low-data ideal and let
\(\mu_3\) be the relevant rho3 pivot determinant.  Neither
\(I_{127}\) nor \(\mu_3\) is displayed in this checkout.  Once they are
reconstructed, the required set-theoretic statement is

\[
V(I_{127})\cap D(\Delta\mu_3)\subseteq V(J_k),
\]

equivalently

\[
J_k\subseteq
\sqrt{\,I_{127}:(\Delta\mu_3)^\infty\,}.
\tag{159.4}
\]

The stronger scheme-theoretic equality
\(I_{127}:(\Delta\mu_3)^\infty=J_k\) would also suffice.  Proving only the
properties of \(J\) above does not address (159.4).
