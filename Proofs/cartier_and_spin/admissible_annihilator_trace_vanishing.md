# Proof: a fixed affine congruence forces four annihilator traces to vanish

Use the hypotheses and explicit rows of the
[statement](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/admissible_annihilator_trace_vanishing.md).
The argument extends the low-moment mechanism in the incoming audited
degree-ten trace-zero exclusion. Multiplication by the full quartic $A$,
followed by a further congruence, removes its selected-support hypothesis.

## Local bounds independent of the covering degree

Write $q_s=(Q-L_0^5)/y^5$, $Z=B_0-L_0$, and
$w=W+Z/y=b+B_0/y$. Then
\[
\phi=W^5+q_s=w^5+q,\quad q=(Q-B_0^5)/y^5,\quad
\operatorname{div}(\chi)=5G-E.
\]
The function $q$ is affine regular by $P^2\mid Q-B_0^5$.
At a finite $E$-point, $y$ is a unit and $q_s,\phi$ have order
at least three, so $W^5=\phi-q_s$ has order at least three. Its
integral valuation implies $\operatorname{ord}W\ge1$. Thus
$\chi W^j$ is regular there for $j\ge1$, and multiplication by
the simple zero of $A$ removes the possible pole of $\chi$.

At a finite point of $G$-multiplicity $g>0$, regularity of $q$
and $\operatorname{ord}\phi=-5g$ give $w$-pole exactly $g$.
If $y=0$, it is a uniformizer, $E$ is absent, and $W=w-Z/y$
has pole at most $\max(g,1)$. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{ord}(\chi y^jW^j)
\ge5g+j-j\max(g,1)\ge0\qquad(1\le j\le4).
\]
The same conclusion holds with $g=0$. Where $y$ is a unit the
corresponding bound is $(5-j)g\ge0$. These bounds cover all finite
multiplicities of $G$.

At infinity $q_s$ has pole exactly seven. Since the map is étale over
an algebraically closed field, its completed fiber is a disjoint union
of copies of the base disk; all valuations are integral, and trace is
the sum of the Laurent series in one base uniformizer. A sheet in $E$
has $G$-multiplicity zero, $\phi$-pole seven, $W$-pole at most one,
and $\chi$-pole one. A sheet outside $E$, of $G$-multiplicity $g$,
has $\phi$-pole $10+5g$, hence $W$-pole $a=2+g\ge2$, and
$\operatorname{ord}\chi=5(a-2)$. Consequently for $0\le j\le4$
\[
\operatorname{pole}_O m_j
\le\max\bigl(j+1,\sup_{a\ge2}(ja-5(a-2))\bigr)
=(1,2,4,6,8)_j.
\]
This is an upper bound, including when a function is zero or a type of
sheet is absent. No number of sheets or infinity partition was used.

Put $M_0=Am_0$ and $M_j=y^jm_j$, $1\le j\le4$. The finite
bounds and the infinity pole orders of $A,y$ give
\[
M_0\in\mathcal L_{13},\quad M_1\in\mathcal L_{12},\quad
M_2\in\mathcal L_{24},\quad M_3\in\mathcal L_{36},\quad
M_4\in\mathcal L_{48},\qquad \mathcal L_d=L_X(dO).
\]
The affine ring is $k[x,y]/(y^3-P)$, and the pole orders of its
monomials $x^ay^r$, $0\le r\le2$, are $3a+10r$, all distinct.
Thus the three component degree bounds are respectively
$(4,1,-)$, $(4,0,-)$, $(8,4,1)$, $(12,8,5)$, and $(16,12,9)$.

## Fixed affine congruences

At every zero of $y$, $\chi w^j$ has order at least $(5-j)g\ge0$
for $0\le j\le4$. Hence the affine-regular expression
\[
C_j=Z^jM_0+A\sum_{i=1}^j\binom ji Z^{j-i}M_i
=Ay^j\operatorname{Tr}(\chi w^j)
\]
is divisible by $y^j$. For a reduced expression
$C_0(x)+yC_1(x)+y^2C_2(x)$, this means
$P^{\lceil(j-r)/3\rceil}\mid C_r$ for $r<j$. All congruences
below take place in $k[x]/(P)$; $A$ is a unit there.

We use three fixed calculations over $B=\mathbf F_{25}$. The first
includes the next congruence, rather than an incorrect claim that its
square matrix is invertible. Set
\[
R_1=Z/A\bmod P=(0,15,9,19,18,17,4,20,1,22),
\]
\[
R_2=Z^2/A\bmod P=(12,9,15,19,0,14,0,14,10,3).
\]
For $Zp_4+Aq_4\equiv0$, the coefficients of degrees five through
nine in $R_1p_4$ form the matrix
\[
K_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
17&20&9&8&14\\
4&1&18&22&5\\
20&16&5&15&11\\
1&10&21&2&2\\
22&19&16&13&7
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is zero, while the first four rows and columns have
determinant $[17]\ne0$. Its kernel is generated by
\[
p_*=(3,5,15,24,1),\qquad q_*=(4,13,16,17),\qquad
Zp_*+Aq_*\equiv0\pmod P.
\]
Moreover $[x^9](Zq_*\bmod P)=[10]\ne0$. Thus if also
$Zq_4+r_8\equiv0\pmod P$, then $p_4=q_4=r_8=0$.

For the congruence $Z^2p_1+2cAZ+Aq_4\equiv0$, divide by $A$
and inspect degrees five, six and seven. The coefficients of the two
entries of $p_1$ and of $c$ have matrix
\[
K_2=\begin{pmatrix}14&15&8\\0&19&1\\14&7&18\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det K_2=[15]\ne0.
\]
It follows that $p_1=c=q_4=0$. Finally, for
$Zp_2+q_6\equiv0$, inspect degrees seven through nine:
\[
K_3=\begin{pmatrix}24&0&13\\18&16&2\\16&5&14\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det K_3=[23]\ne0.
\]
Thus $p_2=q_6=0$. All of these kernel conclusions remain valid after
extension from $B$ to $k$.

## Successive vanishing

Write $M_0=p_4+yp_1$ and $M_1=q_4+yc$. The constant component
of $C_1$ gives $Zp_4+Aq_4\equiv0$. The constant component of
$C_2$ gives
\[
Z^2p_4+2AZq_4+A(M_2)_0\equiv0.
\]
Using the first congruence and the unit $A$, this becomes
$Zq_4+(M_2)_0\equiv0$. Since $(M_2)_0$ has degree at most eight,
the $K_1$ kernel and nonzero degree-nine obstruction force
$p_4=q_4=(M_2)_0=0$.

Now the $y$-component of $C_2$ is
$Z^2p_1+2cAZ+A(M_2)_1\equiv0$. The $K_2$ calculation gives
$p_1=c=(M_2)_1=0$. Therefore $M_0=M_1=0$, and
$M_2=y^2p_1'$, $\deg p_1'\le1$.

Divisibility of $C_3=A(M_3+3Zy^2p_1')$ by $P=y^3$ gives
$(M_3)_0=Pp_2'$, $(M_3)_1=0$, and
$(M_3)_2+3Zp_1'\equiv0\pmod P$. The last is a subcase of
$K_3$, so $p_1'=(M_3)_2=0$. Thus $M_2=0$ and
$M_3=Pp_2'$, $\deg p_2'\le2$.

Finally $C_4=A(M_4+4ZPp_2')$ is divisible by $y^4$. Its
constant component must be divisible by $P^2$: first
$(M_4)_0=Pq_6'$, then $q_6'+4Zp_2'\equiv0\pmod P$.
$K_3$ makes both polynomials zero. The other components give
$(M_4)_1=P\widetilde p_2$, $\deg\widetilde p_2\le2$, and
$(M_4)_2=0$. Hence
\[
M_0=M_1=M_2=M_3=0,\qquad M_4=yP\widetilde p_2=y^4\widetilde p_2.
\]
Division by the defining factors proves the assertions for $m_0,\ldots,m_4$.
Since $W^5=\phi-q_s$,
\[
m_5=\operatorname{Tr}(\chi W^5)
=\operatorname{Tr}(u)-q_s m_0=\operatorname{Tr}(u).
\]
The divisor of $u$ has no finite poles and pole at most ten at every
infinity sheet. Actual étale trace therefore puts this function in
$L_X(10O)$.

## The raw polynomial has constant Frobenius remainder

This part uses the established
[uniform admissible norm theorem](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/uniform_admissible_norm.md):
under the actual divisor hypotheses, even before the existence of $u$,
$\operatorname{Nm}_{k(S)/k(X)}(\phi)\in k(X)^{*5}$. Assume $b$ primitive,
put $K=k(X)$, and let $F$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $W$.
The function $q_s$ is not a fifth power: its differential equals
$df\ne0$. Thus for $\alpha^5=-q_s$ the purely inseparable extension
$K(\alpha)/K$ has basis $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^4$.

The norm identity gives
\[
\operatorname{Nm}(\phi)=
\prod_i(W_i^5+q_s)=((-1)^nF(\alpha))^5.
\]
Its left side is $c^5$ for some $c\in K^*$. Injectivity of Frobenius
in the field $K(\alpha)$ implies $F(\alpha)=(-1)^n c\in K^*$.
Divide $F$ by $T^5+q_s$. Its remainder has degree at most four;
the displayed basis then forces that remainder to be a constant
$\tau\in K^*$. Therefore
\[
F=(T^5+q_s)\mathscr H+\tau,\qquad F'=(T^5+q_s)\mathscr H'.
\]
Separability makes $\mathscr H'\ne0$. A degree-five $F$ would make
$\mathscr H$ constant and $F'=0$, so a primitive source has $n\ge6$.

For a monic separable polynomial of degree $n$ the power-basis trace
formula is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(g(W))=[T^{n-1}](g(T)F'(T)\bmod F).
\]
One proves it by evaluating in the split algebra and using
$\sum_i W_i^j/F'(W_i)=0$ for $j<n-1$, and $1$ for $j=n-1$.
Taking $g=W^j/\phi$ cancels $\phi$ algebraically, leaving
$T^j\mathscr H'(T)$, of degree at most $j+n-6<n-1$ for $j\le4$.
Hence $\operatorname{Tr}(W^j/\phi)=0$ for $0\le j\le4$.
This is the same trace-dual mechanism as the
[source quadratic calculus](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/source_quadratic_calculus.md);
no local inverse of a critical derivative is used.

If $b$ is not primitive, let $S'$ be the smooth proper normalization
of $X$ in $k(X)(b)$, and write $S\xrightarrow p S'\xrightarrow {h'}X$.
Both maps are finite étale: ramification indices in either intermediate
extension divide those of the actual étale cover. At a finite point,
the value of $\operatorname{ord}\phi$ uniquely determines whether it
is in $E$ (order three), in $G$ (negative order divisible by five), or
neither. At infinity it uniquely determines the alternatives
$E$ (order negative seven) and $G$-multiplicity $g$ (order
$-10-5g$). Étale pullback preserves these valuations. Thus
$E=p^*E'$ and $G=p^*G'$ for reduced $E'\le h'^*R$ and effective
$G'$, and their degrees are $5\deg h'$ and $\deg h'$.
The same divisor hypotheses therefore hold on $S'$. The primitive
argument gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{S'/X}(W^j/\phi)=0$, and trace
transitivity gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{S/X}(W^j/\phi)
=\deg(p)\operatorname{Tr}_{S'/X}(W^j/\phi)=0.
\]
The multiplier $\deg(p)$ is allowed to vanish in characteristic five.
No annihilator or torsion hypothesis has been descended in this step.
In particular $\chi$ need not belong to $k(S')$.

The same descent gives the support criterion for primitivity. Put
$e=\deg p$. Applying the uniform norm theorem to $S'$ gives
$h'_*E'=5B'$ for an integral effective divisor $B'$. Therefore
\[
5B=h_*E=h_*p^*E'=e h'_*E'=5eB',\qquad B=eB'.
\]
Thus $e$ divides every nonzero coefficient of $B$. If $c(B)=1$,
then $e=1$ and $b$ is primitive. The
[degree-ten/eleven support reduction](../../Theorems/cartier_and_spin/admissible_degree_ten_eleven_reduction.md)
gives $B=(n-9)O+x^*(r_1+r_2+r_3)$ with distinct roots of $A$;
its nine finite coefficients are one, proving automatic primitivity
in those two degrees. Torsion need not descend to $S'$ for this
argument; the norm theorem only used the descended divisor identity.

## Primitive annihilator interpolation

Suppose $W$ is primitive and $F$ is its monic minimal polynomial of
degree $n$. Separability gives $n$ distinct roots $W_i$, with the
corresponding conjugates $\chi_i$. The polynomial
\[
U(T)=\sum_i\chi_i\frac{F(T)}{T-W_i}
=\left(F(T)\sum_{j\ge0}m_jT^{-j-1}\right)_+
\]
has coefficients in $k(X)$. Four vanishing moments give
$\deg U\le n-5$. Evaluation yields
$U(W)=\chi F'(W)=u\mathscr H'(W)\ne0$.
This argument never divides by $n$.

For the degree-ten raw polynomial $F_s$, the same construction with
its leading coefficient $v$ gives $\deg U\le5$. Characteristic
five makes $F_s'=(T^5+q_s)S_s'$; consequently
$U(W)=\chi\phi S_s'(W)=uS_s'(W)$. It is used by multiplication,
without removing critical collisions or assuming a local inverse of
$S_s'$.

## Direct coefficient bounds from a supplied actual infinity profile

Use the raw polynomial $F_{\rm raw}=v\prod_i(T-W_i)$, and form
$U_{\rm raw}=\sum_i\chi_iF_{\rm raw}(T)/(T-W_i)$ as before.
The coefficient of $T^j$ in each quotient is a sum of products of
$n-1-j$ roots other than $i$. A product's pole is at most the
sum of their pole upper bounds; this includes regular or vanishing
small roots, for which the upper bound one remains valid.

Suppose there are $e$ small roots and the big roots' pole orders sum
to $A_{\rm big}$. For $j\le e-1$, a quotient omitting a small root has at
most all big roots and $e-1-j$ small roots. The factor $\chi_i$
has pole one, so its coefficient contribution has pole at most
\[
d+A_{\rm big}+(e-1-j)+1=d+A_{\rm big}+e-j.
\]
A quotient omitting a big root of pole $a\ge2$ can contain the
remaining big roots and $e-j$ small roots. Now $\chi_i$ has
order $5(a-2)$, so its contribution has pole at most
\[
d+A_{\rm big}-a+(e-j)-5(a-2)=d+A_{\rm big}+e+10-6a-j
\le d+A_{\rm big}+e-2-j.
\]
These bounds persist upon summing, and give the asserted direct
coefficient bounds, for the degrees that remain after moment vanishing.
No residual discriminant, distinctness or interpolation of small
residues is required.

In degree ten use the established raw normalization
$\operatorname{div}(v)=h_*G-10O$. The support gives five small sheets.
The finite zeros of $v$ have degree $d$, so the big infinity
$G$-multiplicities sum to $10-d$ and their five pole orders sum
to $A_{\rm big}=20-d$. This gives the bound $25-j$ for $j\le4$.
The top identity $A_5=vm_4$ gives its separately sharper bound.
In degree eleven use $\operatorname{div}(v)=h_*G-11O$.
There are ten small sheets and one big sheet
of pole $a=13-d$; hence $d+A_{\rm big}+e=23$ and the direct bound
applies to every $j\le6$.

## One, two or three pole-two infinity sheets are impossible

Let $r=x^3/y$ be the base uniformizer. On a pole-two sheet write
$W_i=\beta_i r^{-2}+\cdots$ and $\chi_i=\kappa_i+\cdots$,
with $\beta_i,\kappa_i\in k^*$. For $m_2$, these sheets alone
can contribute at pole four: the small sheets have pole at most three
and every other big sheet has pole at most one. Their leading pole-six
contribution to $m_3$ is likewise isolated, since every other sheet has
pole at most four. Their pole-eight contribution to $m_4$ is isolated,
since every other big sheet has pole at most seven and every small sheet
at most five, while $m_4$ has pole at most six. Thus
\[
\sum_{a_i=2}\kappa_i\beta_i^2=
\sum_{a_i=2}\kappa_i\beta_i^3=
\sum_{a_i=2}\kappa_i\beta_i^4=0.
\]
These assertions do not assume that the leading residues are distinct.

For $\operatorname{Tr}(W^4/\phi)$, a pole-two sheet contributes
$\beta_i^{-1}r^2+\cdots$. A small sheet contributes order at least
three, and every other big sheet contributes order at least three.
The raw-polynomial trace vanishing therefore gives
\[
\sum_{a_i=2}\beta_i^{-1}=0.
\]

Suppose the number $t$ of pole-two sheets is between one and three.
Group their residues by the distinct values $\beta$. The first three
equations form a weighted Vandermonde system in the aggregate weights
$\sum_{\beta_i=\beta}\kappa_i$. Since there are at most three
distinct nonzero residues, every aggregate weight is zero. No group
can be a singleton because its weight would be a nonzero $\kappa_i$.
With at most three sheets this forces all residues to be equal
(and already rules out $t=1$). The reciprocal equation now reads
$t/\beta=0$, impossible in characteristic five for $1\le t\le3$.
This proves the stated exclusion in every covering degree, without
primitivity: the unweighted trace has just been proved on the original
$S$, and the annihilator moments were proved there from the start.

If there are no pole-two sheets but exactly one pole-three sheet,
its contribution to $\chi W^4$ has pole exactly seven. Other big
sheets have pole at most six, and small sheets at most five.
Thus $m_4$ has pole exactly seven, contradicting its pole bound six.

In degree eleven the established support has coefficient two at $O$,
so $h_*E$ has coefficient ten there. There are ten small sheets and
one big sheet. Since $h_*G\sim11O$, take a nonzero
$v\in L_X(11O)$ with $\operatorname{div}(v)=h_*G-11O$.
Its affine zeros have degree $d=\operatorname{pole}_O v$, and the
unique big sheet has $G$-multiplicity $11-d$, hence pole $13-d$.
The monomial basis for $L_X(11O)$ is $1,x,x^2,x^3,y$.
Thus $d\in\{0,3,6,9,10\}$; the last value would give a unique
pole-three sheet and is excluded. The remaining assertion follows.
