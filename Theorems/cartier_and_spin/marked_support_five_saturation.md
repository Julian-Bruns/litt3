# The marked divisor group has no five-primary torsion

Version1,29 September2026. Use the fixed X:y3=P(x), O, and the
twelve unramified points Z over A=0 in the
[supported-function theorem](supported_units_require_three_characters.md).
Let Gamma be the subgroup of J(X)(k) generated by [R-O], R in Z.

Gamma has order prime to five. More precisely it is annihilated by
the explicit prime-to-five resultant of the Weil polynomial Pi_X(T)
and T8+T4+1. Consequently:

1. If a rational function g has divisor supported on Z union{O},
   and every valuation of g is divisible by five, then g is a fifth
   power in k(X).
2. Let g be regular off O, with zeros only in Z. If its three sheet
   multiplicities at each root alpha_i agree modulo five, let e_i in
   {0,...,4} be their common residue. Then
   g=c product_i(x-alpha_i)^(e_i) h5, where h is regular off O and
   has zeros only in Z. Its pole degree is
   (pole_O(g)-3 sum_i e_i)/5.
3. If, in addition, pole_O(g)<=144, then g is a polynomial in x,
   using the established supported-function classification through28.

The modulo-five hypothesis in2 is on the TOTAL sheet multiplicities,
not merely the constant phase differences in the new trace theorem.
It is not proved for arbitrary comparison poles. Thus3 is a new
conditional reduction, not norm invariance through144 or a common-cover
exclusion. No assertion that J(X)(k) itself has no five-torsion is made.
