# An explicit ordinary, Jacobian-orthogonal parameter in the cubic Kummer diamond

## Status

**Proved by exact point counts; self-check complete.**  The scalar
enumeration is reproduced by
`EXPLICIT_C3_CUBIC_DIAMOND_WEIL_CERTIFICATE.sage`.  It uses no finite-field
matrix rank routine.

This note supplies an arithmetic parameter for the geometric cubic Kummer
diamond.  It is a nonvacuous test family, not a counterexample to the common
etale-cover question: in the diamond the two displayed maps from the common
curve are already finite etale.  The construction, freeness, etaleness, and
joint minimality are proved in
`NONZERO_MIXED_TORSION_CONSTANT_IN_AN_ACTUAL_ETALE_C3_PRODUCT.md`;
the present note supplies the ordinary/Jacobian-orthogonal parameter left
open there.

## 1. The parameter and the three genus-two curves

Let

\[
 k_0=\mathbf F _5[a]/(a^2-a+2),\qquad \lambda=a+3.
\]

Thus `k_0=F_25` and \(\lambda\notin\{0,1\}\).  Let \(X\) be the smooth
projective normalization of

\[
 u^3=t,\qquad v^3={t-1\over t-\lambda}.
                                                        \tag{1.1}
\]

If \(\sigma(u,v)=(\zeta u,v)\) and
\(\tau(u,v)=(u,\zeta v)\), the quotients by
\(\langle\sigma\tau\rangle\) and
\(\langle\sigma\tau^{-1}\rangle\) are respectively

\[
 X_+: z^3={t(t-\lambda)\over t-1},\qquad
 X_-: z^3={t(t-1)\over t-\lambda}.                     \tag{1.2}
\]

Put

\[
 Y: w^3={t(t-1)^2\over t-\lambda}.                     \tag{1.3}
\]

All three curves in (1.2)--(1.3) have genus two.  The cubic Kummer
construction gives a common smooth curve \(W\) with finite etale maps
\(W\to X\) and \(W\to Y\) of degrees three and nine, respectively.

### Theorem 1

For the parameter above,

\[
             Y\text{ is ordinary},\qquad
 \operatorname {Hom}_{\overline{\mathbf F}_5}(JX,JY)=0. \tag{1.4}
\]

In particular, the explicit cubic Kummer diamond can simultaneously have
an ordinary genus-two endpoint and Jacobian-orthogonal endpoints.

## 2. Exact Weil polynomials

For a cubic cover \(z^3=R(t)\), each of the four branch values contributes
one rational point.  At every other \(t\), the number of points in the fiber
is the number of cube roots of \(R(t)\).  Direct scalar enumeration in
\(\mathbf F_{25}\) and \(\mathbf F_{625}\) gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 C&\#C(\mathbf F_{25})&\#C(\mathbf F_{625})\\ \hline
 Y   &28&724\\
 X_+ &22&592\\
 X_- &10&598.
\end{array}                                             \tag{2.1}
\]

For a genus-two curve over \(\mathbf F_q\), if

\[
 P_C(T)=T^4+c_1T^3+c_2T^2+qc_1T+q^2,
\]

then the first two point counts determine \(c_1,c_2\).  With \(q=25\),
(2.1) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_Y(T)&=T^4+2T^3+51T^2+50T+625
             =(T^2+T+25)^2,\\
 P_+(T)&=T^4-4T^3-9T^2-100T+625,\\
 P_-(T)&=T^4-16T^3+114T^2-400T+625
             =(T^2-8T+25)^2.                           \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Since the middle coefficient of \(P_Y\) is \(51\), prime to five, \(JY\)
is ordinary.  Equivalently, \(JY\) is isogenous over \(\mathbf F_{25}\)
to the square of the ordinary elliptic isogeny class

\[
                         T^2+T+25.                      \tag{2.3}
\]

## 3. The geometric Hom certificate

First, the two quotient pullbacks give an isogeny

\[
                         JX_+\times JX_-\longrightarrow JX. \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed, the invariant differential spaces of the two distinct order-three
subgroups both have dimension two.  Their intersection is invariant under
the group generated by those subgroups, namely \(C_3^2\), and hence comes
from \(X/C_3^2\simeq\mathbf P^1\); it is zero.  Their dimensions therefore
sum to \(g(X)=4\).

The middle coefficients \(-9\) and \(114\) in (2.2) are also prime to
five.  Thus \(X_+\) and \(X_-\), and consequently \(X\) via (3.1), are
ordinary as well.

Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(T^2+T+25\).  Then

\[
                  \mathbf Q(\alpha)=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-11}). \tag{3.2}
\]

Moreover \(\mathbf Q(\alpha^n)=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-11})\) for every
\(n\ge1\).  To see this, \(\alpha/\bar\alpha\) lies in this quadratic field,
whose only roots of unity are \(\pm1\), and it is neither \(1\) nor \(-1\).

The roots of \(P_-(T)\) lie in \(\mathbf Q(i)\).  The polynomial \(P_+(T)\)
is irreducible and a root generates

\[
 L=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt7).                         \tag{3.3}
\]

For completeness, over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt7)\) one has

\[
 P_+(T)=
 \bigl(T^2+(-2+3\sqrt7)T+25\bigr)
 \bigl(T^2+(-2-3\sqrt7)T+25\bigr),                     \tag{3.4}
\]

and the two quadratic discriminants are
\(-3(2+\sqrt7)^2\) and \(-3(2-\sqrt7)^2\).  The four
Galois conjugates are distinct, proving both irreducibility and (3.3).
The three quadratic subfields of \(L\) are

\[
          \mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3}),\quad \mathbf Q(\sqrt7),
          \quad\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-21}).                   \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus

\[
 \mathbf Q(\sqrt{-11})\cap\mathbf Q(i)=\mathbf Q,
 \qquad
 \mathbf Q(\sqrt{-11})\cap L=\mathbf Q.                \tag{3.6}
\]

If a nonzero geometric homomorphism existed from either \(JX_+\) or
\(JX_-\) to \(JY\), it would be defined over some finite extension
\(\mathbf F_{25^n}\).  Tate's finite-field isogeny theorem would then give
a Frobenius root \(\beta\) of the relevant polynomial \(P_+\) or \(P_-\)
such that

\[
                            \beta^n=\alpha^n             \tag{3.7}
\]

for one of the two roots \(\alpha\) in (2.3).  But the right side generates
\(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-11})\), whereas the left side belongs respectively to
\(L\) or \(\mathbf Q(i)\), contradicting (3.6).  Hence both Hom groups
vanish.  Equation (3.1) proves (1.4). \(\square\)

## 4. The full Jacobian packet on the common curve

The common curve can be written as the normalization of the
\(C_3^3\)-cover

\[
 u^3=t,\qquad z^3=t-1,\qquad b^3=t-\lambda.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Its four inertia vectors are \(e_1,e_2,e_3,-e_1-e_2-e_3\).  For a
nonzero character \(\chi=(i,j,k)\in\mathbf F_3^3\), put

\[
 r(\chi)=\#\{i,j,k,i+j+k\text{ which are nonzero}\}.
\]

The cyclic quotient attached to the pair \(\{\chi,-\chi\}\) has genus
\(r(\chi)-2\).  Among the thirteen character pairs, the exact distribution
is

\[
 \#\{r=2\}=6,\qquad \#\{r=3\}=4,\qquad \#\{r=4\}=3.     \tag{4.2}
\]

The three \(r=4\) packets are exactly the two factors of \(JX\) and the
factor \(JY\).  Each of the four \(r=3\) quotients is an elliptic curve
with a fixed-point automorphism of order three, hence has \(j=0\).  In
characteristic five it is supersingular.  The six remaining quotients
have genus zero.  The prime-to-five group-algebra decomposition therefore
gives

\[
             JW\ \sim\ JX\times JY\times E_{\rm ss}^{\,4},              \tag{4.3}
\]

over \(\overline{\mathbf F}_5\), where \(E_{\rm ss}\) is the supersingular
\(j=0\) elliptic isogeny class.  In particular

\[
 g(W)=10,\qquad f(W)=6,
 \qquad g(W)-f(W)=4.                                  \tag{4.4}
\]

Because the isogeny in (4.3) has kernel killed by a power of \(3\), it
also identifies the 5-divisible groups.  Hence the four supersingular
elliptic packets give

\[
                              a(W)=4.                  \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the origin belongs to the two-leg restricted Raynaud bad locus on
\(JX\times JY\).  Nevertheless that locus is proper: either abelian leg
has abelian prime-to-five Galois group, so the line-filtration criterion
of `RESTRICTED_RAYNAUD_THETA_SUFFICIENT_CONDITIONS_AND_STABILITY_BOUNDARY.md`
already makes its axis restriction proper.  This is a concrete warning
that properness of the restricted locus is substantially weaker than
ordinarity of the common cover.

## 5. Scope

For \(\lambda\in\mathbf F_5\), no parameter has both desired properties:
at \(\lambda=2,3\), \(Y\) is ordinary but its Weil polynomial equals that
of one of \(X_+,X_-\), while at \(\lambda=4\), \(Y\) is nonordinary.
Passing to \(\mathbf F_{25}\) is therefore substantive, not merely a change
of notation.
